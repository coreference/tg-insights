# Identifying your values and a value action plan

## What this covers

This topic covers the book's three "Identify your values" pages in the Mindfulness section: a definition of values and why they help, reflection questions and a list of example values, a worksheet for sorting values by life area, and a Value Action Plan for putting values into practice. According to the author, discovering your values can be helpful in stressful situations such as work, studying or relationships because values guide your choices and actions [msg #25, #26, #27, pp. 22–24].

## Core ideas

- **Values give meaning and priorities.** Values are your morals, ethics, principles, or what you value and how you aspire to live; they give life meaning and importance and help you define your priorities. Everyone has different values [msg #25, p. 22].
- **Values help under stress.** Knowing and remembering your values helps with making choices, tolerating stressful situations, and responding to your needs accordingly [msg #25, p. 22].
- **Values can differ by life area.** "It's okay if you value different things in different areas of your life!" [msg #26, p. 23]
- **Values become real through actions.** The action plan asks what you can do to prioritise your values — small everyday things or things you want to remember to do more of [msg #27, p. 24].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Values reflection questions
Purpose: questions "to help you identify what's important to you." Answer each in writing or in thought [msg #25, p. 22]:
1. When were you happiest? What were you doing?
2. When were you most proud?
3. When have you been most satisfied and fulfilled?
4. What are your special interests?
5. What do you feel strongly about?
6. Have there been times you have stood up for something?

### Explore the example values
The book offers examples of different values to explore: "See if you identify with some of them or any of them. You can also add your own." The complete list of 18 [msg #25, p. 22]:
1. connection
2. pleasure
3. courage
4. wisdom
5. strength
6. freedom
7. authenticity
8. creativity
9. honesty
10. passion
11. empathy
12. fairness
13. adventure
14. compassion
15. security
16. learning
17. humour
18. kindness

### Identify your values worksheet (by life area)
Purpose: "It might be helpful to identify what your values look like." The worksheet has four boxes to fill in with the values that apply in each area, accepting that they can differ between areas [msg #26, p. 23]:
1. **My social values** — the values that apply in your social life.
2. **My personal values** — the values that apply to you personally.
3. **My political values** — the values that apply in politics.
4. **My work values** — the values that apply at work.

(!) The book gives only the four headings; the glosses after each heading simply restate the area name [msg #26, p. 23].

### Value Action Plan
Purpose: answer "What can you do to prioritise your values in your life?" The actions can be "small everyday things or things you might want to remember to do more of in your life" (the book prints "These actions come be small" [sic]) [msg #27, p. 24].

How to fill it in: the worksheet repeats the same pair of boxes three times, so you can plan for three values [msg #27, p. 24]:
1. **Identify your value** — write one value (for example one you chose from the list or your own).
2. **Identify some actions that align with your value** — write the concrete actions that put that value into practice.
3. Repeat for a second and a third value.

## Detailed insights

### Why values matter in DBT-style coping
The author places values within mindfulness and connects them to everyday stress: in stressful situations "such as work, studying or relationships", values can "help guide your choices and actions". Knowing and remembering them supports three things: making choices, tolerating stressful situations, and responding to your needs accordingly. [msg #25, p. 22].

# Wise Mind: balancing logical mind and emotional mind

## What this covers

This topic covers the book's opening framework in the Mindfulness section: DBT's three states of mind — the logical mind, the emotional mind and the wise mind [msg #21, p. 18]. It describes each state, what each is good for, a diagram of what each mind is "ruled by" [msg #22, p. 19], a worksheet for describing your own three minds [msg #23, p. 20], and four strategies for practising engaging wise mind [msg #24, p. 21]. According to the book it matters because we can get stuck in logical or emotional mind, and while both have strengths, difficulties occur when we remain in either one [msg #21, p. 18].

## Core ideas

- **Three states of mind.** "In DBT, there are three states of mind. These are described as: the logical mind, the emotional mind and the wise mind." (In DBT there are three states of mind: logical, emotional and wise.) [msg #21, p. 18]
- **Wise mind takes the best of both.** The goal of wise mind is to take the best of both worlds from the logical and the emotional mind [msg #21, p. 18].
- **Wise mind is individual.** The author frames wise mind not as one fixed state but as something each person defines: "We all have our own unique wise mind." [msg #21, p. 18]
- **Neither logic nor emotion is the enemy.** Both logical and emotional mind have strengths and benefits; the problem is getting stuck in one and remaining there [msg #21, p. 18].
- **Wise mind can be practised.** Mindfulness, curiosity, compassion and considering your values are offered as ways to engage it [msg #24, p. 21].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### The three states of mind
**Purpose:** to recognise which state of mind you are in, appreciate what each is useful for, and aim for a wise mind that combines both [msg #21, p. 18]. The book notes that "Often, for whatever reason, we can get stuck in our logical mind or emotional mind" (often, for whatever reason, we get stuck in one of them) [msg #21, p. 18]. The diagram on the next page adds adjectives and what each mind is "ruled by" [msg #22, p. 19]; both are merged below.

1. **Logical mind.** "A state of mind that is based on facts, reason and logic." In the logical mind, emotions are not taken into consideration; when using it you might be looking at things analytically [msg #21, p. 18].
   - *Might be good for:* planning, researching, making decisions, analysing, control, grounding, learning skills [msg #21, p. 18].
   - *Diagram descriptors:* cautious, analytical, logical, factual [msg #22, p. 19].
   - *Ruled by:* facts, logic, control [msg #22, p. 19].
2. **Emotional mind.** "A state of mind during which your emotions influence your behaviour and thoughts." In the emotional mind it can be hard to access the logical mind and think clearly; you may act impulsively and intensely [msg #21, p. 18].
   - *Might be good for:* passion, motivation, creativity, empathy, adapting, relationships, communication [msg #21, p. 18].
   - *Diagram descriptors:* impulsive, creative, passionate, mood-dependent [msg #22, p. 19].
   - *Ruled by:* urges, feelings, emotions [msg #22, p. 19].
3. **Wise mind.** "A state of mind that's a combination of both the logical mind and emotional mind." In wise mind "we respect and listen to both our emotional and logical mind" (we respect and listen to both) [msg #21, p. 18].
   - *Might be good for:* communicating, making decisions, responding to events/situations, relationships, balance [msg #21, p. 18].
   - *Diagram descriptors:* "THE MIDDLE PATH" and "INNER WISDOM" [msg #22, p. 19].
   - *Ruled by:* choice, facts, reason, values, emotion [msg #22, p. 19].

Note that "making decisions" appears as a strength of both the logical mind and the wise mind, and "relationships" and "communication/communicating" as strengths of both the emotional mind and the wise mind [msg #21, p. 18].

### Finding Your Wise Mind worksheet
**Purpose:** "Use this space to describe your logical mind, emotional mind and wise mind." — to map how you personally behave in each state, which supports the book's idea that everyone's wise mind is unique [msg #23, #21, pp. 20, 18]. Complete three sentence stems:
1. "WHEN I AM IN LOGICAL MIND, I TEND TO..." (when I am in logical mind, I tend to…) [msg #23, p. 20]
2. "WHEN I AM IN EMOTIONAL MIND, I TEND TO..." (when I am in emotional mind, I tend to…) [msg #23, p. 20]
3. "WHEN I AM IN WISE MIND, I TEND TO..." (when I am in wise mind, I tend to…) [msg #23, p. 20]

### Engaging the Wise Mind — four strategies
**Purpose:** "Below are some strategies you can use to practice engaging your wise mind." (strategies to practise engaging wise mind) [msg #24, p. 21].
1. **Mindfulness.** Notice your feelings, reactions, thoughts and any sensations happening in your body. Observe what is happening "without any judgement or criticism" [msg #24, p. 21].
2. **Curiosity.** Investigate what is happening with curiosity and compassion. Ask yourself: "What is this reaction trying to tell me?" and "What do I need right now?" [msg #24, p. 21]
3. **Compassion.** Practise being compassionate, caring and understanding with yourself, "just like you would a friend". The book adds: "You can even have a conversation with yourself out loud." [msg #24, p. 21]
4. **Consider your values.** "One way to engage the wise mind is to remind yourself of your values." Check: "Does the action, behaviour or choice align with a value?" [msg #24, p. 21]

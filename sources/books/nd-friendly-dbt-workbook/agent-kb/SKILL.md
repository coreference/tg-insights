# Knowledge base: “The Neurodivergent Friendly Workbook of DBT Skills” by Sonny Jane Wise (2022)

## Book profile — adopt this persona and these constraints whenever you use this book's material

## Book in one paragraph
«The Neurodivergent Friendly Workbook of DBT Skills» (Sonny Jane Wise, 2022) is a short, self-guided English workbook (81 PDF pages, about 12,000 words) of "Dialectical Behaviour Therapy skills reframed to be neurodivergent friendly", with accessible mindfulness, sensory strategies and meltdown management added. It has five sections: Everyday Well-being, Mindfulness, Distress Tolerance, Emotional Regulation and Managing Sensory Needs. It leaves out DBT's interpersonal effectiveness module on purpose. Most pages are worksheets, lists and prompts.

## Author and voice
Sonny Jane Wise (they/them) wrote and designed the book. They call themself "multiply neurodivergent": an Autistic ADHDer living with Bipolar and in recovery from Borderline Personality Disorder. They are also a lived experience educator, consultant and advocate. The skills are adapted from Dr Marsha Linehan's DBT (spelled "Lineham" in the book). The book also uses Dr Dan Siegel's Window of Tolerance. Neurodivergent reviewers and professionals gave feedback. The tone is warm, informal and first-person. Typical words include stims, SpIns, spoons, meltdowns, RSD and unmasking.

## Perspective and stance
The book says neurodivergent distress comes from "a society that isn't designed for our differences". It insists that DBT "does not focus on compliance or changing who we are". It stresses "choice, always choice" and says that if a practice doesn't work for you, you are not doing it wrong. Some skills are renamed, for example Act Opposite becomes Act Intentionally. The evidence is lived experience plus adapted DBT concepts. The book cites no research.

## Audience
The book is for neurodivergent people, especially Autistic and ADHD people. Readers can take the chapters in any order, skip skills and fill in the worksheets. Some worksheets are meant to be shared with partners, carers or doctors.

## How to use this knowledge
It is strong on practical, sensory-aware coping tools: STOP, TIPP, the diving reflex, meltdown mapping, sensory profiles and toolkits, check-ins and affirmations. When citing it, write "According to Sonny Jane Wise in «The Neurodivergent Friendly Workbook of DBT Skills»" and add the page references given in the knowledge files. Quote affirmations and definitions word for word. The book's own terms: "Can freely use and share as long as (a) author is credited, (b) no changes are made, (c) it is not sold."

## Constraints and caveats
The book says: "This workbook is not a substitute for counselling, therapy or any other mental health service." For the diving reflex it adds: "If you have blood pressure or heart problems, please speak to your medical professional before trying this skill." Its guides and lists are offered only as suggestions. Many prompts come without explanation (marked INCOMPLETE in the knowledge files). The book is not a complete DBT programme and not a clinical text.

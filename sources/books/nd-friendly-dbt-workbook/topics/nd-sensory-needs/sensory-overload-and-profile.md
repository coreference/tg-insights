# Sensory overload and mapping your sensory profile

## What this covers

This topic explains sensory overload — what it is, why neurodivergent people are more prone to it, how it shows up and how to respond — and provides two worksheets for mapping your own sensory profile [msg #76, #77, #78, pp. 73–75]. According to the book, the best response to overload is reducing or removing the input causing it, which requires understanding your own sensory needs and differences — the purpose of the profile worksheets [msg #76, p. 73].

## Core ideas

- **Sensory processing varies day to day.** Neurodivergent individuals "process sensory information differently every day", and may be hypersensitive or hyposensitive, "or even both, at different times" [msg #76, p. 73].
- **Sensory differences have wide effects.** They can affect how we feel and cope and "can have a significant impact on our lives", including functioning and everyday life "from emotional regulation to getting things done to socialising" [msg #76, p. 73].
- **Society is part of the problem.** Because we live "in a society that is full of sensory input that doesn't necessarily accommodate our needs, we can end up overstimulated from our environments" [msg #76, p. 73].
- **Everyone has limits; neurodivergent people reach them sooner.** It is "normal and common for every human to have limits on the amount of sensory input we need and can handle", but because neurodivergent brains receive and process input differently, "we're more likely to reach our limits and experience sensory overload" [msg #76, p. 73].
- **Respond by reducing input, which requires self-knowledge.** The best response is reducing or removing the contributing input; to do that you need to understand your sensory needs and differences [msg #76, p. 73].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### What sensory overload is
"Sensory overload is what happens when we receive too much input from our senses." (sensory overload is what happens when we get too much sensory input.) The senses involved are the five known ones — visual, touch, smell, taste and sound — and the three internal senses — vestibular, proprioception and interoception [msg #76, p. 73]. When some or even all of these senses receive too much input for the brain to process and handle, "we become overwhelmed which can cause a lot of discomfort, pain and distress" [msg #76, p. 73].

### Signs of Sensory Overload
The book lists eleven signs [msg #76, p. 73]:
1. Irritability [msg #76, p. 73]
2. Restlessness [msg #76, p. 73]
3. Fidgeting [msg #76, p. 73]
4. Increased stimming [msg #76, p. 73]
5. Need to escape or avoid [msg #76, p. 73]
6. Everything is moving too fast [msg #76, p. 73]
7. Difficulty focusing [msg #76, p. 73]
8. Feeling wound up [msg #76, p. 73]
9. Headaches [msg #76, p. 73]
10. Freeze response [msg #76, p. 73]
11. Feeling instantly exhausted [msg #76, p. 73]

### Responding to sensory overload
1. **Reduce or remove the input.** "The best way to respond to sensory overload is by reducing or removing the sensory input that is contributing to the sensory overload." [msg #76, p. 73]
2. **Know your sensory needs first.** To do this "you need to have an understanding of your sensory needs and differences" [msg #76, p. 73].
3. **Build that understanding** by filling in the sensory profile worksheets (below) and exploring ways to manage your sensory differences [msg #76, p. 73].

### My Sensory Profile — rating worksheet (hypersensitivity and hyposensitivity)
**Purpose:** to rate, for each sense, how much your hypersensitivity and your hyposensitivity affect you [msg #77, p. 74]. The page has two parts with the same instructions:
- "FILL IN THE LEVEL OF HYPERSENSITIVITY YOU EXPERIENCE WITH EACH OF THE SENSES" [msg #77, p. 74]
- "FILL IN THE LEVEL OF HYPOSENSITIVITY YOU EXPERIENCE WITH EACH OF THE SENSES" [msg #77, p. 74]

Each part covers eight senses: visual, smell, sound, taste, vestibular, proprioception, interoception and touch [msg #77, p. 74]. Rate each sense on this five-level scale [msg #77, p. 74]:
1. **1 – no impact on my quality of life** [msg #77, p. 74]
2. **2 – occasionally but minimal impact** [msg #77, p. 74]
3. **3 – sometimes but easy to manage** [msg #77, p. 74]
4. **4 – regularly interferes** [msg #77, p. 74]
5. **5 – uses up all my spoons** [msg #77, p. 74]

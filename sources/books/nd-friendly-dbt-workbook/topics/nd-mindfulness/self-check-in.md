# Self check-in: noticing emotions, body, energy, breathing and focus, then acting on it

## What this covers

This topic covers the book's Self Check-in skill from the Mindfulness section: a set of prompts for noticing your emotions, thoughts, mood, energy, breathing, focus and body, followed by a "What next?" page that pairs each area with concrete actions to take. According to the author, "Checking in with ourselves is probably the most helpful thing we can do every day", because it lets you figure out your needs in the moment and use that information to take care of yourself [msg #29, #30, pp. 26–27].

## Core ideas

- **Check in daily to find your needs.** When we check in, we can figure out what our needs are in the moment and use that information to respond to them and take care of ourselves [msg #29, p. 26].
- **Basic and sensory needs both count.** The skill can be used for basic needs — making sure you've had some water or eaten food — and to determine whether you need a sensory break or support [msg #29, p. 26].
- **Noticing leads to acting.** The check-in is paired with a menu of actions for each area, with space to write your own [msg #30, p. 27].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Self check-in prompts
Purpose: "You can use the prompts below to guide your self check-in." Work through them in order, noticing (and optionally writing) your answer to each [msg #29, p. 26]:
1. **What emotions am I feeling right now?** [msg #29, p. 26]
2. **What are these feelings trying to tell me?** [msg #29, p. 26]
3. **How is my body reacting?** [msg #29, p. 26]
4. **What am I thinking right now?** [msg #29, p. 26]
5. **How is my mood?** — sub-questions: how am I feeling? are my emotions fleeting or long lasting? [msg #29, p. 26]
6. **How is my energy?** — sub-questions: am I tired, energetic or average? do I need to eat something or rest? [msg #29, p. 26]
7. **How is my breathing?** — sub-questions: am I breathing fast or slow? am I exhaling from my stomach or chest? [msg #29, p. 26]
8. **How is my focus?** — sub-questions: am I able to concentrate or am I distracted? am I able to focus for a long or short time? [msg #29, p. 26]
9. **How is my body doing?** — sub-questions: do I have any tension or pain? do I need to rest my body? [msg #29, p. 26]

### What next? action table
Purpose: once you've checked in, choose an action that matches what you noticed. The page is headed "What next?" and includes a "Write your own" space for adding your own actions. The complete table [msg #30, p. 27]:

1. **How is my mood?** → emotional regulation · have a nap · take a break · talk to a friend [msg #30, p. 27]
2. **How is my energy?** → eat something · have a nap · drink water · sensory output [msg #30, p. 27]
3. **How is my breathing?** → practice mindfulness · stim · use senses to soothe · breathing exercises [msg #30, p. 27]
4. **How is my focus?** → have a break · use a fidget toy · change of scenery · make it a game [msg #30, p. 27]
5. **How is my body?** → take your meds · have a nap · stim · stretch [msg #30, p. 27]
6. **My sensory needs?** → dim the lighting · turn off sounds · stim away · do heavy work for input [msg #30, p. 27]

"Write your own" — add your personal actions for any area [msg #30, p. 27].

## Detailed insights

### How the two pages work together
The prompts page is for noticing; the "What next?" page turns each noticed state into a choice of response. Mood, energy, breathing, focus and body appear on both pages; the action page adds a separate row for sensory needs, matching the author's statement that the skill can be used "to determine whether you need a sensory break or support." Several actions recur across areas — "have a nap" appears for mood, energy and body, and "stim" appears for breathing, body and (as "stim away") sensory needs [msg #29, #30, pp. 26–27].

# Ethics and safety in inner work: choosing teachers and practitioners

## What this covers

This file collects what the deep mind channel taught (2019–2024) about ethics and safety in inner work: what transformational programs are and when to attend them; a simple definition of ethics; why trauma makes ethics and safety indispensable; the risks of neo-tantra and other "breakthrough" methods (retraumatization, abuse, dependence); why "energy" is not acceptable as operational client language; how non-experts should speak and write about psychology; how to choose teachers via benchmarks, lineage and education; and the red flags of pseudo-gurus and cults, illustrated by the Bikram and Artur Sita cases. According to the author, the field is full of unqualified people, and people learn to tell good from bad only "через боль и потерянные время и деньги" (through pain and lost time and money) [msg #440], so ethics must be studied and checked by the client before entrusting oneself to someone [msg #1174].

This part collects the channel author's posts arguing that ethics is the "cornerstone" of inner work and giving readers concrete ways to tell safe specialists from unsafe ones: phrases and attitudes that are red flags, the "operational language" test, the quasi-religious dynamics of media gurus and "instant teachers", the blind spot of leaders who deny their role, and a catalogue of body practices with a heightened risk of retraumatization. According to the author, an unethical setup gives no sustainable result but brings risks from the very start, and with today's knowledge of the nervous system and trauma, ignorance of the basics is either amateurism or manipulation [msg #1369, #1610].

Four 2026 posts by the channel author Max Rodin extend the existing topic in three directions. First, a practical participant's checklist for group practices (retreats, neo-tantra, somatic and gender circles): seven green flags of a qualified facilitator and nine red flags at which the author leaves [msg #1900]. Second, a first-hand, evidence-based critique of family constellations, separating legitimate spatial-metaphor work from the claim that a "field" gives knowledge [msg #1907]. Third, two posts on the ethics of communication and public perception: unethical recruitment as a preview of an unethical process [msg #1930], and why everything in inner work gets labelled "esoteric" plus four criteria for telling sound practice from bullshit [msg #1981].

Why it matters according to the source: on the post-Soviet space traumatized people are the majority; such a person goes into a process to feel better but often makes themselves worse, and retraumatization is "a very real damage for the nervous system and a person's life" [msg #1900]. Demand for group formats has grown faster than facilitator qualification, and the market is such that participants have to monitor the ethics of a process themselves [msg #1900].

Relation to the existing knowledge file: these posts extend *Three mechanisms of retraumatization* and *Body practices with elevated retraumatization risk* (#1799) with a concrete in-the-room checklist; extend *Operational language as a green flag* (#1602–#1611) by applying the "field" critique to constellations; extend *Red flags of pseudo-gurus and cults* (#1174) and *Quasi-religiosity* (#1668) with guru-dynamics and pressure-in-recruitment flags; and extend *Choosing teachers* (#1100, #440) with four compact criteria. No direct contradiction with earlier parts was found; see *Caveats* for nuances.

## Core ideas

- **Ethics is the key criterion for any inner-work program.** Specialists must be trauma-informed, intend to benefit the client, and not impose their single truth — "No guru, please." [msg #21]
- **Trauma makes ethics and safety not merely nice but necessary** in any work with people — from management to therapy, mindfulness practices, open dialogues, banya or tantra. [msg #440]
- **Professional work with people requires education and regular verification by a professional community** of one's skills and adequacy; a single insight from a book or retreat does not entitle one to teach. [msg #440]
- **Neo-tantra does not heal trauma and often retraumatizes**; trauma is healed by trauma therapy plus whatever restores the nervous system, while ethical group practices of contact, rhythm and synchrony support healing. [msg #719]
- **"Energy" is not operational language**: it is vague, so client work built on it becomes unethical and shifts responsibility for lack of results onto the client. [msg #878]
- **Working with the psyche is as dangerous as (or more dangerous than) work on physical health**, so teachers deserve the care we give to choosing a neurosurgeon (Lobsang Tenpa). [img #769]
- **Choose with benchmarks**: people pick poor specialists because they have never experienced a really good one; seek the best teachers you can reach, with lineage and education. [msg #1100]
- **Authority creates power**: anyone with an audience who speaks confidently beyond their competence spreads bullshit and manipulates, even if unconsciously. [msg #1143, #693]
- **Systems that promise results without effort usually end badly**; focus on money, coercive payment, gender-role content and isolation are cult red flags. [msg #1174]

- **Ethics is binary and foundational.** A process is either ethical or not — "no half-tones". Without ethics any developmental process (therapy, coaching, neo-tantra, even corporate training) collapses; see *Ethics as the cornerstone* [msg #1369].
- **There is a known "hand-washing" baseline.** Like nutrition science, inner work has disputes but also a consensus; the deeper the processes, the wider the baseline one must know [msg #1369].
- **Clarity of language is an ethical issue.** Vague language creates a power asymmetry and dependence; operational language lets the client check, calibrate and choose [msg #1605, #1609, #1611].
- **Power that is not named is still power.** Quasi-religiosity and "I am not a teacher" disclaimers are two ways influence operates while responsibility is evaded [msg #1668, #1706].
- **Influence creates a duty of care.** Responsibility is defined by real influence, not by the label of the role [msg #1706].
- **Body work can retraumatize without any talk about trauma**, through three physiological mechanisms; trauma-informed training is today's adequate standard for providers [msg #1799].

- **Unpleasant is normal; falling out of the window of tolerance is not.** The danger in group practice is not discomfort but a state where the nervous system leaves its window of tolerance and starts surviving instead of stabilising and widening its range — often invisible from outside [msg #1900].
- **Holding the boundary is the facilitator's professional job, but in today's market the participant must check.** Hence a green/red-flag checklist [msg #1900].
- **Trauma-informedness is now part of the professional standard** for anyone leading practices: training, supervision, knowing what to do when a person is overwhelmed [msg #1900].
- **Stopping is not weakness.** A participant who stops a practice is training exactly the regulation they came for [msg #1900].
- **Constellations: spatial metaphor is fine, the "field" is not.** Externalising the inner world is a century-old working mechanism; claiming a "field" yields knowledge through untrained substitutes and a facilitator in a position of power is unverified and, per a 2026 review, unsupported by evidence [msg #1907].
- **"Possibly helped someone, certainly harmed someone."** In a Hungarian randomized study, 36% of participants reported negative changes one month after a one-day constellation seminar [msg #1907].
- **How you are invited into inner work previews how you will be worked with.** Blurred roles and pressure at entry mean the same inside the process, where you are more vulnerable [msg #1930].
- **The "esoteric" label sticks because the field is unregulated and full of bullshit**; the professional community has to draw the boundary itself or bloggers will draw it for them [msg #1981].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Transformational programs: what they give, when they help, when not to go
Purpose: to understand what a "transformational" program is and decide whether to attend one. The author (Max Rodin) defines transformational programs as those "Затрагивающие достаточно глубокие слои личности, чтобы привести к серьезным изменениям в жизни" (touching deep enough layers of personality to lead to serious changes in life). They transmit knowledge less and instead create a space in which one can live through an important experience — experience often impossible in ordinary conditions yet very needed for health and moving forward. [msg #21]

Examples of such experiences (all four from the source):
1. High-quality, correct and multifaceted feedback. [msg #21]
2. Initiation — a form of socialization and growing up that, per the author, has been almost completely lost in modern Russia. [msg #21]
3. The experience of unconditional, non-judgmental love and acceptance. [msg #21]
4. The experience of honest open communication in which you do not have to show off ("не надо выделываться"). [msg #21]

When it is useful (three situations):
1. When you need to jump to the next level in life. [msg #21]
2. When you are fed up with suffering for various reasons and cannot find a way out. [msg #21]
3. If you want to develop and improve quality of life by mastering new techniques and perspectives. [msg #21]

The author's conclusion: in his view it is always useful — *if* you have psychological and time resources. It is not useful to go from one program straight to another, or to attend while in an active phase of grief or mental disorder. [msg #21]

Group vs individual work: the author ignored the group format for many years because he was in personal therapy and meditating regularly. After attending Holoscendence (Холосценденция), a "therapeutic+" group in Ken Wilber's integral approach, he realized the value of the group: "Группа - катализатор психических процессов" (the group is a catalyst of psychic processes). With proper facilitation, emotions, triggers and traumas surface much faster, and working through them also goes faster. [msg #21]

No strict boundaries: any group program works with several levels of the psyche and aims at multifaceted work with the personality; likewise in one's own development one has to work with therapeutic tasks, states of consciousness and goals together. Example: you cannot become a good facilitator without working through your anger. Still, the author draws a conditional line between psychotherapeutic programs (aimed at healing past traumas) and coaching programs (aimed at life results and a better future). [msg #21]

The key criterion is ethics: be sure the program's specialists are trauma-informed, intend to benefit the client, and do not act from their own single truth, planting it in the client's head. "No guru, please." [msg #21]

### Simple definition of ethics and the attention triangle (Alexandra Vilvovskaya)
Guest author Alexandra Vilvovskaya (embodiment community leader) explains ethics in relationships and work. [msg #433]

Context — the environment is changing: more people say "Не надо так со мной обращаться!" (Don't treat me like that!) or that they are uncomfortable / not treated decently. A generation has grown up with somewhat better (sometimes hypertrophied) personal boundaries; the employee's profile and expectations of what should and should not happen at work are changing. Other generations may misunderstand this and conclude that young people find emotional communication hard. Vilvovskaya ties this to objective processes: people en masse are dealing with their trauma and mental health and better understand which treatment leads to problems; and tolerance for violence in any form is declining worldwide (she links to Steven Pinker's book on declining violence). [msg #433]

Words like mindfulness toward people (внимательность к людям), ethics, or trauma-informedness can be debated, but in any version they describe understanding the other person and the quality of attitude toward them. Behind the smart words is something simple — three statements:
1. "Я знаю, вижу и чувствую, что другой человек — это живой человек." (I know, see and feel that the other person is a living person.)
2. "Я не хочу, чтобы ему(ей) было плохо." (I don't want him/her to feel bad.) — rendered in the source with bold breaks: "**Я не хочу**, **чтобы ему(ей) было плохо**".
3. "Стараюсь поступать в соответствие с этим." (I try to act accordingly.) [msg #433]

In organizations and society this lens lets you notice what reactions your actions produce in others. Example: if you shout at a person, they become scared or angry and will eventually find an available way to pay you back. [msg #433]

"Do nothing so as not to harm?" — No. An ethical person does not neurotically think all the time about not hurting others; we need not force ourselves to feel only positive emotions toward everyone, nor bottle up anger, irritation and fear. People inevitably cause each other discomfort and emotions. But an ethical person can *know and take into account* their own state and others' states, and that their words and actions affect these states. [msg #433]

The attention triangle: "Есть я, есть другие и есть внимание, которое я уделяю себе и другим." (There is me, there are others, and there is the attention I give to myself and others.) To be ethical: train attention and widen the range of words and actions. [msg #433]

In practice it is harder: ethics requires training attention to self and others, developing sensitivity, building state-management skills (so as not to blurt something out in anger or under stress), and understanding how people work, what triggers them and what results from their triggered reactions. At work it requires accounting for colleagues' different contexts — not only race, skin colour and sexual preference but often simpler things like time zones. So being more ethical means time, effort and choice; making that choice, we perhaps align with a natural flow of life toward less violence, more integration and resilience of society, and show healthy adaptivity to environmental change. Signature line: "Этика требует внимания и чувствительности. Но вообще-то этика — это просто." (Ethics requires attention and sensitivity. But actually ethics is simple.) [msg #433]

### Basic professional ethics and the seven signs of chaos in the Russian inner-work field
After watching the film *The Wisdom of Trauma* about Gabor Maté and the surrounding conference, the author concludes that trauma makes ethics and safety a necessary, most important link in any work with a person — management, all kinds of therapy, mindfulness practices, processes of psychic and emotional opening such as open dialogues, banya or tantra. From the scientific data on trauma, trauma-informedness has become a key trend in Western helping professions. [msg #440]

The Russian field, by contrast, contains people without certifications or diplomas, without supervision and professional communities, people guaranteeing results and helping everyone with everything. The author says this field often ignores not only trauma but more basic ethics: "для профессиональной работы с человеком нужно образование и регулярная верификация сообществом навыков и твоей адекватности" — rendered with bold breaks as "**для профессиональной работы с человеком нужно образование** и **регулярная верификация сообществом**" (professional work with a person requires education and regular verification by the community of skills and your adequacy). [msg #440]

The insight trap (mechanism): in inner work, after a book or retreat you often get an insight that is truly huge for you, since your life really looked different before it. You then think you understand everything and — the scariest part — have the right to share it with others. This is a delusion that good education solves: it draws the boundaries of your not-knowing, explains that development takes years and there will be many insights, and that what is needed is not a single insight or experience but a system. [msg #440]

Seven manifestations of the "bacchanalia" that will continue while people do not examine specialists' quality:
1. Psychologists without diplomas. [msg #440]
2. Emotion-work groups led by people not prepared for it in any way. [msg #440]
3. Advertising in business communities of tantra specialists for whom no certifications can be found, nor the trauma-informedness so needed in tantra. [msg #440]
4. Posts and texts on human development and psychology containing unsystematic nonsense due to not knowing the limits of one's own ignorance. [msg #440]
5. Retreats by teachers without their own teachers and traditions; vipassanas by non-Buddhists. [msg #440]
6. Trainings with very loosely adapted original practices, without the trainer stating this fact. [msg #440]
7. Breaking ("ломающие") motivational trainings, which only reinforce trauma and the pattern of violence toward oneself and others. [msg #440]

The author's stance: he does not want to point fingers ("all adults"), but staying silent is strange; deep mind builds its own safe products and informs about ethics, and he wonders whether to describe unethical cases (with or without names) and explain why they are bad and what they threaten. People will eventually figure things out, but through pain and lost time and money — "Сам знаю" (I know it myself). [msg #440]

### Neo-tantra and trauma: how resistance-trauma is healed ethically
The author addresses a recurring community debate: do tantric trainings heal trauma or create new ones? [msg #719]

What tantra is not: mass consciousness wrongly considers tantra a "yoga of sex". Sexual practices were never the goal or even central; in some schools they were only an auxiliary means to reach transcendent states (broad states of consciousness beyond the persona). Neo-tantra on today's trainings usually has little to do with original teachings — rather a fairly chaotic set of exercises exploring contact, intimacy and one's relational patterns plus some ecstatic practices. Sometimes it rests on sound foundations (tradition, science, ethics) and the teachers know how to work with these very subtle, deep topics without harming or retraumatizing people; "Иногда [чаще] — нет" (Sometimes [more often] — not). [msg #719]

The typical harmful scenario: roughly several times a month the author hears stories of participants of body-relational retreats being "thrown into trauma" (a state of intense emotional and bodily pain, abandonment, helplessness, inability to behave adequately) while the facilitator says "это твое сопротивление, надо просто через это пройти, продолжай" (this is your resistance, you just have to go through it, keep going). [msg #719]

What that "resistance" often is: past trauma, which people have already learned to heal ethically and safely in three ways:
1. Building knowledge about the specifics of the nervous system. [msg #719]
2. Developing self-regulation skills. [msg #719]
3. Accumulating enough psychological resource to cross the frightening edge. [msg #719]

Otherwise the person is simply forced to meet what they may not be ready for, and again faces an event they cannot live through to the end — retraumatization, which can end in days, months or years thrown out of life. [msg #719]

Balanced verdict on neo-tantra: it can give many pleasant sensations; body exercises are often rich in insights; contact is useful and necessary for healing the nervous system. But passing some edges and getting euphoric relief there does not make neo-tantra a good trauma-healing method — while it is easy to consider it a frequent method of retraumatization. Tantra does not treat trauma but can give a feeling of going beyond it and strong momentary relief. That is good insofar as it gives an exit from the suffering of trauma; bad if it does not lead to trauma therapy, because then we do not actually heal but easily become dependent on a method that gives the feeling of a breakthrough. [msg #719]

Mechanisms:
- Ecstatic breakthrough methods work like a magnet for people with trauma, because the emotional charge of the past constantly seeks release and relief. [msg #719]
- Bad treatment in childhood, which becomes the basis of developmental trauma, makes it hard for adults to understand their boundaries and what behaviour toward them is acceptable — hence the many abuses observed in neo-tantra. [msg #719]

The positive counterpart: if you don't experiment with edges and intimacy in dubious places, group work is fully indicated for healing. Any ethical and safe practices of contact, rhythm and synchrony are definitely recommended to support trauma therapy and may even be more significant for growth and healing: connectedness with others gives the nervous system the sense of regulation that may have been lost through traumatic childhood experience or difficult events. [msg #719]

### Three principles for non-psychologists speaking publicly about psychology
Purpose: leaders increasingly touch psychological topics in talks because society is rapidly psychologizing; how to do it without overstepping competence, talking nonsense, or being misunderstood. Deep mind uses these rules to prepare its conference speakers to speak ethically about inner work, thinking, emotions and mindfulness. [msg #693]

1. **Check professional words and facts with experts.** Speaking about inner experience is hard for two reasons: emotions and states often form before language structures, so they feel hard to express; and we simply don't know the right words or their exact meanings, not being psychologists or scientists. Using wrong terms is fine if we warn about our position (see principle 2), but it is better for everyone if we help form a common vocabulary — e.g., stop calling trance states from holotropic breathwork "mindfulness". [msg #693]
2. **Speak from expertise in your own experience, not in psychology or science.** Because of the belief that a speaker must be smart, entrepreneurs, product managers and other non-psychologists tend to confidently explain neurotransmitters, brain structure or emotions based on a few books. A non-scientist's account of science often looks bad and becomes plainly unethical. Be smart within your competence — at minimum not to look ridiculous, at maximum not to spread inaccurate or misleading knowledge ("плохая карма!" — bad karma!). If you have read so much you feel like an expert without a diploma, take the position "я не врач, но пациент-эксперт" (I'm not a doctor but a patient-expert) — saying so yourself, not when asked, and explaining what your knowledge is built on. [msg #693]
3. **Personal subjective experience put into clear words is the most valuable part of a talk.** When preparing a story of inner discoveries, slide words may seem banal and you may worry whether it matters to listeners with different life experience. This is a paradox of humanities knowledge: it seems obvious once formulated and proven. Sharing knowledge, life hacks and frameworks matters, but none compares in depth or impact with "чудом человеческой жизни, которая сама себя осознает" (the miracle of a human life that becomes aware of itself); simple conclusions about life "from inside", made over years, can touch and change more than anything else. Have the courage to show them, and reveal these "simple conclusions" through examples and story so others understand them. [msg #693]

The author notes the field is at the beginning and ethics here is only forming, but attention to the question can already help avoid unnecessary suffering and inaccuracies. [msg #693]

### Two reasons "energy" is not operational language
Context: a team asked deep mind whether "energy" is a scientific concept and whether team development can focus on it. [msg #878]

Scientific sense: energy is a scientific concept — mitochondria produce physical energy in the body, affecting wellbeing and working capacity; there are researched factors that lower or raise mitochondrial production; subjectively people can mostly say whether they feel energetic. Feelings and states fit the scientific picture of the world. [msg #878]

Yet deep mind trainers do not use the word *energy* in client work, because it does not belong to operational language: it is not understandable, so client work becomes unethical — it is unclear what is discussed, where the work is going, what experience the client describes and how to check the result. Such language helps hang responsibility for lack of results on the client and is characteristic of an unethical field of methods and people deep mind does not want to belong to. [msg #878]

The two reasons:
1. **"Energy" is used often and in any contexts, including pseudo-scientific or entirely unscientific ones.** In any group there will be people developing sexual energy in neo-tantra or kundalini yoga, people whose energy "flows" or "stalls" in conversation, people feeling the energy of life, sport, body, universe — everyone with their own fixed ideas or mix. The goal is for the client to understand more clearly what is happening to them and why, not to become dependent on the facilitator after "энергию ему раскрыли" (his energy was opened) without understanding how. [msg #878]
2. **"Energy" is completely unclear; "energeticness" is somewhat clearer but still describes a composite experience, so people misunderstand each other.** Tension or relaxation in the body, fatigue, anxiety are direct states that are easier to explain, understand and agree on. Energy and energeticness are always an individual mix of focus, relaxation, presence or absence of pain and so on. [msg #878]

Hence "decent" psychologists, coaches and trainers mostly try not to appeal to energy today. But deep mind does not ban words or shy away from them (jokingly, except "egregores"): there are cases where this word is clearest for a client to start with and in their context. [msg #878]

### When "energy" is acceptable vs suspicious — decision rule
- Acceptable in historically established traditions where it is a customary term, e.g. tantra and yoga — though modern trauma-informed versions have learned to use more operational language so teacher and student are really on the same page. [msg #878]
- In sexual neo-tantra energy requires clear definition, because neo-tantra is not a tradition and often offers an untested author's mix of ideas and practices instead of traditional ones. [msg #878]
- Acceptable in a relatively scientific approach: example Dave Asprey, a Silicon Valley entrepreneur culturally close to modern teams, who built a business around increasing energy (his book exists in Russian — the linked page is a brain-biohacking book), with a clear understanding that it is about mitochondria and our energeticness. [msg #878]
- Suspicious: if a facilitator "opens the group's energy" or a retreat "connects us with its source", the author suspects "небезопасной и непрофессиональной херней" (unsafe and unprofessional crap). Then the question to oneself: did I check the trainer for modern education for client work? Or why am I choosing methods that don't give me a clear image of the result? [msg #878]

Bottom line: energy may be fine if used in a traditional or scientific context and we as clients understand this. [msg #878]

### Two harms of non-experts writing with authority, and remedies
Case: a sizeable channel by a non-psychologist published a confident psychological post claiming "дружба — преувеличенный социальный конструкт, который для человека равен примерно комфортной городской среде" (friendship is an exaggerated social construct, roughly equal for a person to a comfortable urban environment). The author wrote a 5-point rebuttal, highlighting wrong conclusions and giving facts from interpersonal neurobiology, which shows how the human mind arises intersubjectively, including through friendship. Substantive comments were quickly deleted, as was the discussion that the author of the post is in a position of power they seem unaware of. [msg #1143]

In a few years people of very different background and motivation gained access to (often large) audiences; their lack of modern ethics creates two problems for the "consumers of meaning":
1. **Spreading wrong concepts — multiplying bullshit.** We transmit our distortions rather than a relatively objective truth rooted at least somewhat in a systematic scientific picture; others believe it, form a false world picture and make bad decisions on it. In contemplative traditions this counts as bad karma, since we harm by adding illusions to already unclear minds. [msg #1143]
2. **Multiplying unethicality via unethical communication and abuse of privilege.** The author believes it is usually not psychopathic malicious manipulation but an unaware impulse from one's own ethical immaturity. Presenting something as truth from a position of authority without due grounds is in effect manipulating the audience, usually done automatically to build extra authority — being knowledgeable earns loyalty, approval and trust for oneself and one's channel. Even with 1000 readers, the phenomenon of power (or, less triggeringly, influence) arises: words weighted by audience authority are not equal to an average reader's words. [msg #1143]

What to do:
- **Authors:** first, try not to bullshit — don't write from an authoritative position about what you have no confirmed competence in. If you still want to reason about it, either remind the audience of your non-competence so the lack of expertise is transparent, or get supervised by an expert and say so (the author notes he himself decided to write once he was supervising texts). Apologize and admit mistakes if you bullshitted. [msg #1143]
- **Authors:** learn modern ethics, which helps you see your privileges and position of power and not violate consent even in information space; without understanding your position and impact, abuses and manipulations arise, harmful for ourselves too. [msg #1143]
- **Community:** point out to authors when they are unaware of privilege and power or are incorrect/non-transparent about their expertise; unsubscribe if they refuse to take responsibility. [msg #1143]

### Two typical cases of misunderstanding ethics
The author says he most often sees problems with understanding ethics in two cases [msg #1369]:
1. **Participants who unconsciously get drawn into revealing/opening but unethical processes.** However one explains, some do not want to hear rational arguments. A secondary gain («вторичная выгода», secondary gain) has already appeared, or the person is attached to a dynamic in which they are being rocked («раскачивают») — which in fact only reproduces and exploits the trauma [msg #1369].
2. **"Specialists" who themselves act unethically.** Example: a therapist confidently explaining the client's experience through his own theories. This looks like "expert interpretation" but is essentially manipulation: he does not actually know what the person is experiencing, yet the confident tone and a coherent concept give a position of power that creates dependence — and that dependence is profitable. Sometimes this is simply immaturity and the specialist's shadows; sometimes it is conscious manipulation, "and that is truly frightening". Such specialists often do not understand the point of ethics at all, or consider it conditional [msg #1369].

### Markers of an ethical vs unethical specialist
Red-flag phrases the author hears from unethical specialists — "from them you need to walk away quickly and far" («От них надо быстро и далеко уходить») [msg #1369]:
- «У меня нет диплома психолога, потому что я не хочу засорять сознание чужими концепциями» (*"I don't have a psychology degree because I don't want to clutter my mind with other people's concepts"*) [msg #1369].
- «Нет универсальной этики — всё относительно» (*"There is no universal ethics — everything is relative"*) [msg #1369].

What an ethical specialist will **not** say [msg #1369]:
- "I will definitely help" or "it always works with me". Reason: the result is the client's zone of responsibility; the specialist cannot control how the client lives between sessions, whether they apply insights, and what resources they have.
- An ethical body practitioner will not say: «Размотало на интимной практике — твоя ответственность. Мы же тут взрослые, тебя предупредили и ты решил участвовать» (*"You got unravelled at an intimate practice — your responsibility. We're adults here, you were warned and you decided to take part"*). Reason: body work is not just an emotional process; it is an intervention into the nervous system, and accompanying the client through retraumatization is part of professional responsibility.

Further markers from other posts: vague non-operational language (see *Operational language*), interpreting participants' experience from a position of authority, charisma valued over training, and disclaimers that deny the role (see *Influence creates a duty of care*) [msg #1611, #1668, #1706].

### Operational language as a green flag (10-card carousel)
Purpose: a "most important marker" to orient by in any individual or group process involving the body, emotions and inner systems; the author's thesis is that our coach, therapist, meditation teacher and tantra teacher must speak so that we can really understand and check their words — otherwise the benefit is doubtful and the interventions are often unsafe. The cover card states that safety and results begin with the specialist's words [msg #1602, img #1602]. The author invites specialists to repost the cards to grow a culture of inner work.

1. **Definition.** Operational language is language that can be verified by action. It describes observable phenomena — body reactions, emotions, behaviour, thoughts, context. It clarifies rather than confuses [img #1603].
2. **Contrast example.** «ЧЕЛОВЕК НАПРЯГСЯ, ОПУСТИЛ ПЛЕЧИ, ПЕРЕСТАЛ СМОТРЕТЬ» — ЭТО ОПЕРАЦИОНАЛЬНО (*"The person tensed up, dropped their shoulders, stopped looking" — that is operational*). «В ПОЛЕ ПОЯВИЛАСЬ ТЯЖЕСТЬ» — ПОЭТИЧНО НО НЕЯСНО (*"Heaviness appeared in the field" — poetic but unclear*) [img #1604].
3. **Why it is a question of ethics.** Unclear language creates an asymmetry of power: the client is forced to trust interpretation rather than facts, which substitutes authority for exploration. Instead of developing autonomy the client becomes dependent on the "knowing" specialist. Dependence is the opposite of maturity in general and specifically of the client's agency («субъектности клиента»), which any helping practitioner is supposed to serve. Clarity and transparency of language are therefore not just a form of care but a necessary foundation of a helping and developmental position in a relationship [img #1605].
4. **Worked example: the word "field".** In most cases «поле» (*"field"*) is non-operational. Originally it meant the context of interaction, but now it is applied anywhere and anyhow: «Держу поле» (*"I'm holding the field"*), «В поле стало тревожно» (*"It became anxious in the field"*), «Мне нравится твое поле» (*"I like your field"*). These can mean anything — from the specialist's own emotions to the group's reactions; their meaning is foggy and unverifiable [img #1606].
5. **The key questions.** Can a specialist help a client, and can the client rely on the specialist's methods, if they do not clearly understand what they are talking about? Can they verify the result of inner work if there is no clarity in discussing the path and results? [img #1607]
6. **Examples of non-operational language** — words that create an illusion of meaning but describe nothing concretely [img #1608]:
   - «в поле появилась энергия» (*"energy appeared in the field"*)
   - «держу пространство» (*"I'm holding space"*)
   - «пошёл процесс» (*"the process has started"*)
   - «чувствую поток» (*"I feel the flow"*)
   - «вышла травма» (*"the trauma came out"*)
   - «энергия заблокировалась» (*"the energy got blocked"*)
7. **What the operational equivalent sounds like.** Instead of «система сопротивляется» (*"the system is resisting"*): «Ты замолчал, перестал смотреть в экран - кажется, тема вызывает напряжение» (*"You fell silent, stopped looking at the screen — it seems the topic is causing tension"*). This is a different level of responsibility for the process: the coaching client or group participant can understand the specialist's interventions, calibrate them for themselves and make adult choices about how to relate to them [img #1609].
8. **Maturity of language = maturity of the profession.** Poetry can inspire, but when it interferes with transparency it becomes a form of power. Today, when we know how the nervous system and trauma work, non-operational language is a sign of unethical practice and unprofessionalism in any individual or group inner work that touches a person's body, emotions and inner systems [img #1610].
9. **Rule for the client.** Operational language is a green flag. If your specialist uses unclear language like «поток» (*"flow"*) or «энергия пошла» (*"the energy started moving"*) and you do not understand what is actually meant, that is a red flag. «Неясность не признак духовности, а показатель некомпетентности» (*"Vagueness is not a sign of spirituality but an indicator of incompetence"*) [img #1611].

### Quasi-religiosity in inner work and "instant teachers"
Purpose: to describe a layer of practitioners that, according to the author, actively produces quasi-religious forms of inner work which are better avoided [msg #1668].

### Influence creates a duty of care (the "I am not a teacher" blind spot)
Purpose: to evaluate leaders who say «Я не учитель. Я просто делюсь тем, что мне передали. Это не обучение, это не ретрит.» (*"I'm not a teacher. I'm just sharing what was passed on to me. This is not training, this is not a retreat."*) [msg #1706].
1. **The claim.** Sometimes this sounds like a careful position, but often it is a simple way to shed the complexity of a role one has, for whatever reason, ended up in [msg #1706].
2. **Principle: responsibility follows real influence.** Modern ethics of inner work shows that responsibility is defined not by the name of the role (or its absence) but by the real influence arising in the relationship. If someone gathers people around practices touching body, emotions, trauma, sets the frame of the process, and interprets participants' experience, they are already in a position of influence [msg #1706].
3. **Mechanism: transference.** Participants automatically begin to perceive the leader as a figure of orientation, safety or knowledge. In inner work this can be multiplied: attention to inner experience and contact with vulnerable material lower habitual psychological defences and increase dependence on leading figures [msg #1706].
4. **Rule: if there is influence, there is a duty of care.** Not total and not parental, but the responsibility to know boundaries, understand the risks of traumatisation and the principles of how the nervous system works; to notice what happens to people inside the process; and to be able to integrate experience. "Not only all this, but this is the base" [msg #1706].
5. **Two ways leaders respond as culture matures faster than their qualification.** The field develops fast: research on the risks of intensive practices, conversations about boundaries, responsibility, group dynamics; the community demands more adequate qualification. Some leaders grow into it — they study ethics and crises of practice, get supervision, and stop entering zones where their competence is insufficient. Others try to deconstruct the role and formats with words: lacking qualification and facing expectations of responsibility, they defend themselves by disowning the role. This distance seems to lower external demands but does not reduce real influence, so responsibility gets blurred exactly where it is most needed [msg #1706].
6. **Evidence the author invokes.** Research on crisis states in practice shows that in difficult states participants still turn to and lean on the leader even if the relationship was never formally defined. «Психика ориентируется не на дисклеймеры, а на фигуры опоры» (*"The psyche orients not to disclaimers but to figures of support"*) [msg #1706].
7. **When the disclaimer is healthy.** Modern standards do not require the leader to be a therapist or lifelong mentor, but they require explicit transparency and responsibility. Role boundaries are healthy only when they structure responsibility rather than help avoid it. "I'm not a teacher / this isn't a retreat" can express a mature position that avoids excess projections and the leader's ego inflation — when the person clearly marks the limits of their competence, does not interpret participants' personal experience, and does not become a support in the transformation process [msg #1706].
8. **When it is a warning sign.** Often, attempts to disown the role or format indicate insufficient reflection on power in relationships of vulnerability — power that inevitably arises in bodily, emotional, psychological, neo-tantric, sexual and meditative practices. «Любое пространство трансформации априори становится пространством влияния» (*"Any space of transformation a priori becomes a space of influence"*) [msg #1706].
9. **The alternative stance.** Possibly one current form of professional maturity: «Я понимаю, какое влияние возникает вокруг этой работы, и я беру ответственность учиться обращаться с ним бережно.» (*"I understand what influence arises around this work, and I take responsibility for learning to handle it carefully."*) — with the author's addition «[ну и беру ее правда]» (*"[and actually take it]"*) [msg #1706].

### Three mechanisms of retraumatization in body work
Purpose: to explain why body practices can retraumatize. None of the three requires any conscious turning toward traumatic material, unlike, for example, discussing it in psychotherapy [msg #1799].
1. **Interoceptive exposure («Интероцептивная экспозиция»).** Deliberate attention into the body. For a person with trauma the body itself often equals danger — what is unbearable is stored in the body. Directing attention into the body can trigger nervous-system activation along the traumatic pattern [msg #1799].
2. **Physiological trigger.** The body's physiological parameters come into a state similar to the one during a difficult past event: hyperactivation in breathing practices, sharp cold, intense physical exertion — anything that changes physiology — and the nervous system recognises the arousal as familiar and dangerous [msg #1799].
3. **Sensory trigger.** Touching a specific zone engages somatic memory — like a smell or sound, only through the skin [msg #1799].
Common outcome: each mechanism has its own logic, but the nervous system activates as if the threat is happening right now. The author's recommendation: better to avoid such risks if there is traumatic experience, especially PTSD / C-PTSD («ПТСР/к-ПТСР»), and if one's stress load is elevated [msg #1799].

### Body practices with elevated retraumatization risk
The author's list of "practices with an obvious risk" [msg #1799]:
1. **Breathing practices.** Holotropic breathing and rebirthing — hyperventilation strongly changes the state and can trigger flashbacks. Pranayama with breath holds — activates the sympathetic system and can feel like a threat. Diaphragmatic breathing — in some people it intensifies panic [msg #1799].
2. **Body-scan meditations.** The classic body scan is direct interoceptive exposure that can set off a trigger [msg #1799].
3. **Yoga.** Especially poses associated with vulnerability — open pelvis, backbends, shavasana. David Emerson developed a separate trauma-sensitive yoga protocol precisely because ordinary yoga retraumatizes [msg #1799].
4. **Massage and manual practices.** A stranger's touch is an obvious risk, but self-massage too: some body zones store memory of traumatic events [msg #1799].
5. **Dance-movement practices.** Spontaneous movement can release affect faster than the person manages to integrate it [msg #1799].
6. **Steam and cold practices** (baths, saunas, Wim Hof and similar). A sharp physiological stressor can activate the nervous system directly, without any attention to the past [msg #1799].
7. **Progressive muscle relaxation and yoga nidra.** The tension–relaxation cycle or deep relaxation can trigger emotional affect [msg #1799].
8. **Neo-tantra.** «В большинстве случае лучше вообще таким не заниматься» (*"In most cases it is better not to do this at all"*). It combines all three retraumatization mechanisms and adds a bundle more — sexual vulnerability, altered states, "energy work"; the author says it deserves a separate text [msg #1799].

Standard for providers: today's adequate standard is trauma-informedness («травмаинформированность») of the providers of any such experiences — they should have completed the relevant trainings and hold corresponding certificates. The author says he will separately write what practitioners themselves should do to be safer (!) INCOMPLETE: those safety instructions are not in this source [msg #1799].

### Green flags of a qualified group-practice facilitator (7 items)
Purpose: what the author, who has attended and led group practices for many years, looks for to judge that a facilitator is qualified ("Зеленые флаги — ведущий квалифицирован" — *green flags — the facilitator is qualified*). Use it before and during any retreat, neo-tantra, somatic or gender circle [msg #1900].

1. **Health and state screening before the practice.** They ask about medications, psychiatric history, heart, pregnancy, recent losses. For many processes these are "не формальность, а противопоказания" (*not a formality but contraindications*) [msg #1900].
2. **The facilitator invites rather than commands.** «Если хотите, можете прикрыть глаза» вместо «закройте глаза» (*"If you like, you can close your eyes" instead of "close your eyes"*); «Попробуйте, если отзывается» вместо «положите руку на живот» (*"Try it if it resonates" instead of "put your hand on your belly"*). Why: it sounds like a trifle, but this is exactly how a person's agency (субъектность) is returned — the agency we lose in traumatic experience [msg #1900].
3. **Participants are told in advance what will happen**: what we do, why, how long it lasts, what may come up, how to exit — before the start, not along the way [msg #1900].
4. **You can stop your process at any moment and leave it** without permission and without group pressure [msg #1900].
5. **Touch is agreed in advance** and happens only with real permission [msg #1900].
6. **There is time to ground after the intensive part**, not "thanks everyone, see you" one minute later [msg #1900].
7. **The facilitator says out loud what to do if someone gets overwhelmed** ("если кого-то накроет") and what he himself will do in that case [msg #1900].

### Red flags in group practice — when the author leaves (9 items)
"Красные флаги — я ухожу" (*red flags — I leave*) [msg #1900]:

1. **No questions asked.** Nobody asked about health, medications, or whether you are currently in therapy [msg #1900].
2. **«Доверься процессу», «отпусти контроль»** (*"Trust the process", "let go of control"*). You are asked to hand control over to a stranger while in an altered state [msg #1900]. (This extends the earlier flag "it's your resistance, keep going" from #719.)
3. **«Сопротивление — это твое эго»** (*"Resistance is your ego"*). The author's counter: "Чаще сопротивление — это здоровая нервная система, которая говорит, что процесс идет слишком быстро" (*more often resistance is a healthy nervous system saying the process is going too fast*) [msg #1900].
4. **«Пробить блок», «дожать», «продышать до конца»** (*"break through the block", "push it through", "breathe it through to the end"*). "Так с травмой не работают" (*that is not how trauma is worked with*): trauma discharges in small portions, it is not broken through [msg #1900].
5. **Catharsis and intensity as the measure of success.** Mechanism: when tears, moans and shaking become the measure of a group's quality, the facilitator starts extracting them and participants start producing them [msg #1900].
6. **Guru dynamics.** The master is at the centre and either does not work with this dynamic or disowns power that cannot be disowned ("снимает с себя неснимаемую власть"); there is an inner circle, decisions are not discussed, transparency is suppressed [msg #1900]. (Consistent with *Influence creates a duty of care*, #1706.)
7. **A circle where refusing is awkward** — as if you have to go deeper than is comfortable right now [msg #1900].
8. **A multi-hour intensive without pauses and without integration** [msg #1900].
9. **The answer to "what if I feel bad?"** is irritation instead of an answer, or something like «тут все взрослые люди — вы сами отвечаете за свое состояние» (*"everyone here is an adult — you are responsible for your own state"*) [msg #1900]. (Matches the "we're adults here" red-flag phrase in #1369.)

Why bother: "Все это может звучать занудно, но скучно ровно до того момента, пока мы не ретравматизируемся" (*all this may sound tedious, but it is boring exactly until we get retraumatized*) — or until a catastrophe happens after a group; the author says he knows many such stories. Conclusions: for facilitators, trauma-informedness is part of the professional standard (training, supervision, understanding what to do when a person is "carried away"); for participants, remember safety and your right to stop at any moment — doing so is not weakness but training the very regulation you came for [msg #1900]. The cover card of the post reads "Ок / не ок в групповых практиках" (*OK / not OK in group practices*) [img #1900].

### Two things that stick together in constellations — spatial metaphor vs the "field"
Purpose: the author says conversations about constellations constantly merge two different things that must be separated [msg #1907].

1. **Work with spatial metaphor — no questions.** Bringing the inner outward and looking at it from the side is a working mechanism about a hundred years old. Examples the author lists: Moreno's psychodrama, Satir's family sculpture, the empty chair in gestalt, externalisation in IFS, body figures in SPT [msg #1907].
2. **The "field" as a source of knowledge about what the person did not know about themselves — many questions.** Plus access to the "field" through untrained people and through interpretations by a facilitator who is in a position of power. It is something unclear, unverified and unproven, used by other people with unknown motivation and training. The main question: what is the benefit of using such a doubtful method when there are plenty of undoubtful ones ("когда есть куча несрмнительных" [sic]) [msg #1907].

### Recruitment communication as a preview of the process
Purpose: judge a group therapeutic process, retreat or community by how it recruits you [msg #1930].
1. **Notice role blurring.** The author received screenshots of how people are lured to a currently popular (in some circles) leader of group therapeutic processes — to participate and even to volunteer. The recruiter writes "подруга" (*girlfriend/friend*), "подумала именно про тебя" (*I thought of you specifically*), "сделаю тебе подарок" (*I'll give you a gift*), and only when asked directly does it turn out she works with the organisers. The author says in such communities "literally all ethics is broken", and the public communication also "reeks" — not by accident [msg #1930].
2. **Notice pressure, including subtle pressure.** "Pressure" often hides much subtler things than literal coercion to buy — including pressure by authority. Nobody should pressure us when inviting us into group or individual inner-work processes. Objective limits (deadlines, number of places in a group) do exist and are fine [msg #1930].
3. **Extrapolate.** "То, как тебя зовут во внутреннюю работу — это превью того, как с дальше будут там работать" (*how you are invited into inner work is a preview of how they will work with you there*) [sic]. If roles are blurred and there is pressure at the entrance, the same will happen inside the process, where we are already more vulnerable. Reason: a person comes to inner work with pain and trusts the facilitators; exploiting that means earning precisely on what you supposedly undertook to help with [msg #1930].
4. **Trust your sense.** "если вам кажется, что что-то не так, то обычно не кажется" (*if it seems to you that something is wrong, usually it doesn't just seem so*) [msg #1930].

Why communication matters: it is a standard and an important part of the profession — the same reason good therapists do not even have reviews on their websites. If communication is unethical, the process most likely is too: "помогающего там уже нет, но оно им прикидывается" (*the helping is no longer there, but it pretends to be*). Two possible causes: people do not understand what they are doing (they lack the needed lenses), or they understand perfectly but money and hype win over the benefit a helping practice should bring the client. Harm to the field: it affects normal communities through the experience people get — "Если вы готовите хреновый кофе, некоторые могут подумать что это такой напиток в принципе" (*if you make lousy coffee, some may think that's what the drink is in principle*); "Отсутствие этики, друзья, портит общий воздух" (*a lack of ethics, friends, spoils the common air*). The author admits it makes him angry and that his attitude to such communities a priori cannot be very friendly [msg #1930].

Scam example (same post): the author reminds readers that his team never writes to anyone without an application and never tries to persuade people to transfer money faster; scammers exploit program launches. A reader's screenshot describes a fake offer, supposedly from him, of a "social place" (subsidised spot) for 40,000 rubles; red flags she noticed were that the contact's name and photo had been changed 10 minutes before the conversation, and the presence of pressure levers so she would pay immediately without thinking; she stopped the dialogue, and the next day the conversation had disappeared [msg #1930, img #1930].

### Ethics as the cornerstone — the author's position and his own mistake
The author has many times seen disappointment on people's faces when asked about yet another training leader and he replied that he does not consider that leader ethical and would never attend their processes. Many read his position as fussiness, arrogance or moral superiority («белое пальто», literally "white coat", i.e. self-righteousness). He denies this and gives his own example: recently, due to overload, while simultaneously launching psychedelic-assisted circles in California and the first cohort of his programme Resonance, his team realised after the fact that some interventions had been unethical and they had missed it. "There are no half-tones here — a process is either ethical or not." They had to take restorative actions: additional work with participants and changes in the structure of subsequent groups. Lesson: even an ethics-focused leader can err under overload; the response is acknowledging it and repairing with participants and structure [msg #1369].

He argues attention to ethics is neither arrogance nor fussiness: ethics is the foundation without which any developmental process collapses — therapy, coaching, neo-tantra, even corporate training. In an unethical setup "we won't get a sustainable result, but we'll get risks right at the entrance" [msg #1369].

### Nutrition-science analogy: the baseline is known
Nutrition science has many disputes, but there is a consensus: berries are healthy, fish is worth eating, food should be processed and hands washed. Likewise in inner work, «руки надо мыть» (*"hands must be washed"*). The baseline is known, and it becomes wider the deeper the processes we work with and the further we lead people. Those who ignore it are amateurs or manipulators [msg #1369].

### What ethics costs — and why it is still worth it
The author concedes that ethics "destroys the magic", turning *special* leaders into quite ordinary ones; it limits power, shows that everyone has to work, and complicates commerce. But it is exactly what makes a space of development real. He admits feeling sadness and powerlessness — six years of his talking about ethics seem insignificant against the popularity of superficial and sometimes openly harmful unethical spaces, "a drop in the ocean" — but finds hope in seeing more people arriving at Resonance who are attentive to the ethics of the process [msg #1369].

### The author's constellation visit and its lessons
The author says that for entertainment he tries things in inner work he normally avoids; he admits bias against constellations and occasionally tests it, so he accepted a special invitation to a closed process "с умом новичка" (*with a beginner's mind*). He judged it a fairly high-quality example: he liked the facilitator and was not particularly triggered. A good practice he noted: the case was depersonalised — only the most general basics of the main participant's situation were voiced, figures had only numeric labels, nobody said "you are dad" or "you are the investor", and he did not know whom he represented; he thinks this reduces projections, dramatisation and play. Still, he says liking the facilitator and an anonymised case are not enough for the work to be "clean", and his verdict remained: "метод ой какой сомнительный" (*the method is very doubtful indeed*) [msg #1907].

His reasoning: he believes people do feel something, and that we have an inner system whose parts' interactions strongly influence our view of life, behaviour and decisions. But he does not believe substitutes — people without serious introspection skills and trained attention — can separate their own impulses, projections and material from what belongs to someone else's inner system; even with such skills it is "задачка со звездочкой" (*a problem with an asterisk*, i.e. extra-hard). The group space felt to him heavily clouded by personal content and projections and unreliable for real work [msg #1907].

On the mechanism: substitute perception has never been empirically studied — whether the substitute's experience actually matches that of the person they represent in the case-giver's life, how often and under what conditions. There is only a large body of anecdotal evidence, yet the phenomenon is taken as given in the professional constellation milieu and literature. It has no scientific basis and is explained via the "knowing field" or Sheldrake's morphic resonance. The author's argument: "он объясняет одно неизвестное через другое неизвестное. Ощущение понятности возникает, знания не прибавляется" (*it explains one unknown through another unknown. A feeling of understanding arises, knowledge does not increase*) [msg #1907].

Nuance: methods sometimes work "just because they work" — the author cites EMDR therapy as having been like that for a while — but here reliance on the "field" seems doubtful to him because it passes itself off as science [msg #1907].

### Why people feel better after constellations anyway
The author grants that people may really feel better and offers non-metaphysical reasons: sorting out your case in 2 hours instead of two years of therapy is tempting; you get a story with a beginning, climax and resolution, which therapy cannot offer — it is much more boring and requires much more active work; and 30 people watch your pain and take it seriously, which for many is the only such experience in years and heals by itself, "безо всякого «поля»" (*without any "field"*). His open question is why this benefit comes bundled with metaphysics that can be neither verified nor refuted, and whether it must be so. Risk: "Людей на группе открывают, а закрывать их обратно не факт, что умеют" (*people get opened up in the group, but it is not certain they know how to close them back*) [msg #1907].

### Alternatives the author recommends instead of constellations
In his taste it is far more reliable to do such work within an IFS-type approach. If one is drawn to placing figures in space, one can turn to Theory U practices: move in SPT (Social Presencing Theater) "but ecologically", or in 3D Mapping sculpt from plasticine all the forces involved in one's situation, look at them from the side and see where they want to move — without cluttering one's space with other people's projections, hallucinations and power [msg #1907]. He labels all this his subjective experience and opinion, and remembers that the figures that day were real living people; if some of them gained insights and benefit, he is "only for it" [msg #1907].

As a result the field has filled with so much unprofessionalism and bullshit that an outsider can no longer distinguish a scientifically grounded emotional-intelligence training ("весьма оправданный в 2026 году", *very justified in 2026*) from a Human Design reading, constellations and tarot cards. The video author lumped everything together without journalistic ethics or simple logic, "но куча и правда существовала еще до него" (*but the heap really existed before him*). He adds that an unhealthy version of media presence largely creates this problem [msg #1981].

His conclusion: the professional community must stop believing that everything has a right to exist and will somehow resolve itself — "Вот как оно рассосалось" (*this is how it resolved itself*). He asks what helping-practice communities, associations and opinion leaders have done about this problem in recent years and how well it worked: "пока мы сами не провели эту границу, ее будут проводить за нас такие вот блоггеры" (*until we draw this boundary ourselves, bloggers like this will draw it for us*). Then see *Four simple criteria* [msg #1981].

## Practical playbook

A pre-commitment checklist assembled only from the rules stated above (each is explained in its section):
- Before a transformational program: do you have psychological and time resources; are you not jumping program to program; are you not in active grief or mental disorder? [msg #21]
- Are the specialists trauma-informed, benefit-oriented, not imposing a single truth? [msg #21]
- Does the specialist have education, supervision, a professional community/association, lineage and their own teachers? Do they guarantee results or "help everyone with everything" (bad sign)? [msg #440, #1100]
- If a facilitator answers your overwhelm with "it's your resistance, keep going" — that is the retraumatization pattern. [msg #719]
- If the language is "opening energy" or "connecting to its source" — check the trainer's modern education and whether the method gives a clear image of the result. [msg #878]
- Does the method promise results without effort, fixate on money, push roles of "real" men/women, isolate you? — red flags. [msg #1174]
- Don't choose a less legitimate training because it is 30% cheaper; prefer the community stemming from the method's founder. [msg #1100]

## Evidence and numbers

- A study by Urban (on modernity and neo-tantra), as cited by the author, notes that neo-tantra often leads to participants' retraumatization, especially when the leader lacks proper training [msg #1369].
- A study by Lieberman, as cited by the author, showed that 10% of participants in groups of the pseudo-therapeutic method of *provocative therapy* (attack therapy) suffered psychological damage that persisted for more than half a year [msg #1369].
- The author says research on crisis states in practice shows participants in difficult states lean on the leader even without a formally defined relationship (no specific study named) [msg #1706].
- The author's own experience: retraumatization cases observed "on a large sample" of users of a published body practice [msg #1799].
- The author has been talking about ethics for 6 years [msg #1369].

- A review from April 2026 (medRxiv preprint, as cited by the author) included only RCTs of constellations: across the whole history of the method only 5 were found, all at high risk of bias, with certainty of evidence rated very low. The author's "honest formulation": we do not know whether it works; science has no grounds yet to consider that it does [msg #1907].
- A Hungarian randomized study (registered as NCT05051462, as cited by the author): one month after a one-day constellation seminar, 36% of participants reported negative changes they did not rule out as related to the process — worsened mood, aggravated family conflicts, uncontrollable crying, intensified eating-disorder symptoms (the source itself ends this list open-endedly with "и других"). After six months, 25% spoke of possible worsening. The author: "Это слишком много!" (*That is too much!*) [msg #1907].
- Fake-offer scam: a "social place" offered for 40,000 rubles; the impersonating contact's name and photo were changed 10 minutes before contact [img #1930].
- The author says deep mind had never been called "infobiz" in 7 years of work [msg #1981].

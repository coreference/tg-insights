# Keeping an inner stance through war and repression

## What this covers

This file collects what the deep mind channel taught, from the first day of the full-scale war (24 February 2022) through spring 2026, about how a person, mainly a Russian reader who disagrees with the war, can keep an inner stance: stay physically and nervously regulated, keep agency (субъектность) when events are outside one's control, refuse to normalize violence and lies, carry the ethical load and moral injury of living inside or near the conflict, keep self-respect and dignity, and remain humane. It ranges from first-aid style self-care checklists and resource guides to essays on numbness, propaganda space, ethical overload and the moral meaning of memory and silence [msg #581, #591, #598, #610, #682, #1722, #1789].

According to the source, this matters because the cost of failing is double: individually, numbness and suppressed horror turn into chronic anxiety or depression; collectively, the anesthesia of a society is part of what allowed unrestrained authoritarianism and totalitarianism to arise. Inner work is therefore framed as "not just part of our individual well-being but a responsible political and social action" [msg #682].

## Core ideas

1. **Agency survives even when control does not.** Events lie outside our control, yet we can still choose actions that keep us from being victims: expressing a position, mutual help, quiet participation and self-regulation [msg #581]. "Inability to change the situation does not mean helplessness" and self-respect is something no one can take, only give away [msg #1465]. Freedom, too, cannot be taken until we hand it over [msg #800].
2. **When external supports fall away, it is time for inner ones.** Falling apart is normal, but supports must be found gradually inside oneself; and in collapse, people find new supports in each other: teams and communities [msg #591, #786].
3. **Keep humanity by holding two "shores": acknowledge that something terrible and abnormal is happening, and expect more and more absurd lies.** Both protect against frustration-driven retreat into rigidity, which means accepting lies or ignoring reality [msg #610].
4. **Numbness is an evolutionary defense with a political cost.** Psychic defenses and trauma create individual and collective anesthesia; it breeds chronic anxiety and depression and removed society from where restraint of power could have occurred. Today's psychological tools make exiting anesthesia possible, so inner work becomes civic action [msg #682].
5. **"Normal life" during violence sends a signal.** Entertainment may be a psychic defense, but collectively it tells everyone, including the aggressor, that "everything is normal". The ethical option always available: remember and transmit that one must not get used to violence [msg #682, #1465].
6. **Build a clear-space against evilspace.** Propaganda creates a space of hatred and fakes that recalibrates moral compasses; the internet alone does not free you. Each person must build their own space of clarity, complexity and compassionate meta-position [msg #634].
7. **Compassion needs wisdom, or it turns into depression.** Knowing reality is part of adult responsibility, but sliding into pessimism multiplies suffering; wisdom seeks the action that most reduces suffering and the way of not participating in evil [msg #951].
8. **Ethical load is physiological and moral injury is real.** Since the war, ethics moved to the center of the nervous system; moral injury arises not only from one's actions but from powerlessness and felt complicity; ethically right choices can be painful, and suffering after them is not proof they were wrong [msg #1722].
9. **Under oppression there are two basic survival strategies, both costly; dignified stories show more perspectives.** Submitting means living in lies; open resistance risks being pushed out or broken [msg #1785].
10. **Violence carries its own weakness; memory must be kept honest.** Suppression compresses a spring of anger that eventually overrides fear and turns on the oppressor; the only things to do with trauma are to keep memory, not produce lies, and heal the past rather than distort it [msg #1789].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Four things you can still do to keep agency (24 Feb 2022)

Purpose: the author's answer, on the first day of the invasion, to "what can we still do so as not to be victims and to keep our subjecthood" when facing negative events outside our control ("Что же мы все таки можем сделать, чтобы не быть жертвой и сохранить свою субъектность." — What can we still do so as not to be a victim and to keep our agency.) [msg #581]

1. **Speak about disagreement** (the heading reads "МОЛЧАТЬ о несогласии" — literally "BE SILENT about disagreement", see the UNCLEAR note in Caveats). The explanation: it may not stop the war today, but showing one's position influences the information field and makes propaganda less effective ("проявление позиции влияет на информационное поле и делает пропаганду менее действенной" — expressing a position influences the information field and makes propaganda less effective) [msg #581].
2. **Help each other.** The author discloses his own need: large financial losses from the stock crash and strong stress from uncertainty about the future, and says many people, perhaps the majority, are in the same position. The reasoning: "Поддержку друг другу можем дать только мы сами." (Only we ourselves can give each other support.) [msg #581]
3. **Be a participant of quiet pickets** (the heading reads "быть НЕАКТИВИСТОМ тихих пикетов", "be a NON-ACTIVIST of quiet pickets", with capitalized inverted wording). The author calls it the form of political participation against the war that is the only one left in the current situation [msg #581].
4. **Self-regulate and relieve stress.** Bring yourself into a clearer state and do practices that help remove the traumatic response. Named practices: tapping (two video demonstrations) and the EMDR "butterfly" (a self-applied bilateral tapping technique, shared as a video). Under severe stress specifically: walk, look into the distance, drink water slowly in small sips, hug loved ones, and breathe consciously with a long exhale [msg #581].

### Seven requests for self-care and stability (27 Feb 2022)

Purpose: the author frames readers as "a support for ourselves, our relatives and all the sane voice that remains in Russia" ("мы сейчас опора для нас самих, наших родных и всего здравого голоса, который остался в России") and makes seven requests [msg #591].

1. **Take care of yourself.** Why: "У нас есть паттерн помогать другим, это крайне важно, но если мы развалимся, мы не сможем это делать." (We have a pattern of helping others; it is extremely important, but if we fall apart we will not be able to do it.) If you cannot even understand what self-care is right now, run the *self-care inventory check* below [msg #591].
2. **In strong anxiety or panic, do not stay in your thoughts.** Physically change your surroundings and go somewhere; see *Walking protocol against acute anxiety* [msg #591].
3. **Do not work if work worsens your state.** If work is habitual and, on the contrary, creates a support, then work, but do not expect much of yourself [msg #591].
4. **Check social media and news at set hours, not chaotically.** Unsubscribe from channels and read digests such as those prepared by Meduza [msg #591].
5. **Work out exit scenarios** that increase your safety and reduce risks, but do this when you have the resource for it [msg #591].
6. **If you have no resource, ask for help**: here in the community, from friends, relatives and loved ones [msg #591].
7. **Engage in activism if you can**, because it is needed by ourselves and by "our brothers and sisters in Ukraine" [msg #591].

Closing principle: "Многие из нас сейчас разваливаются, и это нормально. Но потихоньку надо находить в себе опоры. Когда внешние опоры отходят - наступает время внутреннних." [sic] (Many of us are falling apart now, and that is normal. But little by little we must find supports in ourselves. When the external supports go away, the time of the inner ones comes.) [msg #591]

### Self-care inventory check

When to use: when it is hard even to understand what caring for yourself means. Check, item by item [msg #591]:

1. How you have been eating.
2. Whether you drink water.
3. Whether you move.
4. Whether you sleep.
5. Whether you meet people.
6. Whether you go to therapy.
7. Cry if you want to.
8. If you have enough strength, do sport and meditate.

### Walking protocol against acute anxiety and panic

Combined from both early posts [msg #581, #591]:

1. Get out of your thoughts by physically changing surroundings and going somewhere [msg #591].
2. While walking, look left and right and examine objects; look into the distance [msg #581, #591].
3. Feel the support under your feet and breathe; breathe consciously with a long exhale [msg #581, #591].
4. Drink water slowly in small sips; hug loved ones [msg #581].

### Two inner shores of the Human ("берега Человека")

Context: "Выплываем в шторме общественного психоза" (Swimming out in the storm of social psychosis). Psychosis is defined as "когда белое выдают за черное и наоборот" (when white is passed off as black and vice versa); it is progressing and "we need supports of health". To preserve the most important thing, humanity, "которая выражается в способности чувствовать и со-чувствовать" (which is expressed in the ability to feel and to co-feel), one must maintain one's own inner shores of the Human [msg #610].

1. **First shore: acknowledge and hold the fact that something terrible and abnormal is happening.** A normal person cannot pretend it is not there; it is hard not to think about it and to carelessly do unrelated things. One wants to call evil evil, not other words, and to relate to it that way, and that is normal. War is not something inherent to humanity by nature that we should accept: humans alone among living beings have a deep understanding of the consequences of their actions and of death itself. War is "помутнение сознания до уровня животного" (a clouding of consciousness to the level of an animal), and if someone has descended there, it does not mean we should. Pitfall: during violence and killing, a philosophy of "truth somewhere in the middle" can create such inner moral dissonance that it forces one to lie to oneself more, ending up in a very disadvantageous bubble of absolute rigidity; the author notes readers probably see enough such bubbles around them and do not want to become one [msg #610].
2. **Second shore: understand that there will be more lies and they will be more absurd.** Mechanism: authoritarian equilibrium rests on lies, fear and economic prosperity; economic prosperity is gone ("RIP"), so lies and fear will only grow. For a normal person it is hard to see and hear outrageous lies; it seems each day breaks through another bottom. If this frustrates you, one day you will tire of endless frustration and turn away from your feelings, into the rigidity described above, expressed as accepting the lies or ignoring what is happening. "Этого от вас и ждут" (that is exactly what is expected of you), but you will not do it because you will know: the bottom will be broken more than once; the regime has never worked on truth and creation; by nature its simple task is to repeat one and the same thought: be afraid / enemies all around. Why this works: we get frustrated when expectations diverge from reality; here one wants acknowledgment of truth or something resembling it, but "не стоит ждать любви в борделе" (one should not expect love in a brothel); better go straight to places suited to real dates [msg #610].

Closing instruction: find the truth inside yourself and protect it. Inside each of us there is an understanding that empathy, reducing suffering and helping others are "главная правда Человека" (the main truth of the Human); this is the basis of health and nobody but ourselves can take it away [msg #610].

### Evilspace versus clear-space (guest essay by Dmitry Solovyov)

**Evilspace** — a virtual information space of hatred, fakes and lies that changes the moral orientation of people who fall into it, existing and growing in Russia. The term is coined by analogy with "cyberspace", introduced by William Gibson in Neuromancer in 1984. It is created by television and online propaganda; people spent a decade in it, and it distorted their vision and recalibrated their moral compass [msg #634].

Properties described:
1. **No clear ideology; apophatic values.** One hears what is bad (the West, traitors) but little about what is good or what alternative to build. There is a shortage of ideologists; the space's aim is to confuse, frighten and turn familiar moral orientations upside down to create hatred of external enemies and a sense of patriotism: "«Моя страна не может быть не правой»." (My country cannot be not right.) [msg #634]
2. **Emotional control via content.** Like cartoons that hook children with "visual sugar" so they cannot stop watching, "brain sugar" is now being created. Solovyov's quip: in Russia's brand book the brand idea is poorly formulated; all the effort went into tone of voice [msg #634].
3. **No boundary with the "real world".** In 1984 the internet was separated from reality; now the internet has colonized the real world. Evilspace was built as a TV space broadcast from one source, isolating people from other points of view and from understanding the complexity and interconnectedness of the world. "Абсолютное зло — это единственная позиция без альтернатив." (Absolute evil is the single position without alternatives.) [msg #634]
4. **Pitfall: the internet is not an automatic exit.** The internet excels at information bubbles and filter walls, placing people in whatever space the beneficiaries of the information or platform need [msg #634].
5. **Why propaganda works on most people.** According to value research Solovyov cites, only 10–15% of people choose and understand the values of parties or ideologies; the rest choose the loudest one or the one that joins them to a strong group. The current situation showed how powerful propaganda can be at changing moral attitudes, which then turn into actions such as denunciations [msg #634].

**Clear-space** — the response: in conditions of evilspace it is important to create your own clear-space (by analogy with "Clean Language" or a space of clarity), where:
1. different points of view exist and are not destroyed;
2. events are presented in their complexity, with an understanding of cause-and-effect;
3. instead of hatred there is a compassionate meta-position [msg #634].

Why it must be personal: no analyst on any side can give such a picture of the world; everyone can only create it independently. Actions taken from this space bring more benefit to others, so creating such spaces "и есть личная важная задача для каждого из нас сегодня" (is precisely an important personal task for each of us today). Self-caveat: the name evilspace is emotional and may amplify evil; more precisely it should be called "пространством помутнения или омрачения" (a space of clouding or darkening) [msg #634].

### Mechanism of numbness: defense, trauma, anesthesia

From the essay "The illusion of normal life, or is it ethical to keep living as usual during a war" [msg #682]:

1. **Trigger.** Physiologically we cannot long endure terrible events we can hardly influence [msg #682].
2. **Psychic defense.** To resolve this, defenses start working: denial, rationalization, dissociation or other defenses [msg #682].
3. **Trauma / split.** If events are too intense and simple defenses fail for long, psychic trauma arises, a split in the psyche that pushes part of the unbearable reality into the body and remote corners of the psyche so that we can somehow keep living [msg #682].
4. **Price: loss of awareness and numbness.** These "smart evolutionary mechanisms" cost a loss of awareness and the acquisition of numbness ("плохо переводимой на русский язык numbness — общей нечувствительности или притупленности", numbness, hard to translate into Russian — general insensitivity or dulledness). Individually and collectively we acquire an anesthesia that must be constantly maintained so as not to feel unbearable emotions [msg #682].
5. **Long-term outcome.** Because difficult feelings touching our deepest vulnerabilities remain behind the anesthesia, we acquire chronic anxiety or depression [msg #682].
6. **Historical layer.** Because of all the state violence of the 20th century and other social shocks, Russian society lived for recent decades in such anxiety, depression and anesthesia; as compensation it pursued consumption and comfort and tried hard to avoid contact with politics and the state wherever possible. So psychologically there is nothing new for us in adapting to violence; we are used to it, and the habitual intensifies under stress [msg #682].
7. **Political consequence.** This anesthesia surely has its role in the emergence of unrestrained authoritarianism and totalitarianism "в котором возможно невозможное" (in which the impossible is possible), because, due to anesthesia, we as a society were not there where restraint could have arisen [msg #682].
8. **Exit.** Today we have tools and knowledge of psychology unavailable in such form to any generation in history: psychotherapy, trauma-therapy practices, nervous-system regulation. Exiting anesthesia this way, we can cope with horror better than those before us; inner work becomes "не просто частью нашего индивидуального благополучия, но и ответственным политическим и социальным действием" (not just part of our individual well-being but a responsible political and social action) [msg #682].

### Seven reflection questions for a year of war

Context: on the first anniversary ("Год преступной войны", A year of criminal war), the author says that whatever we feel — anger, powerlessness or fear — "никто не может отобрать у нас свободу, пока мы ее не отдадим" (no one can take our freedom away until we give it up). Having witnessed or directly experienced evil and violence all year, he offers self-compassion ("Я сострадаю себе и сострадаю тебе" — I have compassion for myself and for you) and, to support the psyche, reflects and invites reflection on [msg #800]:

1. What lies in the zone of my responsibility?
2. What have I learned?
3. Who was beside me?
4. How have I grown?
5. How have my priorities changed?

### Awareness, compassion and wisdom

Context: the news that Sasha Skochilenko was sentenced to 7 years in a penal colony for anti-war price tags [msg #951].

1. **Awareness: keep looking.** The author does not want to see such news but can no longer not look: part of his responsibility or adulthood consists in knowing reality. "Если не знать и отворачиваться, зла станет больше, а его итак уже слишком много." (If you do not know and turn away, there will be more evil, and there is already too much.) Adults live with the complexity and simultaneity of different events and feelings: a sunny day when he thinks where to have breakfast is also a day when someone causes pain and someone feels it ("It is as it is") [msg #951].
2. **Compassion: imagine the other's reality.** After a long shoot where everyone forgot to eat, his stomach began to hurt while waiting for delivery; he cannot imagine eating prison slop for 7 years without choosing food, and wants to think of her at least for a second "чтобы она была в этом не одна" (so that she would not be alone in this). He also admits he usually stays silent about such news and feels ashamed, although he almost never feels shame [msg #951].
3. **Wisdom: avoid sliding into despair.** His mindfulness teachers repeatedly said that withdrawal into pessimism and extra suffering is not a way out; when we get depressed and give up, our ability to help others, and so to live the fullest life realizing our most important potential, decreases. Definition: "Мудрость — это качество, которое позволяет нам понимать связи, причины и следствия и искать такой способ действия, который будет оптимальным для снижения страданий для максимального количества людей, как в близкой, так и в отдаленной перспективе." (Wisdom is the quality that lets us understand connections, causes and consequences and look for the course of action that is optimal for reducing suffering for the largest number of people, both in the near and the distant perspective.) Diagnostic rule: if compassion slides into depression, suffering in the world only increases, which means wisdom is lacking [msg #951].

What wisdom means in this historical moment, the author says he does not know and has no ready answer, which is probably normal in difficult circumstances; but he must not stop searching until he understands how best to act "в парадоксе сегодняшнего момента" (in the paradox of the present moment). He also knows that this is a time to define one's actions very attentively and look for a way of non-participation in evil, because "за содействие спросит мой будущий Максим у самого себя" (my future Maxim will ask himself about complicity) [msg #951].

### Ethical load model ("Этическая нагрузка и перегрузка")

Written on the fourth anniversary (24 Feb 2026). The author is tired of writing about the war but cannot pretend nothing is happening, since his fatigue does not compare with the suffering around this evil; anniversary dates trigger memories and the nervous system. Ethical load is a phenomenon from the helping professions that "burst into" many people's lives over the past 4 years and still has almost no language [msg #1722].

1. **Sharp expansion of ethical load in society.** When the war started people experienced shock, fear and anger, but for many a more fundamental shift happened: ethics stopped being background and moved to the center of the nervous system, instantly generating a broad layer of analysis, actions and consequences to digest. On top of managing a more complicated life with abruptly changed trajectories came extra complexity: how to communicate, how to pay taxes, whom to donate to, how to speak, what to write, which position to take. Ethical load is "не метафора, а физиологическая реальность" (not a metaphor but a physiological reality) that can either traumatize us or lead to a life without inner splits, and also to deep exhaustion. It affects those who left and those who stayed, those who speak and those who are silent [msg #1722].
2. **Inner splits come from gaps between ethics and reality.** Psychology describes moral injury: a rupture between the inner ethical code and the reality in which you are forced to live. It was actively researched in military personnel (the source cites a research paper on this), but it later became clear that moral injury arises not only from one's own actions but also from powerlessness, inability to prevent evil, or the sense of complicity in a system you disagree with [msg #1722].
3. **Ethically right choices have hard consequences.** The reverse is also difficult: deciding from one's inner code often brings conflict with the familiar fabric of life. Emigration, breaking with loved ones, refusing a job, public statements may be honest steps, but their consequences are often painful and destabilizing. Example: a rupture of belonging is a neurobiological stressor the nervous system must process at real resource cost; research on social pain (published in Science, per the source citation) shows rejection and loss activate neural structures close to those involved in physical pain [msg #1722].
4. **Ethical coherence does not guarantee comfort.** We may apply the usual logic of control to consequences of ethically weighed decisions, but here it is very dubious: if you feel bad with the consequences, the choice may seem wrong. This is an attempt to regain agency in chaos that ignores adult features of the world. Life is nonlinear; there can be many truths of the contexts affecting us, contradicting one another; for inner integrity and preserving the personality, the ethical context may at times be not less but more important than others. This integrity often has a high price, and "страдание после сложных решений не является доказательством их неправильности" (suffering after difficult decisions is not proof that they were wrong) [msg #1722].
5. **Acknowledging ethical load reduces it a little.** Acknowledging fatigue and difficulty does not mean abandoning principles. By noticing all this and giving it space we can return a little to a human scale: act according to our values without being responsible for everyone and everything. Message to all who took on ethical load: "это правда тяжело! Но вы не одни!" (this is truly hard! But you are not alone!) Together people have not only moved toward some reduction of harm and evil but in places travelled a significant path and probably grew internally along the line of development of ethical intelligence. Greater maturity on that line means it matters not only to *choose right* but to allow oneself to be simply a human inside a historical crisis, with a right to rest, recovery and private life, even while staying engaged with the ethical context of one's era [msg #1722].

### Grounds of moral judgment self-inquiry ("что такое хорошо, а что такое плохо?")

Context: a story told to the author: a mother physically abused her child; he filed a police report; guardianship services said they would take him; the mother said "take him"; he spent time in an orphanage and eventually returned, although she did not want to take him back, and "now behaves as smooth as silk". In comments a huge number of people considered the mother's behavior normal. A similarly surprising spectrum of what people consider normal or abnormal, good or evil, appears under almost any charged story (the author names the cases of Oxxxymiron and Epstein). His diagnosis: we seem to live in an era with no social consensus, having lost some ability to rank norm and non-norm; we exist in a fragmented reality where good and evil differ among "tribes" from different bubbles [msg #1735].

Self-inquiry exercise:
1. Notice: when you judge "this is normal / abnormal", "bad / good", what becomes your ground? The offered list: benefit, rule, empathy, law, consequences, experience, faith, fear, care, knowledge [msg #1735].
2. Describe, without discussion or mutual evaluation, what you stand on, in one phrase or 1–2 sentences [msg #1735].
3. Separately: notice what you do when your grounds collide with one another [msg #1735].

### Two basic survival strategies under oppression, and the dignified third path

From "Как жить, когда тебя подавляют" (How to live when you are being oppressed). Stories of social injustice and abuse of power are so many that they seem the norm, and this "norm" is born from some people's wish to live at the expense of others, from ideas of superiority, special right, exceptionalism. People face oppressive structures in every country; the most glaring examples are authoritarian and totalitarian regimes, plus sexism, racism, nationalism and other "isms" [msg #1785].

1. **Surrender and fit in**: obey those above, oppress those below, convincing yourself there is some wisdom in it. Cost: you have to live in lies and distort reality [msg #1785].
2. **Openly resist and refuse to obey.** Cost: you risk being pushed out or physically broken [msg #1785].

Both avoid half-tones and have their price. The three stories the author recommends (see *Tools and resources*) "add perspectives": they are about how people lived an oppressed position with dignity and preserved themselves. In each he finds not only the worst human manifestations and the hopeless chthonic darkness people inflict on each other, but always some quiet hope, radiance and a sense of genuine strength that can be neither broken nor taken away [msg #1785].

## Detailed insights

### The illusion of normal life and its ethics

The author observes outings, festivals, people dressing up lavishly and celebrating in beautiful restaurants, and says that to him this simply looks creepy or strange while a war goes on to which "we have a direct relation" and where someone's relatives and friends are dying. He explicitly does not moralize or judge behavior against a norm; he is interested in psychology and ethics, defining ethics as "последствия наших поступков или их влияние на мир, а не какой-то там культуральный конструкт" (the consequences of our actions or their influence on the world, not some cultural construct) [msg #682].

Ethical verdict: entertainment and other things strange for a healthy person to do while violence happens nearby may simply be a manifestation of psychic defense and may even briefly benefit a small number of people. But to everyone else, and to the aggressor, they send a clear signal: everything is normal, nothing terrible is happening. Collectively they sustain the anesthesia and dulledness. This does not mean living in mourning; and there is nothing wrong with a depression adequate to circumstances, because "она даст гнев и силу для последующих действий по исправлению ситуации" (it will give anger and strength for subsequent actions to correct the situation). It may be useful during this time to pay extra attention to the ethics of our actions and to check which future they contribute to. There is always a choice that avoids unethical messages: in any situation remember, and transmit to others, that one must not get used to violence. "Нам есть, куда вернуться. Текущая ситуация не нормальна." (We have somewhere to return to. The current situation is not normal.) [msg #682]

### Self-respect, words and complicity ("28 человек сегодня")

Written on a day when 28 people were killed. The author asks: when will it end, what is it all for, why can no one stop it; and says he must somehow live with it. Friends "from there" will write about it, while friends "not from there" will post photos with scooters in parks. He understands them: one's own grief does not help another's; words seem to change nothing and carry known risks and costs. If he writes, 100 people may unsubscribe, those who think it right or who simply do not want negativity in an already difficult or perhaps deserving-only-good life; and something worse than financial losses may come. "That is how we live": ordinary life against the background of catastrophe; an illusion of separateness from "there", from regions where everything is felt many times more acutely, and where the gap between words on TV and reality would frighten Orwell himself [msg #1465].

His stance: "лучше потерять деньги, даже свободу, чем человеческий облик и присущее человеку достоинство" (better to lose money, even freedom, than human form and the dignity inherent in a human). However the sun shines, he cannot not see, not know and ignore. He feels uneasy with himself when he watches a video from friends "there", scrolls past it and goes on talking to a friend or working "as if nothing happened"; "В это есть что-то шизофреническое." [sic] (There is something schizophrenic in this.) [msg #1465]

On words: apart from truly monstrous things, what angers him most is the need to choose words. Distorting reality in words only makes what is happening more possible. Starting to distort meanings, closing eyes, sweeping things under the rug, "мы становимся актерами в чужой плохой пьесе" (we become actors in someone else's bad play). Then, as in every moment of our silence, we seem to help yet another ordinary person rejoice at a strike on some factory, and help another, humiliated by lack of prospects and intoxicated by money and the chance to create some meaning in life, to go "play the hero" and vanish without trace a week later. The TV does not fall silent; deputies tour factories telling stories; these people have their own utterly disgusting role of pushing others toward who-knows-what and death while knowing what they serve [msg #1465].

On helplessness versus self-respect: he cannot seriously change the situation now, and so probably has to accept that it is being done at his expense and at the expense of the well-being of future generations, "such cannibalism in one's own family". But he can no longer accept that they want to put his name under it, and, through silent consent, actually do. "Невозможность изменить ситуацию не означает беспомощность. Не означает бессилие. Не означает автоматическую потерю уважения к самому себе." (The impossibility of changing the situation does not mean helplessness. It does not mean powerlessness. It does not mean an automatic loss of self-respect.) Living through this while finding some way to keep self-respect and be able to look oneself in the eye in the future is something no one can simply take, but we can give it away ourselves, as many people around do [msg #1465].

Not losing self-respect by blurring one's view with fear and allowing complicity "on one's own floor" is "one of the largest-scale life challenges", and the history of totalitarianism in different countries shows that most people fail it. His working method: look only at himself and think how he will see his thoughts and actions from the future, asking: "Насколько мог сделал ли я хоть что-то, чтобы это не становилось нормой и в моем обществе не путали черное с белым?" (Did I do at least something, as much as I could, so that this would not become the norm and in my society black would not be confused with white?) [msg #1465]

### When systems collapse, people emerge

At year-end 2022 the author facilitated several team sessions in companies to sum up and integrate a hard year. Lesson: however much one wants to jump over the pain and leave it in the past, we cannot do that without a small stop [msg #786].

His characterization of the year: the war is the main event, a catastrophe senseless in its meaninglessness and consequences, arranged by a group of people striving to hold on to power and evade responsibility for years of violence against society: corruption, killings of opponents and destruction of civic agency. The price already paid: hundreds of thousands of lives of people who could have created and built, emigration of excellent specialists, personal financial losses and destruction of the economy for future generations. It is an incredible collapse of the political system for an era of technical progress, destructive of truly traditional values: the family, which historically supported people in any conditions, stopped doing so for very many people [msg #786].

Here Otto Scharmer's 2020 phrase resonates: "когда системы коллапсируют, возникают люди" (when systems collapse, people emerge). In all the team sessions the author saw much gratitude and a sense of stability and safety that had arisen in teams that year. Mechanism: when familiar supports fall from under our feet, we turn to what can truly support us, here the people around us and our groups (teams and communities); a new basis for warm, living connection seemed to arise as a consequence of the catastrophe, with a more humane and horizontal view showing up in teams [msg #786].

Examples of support he received (stories carrying the lesson that human solidarity crosses borders): in March community members donated substantial funds so that deep mind would not collapse in the crisis, and Buddhist monks lectured for them with love and openness; in September at Tbilisi airport a stranger bought him water and a doughnut after seeing his failed attempts to pay with UnionPay; in December poor travellers from Slovakia were ready to send him money when the same happened in Pokhara with a non-working card and no cash; at a recent training in Kathmandu they did a joint performance with Ukrainians, and a German woman, learning he was from Russia, asked so sincerely whether his loved ones were safe that he nearly cried [msg #786].

Outlook: 2023 brings new challenges: adapting to changes and living through the consequences of traumatic events that have touched or will touch everyone through losses and transformations. Yet although a single human life can be destroyed completely, the flow of life itself cannot be reversed; no one has managed it for long, just as no one has avoided death or fully killed the true human foundation: love and compassion. So all the skills, connections and "рожденная в муках человечность" (humanity born in torment) will be very useful in the future "когда небо немного рассеется" (when the sky clears a little). His wishes: do not suppress this year's pain, do not forget to own your achievements, and truly rest — for at least a couple of days fully let go of clinging to hopes and fears; we are needed rested by ourselves and others even more than usual. The post ends with lines of an English song beginning "I wish I knew how it would feel to be free" about breaking chains, saying things "loud" and "clear", and removing "all the bars that keep us apart" [msg #786].

### Anniversaries as retraumatization triggers and the Fire teaching

The anniversary days of the start of wars that directly affect us are a trigger for retraumatization if trauma happened. On these days we may feel anxious, detached, unusual or simply bad. These days are an occasion to practice self-care, to seek connectedness and solidarity with others, and to discover wisdom and mercy in oneself through understanding the nature of suffering [msg #1006]. The 2026 post repeats that dates trigger memories and the nervous system [msg #1722].

The 2024 post then gives a text on fire (the source does not name it): everything is burning; the eye and visual images are burning; the ears and the sounds they hear, the nose, tongue, body and mind are burning. With what fires? "Огнями алчности, ненависти, неведения, огнями тревоги, ревности, потери, разрушения и горя." (With the fires of greed, hatred, ignorance, the fires of anxiety, jealousy, loss, destruction and grief.) Seeing this suffering, one who follows the path grows weary of these fires, weary of the greed and hatred that feed clinging to images, sounds, smells, body and mind; growing weary, he turns away from this clinging, and in the absence of clinging he is liberated. Recommended practice for the anniversary and surrounding days: metta, the cultivation of kindness and compassion (an audio practice by deep mind) [msg #1006]. ### Memory, Victory Day and not speculating on ancestors' deaths

On 9 May 2026 the author says that seeing congratulations on victory or slogans that "we will win" in the future evokes more questions than answers: bewilderment and sadness rather than festivity [msg #1789].

Personal story with a lesson: in his second university year he went near Rzhev with volunteers restoring monuments to Soviet soldiers; adults and children lived in tents, ate buckwheat and dug the earth; he liked being in nature with a group pursuing a meaningful goal; together with schoolchildren they found cartridge cases and human bones. The collective grave they built has probably collapsed again, since nobody cares, "это ведь не парад на площади проводить" (it's not like holding a parade on a square). He now wonders what it was like for the much younger children, and does not fully understand what it was like for himself. Then he did not know he was digging the ground of battles that had been hidden: the Rzhev battles are one of the hardest and most hushed-up episodes of the Great Patriotic War, with no obvious success but enormous losses, which probably became one of the reasons for the silence, since they could have led to a critical rethinking of the cost of war and of the leadership's competence [msg #1789].

His ethical conclusion: it would have been right if they had led to that rethinking, because "смерть предков нам не принадлежит и на ней нельзя спекулировать, а мы в итоге стали" (the death of our ancestors does not belong to us and one must not speculate on it, and yet in the end we did). "Все, что мы можем делать с травмой — это хранить память и не продуцировать ложь, исцелять прошлое, а не искажать его." (All we can do with trauma is keep memory and not produce lies, heal the past and not distort it.) Done with dignity, this work protects us from repeating such events and, even more, from becoming initiators of evil ourselves. Since this was not done, he finds it hard to share any festive feeling; he rejects accusations of ingratitude — the question of the colossal sacrifice of the peoples does not arise — but the fog around what for and how exactly it was made is now as thick as can be: was it so that all this could happen? Closing: the death of ancestors does not belong to us; only the present is in our hands, which can somehow influence the future, and his hope is that we will manage it better than we managed the past [msg #1789].

## Practical playbook

- **Information hygiene in acute crisis:** read news at fixed hours, unsubscribe from channels, rely on digests [msg #591].
- **Choosing practices by stability level:** when unstable, centering and free movement; when relatively stable, pleasure concentration and self-compassion; on anniversaries, metta [msg #598, #1006].
- **Daily ethical stance in any setting:** remember and say aloud to others that one must not get used to violence and that the current situation is not normal [msg #682].
- **Future-self check:** look at today's thoughts and actions as you would from the future; ask whether you did at least something so that this does not become the norm, and seek a way of non-participation in evil [msg #951, #1465].
- **After a hard year:** do not suppress the pain, own your achievements, and rest fully for at least a couple of days, letting go of hopes and fears [msg #786].
- **Under ethical overload:** name the load, accept fatigue without dropping principles, act from values without being responsible for everyone and everything, and claim the right to rest, recovery and private life [msg #1722].

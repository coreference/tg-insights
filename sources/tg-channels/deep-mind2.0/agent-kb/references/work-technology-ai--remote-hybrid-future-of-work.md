# Remote, hybrid and the future of work

## What this covers

This file collects everything the deep mind channel (a consultancy on mindful leadership, corporate culture and employee well-being) taught about where work is heading: remote and hybrid models during and after COVID, the return-to-office tension, trust and engagement in distributed teams, what employees will demand from employers, flexible schedules matched to personal rhythm, the BANI description of reality, and the breakdown of stable careers toward solo-preneurship. It also transcribes the data the channel shared: a Harvard study on work-from-anywhere, HBR research on matching work format to preference, a 2021 expert prediction consensus, and a PwC survey of 19 European startup hubs [msg #48, #98, #161, #210, #211, #282, #360, #365, #370, #396, #426, #451, #531, #619, #645, #797, #1014, #1734].

According to the channel, the topic matters because competition for the best hires will grow and flexible, human-centred companies gain the advantage; because the way companies organise work now decides whether they can keep talent; and because individuals need a new footing in a world where external institutions no longer guarantee security [msg #98, #426, #797, #1734].

## Core ideas

- **Flexibility is a competitive advantage in hiring.** Companies that are flexible toward employees will win the growing competition for the best people; work-from-anywhere is associated with higher productivity (see *Evidence and numbers*) [msg #98].
- **The pendulum stops in the middle: hybrid.** Full remote is hard and leads many to burnout, pure office is no longer wanted; the new generation chooses a balanced office+remote mix [msg #282, #370].
- **Remote work exposed who was prepared.** COVID was the tide going out; companies with modern culture "were swimming dressed" and coped with remote work better [msg #161].
- **Return-to-office splits people by mindset.** Post-conventional employees experience being called back as a developmental rollback and quit; conventional employees and cultures prefer daily office attendance (see *Conventional vs post-conventional reaction*) [msg #426].
- **Hybrid teams need deliberate trust-building**, centred on diversity, psycho-emotional state and rank differences (see *Three focus areas*) [msg #396].
- **Future employees will choose employers by health, family fit, learning support and flexibility for a multistage life** (see *Four future employee demands*) [msg #797].
- **Work fitted to the person beats the person fitted to the work.** The 9-to-6 manufacturing model is outdated; matching schedule to rhythm raises productivity and lowers burnout and attrition (see *Splitter vs blender*) [msg #1014].
- **HR and IT will dominate winning companies' structure** [msg #531].
- **The stable career contract is collapsing**; the person becomes the point of support, but this idea must be read as a "new language", not an instruction, especially where survival rather than development is the mode (see *Four shifts from job to value creation*) [msg #1734].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Three focus areas for trust in hybrid/remote teams (Inna Laletina)
Purpose: a lens for team leads ("тим-лиды") who need to keep building skills for hybrid teams, since the number of people not coming to the office daily will probably stay high after the pandemic. Written by Inna Laletina, process facilitator at deep mind, as part 1 of a series on "Траст и вовлеченность в удаленных командах" (trust and engagement in remote teams) [msg #396].

The three skills or focuses of attention for creating trust and effective interaction:
1. **Awareness of and accounting for diversity** ("Осознание и учитывание разнообразия (diversity)") — developed in full below [msg #396].
2. **Attentive attitude to employees' psycho-emotional state** ("Внимательное отношение к психо-эмоциональному состоянию сотрудников") [msg #396].
3. **Facilitating differences in ranks and statuses** ("Фасилитация различий в рангах и статусах") [msg #396].

(!) INCOMPLETE: the post explains only the first point ("Давайте сегодня поговорим про первый пункт"); points 2 and 3 are named but not explained in this message [msg #396].

**Why diversity becomes visible in remote work — three mechanisms** [msg #396]:
- **Different abilities to learn, adapt and stay resilient.** Remote work requires new ways of interacting, new technologies and tasks, and these surface differences. Some people work well alone — they easily prioritise, structure and organise themselves. Others need constant contact with the team to understand which tasks to do now [msg #396].
- **Blurred boundaries.** Remote work erases the borders between office and home, working hours and personal space. It is harder for some than for others: parents or people with little space in their flat may find it harder to work without breaks or constant distractions [msg #396].
- **Different needs for contact.** Some are fine alone; others feel isolated and need more interaction to feel motivated and connected [msg #396].

The recommendation: expect and account for these different needs, styles and circumstances — then each team member gets the chance to show their best side [msg #396].

### Five practices for supporting diversity in remote teams
How to do the above in practice, as listed by Inna Laletina [msg #396]:
1. **Hold regular 1-1 syncs.** At minimum ask three things: "Как дела?" (How are you?), "Что вам нужно?" (What do you need?), "Чем я могу помочь?" (How can I help?) [msg #396].
2. **Account for team members' time zones.** Even in a single time zone some people will always have childcare problems at 16:00 or in the mornings. In a global team, adjust meeting times so that the same person does not carry heavy stress from their non-work circumstances (i.e. rotate the burden) [msg #396].
3. **Regularly clarify your expectations of the team.** Clearly state scope, expectations and deadlines for work processes, and check in advance whether there are obstacles [msg #396].
4. **Start meetings with informal warm-ups.** Introduce new participants, and have everyone voice their context: "где я, как я, в каком я состоянии" (where I am, how I am, what state I am in). This, as it were, brings people into one room, helps them contribute, and also demonstrates the team's diversity [msg #396].
5. **Develop a culture of compassion and attentiveness.** Be a bit more compassionate and attentive to yourself and others. We are all still adapting and stressed more than usual; healthy compassion and attention to detail help people value each other and reduce tension [msg #396].

### Conventional vs post-conventional reaction to return-to-office
Context (May 2021): Thailand and India were going through a third COVID wave while the US and Europe were lifting restrictions and returning to "normal" life. Western psychotherapist colleagues of the channel report a high level of resignations among highly qualified specialists who are being called back to the office [msg #426].

The model, as the channel's author argues it:
- **What remote work does.** It gives employees a high level of personal freedom and raises the logic of relationships in a company to the "взрослый-взрослый" (adult-adult) format [msg #426].
- **Post-conventional people.** People with post-conventional thinking, who value efficiency and opportunities for self-realisation, perceive further restrictions of freedom as a rollback in development: "Это болезненно и порой даже невыносимо" (This is painful and sometimes even unbearable) [msg #426].
- **Prediction for competitive companies.** The author supposes that companies competitive on international markets will settle on hybrid models; their own office-remote hybrids preserve the paradigm of mature relationships and employees' freedom, so leading companies can keep attracting the best people [msg #426].
- **Conventional cultures.** In locations and companies dominated by conventional thinking ("синие по спиральной динамике" — blue in Spiral Dynamics), where social approval and norms matter, hybrid models and remote work do not take root; they return to fully office work at the first opportunity [msg #426].
- **Why conventional employees prefer the office (four reasons).** Paradoxically, it is more comfortable for them to physically go to work every day: (1) it is hard for them to organise themselves and their work at home; (2) they feel more acutely the need for a collective for self-identification; (3) — "весьма важно" (quite important) — remotely they feel more pressure from the need to show a concrete result every day, (4) whereas in ordinary office life simply showing up at the workplace is often enough [msg #426].

The post also cites RBC Trends naming trend no. 1 for 2021 as moving business online and trend no. 2 as the transition to remote work [msg #426].

### Four future employee demands (after Lynda Gratton)
Purpose: to show what people will want from a workplace "already tomorrow". Based on the research of London Business School professor Lynda Gratton (organisation theory), and grounded, per the post, in large objective processes — growing longevity, automation and the transformation of social norms [msg #797].

1. **Stay healthy ("Оставаться здоровыми").** Health becomes a key factor in deciding to take a job. People will rather choose a company that does everything to care for their state, and leave more easily if an organisation harms their health. People prepare to live — and therefore work — much longer than before and want to stay in shape for a long time. Already now employees want enough autonomy to sleep 8 hours a day, to work on a predictable schedule so they can see family and friends, and to be in a caring, ecological ("экологичной") organisational culture [msg #797].
2. **Actively account for their family circumstances.** With the move to remote and hybrid office, relationships and family weigh more in decisions about working at a company; when people choose, they also need to consider the second working person in the family. The structure and norms of relationships keep changing fast: a man's job stops being the priority around which everything revolves. In 2019 the phenomenon of dual career ("dual caree" [sic]) began to be widely discussed in books and articles as something different from the familiar format where one person builds a career and the other supports the household. Ever more diverse models of relationships and parenting demand ever more flexibility from the employer [msg #797].
3. **Learn faster than machines ("Учиться [быстрее, чем машины]").** AI starts replacing not only routine but also creative tasks. Automation can trigger anxiety — "…мое рабочее место заменено машиной?" (will my job be replaced by a machine?) — or inspire growth — "уже сейчас мне нужно обучаться [reskill и upskill], чтобы оставаться востребованным" (I need to learn now to stay in demand). For people who believe their work will certainly change, organisations able to support retraining, growth and moving to a completely different position will matter. Training in soft skills of self-management and interpersonal interaction gains special importance because these remain outside the zone of automation for now [msg #797].
4. **Adapt to a multistage life.** The 20th-century three-stage model — full-time study, full-time work, full-time retirement — is dying. It is replaced by what economist Andrew Scott called the multistage life. People prepare to live longer and automation changes work ever more actively, so the future norm will be constantly reinventing oneself and developing in new fields and professions a second, third and even fourth time. The sequence of work-study-rest becomes more varied and free; evidence cited: the Kauffman index of entrepreneurial activity — the share of entrepreneurs aged 55–64 has been growing in recent years, from 18% in 1996 to 25% in 2018. Because life paths become more variable, flexibility at work becomes a real plus for employees leaving the three-stage model [msg #797].

Conclusion for employers: a company that wants to attract and retain talent must understand both its audience's needs and its own value proposition for meeting them — and change its work model and culture already now [msg #797].

### Splitter vs blender work-rhythm typology
Purpose: to show that adapting the schedule to a person's preferred mode is a lever of productivity and retention [msg #1014].
- **Splitters** prefer to work focused in a 9-to-6 mode [msg #1014].
- **Blenders** like to switch between different activities during the day. The author describes himself as a blender: he likes to work several hours in a row, then switch to other activities for a few hours before tackling another portion of work tasks [msg #1014].
- **Finding.** Per a new study the post attributes to "Gallop" [sic], working according to one's own mode not only increases productivity but noticeably reduces the risk of burnout and of leaving for another company. Employees working in a mode that suits them look for a new job in 46% of cases versus 60% among those working against their preferred rhythm (full chart in *Evidence and numbers*) [msg #1014, img #1014].
- **Why the old model fails.** Traditional 9-to-6 schedules, strict dress codes and fixed workplaces remain the norm in many sectors, but this model corresponds to the rapidly outdated culture of manufactories, against whose efficiency modern science has many arguments. "Она пытается подогнать человека под работу, а не работу под человека" (It tries to fit the person to the job, not the job to the person) [msg #1014].
- **Business logic.** With more data, companies understand how employees' state affects business indicators and look for ways to use this as a competitive advantage — as part of a well-being strategy, a productivity strategy or a talent-attraction strategy. Insightful leaders will increasingly understand that taking into account people's psychological, physiological and biological needs at work, rather than pretending they are irrelevant, is simply profitable [msg #1014].
- **Forecast.** More flexible and adaptive work will soon stop being the prerogative of leading companies that already changed their culture through remote and hybrid office, and will become a universal standard of effective business [msg #1014].

### BANI and three adaptation solutions
BANI is presented as "новый более точный способ описать реальность" (a new, more accurate way to describe reality), having replaced the popular VUCA concept. Its four properties, which the channel says "we are now living through in full" (March 2022) [msg #619]:
1. **Хрупкость — Brittleness/fragility** [msg #619].
2. **Тревога — Anxiety** [msg #619].
3. **Нелинейность — Nonlinearity** [msg #619].
4. **Непостижимость — Incomprehensibility** [msg #619].

Studying this framework can be a useful support in the new reality. The accompanying meme: "Я УЖЕ ПРИВЫК K VUCA-МИРУ!" (I've already got used to the VUCA world!) — "ДА, НО МЫ ЖИВЕМ В BANI-МИРЕ" (Yes, but we live in a BANI world) [msg #619, img #619].

**Origin and claim (deep mind's Telegraph longread "Переход из VUCA в BANI мир" — Transition from the VUCA to the BANI world).** The concept of the BANI world was proposed by the futurologist the article names "Жаме Кассио" (Jamais Cascio). He suggests that the VUCA world — a popular idea in the army and in business since 2007–2008 — stopped describing current reality because the world itself became much more complex. The article states the purpose of the framework: "Фреймворк BANI поможет лучше понимать будущее и послужит опорой в хаосе" (The BANI framework will help understand the future better and will serve as a support in chaos). BANI properties are, per the article, a more fitting description than VUCA properties of the current situation, of people's emotional state and of general interconnections; they began to show vividly from 2020 and will only intensify in the near future [article #619].

**VUCA vs BANI comparison (image card in the article).** The card contrasts the two acronyms [article #619]:
- **VUCA** — dated "From the 1980s", "shaped by the Cold War"; serves to describe a situation of "ambiguity and complexity"; letters: Volatile, Uncertain, Complex, Ambiguous; labelled „WORN-OUT".
- **BANI** — dated "From 2020", "shaped by climate and global systemic change"; serves to describe the situation of "the Next Generation of Business"; letters: Brittle, Anxious, Non-linear, Incomprehensible; labelled „UP TO DATE".
(!) CONTRADICTION: the article text says VUCA has been popular "с 2007-2008 года" (since 2007–2008) and lasted "чуть меньше 20 лет" (a little under 20 years), while the card dates VUCA "From the 1980s" [article #619].

**The four properties explained in full** [article #619]:

1. **B — Brittle — Хрупкий (fragile).**
   - *What it means.* You cannot really rely on things that are fragile by nature. A brittle world means that any systems, ways of acting and organisations we are used to can collapse at any moment. On the surface systems can seem very stable, even unshakeable — and they may truly be so right up until a new disruptive technology or a black swan appears [article #619].
   - *Why fragility arises.* Today it comes from the dissonance between what a system shows on the surface and the foundation it actually rests on. To a large extent this dissonance is a consequence of companies chasing profit or political regimes chasing a pretty information picture. Because of this imbalance, crises that used to affect only one state or region now affect the whole world: "Нет ничего надежного, и никто не защищен." (Nothing is reliable, and no one is protected.) [article #619]
   - *What helps.* Living with the fragility of states and organisations of any scale calls for resilience skills or resilience systems. "Soft" side: meta-skills for developing the resilience of the nervous system and increasing the psyche's resources. "Hard" solutions named: (a) new decentralised WEB 3.0 systems that let you distribute your data outside companies or states without risk of losing it or having it blocked; (b) new-wave services that help talent move freely around the world, quickly obtaining legal status in different countries; (c) organised cloud/local horizontal communities that help people with close values solve any problems. The article points here to deep mind's own mindfulness and crypto communities as examples of such support [article #619].

2. **A — Anxious — Беспокойный (anxious).**
   - *What it means.* The uncertainty of the VUCA world has turned into constant anxiety and even fear. An anxious world keeps everyone in stress all the time. It is impossible to predict what will happen next; we more often feel helpless and do not understand how to make decisions. "Тревожность становится основной болезнью." (Anxiety is becoming the main illness.) [article #619]
   - *Amplifier — the media stream.* We become ever more dependent on news and the constant media flow; this information, which increasingly consists of propaganda and fakes, only strengthens the sense that the world is unsafe and feeds our anxiety [article #619].
   - *What people do in a state of anxiety* (four behaviours listed): (1) they live expecting the next catastrophe or cataclysm; (2) they become more passive so as not to make wrong decisions; (3) they feel despair over missed opportunities; (4) they experience unbearable sensations when individual people can uncontrollably make decisions affecting them or whole countries [article #619].
   - *Psychological consequence.* From a psychological point of view this reality increases the risk that mental traumas form or are actualised. However, skilful actions can also foster psychological resilience [article #619].
   - *Who wins.* In an anxious world those will win who manage to develop stable mindfulness skills — specifically stress resistance and healing themselves and their teams from mental traumas — and those who can look at the future positively without denying problems. The article links this to deep mind's work helping teams reduce employee stress and gain emotional resilience [article #619].

3. **N — Nonlinear — Нелинейный (nonlinear).**
   - *What it means.* A nonlinear world does not let you establish clear links between causes and effects, for example between our actions and their result. Because the world has become more interconnected, understanding the consequences of each separate phenomenon becomes ever harder; events have a systemic effect, influencing everything and everyone at once [article #619].
   - *Implication.* Reaching a goal requires numerous systemic actions, because (three features listed): (1) small decisions start to carry disproportionate weight in the overall result — from each such decision we can get either incredible losses or enormous benefit; (2) changes lead to consequences with long delays that become obvious only after time; (3) a lot of effort does not guarantee a significant result [article #619].

4. **I — Incomprehensible — Непостижимый (incomprehensible).**
   - *What it means.* The nonlinear results of various causes and phenomena create a big gap in our ability to understand what is happening and to foresee changes. We lose the ability to grasp the causes of events, because they more often lie somewhere deep in the past or not at all in the sphere logic points to [article #619].
   - *Consequence.* The world becomes incomprehensible, which makes it impossible to build a complete picture of what is going on: "Все знания всегда неполноценны. Данных всегда не хватает." (All knowledge is always incomplete. There is never enough data.) All decisions are made under uncertainty, and every step is always taken by feel ("на ощупь" — groping) [article #619].
   - *Why more information does not help* (three-point argument): (1) what matters is detecting valuable signals, not taking in the increased information noise; (2) meanwhile our biological sensitivity and ways of reacting to information have not changed; (3) therefore a lot of information is more likely to overload our nervous system and lower the ability to make quality decisions [article #619].
   - *Possible way out.* Perhaps the solution will be the development of artificial intelligence, which will take over part of the information-processing tasks in the future (framed as "Возможно" — possibly) [article #619].

**The response to each letter.** "Каждая буква акронима BANI предлагает некоторый ответ на вызовы нового мира." (Each letter of the BANI acronym offers some answer to the challenges of the new world.) [article #619]
- If something has become **brittle** — we can increase **resilience** (card: "Brittleness requires capacity & resilience").
- If we feel **anxiety** — we can increase **mindfulness, empathy and compassion** (card: "Anxiety asks for empathy & mindfulness").
- If something has become **nonlinear** — it requires **context and adaptivity** (card: "Nonlinearity calls for context & adaptivity").
- With **incomprehensibility** — increase **transparency and intuitiveness** (card: "Incomprehensibility demands transparency & intuition") [article #619].

**What the BANI frame gives (closing image card).** It allows a more accurate account of current circumstances; it paves the way for an approach to make the world more comprehensible again; and it creates opportunities to meet each of these challenges appropriately [article #619].

**How long BANI will last.** The VUCA world lasted a little under 20 years, but the speed of change keeps growing, and it is quite possible that we will say goodbye to the BANI world in some 5 years. Probably, living through this turbulence will let humanity create foundations for a more stable and resilient world in the future (the article's own hedges: "вполне возможно", "Вероятно") [article #619].

The extended version (a deep mind article for Netology) proposes three concrete adaptation solutions [msg #645]:
1. **Work with your inner state** ("работать со своим внутренним состоянием") [msg #645].
2. **Recognise the priority of emergent systems over hierarchies** ("признать приоритет эмерджентных систем перед иерархиями") [msg #645].
3. **Learn to make right decisions instead of easy ones** ("научиться принимать правильные решения вместо простых") [msg #645].

### Four shifts from job to value creation (after Dan Koe) with the deep mind caveat
**The old scheme.** "Получить образование ▶ Найти работу ▶ Расти по ступеням ▶ Выйти на пенсию." (Get an education ▶ Find a job ▶ Climb the steps ▶ Retire.) This is a product of the industrial era, when people worked for a company, received stable income and retired. It assumed stable institutions, was tied to the state and corporations, and was viable under linear growth [msg #1734].

**Why it breaks.** Institutions no longer guarantee safety, and the speed of change exceeds systems' ability to adapt. Polarisation, populism and wars can destabilise any plans, and AI is right now technologically changing the foundations of a way of life that held for decades [msg #1734].

**The new logic.** The old industrial contract was: you are loyal to the system, the system takes care of you. That contract is rapidly falling apart; in response, if the system no longer holds, the person becomes the point of support — "Не как личный бренд, а как носитель мышления, смысла, способности создавать ценность и связывать реальность вокруг себя" (Not as a personal brand, but as a carrier of thinking, meaning, the ability to create value and connect reality around oneself). The channel frames Dan Koe's viral X article as formally about work and career but really about what a person can lean on when external supports no longer work [msg #1734].

The four shifts:
1. **Work no longer equals security.** "Должность — не идентичность, компания — не дом, карьерная лестница — не путь." (A position is not an identity, a company is not a home, a career ladder is not a path.) [msg #1734]
2. **Value moves from process to meaning and result.** People are paid not for presence but for contribution, perspective, the ability to think and create [msg #1734].
3. **The future is fragmented.** There is no single right path, only a set of temporary strategies that are constantly reassembled [msg #1734].
4. **The person becomes the key author of their trajectory.** Their thinking, attention and adaptability are the main capital [msg #1734].

Consequence: a time of independent leaders and creators, where those succeed who have the qualities and skills not merely to survive, work and wait for retirement, but to find a path that gives meaning and the possibility to share value [msg #1734].

**The author's own case.** He started implementing this model in 2019, swapping a "stable" career for deep mind, which was full of uncertainty, and focusing daily actions on what creates benefit for others. Despite the difficulties and a temporary change of status that many people found incomprehensible, he now feels even more joy that he moved to the new model, which he finds obviously more adaptive to the contours of the emerging world [msg #1734].

**The caveat — "Но мне сложно писать книгу, когда в deep mind кассовый разрыв"** (But it's hard for me to write a book when deep mind has a cash gap). The world really is changing in this direction, yet such ideas often seem detached from reality. Dan Koe has an America-centric worldview, formed in a modern global economy with strained but still working institutions and minimal fear for basic security. For a person living under authoritarianism, reality is often different: external barriers, economic threats and rules changing on the fly that may threaten physically. Talk of self-realisation and creatorship there sounds either as fantasy or as violence: "Они исходят из режима возможностей, но многие из нас находятся в режиме стабилизация, а не развития." (They come from a mode of opportunities, but many of us are in a mode of stabilisation, not development.) [msg #1734]

**What to do — neither reject nor idealise.** Creating oneself as a project, or even as the whole author of one's life, is very hard ("капец как непросто" — damn hard), the author knows from experience. So he does not want to deprive himself of sensing the future, and anything that helps move toward the new model seems useful — evolutionarily it wins now. But it may be better to look at this field as a new language rather than an instruction, and to remember that at some turns of history what matters is not to unlock potential but simply to preserve health and inner truth: "И это не провал, а фаза." (And this is not a failure but a phase.) [msg #1734]

## Detailed insights

### Remote work as a decade-long trend that COVID forced (March 2020)
The channel quotes Warren Buffett: in a crisis the sea sharply recedes and we see who was swimming naked. Remote work was one of the important trends of the past decade, yet far from all companies invested resources in organising flexible work, preferring traditional models of labour organisation; now they are forced to catch up. Many of the author's acquaintances and clients from effective businesses had moved to home mode about two weeks earlier. He forecast they would face their own difficulties linked to the psychological state caused by isolation — but companies with modern culture "купались одетыми" (were swimming dressed) and are better prepared for remote work [msg #161].

### The lockdown as a 9/11 moment for offices (September 2020)
The current lockdown will affect office work the way 9/11 affected airport security — i.e. a lasting structural change. Already half of employees want to work from home most of the week (3–5 days). The author's view: the pendulum will stop somewhere between full remote — fairly hard and leading many to burnout — and pure office work; the new generation chooses flexibility, an Office+Remote balance. The post relied on a Visual Capitalist infographic on the future of remote work according to startups [msg #282].

(!) INCOMPLETE: the infographic itself is not included in the source, so its specific data are not available [msg #282].

### Scale of remote work in Russia (February 2021)
According to the Russian Ministry of Labour, the number of employees who switched to remote employment jumped 110-fold in the past year: in 2019 officially 30 thousand people worked remotely in Russia, by the end of autumn 2020 the count was in the millions. 25% of Russians who moved to working from home during self-isolation remain remote, and another 15% alternate home and office. Hence the expectation that the share of people not coming to the office daily will remain high after the pandemic [msg #396].

### Post-materialism of the young generation (2019)
The channel quotes an article (Texterra) arguing that for the first time in human and Russian history post-materialism has come to the stage in full — a completely new model of economic and social behaviour carried mainly by young people born at the end of the 1990s and after 2000. "Они не хотят потреблять товары и услуги так, как это принято у нормальных людей." (They don't want to consume goods and services the way normal people do.) They fear "normal" people and do not understand their outdated aspirations, which, per the quoted text, suit hamsters more than members of the new digital civilisation. The channel recommends it as a useful summary on youth who will soon become the core target audience of your brand or the key workforce. Channel's caveat: generational theories over-generalise and are in general pseudoscientific, but they are certainly useful for better understanding trends [msg #48].

### Russia's economic lag as a push toward global quality (January 2021)
Per IMF forecasts cited by the channel, developing economies would recover strongly in 2021, growing 8% on average, while Russia would be among the outsiders with GDP growth of only 2.6%. The author's reading: effective Russian companies will try to export more and enter foreign markets more actively — a trend visible for several years, since "if you can create something great, working only for the Russian market is strange". Possibly the economy's lag will increase the amount of advanced business in Russia. Companies competing globally will have to work on the quality of business processes and products and create a modern corporate culture, which the channel defines through four elements [msg #365]:
1. A culture of continuous employee development [msg #365].
2. Transparency and trust [msg #365].
3. Direct communication [msg #365].
4. Attention to the well-being and state of people in the company at all levels [msg #365].

### Using analytics in uncertainty (January 2021)
The author's personal method for orienting himself amid continuing uncertainty and restrictions ("unfortunately for our nervous system"): calmly loading analytics into himself and gradually digesting it. From his stock-market experience he knows analytics is never accurate — nobody really knows how a situation will develop — but well-collected analytics launches fantasy and intuition. His example: a visionary report by Electric and Presium on the post-COVID world, which takes several hours to read but, in his verdict, is worth it [msg #360].

### HR becomes the key function (November 2021)
While writing a column on modern management for Kommersant and re-thinking all known facts and processes in companies, the author concludes: "Становится все очевиднее, что ключевой скоро станет HR-функция." (It is becoming ever more obvious that the HR function will soon become key.) Winning companies will show a strong preponderance of HR and IT in their structure [msg #531].

### The people question goes mainstream in startups (June 2020)
Commenting on the PwC survey of 19 European startup hubs (including Moscow), the channel concludes from the data on key needed employee skills that the question of the human in the company has definitively become mainstream among startups — i.e. the most important. "Межличностные и социальные коммуникации - самый востребованный навык сегодня." (Interpersonal and social communication is the most in-demand skill today.) The analysis covers five themes: the company's hard challenges, threats to development, financing, skills, and city infrastructure. The full data are in *Evidence and numbers* [msg #210].

## Practical playbook

- **When calling people back to the office**, expect resignations among highly qualified, post-conventional specialists; offer a company-specific office-remote hybrid to keep adult-adult relationships and freedom (see *Conventional vs post-conventional reaction*) [msg #426].
- **To retain talent**, map your audience's needs (health, family, learning, multistage life) against your value proposition and change the work model and culture now (see *Four future employee demands*) [msg #797].
- **Let people work in their rhythm** — ask whether someone is a splitter or a blender and align schedules accordingly (see *Splitter vs blender*) [msg #1014].
- **For orientation in uncertain times**, read well-collected analytics slowly, knowing it will not be accurate but will feed intuition (see *Using analytics in uncertainty*) [msg #360].

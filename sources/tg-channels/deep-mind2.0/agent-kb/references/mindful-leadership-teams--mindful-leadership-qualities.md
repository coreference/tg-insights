# Qualities of mindful and compassionate leaders

## What this covers

This file collects what the deep mind channel (author Max Timofeev and guest authors from its leaders' community) taught in 2019–2023 about the inner qualities of a modern, conscious leader: lists of leader qualities (Ray Dalio, Potential Project, the WEF "AI-proof" skills, the five components of an "adequate" boss), the critique of "ideal leader" models, compassion during hard decisions, growth vs compromise thinking, vulnerability and trust, adult communication, trauma-informed leadership, impostor syndrome of new managers, and leadership from presence rather than from the neurotic urge to fix everything. [msg #26, #31, #121, #175, #321, #706, #900]

According to the source this matters because the model of the lonely heroic leader no longer delivers top results, management is "increasingly becoming practical psychology", the boss's adequacy has become more important to job seekers than company stability, and the leader's behavior constantly sends signals that shape the company climate. [msg #175, #321, #706, #200]

This part gathers the deep mind author's posts on the inner side of leadership: how he got past the loneliness of being at the top, two essays on the leader's ego (healthy vs inflated, and a practice for transcending it), an overview of compassionate leadership as taught by his colleague Rasmus Hougaard and related authors, and his argument that mindfulness-based leadership is shifting from a niche practice to a mainstream necessity. According to the author, these qualities matter because a leader running on anxiety and a fragile ego sees less clearly, finds fewer creative solutions, and creates a toxic environment, while leaders with a healthy ego and compassion build respect, engagement and productivity [msg #942, #958, #1024, #1809].

This part contains a single post (12 November 2026) in which the deep mind author responds to the public bullying of Yandex HR director Dasha Zolotukhina. He uses the case to state what a mature leader's relationship with body, emotions and intuition is, to explain why the Russian-speaking public met it with such aggression, to frame the attack as the price of authentic leadership, and to call the community of "people going into the new" to support each other and show the new leadership culture publicly with more courage [msg #1979].

Relation to the existing topic file: the post **extends** the thesis that mindfulness-based leadership is moving into the mainstream (msg #1305) — here the author describes the moment when that shift reaches Russian-speaking society and meets rejection; it **extends** the earlier explanation that the neurotic mode is mainstream because upbringing through violence is passed down generations (msg #900) and that a person dissociated from body and emotions cannot create a different environment (msg #384), now applied to collective aggression; and it adds a new element to the compassion theme — compassion owed to the attackers themselves. No contradiction with the existing file was found [msg #1979].

## Core ideas

- **Inner work comes first; organizational methods without it are a facade.** Awareness and empathy are the foundation of facilitative, non-violent, engaging leadership; personal practice must precede vertical development, horizontal non-violent communication and deep democracy. See *Equation Awareness = Empathy = Facilitative management* and *Ruslan Zaydullin's manifesto*. [msg #321, #384]
- **There is no ideal leader; management is a team sport.** Ideal-leader models are statistically biased and internally contradictory; it is more useful to gather complementary people and make communication between them direct and fast. See *Adizes lens*. [msg #121]
- **Modern effective leaders are defined by behavior with others, not by intellect.** Potential Project's research points to mindfulness, selflessness (ego management) and compassion (wise usefulness to others). [msg #175]
- **Staying in contact with feelings during hard decisions strengthens rather than weakens a leader.** Turning away from feelings gradually makes leaders dry, cynical and cruel, and drains their vitality. [msg #40]
- **Adult communication replaces parent–child dynamics.** It consists of constantly clarifying the boundaries between people through a simple three-step formula. [msg #819]
- **Vulnerability builds trust.** Openly showing needs is the behavior of strong people and, per neuroscience cited by the author, is linked to team trust and engagement. [msg #651]
- **Leadership from presence, not neurosis.** The leader's habitual "solve and fix" drive is often a fractal of a basic sense of insufficiency; experiences of being present with another's challenge without inner tension create a different foundation for leadership. [msg #900]
- **Soft (state-management) skills become decisive at the top.** The poker analogy: the higher the level, the less pure hard skills help and the more mental state matters. [msg #662]

- **Loneliness at the top is a classic leader's pain, and support removes much of it.** Without shared responsibility, energy goes into anxiety, doubt and fear of failure, and the leader sees less clearly; the author's way out was years of adult vulnerability and open, trusting relationships [msg #942].
- **Leadership needs a healthy ego; an inflated ego is fragile and toxic.** Status and ego are not bad in themselves, but ego that grows from grabbing status rather than from widening care and responsibility blocks reality and feedback and is easy to shake [msg #958].
- **Compassionate leadership is a strategy, not just kindness.** Care, listening and empathy raise engagement, satisfaction and therefore productivity [msg #1024].
- **Mindfulness-based leadership is becoming mainstream.** Fear of falling behind in digital and AI transformation pushes even conservative giants toward deep, mindset-level practices [msg #1305].
- **Every leadership day is a series of ego exams; the stable answer is the witness.** Instead of swelling and shrinking with each yes or no, the leader can notice who is observing and find that he is larger than any outcome [msg #1809].

- **Body, emotions and intuition are a legitimate information source for a mature leader — as a complement to analysis, not a replacement.** The author calls this an ordinary modern position, long mainstream in American leadership culture [msg #1979].
- **The attack on Zolotukhina is a rejection of the new by a society used to expecting bad from change, amplified by accumulated displaced aggression.** Fear of novelty alone does not explain its force [msg #1979].
- **In a bullying dynamic, the very rationality invoked by attackers stops working** — the dispute is no longer about facts [msg #1979].
- **Authentic leadership means saying what you consider true before the environment is ready, and paying a price for it** [msg #1979].
- **The new culture needs conscious care and public courage; the old one reproduces itself.** Those who can feel and speak about it should treat that as a privilege and give attackers compassion as well as resistance [msg #1979].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Ray Dalio's nine qualities of a leader
Purpose: a profile of top leaders. The post says the qualities were formed from Dalio's dialogues with the world's best leaders and were adapted into Bridgewater's recruitment system. [msg #31]

1. **Vision plus an organization to realize it.** Each of them visualizes a stunning concept and builds an organization to embody it. [msg #31]
2. **Independent thinkers.** They will not let anyone or anything stand in the way of their goals. [msg #31]
3. **Clear cognitive maps, eagerly tested.** They have clear cognitive maps of the process, and at the same time are burning to test those maps in real conditions and to change the habitual way of executing the process to make it more efficient. [msg #31]
4. **Heightened stress tolerance.** Because their need to achieve the goal is stronger than the pain they experience along the way (the source text has the typo "болт" [sic] where "боль", pain, is meant). [msg #31]
5. **Large-scale vision.** Either because they can form it themselves or because they can get it from other people who see what they themselves do not notice. [msg #31]
6. **Seeing the big picture, the details and the levels in between.** They synthesize viewpoints obtained at different levels, whereas most other people perceive these discretely. [msg #31]
7. **Creative, systemic and practical at the same time.** [msg #31]
8. **Self-sufficient, yet open to the new.** [msg #31]
9. **Most important: passion, intolerance of low standards, and the wish for big positive impact.** They are passionately devoted to the work, intolerant of colleagues who do not meet their quality standards, and strive to have an enormous positive influence on the world around them. [msg #31]

### Potential Project's 3 key qualities of a leader for 2020
Context: the author did a February 2020 internship in Singapore at Potential Project (PP), a global training company that teaches leaders and teams the skills of a "new management paradigm", fully focused on mindfulness and other practices confirmed by neuroscience and research. The author notes their ideas, approaches and even teachers are very close to his own; he had been promoting the theme for 1.5 years in Russia, they for 16 years in 26 countries. [msg #175]

The research basis: five years before the post PP conducted a study at Harvard of 35,000 leaders to understand which leadership models and qualities lead to outstanding results. It showed that the ideal-leader model of the past — the "lonely hero" à la Jack Welch — is no longer able to deliver high results in global competitive industries. [msg #175]

In PP's view modern effective leaders are defined through four things: [msg #175]
- the leader's manifestations and behavior with others, rather than intellect and knowledge;
- flexibility and the ability to collaborate with others;
- empathy;
- adaptability.

From this PP singled out three key qualities, which it develops in its programs: [msg #175]
1. **Mindfulness** — the ability to be focused and attentively aware of what is happening. It allows one to handle one's tasks well and to focus on the right things. PP calls it the most necessary quality for managing both oneself and other people. [msg #175]
2. **Selflessness** — the ability to manage one's ego: not letting personal desires lead one away from the necessary business tasks. The author's opinion: many Russian executives lack it badly. [msg #175]
3. **Compassion** — the motivation to be useful to others. It must be combined with wisdom and business acumen so the leader sees the right steps for the company, for themselves and for society. Compassion here is about right actions, not about pity for others. [msg #175]

Claimed results: leaders with these qualities attract and retain talent, create high team engagement and manage their own energy level better. The qualities can be developed independently through mindfulness practices. [msg #175]

### Adizes lens on management (instead of ideal-leader models)
Problem: articles and books describe the ideal leader by combining the best characteristics of many different people. The author calls this a significant distortion of little practical use, for two reasons: [msg #121]
- **Confirmation error** — we look at successful cases and do not know how many failed leaders had the same qualities.
- **Contradictory qualities** — the qualities of "ideal" leaders often contradict each other; they can coexist in a model but not in real people.

A more constructive lens, drawn from Adizes (the post points to his book on managing change), in four points: [msg #121]
1. **Ideal leaders do not exist.** You can relax and stop trying to squeeze unrealistic results out of yourself. [msg #121]
2. **Management is a complex process that requires more than one person.** Effective management requires four key functions: [msg #121]
   - delivering results (production);
   - increasing efficiency by ordering activity (administration);
   - searching for new opportunities and positioning oneself as their solver (entrepreneurship);
   - aligning the interests and particularities of all participants in the process (integration).
3. **Because these functions often contradict each other, a complementary team is needed.** [msg #121]
4. **Management lies far more in the plane of assembling the right people and effective communication between them.** It is more useful to think not about how to become the ideal leader, but where to find mutually complementary people to cover all key functions, and how to make communication between them direct and fast, and conflicts learning-oriented, respectful and productive. [msg #121]

### Five self-awareness questions for leaders
Why: at least half of our everyday actions are performed on autopilot, unconsciously. For leaders it is especially important to increase reflectiveness and notice their automatisms, because their actions and manifestations constantly send signals to employees and influence the climate in the company. A number of researchers (unnamed) identified lack of self-reflection as a key factor in many modern leadership problems and failures. The author offers these questions for reflection: [msg #200]
1. Which of your reactions prevent you from being a leader? [msg #200]
2. What in your behavior prevents more effective interaction with team members? [msg #200]
3. What in your behavior, communication style or words reduces employees' willingness to show initiative? [msg #200]
4. What makes them feel unprotected or ignored? [msg #200]
5. If you asked colleagues and friends what is the most important thing you need to change in yourself to improve relationships with others, what would they answer? [msg #200]

### Equation Awareness = Empathy = Facilitative management
Answering readers who asked for more concrete content on management (feeling the channel was more about psychology), the author explains his view of what we must do today to be leading-edge leaders tomorrow: «Осознанность = Эмпатия = Фасилитационное управление» (*Awareness = Empathy = Facilitative management*). Since most of us are beginners in awareness, he focuses on the first two parts of the equation; without working through them, in the coming years we will not become strong, engaging and non-violent ("ненасилующих") leaders. [msg #321]

Reasoning: Covid only accelerates the technological leap — the Fourth Industrial Revolution — and increases the need to develop soft skills and non-directive leadership styles. Conclusion: management increasingly becomes practical psychology, learned not so much cognitively through knowledge as directly through developing concrete embodied skills, for example being aware of one's emotions and listening deeply. [msg #321]

### Six AI-proof leadership skills (WEF list)
The author cites a World Economic Forum piece on six skills that cannot be replicated by AI, as the agenda for leaders: [msg #321]
1. **Non-verbal communication** — the ability to communicate effectively non-verbally. [msg #321]
2. **Deep empathy** — the ability to show deep empathy. [msg #321]
3. **Growth management** — the ability to create an environment for employees' development; based on coaching skills. [msg #321]
4. **Mind management** — the ability to help employees recover from stresses arising from human interactions and from other factors; based on therapeutic counseling skills. [msg #321]
5. **Collective intelligence management** — the ability to ensure high-quality collective interaction: to unite, inspire, facilitate and access collective wisdom. [msg #321]
6. **Realize new ideas in an organization** — the skills not only to come up with new ideas but to launch them fully in companies. [msg #321]

### Five components of a boss's adequacy
Data context: see *Superjob survey* under Evidence. The survey does not explain what "adequacy" of the boss means or how leaders can develop it; deep mind's understanding is that it consists of: [msg #706]
1. **An adult position in relationships**, which implies attention and understanding of acceptable boundaries. [msg #706]
2. **Absence of abuse in any form**, including evaluating the person's personality, neglect and moral pressure. [msg #706]
3. **Developed emotional intelligence, self-regulation and co-regulation of other people.** [msg #706]
4. **Communication skills**, including the ability to speak directly, give constructive feedback, praise and amplify talents. [msg #706]
5. **General consciousness and ethics** — understanding of the broader picture and of the second- and third-order consequences of one's actions. [msg #706]

How to develop it: for most people, developing adequacy will require psychotherapeutic work plus training skills of working with states and emotions (which is what deep mind does). [msg #706]

### Formula for adult communication
Problem it solves: at a Saturday Masterheart for leaders the question was insufficient employee engagement — common in the post-Soviet context, where leaders try to hand over responsibility and involve employees but keep hitting passivity. [msg #819]

The author's diagnosis: he does not find it useful to see this through "innate or unchangeable" traits such as serf mentality, a "Russian management model" or a "special path" — he calls that mindset a harmful product of state propaganda or someone's inflamed imagination. Yes, the unhealed history of repression and authoritarian power lays strong foundations for lack of initiative, passivity and even helplessness in any power relationship, so the trauma lens in organizations and large collective work on acknowledging and correcting state violence are doubly useful. But the parent–child dynamic typical of post-Soviet teams can be changed gradually if someone takes the position of a stable adult for a long time and begins to communicate their needs systematically and clearly, confirming that communication with actions adequate to their rank and power in the organization. [msg #819]

Mechanism: while some pull or suppress others trying to involve them, they look at them from above and automatically pack them into exactly that child role that irritates them. The dynamic changes by helping leaders (or employees) take an adult position — with clear open relationships and boundaries built on consistency, attentiveness, vulnerability and trust. [msg #819]

What adult communication is: in a sense extremely simple — one single action, «постоянным уточнением границ между нами» (*constantly clarifying the boundaries between us*). In practice it is hard because, due to trauma, neurosis and manipulative role models, we poorly know our own needs and boundaries and fear being hurt and speaking directly; so we under-invest what is needed or over-invest excess expectations, making communication problematic for the other side. Communication gets easier over time with therapy, deliberate work on communication skills and growing awareness through meditation. Meanwhile, the formula: [msg #819]

1. **State what I have.** «В этих отношениях у меня есть ровно столько, сколько есть: ресурcов и потребностей. Я расскажу тебе об этом максимально ясно.» (*In this relationship I have exactly as much as I have: of resources and needs. I will tell you about it as clearly as possible.*) [msg #819]
2. **Learn what you have.** «Мне интересно максимально ясно узнать, что и сколько есть у тебя.» (*I am interested in learning as clearly as possible what you have and how much.*) [msg #819]
3. **Decide quickly about gaps.** If one of us lacks something the other needs, we must decide as quickly as possible what to do about it and state our decision as clearly as possible, having learned the other person's decision. [msg #819]

How to apply: whenever something goes wrong, at any moment we have to clarify the relationship, taking a partner position and speaking the situation through this formula. Others should not have to guess what we want from them — «мы не центр вселенной, даже если давно находимся в роли CEO, фаундера или акционера» (*we are not the center of the universe, even if we have long been in the role of CEO, founder or shareholder*). In relationships, romantic or team, there is constant labor of saying clearly what we need or want to avoid, checking whether the other person can give it, what needs they have, and how we can satisfy those needs within our own resources: capabilities, desires and readiness. [msg #819]

### Poker lessons for leaders under pressure
Source: the author's two-hour breakfast conversation with a professional poker player from Germany about preparation and success, offered as observations applicable to leadership and other positions involving hard decisions under pressure. [msg #662]
1. **Success depends on a combination of skill and luck.** Skill can let you play brilliantly, but if you keep getting terrible cards you lose regardless — and vice versa. [msg #662]
2. **In the long run luck matters less than skill.** A beginner can win with a good hand, but the longer the game, the more likely the better-prepared player leaves with the money. [msg #662]
3. **Main attention should go to developing skills.** A logical conclusion from points 1–2; also, it is unclear what to do about luck, but skills can definitely be developed and trained. [msg #662]
4. **Separate hard and soft skills.** Hard: analysis, calculations, techniques, tactics and strategies. Soft: literally all our abilities to influence our own state and approach the game in a high-state performance. [msg #662]

### Growth mindset: enlarge the pie, not slice it
Thesis: «думайте об увеличении пирога, а не о том, как справедливо его нарезать» (*think about enlarging the pie, not about how to slice it fairly*). [msg #45]
- **Diagnosis.** Most executives are trapped in compromise thinking: they act from a scarcity mindset about limited resources and the need to constantly seek compromises over their use. Compromise thinking is a narrow way of thinking where we do not perceive the situation whole, only its parts; instead of creating value for the whole system we focus entirely on distributing duties and benefits among its parts. [msg #45]
- **Mechanism.** People tend to stick to their beliefs and ignore alternatives; our view can be very inflexible. If we see the world as a place of scarcity we see only the need to seize and divide; stuck in our representations we miss opportunities — instead of synergy, innovation and growth we see danger and competition, solve problems and choose painful compromises. This thinking is called the first obstacle to building truly effective companies. [msg #45]
- **Analogy.** In a skid on a race track, to avoid hitting the barrier you must not give in to fear and first turn your gaze to the road, not the wall; to steer out you must not fixate on danger, look for the exit and simultaneously perceive the whole situation. [msg #45]
- **Prescription.** Mindset determines everything — change your view of the world and life changes. The world is a place of unrealized opportunities where there are enough resources for all with a smart approach; if you look for synergy and hidden potential you will find them. Understanding the needs and concerns of all parts of the system — all business stakeholders including employees, authorities, citizens, ecology — is hard, but it makes us deeper and wiser, and a comprehensive view leads to better results. Focus on creating value, not distributing it. [msg #45]

### Loneliness at the top and the remedy of adult vulnerability
**The problem.** The author describes the "классическую боль лидеров — чувство одиночества наверху" (classic pain of leaders — the feeling of loneliness at the top): a lot of responsibility that nobody can fully understand or share. At the top "of a pyramid, a small pyramid or even a simple hill" — i.e. at any scale of leadership — much inner energy goes to anxiety, doubt or plain fear of failure. Without support he saw everything less clearly and found creative solutions with greater difficulty; he says he "точно знаю, что когда такой поддержки не было, я и справлялся гораздо хуже" (know for sure that when there was no such support, I coped much worse) [msg #942].

**It is near-universal.** From conversations with other leaders and from working with owners of corporations, senior officials and public politicians, the author concludes that people very rarely feel truly stable and comfortable in a leadership position, however long they have held it, because the world and we ourselves constantly change, so novelty and uncertainty keep arising [msg #942].

**The result when it is overcome.** For the first time in many years he says he overcame this loneliness: although deep mind faces "the hardest tasks in its whole history", he is calmer than before. Each employee, investor, his partner and even friends seem to take on part of the highest responsibility "so that everything works out"; he feels the challenge is not on his shoulders alone, and the habitual fear or anxiety is gone — while noting he is not "flying in illusions of guaranteed success" [msg #942].

**The method (as he describes it).**
1. **Practice adult vulnerability.** He "старался проявлять взрослую уязвимость" (tried to show adult vulnerability) — deliberately opening up about his challenges rather than carrying them alone [msg #942].
2. **Build open, trusting relationships over years.** This was a long-term investment ("годами строил открытые доверительные отношения" — for years built open trusting relationships), not a one-off act [msg #942].
3. **Use an express format when years are not available.** Leader circles (the author's "Masterheart" format) create such openness and vulnerability quickly, so people can open up themselves "or at least see an example of such openness from the outside"; he states "Это все меняет" (This changes everything) [msg #942].

**Why it matters beyond one leader.** The author wants what is built inside the deep mind team to spread outward, because it encourages "authentic and successful projects" where external results are connected to the leader's inner track and needs [msg #942].

### Inflated vs grown (healthy) ego
**Definition of a huge ego.** A "huge ego" is when the significance of one person's "I" is higher than the significance of most others around. When such an ego is brought into a room everything changes and others are required to perform a special ceremonial [msg #958].

**How inflation happens.** High status does not always mean a big ego, but the rules of our environment tend to push the ego that way: as soon as we seize any ground for raising status, there is a tendency to inflate the size of one's own "I" and to feel entitled to special comfort [msg #958].

**Organic vs inflated growth.** If the ego's growth is organic — coming from an expansion of the circle of our care and responsibility — it is healthy. If not, we face the *inflated ego*, which has two problems [msg #958]:
1. **It blocks reality.** It occupies a lot of space, "заслоняя свет объективной реальности и нарушая цикл обратной связи" (blocking the light of objective reality and breaking the feedback loop) [msg #958].
2. **It is fragile.** Because it rests on an unstable foundation, it is easy to shake [msg #958].

**Life ignores illusory significance.** People who "do not understand" our special status will say or do the wrong thing; illnesses, losses, crises and deaths will not respect our right to special comfort. Example: Ziyavudin, who seemed so big and solid and whose office the author often visited for work, is now in prison — his familiar solidity and significance fell apart literally within months. "Жизнь происходит с каждым Эго, особенно в России" (Life happens to every Ego, especially in Russia) [msg #958].

**How to recognize an inflated ego.** By "puffed-up cheeks": as if success, money, power or another basis of status requires us to put on a "рожа" (a "mug", a pompous face) so that from our smugness and seriousness it is clear we are "не просто так" (not just anybody) [msg #958].

**Antidote: special and utterly ordinary.** When stuck long in such "specialness", it helps to remember that each of us is indeed special and unique — but also "extremely ordinary. So ordinary that it can't get any more so". With this "mug" we too will have to breathe, fall in the mud, age and die like everyone else. A sense of self-importance has never protected anyone from crises in which one loses everything that seemed to belong to them stably [msg #958].

**Reflection questions.** The ego lens is not popular in personal work today; people focus on endlessly changing life circumstances or, in the privileged case, on soft skills. The author suggests sometimes looking at the foundation we act from: "*а какой я человек? Насколько выше других я сам себя ставлю и почему?*" (what kind of person am I? How far above others do I put myself, and why?). He says this is especially important for leadership, management and making a truly significant contribution to the world [msg #958].

**Verdict.** "Ни в статусе, ни в эго нет ничего плохого" (There is nothing bad in either status or ego). But, he says with "a high degree of confidence", leadership, especially its modern forms, requires a **healthy ego**, while an inflated one will get in the way and create a toxic environment around us [msg #958].

**Example of a healthy ego — the boss who asked a 26-year-old.** One of his bosses, holding the highest administrative post at federal level, constantly raised the status of the people around him without raising or lowering his own. He asked the author, then a 26-year-old, whether he was doing everything right. Not often, but given the huge status gap this was unique and created a relationship of respect. Not worrying every minute about his own significance only strengthened his status through the respect it generated. Contrast: with another boss the author had to "jump around" in constant fear of breaking some "Chinese vase" of his "I" [msg #958]. In the 2026 retelling the author adds that the boss asked this when they were alone, that he headed an entire industry, that the young author "could not really answer anything", yet felt respected and still remembers him as an outstanding leader whose team one truly wanted to stay in [msg #1809].

**Cultural obstacle.** Modern culture is quite narcissistic, so a healthy ego can look incomprehensible. When we communicate from a sense of our own normality and do not constantly try to raise our status or lower others', people, being unused to it, often read it as low status — as if "*ты настолько неособенный, раз не пытаешься выделиться, что ничего не значишь вовсе*" (you are so unspecial, since you don't try to stand out, that you mean nothing at all). The author hopes culture may one day change so that healthier forms of ego become what defines high status [msg #958].

### Compassionate leadership (Rasmus Hougaard, Sinek, Brown, Goldsmith)
**Definition.** The author first learned of compassionate leadership from the work of his colleague Rasmus Hougaard, a mindfulness expert. It is an approach to management and leadership that stresses the importance of understanding, sympathy and care for employees [msg #1024].

**Goal and how it is realized.** Per Hougaard, compassionate leaders aim to create a work environment where everyone feels heard, valued and supported. This is achieved by developing soft skills [msg #1024]:
1. **Active listening** [msg #1024].
2. **Openness to other people's ideas and feelings** [msg #1024].
3. **Empathy** [msg #1024].
4. **Personal resilience in difficult situations** [msg #1024].

**Why it is strategic.** Its essence is not kindness for kindness' sake ("хотя это прекрасно!" — though that is wonderful!) but a strategic approach that raises engagement and job satisfaction and, as a consequence, productivity [msg #1024].

**Other authors developing the approach** [msg #1024]:
- **Simon Sinek** — leaders should create conditions in which employees feel safe and valued [msg #1024].
- **Brené Brown** — emphasizes vulnerability, empathy and courage in leadership [msg #1024].
- **Marshall Goldsmith** — his methods stress empathy and active listening for effective leadership [msg #1024].

**Shared idea.** These experts, "among many others", agree that to be an effective leader today one must go beyond simply fulfilling business tasks and goals; compassionate leadership stresses deep, meaningful relationships with employees based on mutual respect, understanding and care. The author calls it "невероятный геймченджер" (an incredible game changer) that can also give leaders themselves other levels of enjoyment of work [msg #1024].

**Context.** The post was written on the day the author met HH the Dalai Lama; compassionate leadership was what the rector of the University of Colorado had come to discuss a week earlier with young leaders from Stanford, Virginia and Boulder [msg #1024].

### The "Who is noticing this?" witness practice
**Purpose.** To turn the constant ego exams of leadership or entrepreneurship into a practice of ego transcendence, so that difficult activity becomes "a path of growth into something larger" [msg #1809].

**The problem it addresses.** The author describes his work, and "any real leadership", as an eternal test for the ego, during which the ego can either grow ever more sensitive and fundamentally fragile, or transcend — move beyond constant inner analysis of oneself. Every day brings a pack of exams: he invites people to meetings — will they agree; a call with a publisher — will they take his book; a b2b sale — will they buy deep mind as team facilitators this time. Usually 5–10 such events pile up each week. If you keep swelling or collapsing with each of them it becomes painful, because active work in reality alternates "zeros and ones", and, second, "я так стану более плохим лидером" (that way I'll become a worse leader) [msg #1809].

**Ego inflation vs the stable model.** With rising in a social hierarchy, *ego inflation* often happens — we get more and expect ever more special treatment. He has seen many such people: on the surface extremely powerful and successful, but internally fragile and volatile — the bigger the ego, the more effort needed to guard it. The more stable model: the leader's focus is on the work itself and on others, regardless of his own status. Then social intelligence is usually well developed and respect for all participants is cultivated — a quality of attention that by its nature helps both with ego inflation and with swelling [msg #1809].

**Supporting view (Hougaard).** The author cites his colleague Rasmus Hougaard's book on leadership in the AI era: we all have an ego; its role is self-preservation, and since it usually puts us ahead of others, it keeps us from acting on deep values and meaning. Research, Hougaard says, shows we get better results when we set the ego aside and cooperate with others; as leaders we succeed by strengthening and inspiring others, not by obsessively clinging to our own self-presentation [msg #1809].

**Origin.** As a new entrepreneur the author first suffered from these exams, tying them to himself and his future; then he got tired of it. Since fluctuations still happen, he devised a way to use each activation of the ego as a chance to step outside it [msg #1809].

**Steps.**
1. **Catch the trigger.** When an event important to the ego happens and an inner fluctuation arises — unpleasant or, "что особенно важно, приятное" (what is especially important, pleasant) — notice it. Pleasant swelling counts as much as painful shrinking [msg #1809].
2. **Stop for a second.** Pause before reacting [msg #1809].
3. **Ask: "Кто сейчас это все замечает?"** (Who is noticing all this right now?) Do not analyse the content of life; look for the one who observes it — including observing the ego shrinking or swelling [msg #1809].
4. **Recognize the witness as larger than the outcome.** Usually it becomes obvious that in essence "I" am larger than any specific outcome: the witness, which is "I" far more than any statuses and social locations, was here before this call and will remain after it — before this failure and success, before any events [msg #1809].
5. **Let the effect arrive.** From this point comes not so much relief as a kind of wonder: "*надо же что я вообще все это проживаю!*" (wow, that I am living through all this at all!). The importance of specific cases is released and joy and gratitude arise [msg #1809].

**Who needs it most.** At deep mind there is no large organization or special status behind the author's ego for it to rest in, so the whole load lands directly on the ego. He expects that in coming years, as many employees leave the corporate world and AI-enforced solo-entrepreneurship grows, more people will be in this position. Behind the ego there may be no big brand or power — and even with them life will knock you over more than once — "Но там всегда остается свидетель, в котором мы можем найти путь" (But there always remains the witness in whom we can find the way) [msg #1809].

### Body, emotions and intuition as an information source complementing analysis
**The case.** On 9 November IT blogger Digital Ниндзя released a video about an interview with Yandex HR director Dasha Zolotukhina, in which she says that in moments when there is no clear answer in her head, she listens to her body and notices what she feels. Within 3 days one phrase from the interview had been turned into the claim that one of the country's top HR people hires people "по вайбику вместо данных" (by vibe instead of data); then came labels like «секта» (sect) and «эйчарка» (a dismissive word for a female HR person), comparisons with Blinovskaya, discussion of her appearance — and the video gathered about one million views. The author says he did not want to write about it at all — "зачем поддерживать вниманием булшит" (why support bullshit with attention) — but wrote because of the scale [msg #1979].

**What she actually said vs what was attributed.** Zolotukhina never said a company should be managed on intuition alone. What she describes is, in the author's words, «**обычной современной позицией зрелого руководителя**» (an ordinary modern position of a mature leader). The components of that position:
1. **Attention to the body** — noticing bodily sensations when the head gives no clear answer [msg #1979].
2. **Attention to emotions** — noticing what one feels [msg #1979].
3. **Attention to intuition** — treating it as data [msg #1979].
4. **All three as a source of information that complements analysis** — «как к источнику информации, дополняющему анализ» (as a source of information complementing analysis); analysis is not discarded [msg #1979].

The author states that in American leadership culture this has long been part of the mainstream, and that what is happening is the leading edge of a cultural shift that has reached Russian-speaking society and met what the new always meets in a society used to expecting bad things from change — rejection («отторжение») [msg #1979].

### Displaced-aggression explanation of public bullying
The author's causal model of why the reaction was so strong (his interpretation, not a cited study):
1. **Accumulated aggression.** «В русскоязычном обществе накоплено колоссальное количество агрессии» (A colossal amount of aggression has accumulated in Russian-speaking society) [msg #1979].
2. **Its source is suppressed pain.** It comes from pain that people living for a long time inside all-pervading systemic violence have to suppress for years, unable to present it to those who cause it [msg #1979].
3. **The psyche seeks a detour.** Under such conditions the psyche looks for a workaround and finds a figure that is visible and at the same time safe to strike [msg #1979].
4. **The target fits almost ideally.** A woman, mother of two children, who openly speaks about feelings from a high position suits this role almost perfectly [msg #1979].
5. **She embodies what others cannot allow themselves.** Dasha becomes a trigger also because she embodies «телесность, чувственность, интуицию, открытость, сострадание» (bodiliness, sensuality, intuition, openness, compassion). When someone nearby lives that openly, one's own tightness/constraint («зажатость») becomes more visible and hurts more [msg #1979].
6. **Rationality collapses.** In this dynamic the very rationality in whose name the attack is waged stops working: words are torn out of context, the person is credited with things they did not say, and no clarification can be heard any more, "потому что спор давно идет не о фактах" (because the dispute has long not been about facts) [msg #1979].

Practical implication within the author's logic: once the dynamic is about displaced pain rather than facts, further clarifications by the target will not be heard [msg #1979].

### The author's call to the community of new leadership
Addressed to "us as a community of people going into the new"; he wants the story not to frighten but to strengthen them and says he is sure it will [msg #1979].
1. **Recognize the shift has already happened.** As the video's author himself admits, Dasha's views have become the norm in IT — so even he confirms that the big cultural shift has already occurred [msg #1979].
2. **Give the new conscious care.** «как малые дети, новое требует большей сознательной заботы, чем былое» (like small children, the new requires more conscious care than the old). Hence: support Dasha, support each other and the culture that is dear to us [msg #1979].
3. **Show new leadership publicly with more courage than before** — "новое лидерство, осознанность, эмбодимент и прочие нормальные элементы зрелости" (new leadership, mindfulness, embodiment and other normal elements of maturity). Why: the old agenda needs no defense and reproduces itself "как пожар в осеннем лесу" (like a fire in an autumn forest), while the new exists only where someone dared to speak aloud [msg #1979].
4. **Handle your privilege carefully and keep compassion for attackers.** The ability to feel, to notice one's body and state, and also to talk about it, remains a huge privilege that millions of people around us lack. And those who attack today deserve our compassion no less than a rebuff [msg #1979].

### Compassion during hard decisions (firing, budget cuts, refusals)

That is why some executives over the years end up dry, cynical, in many ways even cruel; one day they cannot understand how it happened and can hardly remember themselves as soft, kind, loving people. - **Firing example (author's "modest experience"):** even when you tell a person they are fired, if you still see a human being in them, are present with them and empathize, they accept it more easily and understand you better — they understand that you too are part of the system, and may be glad you keep your humanity. [msg #40]
- **The choice:** through our life and actions we can create a world and companies in which even in difficult situations «мы друг другу люди, а не волки» (*we are humans to each other, not wolves*). This choice remains ours and makes us stronger and wiser — even if the system is so harsh it devours us and breaks everything human, it is up to us whether to break or not. [msg #40]

### Occam's Razor infographic: how to be a good boss and a good employee
Shared via the community, translated into Russian by Alexander Likhtman (founder and CEO of 2L-PR Agency). The OCR is scrambled; the content below follows the infographic image. [img #447]

Boss side (6 rules): [img #447]
1. **Do not hold Michelangelo's hand while he paints the Sistine Chapel.** Your job as a boss is to hire the best and then not stand in their way, letting your stars shine.
2. **Explain the strategy — and, crucially, why this particular strategy.** Set aside extra time to periodically explain to the team the core principles of strategic thinking and decision-making within the current strategy.
3. **Your task number one is employees' professional growth.** Sense their strengths and opportunities, recommend learning programs inside and outside the company, help your people grow.
4. **Teach how to think, not what to do.** This pays off in the long run and saves your time in the future.
5. **When setting rules, test them on yourself first.** Your task is to create an inspiring work atmosphere and a defined work order, and to act according to the established rules yourself.
6. **Consciously practice self-analysis.** Ask for feedback; investigate what makes other top managers effective or unproductive.

Employee side (10 rules): [img #447]
1. **Never "bring" a problem.** Bring solutions, or recommend next steps that seem to you a way out.
2. **Be thoughtful.** Ask more questions than required; dig into the problem to find the root cause, not the consequence.
3. **Care about details.** Attention to detail is one more way to achieve outstanding results.
4. **Forecast short and long term.** Learn to see the full picture of events.
5. **Create your personal board of directors.** Find more people from your company, ask their opinion on your situation, learn to look at a problem from another angle.
6. **Do at least one thing outside your team.** Develop outside work; it broadens horizons, solves the networking problem and helps interaction inside the team.
7. **Invest in yourself.** If you want your professional value in some field to meet tomorrow's requirements, remember that achieving it is fully in your hands — so take care of yourself.
8. **Ask for responsibility, not promotion.** By successfully solving higher-level problems you prompt top managers to promote you.
9. **Stay weird.** Be yourself; striving for idealism compromises with the peculiarities that make you you, and that mix leaves an indelible mark in others' memory.
10. **Act in the interests of the company, not the boss.** Defend the right decision for the company and share your expertise.

The infographic closes with a quote attributed to Avinash Kaushik: every person is continuous work; the main thing is to know the patterns, set goals and keep working on being better. [img #447]

### Case: Alexander Likhtman's agency — breakdown, openness and coaching
Guest post by Alexander Likhtman, founder and head of a Moscow PR agency (40+ people), which was a pilot client of the deep mind team before deep mind existed. [msg #453]
- **The breakdown.** The agency set a super-ambitious financial goal for 2018, achieved it — and broke: people stopped seeing joy and meaning in work, resignations and conflicts began, nearly all indicators fell and clients started leaving. [msg #453]
- **What did not work.** The business is creative with few repetitive processes; directive management "with a slant to totalitarianism" may work but is not his style. Organizational consultants and various trainings did not work at all: «На выжженых людей хоть Гандапаса выпускай или Тарасова, ничего не прирастет.» (*You can unleash Gandapas or Tarasov [Russian motivational speakers] on burnt-out people — nothing will grow.*) He came to Max Timofeev with a request roughly like "it seems we're all broken, and it's not about management. Help?" [msg #453]
- **What worked.** The leaders were worked with and taught to formulate messages openly, directly and ecologically, and shown how one can and should work on oneself. This changed the whole game, bringing many past grievances and conflicts to the surface and making the team somewhat more alive and inspired; it showed the role of communication and trust, and he considers that recovery began there. [msg #453]
- **Side effects of openness.** Many side effects of openness surfaced, and all (!) of the managers left; the organization itself transformed strongly into a flat one. Now they are again 40+ people moving ("crawling?") toward self-organization and self-management. [msg #453]
- **Lesson on communication.** Communication turned out to be one of the main stumbling blocks for everyone; how communication is built inside an organization and how openly people talk determines nearly everything. After training as a facilitator he felt team meetings became many times more effective. Structuring communication matters, but for a leader it is even more important to listen, hear and create space for exchanging real facts. [msg #453]
- **Coaching.** He went on to train as a full coach — one who asks questions, listens with engagement, does not evaluate, and helps a person unlock their maximum potential in the best way, by themselves; he calls going to study the most important decision of many years. In his view the word "coaching" is heavily devalued on the Russian market (almost anyone calls themselves a coach, even without skills or competencies); real coaching implies major work on oneself, because you cannot learn to listen to and help another without learning to do it with yourself. These skills will be in high demand in the culture of the future, which requires ever more creative solutions and living an authentic rather than imposed life — and many companies and leaders would benefit from coaching practices already today. [msg #453]

### Impostor syndrome in young managers
When a team member is promoted to a management position, they often feel a kind of empty space: before, they constantly did something with their hands and the result was obvious; now people come to them and they "just" say yes or no. Young managers get frustrated and feel like impostors because: [msg #536]
- it is unclear to them why they have the right to decide for others or evaluate others' work;
- their work used to be visible, and now it seems they are paid more just to sit in meetings;
- it used to be clearer what to do, and now goals are more abstract;
- they want to stay on the same level as their old colleagues, but this somehow does not work well.

All this is disorientation from the change of role, which often ends in an unnecessary rollback of the person to their old position. Good companies create internal mentoring and onboarding for such cases, because the vertical transition involves many changes — required skills, status and the main focus of attention — that are hard to sort out alone and quickly. [msg #536]

Definition offered: «Управление можно определить как способность менять восприятие ситуации людей для движения команды в определенном направлении.» (*Management can be defined as the ability to change people's perception of a situation in order to move the team in a certain direction.*) In a managerial position the main work becomes listening and speaking, with listening the key skill, because without it one cannot understand another's reality. [msg #536]

What helps: the young manager adapts more easily knowing that the role truly needs different skills and the content of work is different — not worse or better, just in another plane: not handing out tasks but understanding and creating reality through communication. For most people this plane is much harder because its tasks and skills are very subtle and seemingly invisible; and nowhere in our education were we taught to listen and hear others or to manage our attention. [msg #536]

### Vulnerability, friendship and trust
While conducting entry interviews for the deep mind community, the author repeatedly heard accomplished people say that, among other things, they simply want to find new friends. [msg #651]
- The first reaction may be intuitive rejection and social fears ("you should already have friends, or something is wrong with you"); the author calls this a very debatable claim, since until you start working on yourself you do not start choosing your environment by values. [msg #651]
- For him such admissions reflect back the result of three years of transmitting values of openness, vulnerability and growth: they mean that truly adult people come — people able to trust others and voice their needs. [msg #651]
- He "checked": it is many times easier to solve functional tasks than to share a truly intimate request, for example for friendship or love, because it seems to require admitting a deep deficit and need for relationships where we are accepted simply for who we are. [msg #651]
- «Так ведут себя сильные люди» (*This is how strong people behave*). The deepest thing people can do in life is to be together as honestly and attentively as possible; this moment arises when people show vulnerability and the other accepts it without judgment. Honesty and openness bring out the real person and evoke trust, so they can find friends for a new level of depth and authenticity. [msg #651]
- Open question posed by the author: if we judge some inner deficit so much that we cannot step over it and show it, can we build real relationships at all? «Возможны, ли настоящие отношения там, где мы не настоящие?» (*Are real relationships possible where we are not real?*) [msg #651]
- Leadership link: the author cites neuro-research (an HBR article on the neuroscience of trust) indicating that showing leader vulnerability — strange as it seems to many confused "stoic" managers — is directly linked to the level of trust in teams and employee engagement, and therefore their effectiveness. [msg #651]

### Avoiding bad decisions under stress (Ron Carucci)
The brain reacts more acutely than usual under stress; in difficult moments we reflexively narrow and simplify our actions to "all or nothing" polarities. «Но в мире не существует сложных задач, для которых есть только два возможных решения.» (*But there are no complex tasks in the world for which there are only two possible solutions.*) Deep mind translated an article by consultant Ron Carucci on expanding one's leadership range, which analyzes polarities in four stressful situations: [msg #665]
1. delegating important work;
2. delivering unpleasant news;
3. making high-risk decisions;
4. solving a chronic problem.

### Trauma-informed leadership
If we know which causes in human development lead to certain behaviors in adults, we can see many puzzling situations in relationships or joint work differently. This changes the question we ask: instead of the unconstructive «Что с тобой не так?» (*What is wrong with you?*), asked when people behave strangely by our standards, we ask the question that helps understand the other's reality — «Что произошло с тобой?» (*What happened to you?*). This is the essence of trauma-informedness and the starting point for developing subtle skills of interacting with people. Leading leaders increasingly take into account the science of human development and psychological trauma; these practices are taking shape as a separate field — trauma-informed leadership, which the accompanying card calls an important trend for management and product creation. [msg #757, img #757]

### Leadership from presence instead of the "solve and fix" neurosis
Reflection from the author's experience of Masterhearts (group sessions where leaders discuss personal leadership challenges). [msg #900]
- **The leader's impulse.** Leaders have a special inner impulse that drives them constantly to do, improve and create around them; it works like a large hadron collider with incredible potential. [msg #900]
- **The difficulty.** Because of the ingrained habit of changing reality toward one's goals and putting things in order, it is often hard just to be in attentive listening or contemplation, practicing observation rather than action. Staying aside is especially hard when personal leadership challenges are discussed — it "itches" to jump in and fix everything. Here the habitual pattern of leadership through action — as if dragging reality behind you — is especially visible. [msg #900]
- **Mechanism.** Acting out the inner *solve and fix* pattern, we unconsciously believe that something must urgently be done about discomfort, and then comfort and salvation will come. This pattern resembles a fractal of the original neurosis — a basic personal strategy that makes us feel insufficient and push toward action to win love and get rid of the unpleasant feeling of insufficiency. [msg #900]
- **Hedge and origin.** The author does not claim everyone always acts from their neurosis (a basic dissatisfaction), but says this mode is certainly mainstream, because upbringing through violence has been passed down generations, transmitting neurosis or the belief that one must file oneself down to circumstances for love and safety. Rooted in it, we spend years trying to save ourselves from the fear we met in childhood; but however much we achieve outside, there is no salvation there because the task is inside. «Водой нельзя наесться, хотя все время и кажется, что нужен лишь еще один стакан.» (*You cannot fill yourself up with water, though it always seems you need just one more glass.*) [msg #900]
- **Antidote and method.** See *Tsoknyi Rinpoche's antidote to the fix-it itch*. When in meditation practice, psychotherapy or a Masterheart one manages to experience being present next to someone else's challenge without inner discomfort and tension, this forms unique grounds for leadership — leadership from an entirely different inner space: from abundance, satisfaction and peace. «Лидерство, основанное не на неврозе, а на присутствии, способно привести к совершенно иным результатам в нашей внешней деятельности.» (*Leadership based not on neurosis but on presence can lead to completely different results in our external activity.*) [msg #900]
- **Outlook.** Thanks to the boom in development tools and their accessibility, for the first time in history such leadership can become much more widespread — and perhaps one day we will wake up in completely different corporations and countries. [msg #900]

### Why new leadership is "eating" the old
**The demand shift.** Executives from a large company with hundreds of thousands of employees came to deep mind for programmes: they realize they need an urgent digital transformation or risk serious problems within the next few years. Large-scale change in big structures is extremely hard; history has successful transformations, but they often stall not on technology but on people — established worldviews, habits and communication styles [msg #1305].

**Mindset change is among the hardest tasks in psychology.** The author says this is confirmed both by his empirical experience and by a McKinsey report that a large share of digital transformations fail because of culture: roughly 70% of digital transformation initiatives do not reach their goals, with resistance to change in corporate culture among the key reasons. On the other hand, the obviousness of imminent "death" and fear of it can be a powerful stimulus for change [msg #1305].

**From IT to everyone.** Previously, demand for deep mind's corporate learning and development products came mostly from IT companies, which long ago understood the importance of new approaches to employee care and new forms of leadership and communication. Now the author sees Marc Andreessen's 2011 Wall Street Journal idea "Software Is Eating The World" (IT companies capturing traditional industries) proving right: with AI frightening everyone, even traditional giants in conservative sectors realize they must become more technological, fast. So a wave is coming in which leading — "countercultural, strange, unusual" — practices that work on deeper levels than knowledge transfer become needed by everyone, spread into traditional sectors and start solving increasingly specific tasks [msg #1305].

**Cases cited.** In February deep mind ran a programme for Moscow developer Level.Group (traditional sector), teaching time management through the prism of mindfulness — "deep work" that does not give "прикольные и часто бесполезные фреймворки "делай так"" (cool and often useless "do it like this" frameworks) but changes the psychological patterns themselves. A similar project for the former Arrival team taught product pitching not only through storytelling but also through state regulation [msg #1305].

**Forecast.** The author believes Theory U and other methodologies for introducing mindfulness-based leadership will be in greater demand. Real evolution happens not only when innovators appear — like Qiwi under Sergey Solonin, with whom deep mind worked in 2020 — but when change becomes mainstream, and he thinks we are approaching that stage, which will fundamentally change leadership and the rules of the game at work. He cites a Silicon Valley top manager's January 2025 statement "Mindfulness Isn't a Luxury, It's a Leadership Tool": mindfulness skills and the tools that develop them are no longer a luxury for the most advanced companies but the base for successful leadership [msg #1305].

**Fear and the golden age.** At a recent co-sensing session many participants showed fear and anxiety about large social processes, including the AI revolution; the author offers the alternative view that change can also be seen as movement toward a golden age of humanity, and one can be glad to live in such interesting times [msg #1305].

## Evidence and numbers

- **Superjob survey (2022), as reported by the author.** Superjob's research center annual survey on key factors in choosing a job found that in 2022 the adequacy of the boss became more important to job seekers than company stability. High salary still leads (56%). The importance of management's sanity grew by 10 percentage points since 2019 (from 27% to 37%). Company reliability used to matter more (46–49%) and is now less critical (35%). Employers have not yet caught the trend: they recognize the importance of salary (71%) but somewhat overestimate company stability (52%) and office location (39%). The survey does not explain what "adequacy" consists of (hence the five-component model above). [msg #706]

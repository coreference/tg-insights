# Emigration and relocation psychology

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel author's posts (March 2022 – April 2025) on the Russian emigration that followed the start of the war: the economic and psychological situation of those who left in March 2022 and after the September 2022 mobilization, the four groups into which the Russian-speaking community split, the three tasks the 2022 emigration faces, and a psychoanalytic and developmental model of "national separation". The author treats emigration as part of a shared collective trauma that "has already influenced personality and is forming its new trajectory", and argues that consciously processing it (grieving, finding supports, integrating into other cultures while keeping contact with those inside Russia) decides whether the released energy goes into creation or into destruction [msg #599, #802, #848, #1349].

All posts are written by the channel author in the first person; positions below are his views unless stated otherwise. The author frames the March 2022 post as addressed mainly to Russians, while stating that "first of all we pray for the swiftest ceasefire on the territory of our brother country, Ukraine", and that all other problems pale compared with the suffering there [msg #599].

## Core ideas

- **The former life has ended; the ruptures split life into "before and after".** The task is to grieve, find inner supports and eventually "tie up" the inner split so that it becomes just a stage (see *Four-point situation assessment*) [msg #599].
- **The 2022 leavers are closer to refugees than to emigrants.** Most left unplanned, without hard-currency income, and face a 2–3x drop in living standards (see *Four options*) [msg #599, #848].
- **Grief speed decides creative capacity.** Much energy goes into experiencing the collapse of habitual supports; it must pass through all stages of grieving, and those who pass the cycle faster redirect energy to creation sooner [msg #599].
- **Emigration releases suppressed energy as well as pain.** Losing comfort, people can feel how much more important freedom is than unfreedom [msg #737].
- **Judging others' choice to leave or stay is an intrusion.** Each adult bears responsibility for his own life [msg #737].
- **The 2022 emigration must walk a tightrope:** preserve itself, keep authentic contact with those in Russia, and truly integrate into other cultures — not closing in on itself and not dissolving entirely (see *Three tasks*) [msg #802].
- **The community has split into four parts** that are no longer parts of one whole yet are still united by the war and collective trauma; "everyone has the right to pain" (see *Four parts*) [msg #848].
- **National separation:** living across cultures first makes one feel *more* Russian, and precisely that makes nationality an object one can separate from — becoming autonomous, not fleeing (see *National separation*) [msg #1349].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Four-point situation assessment for 2022 emigrants ("Cloud Russians", March 2022)
Purpose: the author's sober description, written on 7 March 2022 "through the eyes of the current wave of urgent emigrants", of where Russians who left stand. He presents it as four numbered points [msg #599].

1. **The former life has ended.** "Прежняя жизнь закончилась." (*The former life has ended.*) The author says it is important to understand this. One may hope it will change in a not very long time, but it is better not to forecast. In any case, the ruptures that have already happened divide life into "before" and "after" — "разрывы, которые уже произошли, делять жизнь на до и после" (*the ruptures that have already happened divide [sic] life into before and after*) [msg #599].
2. **Prospects are "so-so"** unless one quickly relocated, legalized and found decent work paid in hard currency. This applies both to those abroad without hard-currency income and to those in Russia without such income [msg #599].
3. **Many Russians are already in a very difficult situation.** Savings or income in rubles have disappeared or been devalued. Even a previously good salary of a notional 400k rubles gives a rather low standard of living even in non-rich countries such as Thailand: an ordinary cup of coffee at the new exchange rate is 60 baht = 240 rubles. Relocation also requires additional expenses for visas, legal matters and settling in. Those who stayed are not in a more enviable position: inflation in sanctioned Iran reached 40% a year; citizens inside the country should also look for hard-currency income, and on top of that carry "a huge psychological burden" from living in a country under dictatorship and in isolation [msg #599].
4. **Options for those who left** — see the next framework [msg #599].

### Four options for those who left ("cloud Russians")
The author lists four scenarios, each with his estimate of how likely it is [msg #599]:
1. **Find yourself at your own level and profession, but with hard-currency income, in a new country.** Likely for many IT specialists, product managers, marketers, designers — "in a word, all who make things in demand in the modern world". "Но не для большинства." (*But not for the majority.*) [msg #599]
2. **Start earning substantially more in rubles.** Unlikely, given the collapse ("схлопывания") of the economy [msg #599].
3. **Fall in living standard by 2–3 times for an indefinite period.** Likely for many [msg #599].
4. **Start looking for any additional side-income opportunities, including things we did not usually do.** Likely for many [msg #599].

Conclusion drawn from the options: "Уехавшие находятся ближе к статусу беженца, чем эмигранта." (*Those who left are closer to the status of a refugee than of an emigrant.*) Those whom their company relocated or substantially helped are best off, but even this may not be stable, because Russian employers with remote work, such as Ozon, may collapse later [msg #599].

Overall summary: a large "humanitarian catastrophe" of the 21st century has happened; refugee flows in Europe are the largest since World War II; this is possibly the largest outflow of smart people from Russia since the 1917 revolution. Adapting to the crisis will require living in uncertainty and all kinds of deprivation, possibly experiencing poverty and dispossession — but it will also open opportunities and create new forms of cooperation and mutual aid [msg #599].

### Three tasks of the 2022 Russian emigration (March 2023)
Context: the author notes he sees no voice, media outlet or platform yet where the 2022 Russian emigration takes shape and reflects on itself; he wants one to appear quickly but thinks it will take time because "this wave of leavers does not yet fully realize its emigration". He sees it as an "incredibly interesting group with huge potential" that must solve three difficult tasks **simultaneously** [msg #802]:

1. **Preserve oneself** ("сохранить себя"). The source gives no further explanation of this task beyond naming it. (!) INCOMPLETE: task 1 is named without elaboration [msg #802].
2. **Preserve authentic contact with those who remain in Russia.** Why: otherwise the break will be too dramatic, and later it will be impossible to share the much-needed wealth — the wealth that "on the scale of the country has now been zeroed out" ("которое нам в масштабе страны сейчас обнулили") [msg #802].
3. **Truly integrate into other cultures.** The author says this is exactly the task that failed for a whole part of society that forms modern Russian fundamentalism. Those who genuinely preserve and develop their own culture are not afraid of foreign words and are in constant interaction with the external environment; they are enriched by it, bringing freshness into their own. "В закрытом бункере нельзя ничего создать, кроме помирания." (*In a closed bunker nothing can be created except dying.*) Closed fundamentalist "we against them" thinking always slides into destruction rather than creation [msg #802].

The synthesis image: in the coming years the Russian emigration will probably have a very important and "practically impossible" task — "пройти по канату над обрывом и не упасть ни в какую из сторон. Не замкнуться на самой себе, выйти в свет и не уйти туда полностью." (*to walk a tightrope over a cliff and not fall to either side. Not to close in on itself, to go out into the world and not go there entirely.*) [msg #802]

### Four parts of the Russian-speaking community ("Who we have become", June 2023)
Purpose: the author wants to *feel* (not yet understand — "understanding is impossible for now") how the human community united by the Russian language has changed in 1.5 years, in order to know where he himself wants to go and where his life can be of use. He describes the community as at minimum united by language, ideally by shared values and meanings, at maximum by work partnership, close friendship and "live hugs" [msg #848].

1. **Those who emigrated in the last year, mostly unplanned.** Even with money and a pleasant new picture around, these are very strong, complex changes and still ongoing stress. The author places himself mostly in this part, although he emigrated before the war [msg #848].
2. **Those who stayed in Russia.** They live in a tightening "mental altered state" ("ментальной измененке") with a high level of unsafety and uncertainty, and — more importantly — without the possibility of thinking about and emotionally living through this reality. The author says this part seems to forbid sympathy towards itself, because outside sympathy reminds it of a problem it has no strength to look at — "И все равно безмолвно словно кричит о помощи." (*And still it is as if silently crying for help.*) He hedges his own right to say this: maybe he has no right, since what does he know from the outside; or perhaps it is often clearer from the outside; "and is it really from the outside?" [msg #848]
3. **Those who neither moved nor stayed — earlier emigrants, already settled.** They are still connected with everyone. They too can feel pain, as conscious people do from war anywhere, or because even a long-ago move to the UK, Australia or the USA does not completely cut off the past story. This part is likely much more comfortable than the others, and so it feels as if it has no right to show its pain. "Хотя это не так — у всех есть право на боль." (*Although that is not so — everyone has the right to pain.*) [msg #848]
4. **Those who live in Ukraine or hastily left it.** The author allows that there may be sub-parts there he knows nothing about. He thanks them for "sitting here by the fire, adding wood to it" — which, he says, requires a special resilience, wisdom and kindness — and values being able to talk with this part while keeping some sense of community [msg #848].

Synthesis: everyone keeps living, but not as before. These are no longer parts of a single whole, yet they are united by something: "Нас всех разъединила и одновременно объединила война и коллективная травма" (*The war and collective trauma have both separated and at the same time united us all*) — a shared experience that has already influenced personality and is forming its new trajectory. Whoever reads this is part of this context; the author jokes that he would like to shout "congratulations, it's yours!", but there are no congratulations — it is simply yours, mine and ours, so painful, complex and still unfolding that it is still largely unconscious. Hence we do not yet know ourselves either: "Кем-то новым еще не стали, но становимся прямо сейчас. Как бы почувствовать, кем?" (*We have not yet become someone new, but we are becoming right now. How could we feel — whom?*) The post opens with a disclaimer: it is about the current situation, and those who try to mentally avoid "our common context" should not read it [msg #848].

### National separation (April 2025)
Definition: "национальной сепарацией" (*national separation*) — the process in which a person, especially through emigration or multicultural existence, stops experiencing their nationality as an absolute: as the only possible frame of identity and as the only object of loyalty. The term was proposed by Dasha, with whom the author left Russia 4 years earlier [msg #1349].

How it starts (the paradox): after leaving and going through many borders, countries and cultures, the author unexpectedly began to feel *more* Russian — not from growing national pathos, but because differences became more visible: "Я почувствовал границы своего культурного тела." (*I felt the borders of my cultural body.*) Yet precisely this intensification of the "Russian" became the beginning of letting it go: what had long been background moved to the foreground and became an object, which allowed psychological separation to begin [msg #1349].

The process is explained through three lenses:
1. **Mahler's separation-individuation.** Analyst Margaret Mahler's theory: first we merge with the maternal object, then we begin to differentiate it, sometimes hostilely, and only later acquire the ability to be ourselves in contact, without dependence or denial. The author asks: what if national culture is also such a primary object? [msg #1349]
2. **Klein's positions.** Following Melanie Klein, the first stages of emigration or of living outside one's culture can provoke a splitting position — "я — другой», «мы — они»" (*"I am other", "we are them"*) — sometimes idealizing the new and devaluing the old, or the reverse. Only later is a transition to the depressive position possible, where the object (here, national belonging) stops being only bad or only good, and we gain the ability to hold it inside, keeping a relationship with it without idealization or denial [msg #1349].
3. **Vertical (integral) adult development — the second, parallel process.** As vertical development theory says, the "переход от этноцентризма к мироцентризму" (*transition from ethnocentrism to worldcentrism*) does not mean renouncing "one's own" but recognizing multiplicity. "Я чувствую себя русским — и это не отменяет того, что я живу в мире, а не в нации." (*I feel Russian — and this does not cancel that I live in the world, not in a nation.*) Identity becomes less devoted and subordinated to a form, becoming fluid; it no longer explains everything about him but becomes one of the layers. He reassembles his culture anew — in movement, contacts, experiences [msg #1349].

What separation is and is not: "Это и есть сепарация — не бегство от, а становление в своей автономии." (*This is separation — not fleeing from, but becoming in one's autonomy.*) The author says it matters to him not as a culture researcher but as a person living this experience: through integrating life experience in different cultures he becomes more himself — not from denying "Russianness", nor from total attachment to it, but from the freedom to hold this layer inside "без страха, без исключения, без подчинения" (*without fear, without exclusion, without subordination*) [msg #1349].

The shadow of separation: the sense of roots disappears; the simplicity of answering "where are you at home" is erased; constant adaptation exhausts; sometimes one simply wants to belong, but there is no easy answer to that either. "Это — тень сепарации." (*This is the shadow of separation.*) Yet adult separation is not loneliness: "Это отказ от симбиоза, ради возможности быть в диалоге." (*It is a refusal of symbiosis for the sake of the possibility of being in dialogue.*) [msg #1349]

Hope it gives: new forms of world consciousness and personal identity — not everyone having the same values, but everyone having a deep capacity to remain themselves, "не воюя за форму" (*not warring over form*), gaining flexible and genuine authenticity and a wider view of the world [msg #1349].

## Detailed insights

### Grief, supports and turning "before and after" into a stage (March 2022)
The author says that in the moment it is frightening, even if things are relatively fine for you — frightening because the habitual supports of our everyday world are collapsing. A lot of energy goes into experiencing this destruction, and that energy "должна пройти все стадии горевания" (*must pass through all the stages of grieving*). Those who can pass this cycle faster will also redirect energy towards creation faster. The prescription: "Нам необходимо найти в себе опоры и в итоге связать раскол от этой травмы внутри. Чтобы для нас это не было "до и после", а стало просто этапом." (*We need to find supports in ourselves and ultimately bind the split from this trauma inside, so that for us it is not "before and after" but simply a stage.*) The source does not specify which stages of grieving are meant or how to find the supports [msg #599].

He proposes a collective identity: it would be great to realize ourselves as "облачных русских" (*cloud Russians*) and to start lobbying a voice abroad separate from the regime, "since we can give the world a lot, and it would be a pity not to realize our talents" [msg #599].

His political framing in the same post (his opinion): the regime has long been dead because it has long lacked the main thing — honesty, sincerity and humanity; what is being observed are painful final convulsions and subsequent decay which "alas, may last a decade". He writes that the war (sarcastically called "МИРНАЯ ОПЕРАЦИЯ", *peace operation*) has certainly shortened the life of the current "elite", "and maybe that is even better, for until the tumour is cut out, healing is impossible" [msg #599].

### Building a "new Russia" in the heart and around the world
In a follow-up the author writes that through all these years, despite "this tumour", "we" were building a new Russia: there is a value of creation, a desire to be part of the modern world; "we are smart and talented". The condition he names: if we have enough fearlessness and compassion, the ability to cooperate and to help each other selflessly, we can create something even more beautiful — "Новую Россию в нашем сердце и по всему миру. А когда-то и в самой России." (*A new Russia in our heart and all around the world. And someday in Russia itself.*) He closes: "May it be sooner than one can hope" [msg #600].

### Politics vs one's own goals; not judging others' choices (October 2022, after mobilization)
- **Time to cast stones, time to gather them.** For weeks the author read news and watched political videos almost daily, and war and power came up in every conversation. Politics is everyone's business — "we all understood or at least felt it on our own skin this year". But by immersing consciousness ever deeper in the political game, "we can lose our own goals and miss time" [msg #737].
- **Economic reading of the second wave.** Another emigration wave on top of the March one will create growth in the economies of Georgia, Armenia, Portugal, Thailand, the UK and the USA, and a minus in Russia's. The author expects the final end of "wretched Bolshevism" and transfer of power to take most likely several years even if the war were lost tomorrow; therefore those who can really create something choose more suitable places for it, where at least they will try not to lose the coming years [msg #737].
- **Not discussing others' choices.** He calls it rather strange to discuss this choice — as well as the decision to stay — because such discussion is a crude intrusion into the reality of individual adults. Everyone bears "literally God-given responsibility" for his own life, feeling and deciding what his role should be today. "Пытаться отнять эту ответственность через свои поверхностные суждения о том, что и кому надо делать, можно либо по глупости, либо из потерянности в нарциссизме." (*Trying to take this responsibility away through one's superficial judgments about what whom should do is possible either out of stupidity or out of being lost in narcissism.*) [msg #737]
- **Released energy and freedom.** The shared living-through of hard personal choices and the painful decay of the country is accompanied not only by pain and trauma but also by "высвобождением подавленной энергии" (*the release of suppressed energy*). At a group meeting he ran in Tbilisi, people shared not only pain but joyful relief, a feeling that there is again freedom and real life. Even losing comfort and finding oneself in the difficult position of an emigrant, one can feel how much more important freedom is than unfreedom — and how oppressive and depressive an atmosphere was created by one person's wish to self-proclaim and hold power at the cost of violence and corruption. He likens the people forming the regime to a cancerous tumour or depleted uranium, toxic to everything around [msg #737].
- **Faith wakes earlier "here" than "there".** Probably "here", in a refugee's momentary lostness, a faith awakens in a possible life in a completely different political air — "not free of toxins, but not killing the very essence of life". Probably among those who remained "there" it will appear later [msg #737].
- **Helping as a source of stability.** His week in Tbilisi was saturated with one wish — to help, support, introduce people, share something — and a "wonderful feeling of mutual love" for people, many of whom he saw for the first time and would never have met without the so-called mobilization. This view let him "почувствовать себя дома без дома, спокойно в неспокойствии, устойчиво в катастрофе" (*feel at home without a home, calm in unrest, stable in catastrophe*) and to feel alive again even against the background of "hell on Earth" — and for that it was worth returning to Russia for a couple of weeks at precisely the wrong moment [msg #737].
- **Ambivalent farewells.** Leaving Tbilisi for Southeast Asia he hugged a lot; at each farewell he clearly felt a sense of meeting again soon and simultaneous sadness at its probable deceptiveness. He broke his usual principle and spoke at farewells as if he really could know: he looked people in the eyes and confidently said that everything would be fine. He notes there is much politics ahead, "but there is not only politics" [msg #737].

### Basic goodness and refusing to close off from the world (March 2023)
- The author has been in "endless travel" since September (in fact begun 4 years earlier): Thailand, Russia, Georgia, Thailand, Nepal, Bali. In that time, as since the start of the war, he has felt no negative attitude towards himself anywhere, and on the contrary often feels support from people of many nationalities — Swedes, Americans and Thais hug and sympathize; with Ukrainians he almost always talks warmly and with a felt shared pain [msg #802].
- **Goodness as a fundamental quality of consciousness.** Human goodness is invariably present because, "as is known in contemplative traditions", in the form of love and compassion it is a fundamental quality of consciousness, "that is, something objective". Jean Béliveau, who walked around the globe in 11 years, told "us" that the world is kind and mostly made of good people. "Мир добрый по природе, несмотря на то, что в нем присутствует бесконечное количество зла в виде воин и насилия." (*The world is kind by nature, despite there being an endless amount of evil in it in the form of wars [sic] and violence.*) [msg #802]
- **Where violence comes from.** Often we do not know ourselves and do not see the causes and consequences of our actions; because of traumas and fear we sometimes lose contact — for a minute or a lifetime — with our fundamental basis: the ability to see beyond ourselves, to love and to have compassion. That is how violence "of all shades" arises, because absence of contact with oneself and with other people "pours oil on the fire of fundamentalism" [msg #802].
- **Practical conclusion.** This is no reason to close ourselves off from the whole world, accusing it of darkness. On the contrary, it is a reason to do good more actively and "throw it into the water" in the hope it will sprout somewhere, and that more of us will see how we ourselves create violence inside, towards other people and towards our home — the whole planet [msg #802].

### The new map of the Russian-speaking diaspora (March 2023)
What clearly changed over the year is the presence of Russian speakers in some places outside Russia and Ukraine: Cyprus, Portugal, Thailand, Georgia, Armenia, Bali, Buenos Aires — "new points on the map after 24 February". Friends invite the author to Berlin, saying an incredible energy potential is felt there. Sergey Solonin is building a whole city on Bali created by very talented Russians. One can now join whole entrepreneurial diasporas in Lisbon or Istanbul — the author adds in brackets that they do not develop like national diasporas. Setting aside evaluations of those who left and those who stayed, he poses the question of what this energy — "по масштабу равная андронному коллайдеру" (*equal in scale to the hadron [sic] collider*), splashed out of the country — will turn into, and how it will be connected with those who remained inside [msg #802].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The March 2022 assessment is explicitly provisional: the author says one may hope things change soon "but it is better not to forecast"; his estimates of likelihood ("likely for many", "unlikely") are his own judgments, not data. The coffee price and 400k salary are illustrative ("условные", *notional*) figures; the 40% Iranian inflation figure is given without a source [msg #599].
- The author repeatedly hedges with "probably/perhaps" ("вероятно", "возможно") — e.g. on when faith in a different life will appear among those who stayed [msg #737], on the emigration's task [msg #802], and on his right to describe those in Russia from the outside [msg #848].

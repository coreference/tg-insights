# Coping with anxiety, panic, depression and personal crisis

## What this covers

This part covers how the deep mind author recognised and worked with fear, anxiety, panic and depression during his own practice in late 2019 and through the early 2020 crisis (stock-market crash, oil-price war, Covid-19, lockdowns). It includes his personal fear-exposure practice, a 7-item crisis action list, a list of anxiety symptoms with four responses, an 8-point "best mindset" for a crisis, a model of how one negative thought becomes an emotional spiral, the author's reading of the collective mood, and an investment-analyst report he forwarded during the crash. According to the author, the stakes are practical: in trauma, stress or accumulated fear people make wrong decisions and slide into crude behaviour, so keeping one's psychological state is "the most important asset" in uncertainty [msg #147, #173].

This part covers five posts from the deep mind 2.0 channel written between 2021 and 2026. The topics are:
- the author's own depressive episode during Covid, and the large support team he used for it;
- self-care during a short lockdown;
- the inner conflict and depressive thoughts ("there is no future") caused by the collective political crisis in Russia in 2022;
- "permacrisis" and survey data on rising clinical anxiety and depression in Russia;
- an analysis of whether "snowflake" youth really are weaker.

According to the author, these states are understandable, widespread and normal reactions to what is happening outside. They still need deliberate support, and depression needs professional supervision because it carries high suicide risk. [msg #404, #491, #750, #758, #1859]

## Core ideas

- **Fear is worked with, not avoided.** Deliberately visualising what you fear while staying grounded in the body made the author calmer, reduced background anxiety and nearly cured his insomnia (see *Daily fear-visualisation practice*) [msg #19].
- **Nervous action increases risk.** In markets, jerky back-and-forth actions increase rather than reduce risk — the author calls this "a mathematical fact" and suspects it also holds for the rest of life [msg #139].
- **Accepting the limits of control brings relief.** Hours of thinking about the "right" strategy in the crash led the author to an inner "letting go": no one knows what the market will do; he controls only risk, and not fully; letting the illusion of control go reminded him to trust life more [msg #139].
- **Decisions made in stress, trauma or fear are bad decisions.** Traumatised people — in the author's view the majority in Russia — already live in fear, and frightening events easily push them into a survival state; collectively this makes things worse [msg #147].
- **State is the key asset; being adequate is a service.** The worst thing to do in uncertainty is act under stress and emotion; "Быть адекватным - акт служения обществу" (*Being adequate is an act of service to society*) [msg #173].
- **Crisis can be met by closing or by opening.** People and organisations either close up and try to take from others, or open, let the new in and seek new connections; the author chooses opening (see *Two ways people and organisations react to crisis*) [msg #160].
- **Negative spirals are human and unavoidable, but they can be caught early.** Even a "level 100 zen master" cannot fully escape emotional swings; the skill is to know what the fall looks like and how to "open the parachute" (see *Falling emotional helicopter*) [msg #216].
- **Late-crisis depression is a stage.** The author's reading in June 2020 was that the prevailing collective state was depression — powerlessness, apathy, a negative future — linked to the final stage of interaction with a stressor (Covid) when there is no strength left yet one still has to face it [msg #214].

- **A depressive state can be a predictable result of piled-up circumstances.** It does not have to come from one event. When outside demands draw on inner resources that are already used up, falling to the bottom makes sense. [msg #404]
- **Support yourself because you feel bad, not to make yourself feel better.** The author stops grading his state. States come and go, so you observe them and do the best you can. [msg #404]
- **Bringing faith back is the key action.** Faith that you can get out sometimes disappears. Bringing it back again and again is what matters most. [msg #404]
- **Depression needs a psychotherapist and a psychiatrist working together.** Any advice about depressive periods must start with this supervision. [msg #404]
- **Stress in the past makes anxiety easier to trigger in the present.** Even a short lockdown can raise anxiety. The fitting answer is extra care and extra supports beyond the usual. [msg #491]
- **Hindsight is a cognitive error.** Nobody controls or knows the future of complex systems. In collective catastrophe, anxiety and depressive thoughts are justified, but your state distorts how you see things. [msg #750]
- **Permacrisis is wearing down mental health, and the data show it.** Anxiety and depression rose sharply in Russia in 2022, especially among people aged 18–24. [msg #758]
- **Rising distress among young people is real, but it is not weakness.** The calm of older generations is often numbness, not strength. [msg #1859]

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Daily fear-visualisation (exposure) practice
Purpose: the author's own practice "in the framework of working through fear and anger" (*в рамках проработки страха и гнева*) [msg #19].

1. **Sit in a meditation posture with a sense of support.** He sits down in the meditation pose and keeps "ощущением опоры" (*a sense of support / grounding*) throughout [msg #19].
2. **Visualise everything you strongly fear.** He visualises "весь возможный щит, которого сильно опасаюсь" (*all the possible shit I strongly fear*), adding details and moving from one misfortune to the next; he imagines "Максимально хреновые вещи" (*the crappiest things possible*) [msg #19].
3. **Dose: twice a day, half an hour each, without stopping** [msg #19].
4. **Observe body and fantasy.** While visualising, he watches his bodily sensations and how the fantasy unfolds [msg #19].

What happened: at first it was very unpleasant — he reacted with the full stress response: sweating, poor breathing, contracting. That passed quite quickly and it became easier; he "got tired of fantasising". He was surprised that the themes of death and terrible illness passed after literally 3 sessions, although death should be the most basic fear; his hedged explanations are that Buddhism, with its constant discussion of death and non-attachment to the body, had its effect, "or maybe the fear is simply deeper" [msg #19].

Results: he became calmer and has less background anxiety "because I consciously deal with it at a set time", and he "almost defeated insomnia" [msg #19].

Caveat (author's recommendation): such techniques — like a regular mindfulness practice and psychedelics — should be done only under the supervision of a trauma-informed specialist, "Ибо никто не знает, что там у нас вылезет во время работы с бессознательным" (*Because no one knows what will come up when working with the unconscious*) [msg #19].

### Short list of 7 crisis actions (volatility and panic)
Context: in March 2020 the author noticed signs of panic states in himself after Russia's exit from the OPEC deal, the oil collapse and the rouble's swing; new Covid case and death data came out every half hour, and the US stock market fell with automatic trading halts. He presents this as a short list of actions he recommends to himself, "maybe useful to you too" [msg #147].

1. **Control risks, needs and resources.** Do not "fly down the pipe" by taking bigger risks than you can afford given the resources you have [msg #147].
2. **Complete the stress cycle daily.** 30 minutes of active physical movement give the body the signal that everything is fine; the reason is so that "I won't take actions in an ineffective and extremely unproductive state of stress" [msg #147].
3. **Work with fear in these days.** Be aware of fear, talk about it, go to a therapist [msg #147].
4. **Call older generations.** It is even harder for them because they have less expertise and more contact with the television [msg #147].
5. **Read news reviews, not feeds.** A feed "thickens the colours" so that it starts to seem things only get worse; "Лента - это шум" (*The feed is noise*) [msg #147].
6. **Meditate.** Needed always, now doubly; meditation lets you see things adequately and preserve the main resources: attention, energy and health [msg #147].
7. **Don't fuss, look at facts.** Remember that panics pass, cures are found for diseases, markets grow back [msg #147].

### Anxiety, fear and stress symptom checklist
The author warned that news (a likely state of emergency in Russia), uncertainty and the shift of many to remote work may lead people — and you with them — to be "psychologically bombed". Watch for these symptoms [msg #155]:
1. Raised shoulders.
2. Tense jaws, eyes, hands, belly, pelvis.
3. Regularly faltering breathing.
4. Narrow tunnel consciousness.
5. Obsessive repetitive thoughts.
6. Tremor, wet (sweaty) limbs.
If you are in constant anxiety, fear or stress about the situation, start working with your state immediately — using the four responses below [msg #155].

### Four responses to constant anxiety
1. **Centring (центрирование).** A practice for quick rebalancing of the organism. Done several times a day it brings the body into a resourceful state, returns attention from thoughts to body sensations, lowers anxiety and lets you make good decisions. The author's team offered it as a 3-minute audio guide [msg #155].
2. **Container technique (техника-контейнера).** If nothing lets you feel safe, this may be a consequence of trauma. Simple techniques will not heal trauma but will reduce emotional intensity. Peter Levine, described as the world's leading trauma specialist, shares two such techniques for self-practice in a video. (!) INCOMPLETE: the source does not describe the two techniques themselves; they are only in the video [msg #155].
3. **Mindfulness practice.** Start today, and start with the basics. Especially if you feel unstable, do not experiment with "fancy" practices — non-dual, ego-disidentification, Dzogchen. Best are body scanning, concentration and metta; the author says these are taught on his mindfulness course because they are the safest for beginners [msg #155].
4. **Psychotherapy.** "Профессиональная поддержка живого человека - бесценна" (*The professional support of a living person is priceless*). If you have the moral readiness and financial resources, now is the time to start; the author offered to share contacts of vetted therapists from the deep mind pool [msg #155].
Closing line: "Берегите себя, вы у себя одни." (*Take care of yourselves, you only have one of you.*) [msg #155]

### Best crisis mindset in 8 points
Premise: experts will be wrong in their forecasts and there is little hope in them, but "we can look for the best or worst mindset for the situation". The author's best one [msg #173]:
1. **We want to LIVE, not survive.** This is a normal and responsible search even in crisis; keeping it means not giving up and staying in contact with the world that is emerging. He asks "How to live?" not "How to survive?" [msg #173].
2. **Do not act under stress and emotion.** The worst thing in uncertainty is acting under stress and emotions. Mental state is the most important asset. Being adequate is an act of service to society: we need people who show confidence and clarity [msg #173].
3. **It will be a new normal.** Fundamental changes have already happened in whole industries — retail, HoReCa, medicine are going through a big shift. By various estimates a return to normal will take 12–18 months, but "это будет новая норма" (*it will be a new normal*), and everyone will have to adapt [msg #173].
4. **Accept that you will likely be in the majority that suffers.** In a crisis a minority does well and the majority badly; statistically we are more likely to be in the majority. If you stop guessing whether you will be spared and accept upcoming difficulties as fact, the situation becomes a little more certain [msg #173].
5. **There are positive things too.** Not only horror but incredibly positive things are happening; we will learn a lot from this crisis and "перезапустим человечность" (*restart humanity*) [msg #173].
6. **Ask yourself excellent questions for growth and self-knowledge** [msg #173]:
   - Am I doing the best I can to be among the better part of humanity right now?
   - Am I starting to hate / fear the authorities?
   - Am I focusing on the community around me? What are the boundaries of that community?
   - Maybe I need this challenge right now?
7. **The real problem exposes clumsiness and sharpens political conflict.** The situation will either sweep away the archaic political culture or let it evolve; in the author's view any change is rather a good thing "in our situation" [msg #173].
8. **Quarantine is a time to create.** World War II brought as much horror as a subsequent leap in previously unattainable humanistic values; therefore quarantine is a time to create new skills, projects, communities and values — a time of awareness, action, unity, and supporting immunity. "Я буду в порядке." (*I will be okay.*) [msg #173].

### Two ways people and organisations react to crisis
1. Close up and try to take from others — save your own skin.
2. Open up and let the new in, assist it, look for new connections with others.
The author chose to open: he wanted to make the crisis not a time of fear, pain and hopelessness but a time of new social connectedness, and asked himself "Всем будет сложно - как я могу быть частью помогающего движения?" (*It will be hard for everyone — how can I be part of a helping movement?*) [msg #160].

### Falling emotional helicopter (negative-thought spiral)
Definition and mechanism, in the author's words as a sequence [msg #216]:
1. **Trigger.** Some silly event can for a minute shake your belief in yourself and generate a worthless negative thought: "ничего не получается" (*nothing works out*).
2. **Emotional reaction.** If not stopped in time, the thought launches an emotional reaction — fear, anger, apathy.
3. **Distortion.** The emotions distort the whole picture: you stop acknowledging good results, lose faith in the future, criticise yourself and others.
4. **Multiplication.** In place of one negative thought come hundreds of new ones, amplifying emotions further. "Вертолет начинает падать по кругу" (*The helicopter starts falling in circles*).
5. **Unawareness.** In most cases the fall is completely unconscious — we do not understand what is happening.
Risk conditions from his own case: many projects and tasks, several with a very low degree of control over the result ("the task exists but the result doesn't"); when there are many such situations and attention is loaded, he can miss the first wrong thought [msg #216].
Stance: no one fully escapes emotional swings — "Мы испытываем эмоции и переодически верим негативным мыслям пока живем" (*We experience emotions and periodically believe negative thoughts as long as we live*); this is simply inherent in being human. To avoid falling all the way to the ground, it helps to know what these falls look like, how unawareness of one's state, stress and strong emotions lead to them, and how to "open the parachute". (!) INCOMPLETE: the post does not describe how to open the parachute; it points to a training [msg #216].

### Collective-awareness check-in polls
Why the author runs polls about inner state [msg #214]:
1. He is interested in others' emotional background.
2. It is a small exercise to increase collective awareness: everyone who votes sees their own answer and relates it to others' results.
Effect: we attune to the surrounding field and may suddenly realise our current state is dictated by shared external events; that realisation sometimes gives emotional freedom ("нас попускает" — *it lets us go*). Also, sensing the emotional field, we communicate, manage and interact better [msg #214].

### Multi-specialist support team for depression
**Purpose:** the author describes the support he put together when he fell into a moderate depression at the end of December 2020. He shares it as a "track" of his low state and of the actions he took. He says he does not enjoy turning himself inside out in public, but sometimes sees value in it. [msg #404]

**Everyday supports** ran alongside the specialists. These were his usual practices, sport and the bathhouse (баня), supplements, and support from people close to him. [msg #404]

**The six specialists who worked in parallel**, each with the role the author gives them:
1. **Analytical psychotherapist:** "очень базовые и глубокие отношения с миром" (*very basic and deep relationships with the world*). [msg #404]
2. **Process psychotherapist:** "более полное проживание эмоций и состояний в моменте" (*fuller living-through of emotions and states in the moment*). [msg #404]
3. **Somatic therapist:** works directly with the nervous system. [msg #404]
4. **Psychiatrist:** medication. [msg #404]
5. **Acupuncturist:** "научно доказанная работа с нервной системой" (*scientifically proven work with the nervous system*). This is the author's own claim. [msg #404]
6. **Massage therapist:** "внешнее обучение тела расслабляться" (*teaching the body to relax from the outside*). [msg #404]

**How it went.** Even with this much support, the author kept climbing out of the bottom and then crashing back down. He compares it to "Купался в шторм" (*bathed in a storm*) and says he got very tired of it. [msg #404]

**His own critique.** He admits he may have overdone it and "буквально закидывал свою боль деньгами" (*literally threw money at my pain*). He calls it "горе от ума" (*woe from wit*). [msg #404]

**His reasoning.** Years of inner work taught him to see outside help as a sign of responsibility, strength and self-care, not of weakness. He believes this huge amount of work produced "очередной болезненный виток роста и развития" (*another painful turn of growth and development*). He also says he is not reflecting much on this yet. [msg #404]

**The non-negotiable minimum.** "Депрессивные состояния реальны, широко распространены и содержат высокие суицидальные риски" (*depressive states are real, widespread and carry high suicide risk*). Because of this, any advice about depressive periods must start with mandatory supervision by a qualified psychotherapist and a psychiatrist, "Минимум именно в такой связке" (*at minimum, exactly in this pairing*). [msg #404]

### Four permissions for being at the bottom
This is the stance the author takes toward his depressive low. The first three lines are permissions, and the fourth names the key action. [msg #404]
1. "Нормально быть на дне." (*It's normal to be at the bottom.*) You do not have to fight the fact that you are low. [msg #404]
2. "Нормально пытаться оттуда выбраться." (*It's normal to try to climb out.*) Trying is legitimate, even when it fails. [msg #404]
3. "Нормально не мочь это сделать какое-то время." (*It's normal to be unable to do it for a while.*) Failing for a while is part of the process. [msg #404]
4. "Иногда вера в то, что можно выбраться пропадает, но возвращать ее раз за разом — это ключевое действие." (*Sometimes the faith that you can get out disappears, but bringing it back again and again is the key action.*) [msg #404]

The same stance includes valuing the bottom for what it gives:
- it deepens the personality and reduces egocentrism;
- the suffering and pain at the bottom are an expensive way of growing the capacity to feel compassion for others and help them. [msg #404]

### Lockdown self-care supports
**When to use:** during a lockdown or another period of restrictions. The October 2021 post refers to a lockdown of about one or two weeks.

**The diagnostic point comes first.** "Локдауна вроде и нет, но на самом деле он есть" (*It's as if there's no lockdown, but in fact there is*). If you pretend there are no restrictions, or have not taken them in at all, that may be a defensive reaction. It can point to a stressful or traumatic experience you have lived through. [msg #491]

**Mechanism.** The brain is built so that anything resembling past stress easily activates anxiety in the present. Unless you are one of the people who simply left for the whole lockdown, you are likely to have higher anxiety and stress, "И это нормально" (*and that is normal*). [msg #491]

**Evidence from practice.** From the team's client work, the author says emotional volatility rose that week, with another week of restrictions still ahead. [msg #491]

**The smart solution.** To keep your mood from swinging up and down as much, and to stop mental illnesses from flaring up, treat both yourself and other people with slightly more attention and care. Build "больше опор, чем обычно" (*more supports than usual*) for body and psyche. [msg #491]
1. Eat normally.
2. Sleep normally.
3. See inspiring people.
4. Move.
5. Lower the load on your attention and memory.
6. Do things that are pleasant specifically for you. [msg #491]

The post's image is pixel art of a figure wearing a face mask. It has no text. [img #491]

### Self-support statements in collective crisis
**When to use:** when collective political events produce thoughts such as "I've wasted my time and there is no future" (October 2022). The author shares how he supports his own adaptation: [msg #750]
1. "Жизнь продолжается пока мы живы." (*Life goes on while we are alive.*) [msg #750]
2. "Раньше было не так все уж и ясно." (*Things weren't all that clear before.*) This answers hindsight bias: the past only looks clear in retrospect. [msg #750]
3. People make mistakes, and mistakes are needed for learning and for keeping in contact with life. Grieving and letting go of the past are needed to move forward. [msg #750]
4. "Возможно сегодня — именно тот день, когда стоит пойти и найти таких же живых чувствующих людей, чтобы побыть вместе или что-то сделать" (*Perhaps today is exactly the day to go and find other living, feeling people to be together with or to do something*). [msg #750]

### Unbundling the "youth is weak" thesis
According to the author, the claim "молодежь пошла слабая" (*young people have become weak*) mixes three separate claims: [msg #1859]
1. **They show emotions more often.** Yes. [msg #1859]
2. **They have more diagnoses.** Yes. [msg #1859]
3. **They cope worse with real difficulties and are weaker.** No. There are no data confirming this. [msg #1859]

## Detailed insights

### The author's 2020 market-crash experience and the limits of control
In late February 2020, 95% of the author's communication for 3 days was about the stock market — the fastest fall in 10 years, a long-overdue correction triggered by a disease with, in his words then, very small mortality statistics ("much less chance to die from it than from a coconut") [msg #139]. Losing 10% of the whole portfolio in a couple of days was unpleasant and he several times twitched to sell and lock in losses, but decided not to act on emotion and to wait for a better moment to exit or enter [msg #139].
He describes the dilemma frankly as feeling like a "noob": jumping out in panic means losing money when the hysteria ends; not jumping out means the market may fall another 10–20–30% [msg #139]. His resolution: no twitching now, but act quickly when approaching a threshold that threatens real trouble; if it is a correction there will be a bounce, if a reversal it will give a better chance to exit than the moment of panic. "Быть в кэше, не значит быть в безопасности." (*Being in cash does not mean being safe.*) [msg #139].
The lesson he draws is about non-omnipotence: the collision with this reminder "pleasantly sobered" him, eased him in life generally, the illusion of control let go and he remembered to trust life more; in this inner state he finally started getting beautiful photographs (taken on Nusa Penida) [msg #139].
Later, in March 2020, he reported having been in crisis: he lost 40% of savings ("good that I have them at all"), got stuck abroad and did not know what would happen to his business. Scared, he worked directly on accepting uncertainty, change and trouble — including running along the street, alone for many kilometres, and shouting: "strange, but it helps release emotions and start seeing better". Then the fear passed and he started creating, shifting attention from his own problems to value for others [msg #160].

### Why crises feel like chaos and why panics inflate
- **Events are hard to interpret.** The market fell 7%, the dollar is at 80 — should you run and buy? Tomorrow it may return to 65 and you lose a lot, or fly to 100 the day after. Either we understand nothing (like most of the world) or we have analytical skills and experience but run into very different, accessible opinions of others. "Понятно, что непонятно на самом деле ничего." (*It's clear that actually nothing is clear.*) When the panic ends everything will look obvious, but "everyone is wise in hindsight" [msg #147].
- **Culture feeds on panic (James Hollis).** The author cites Hollis: materialist culture lacks deep meaning, and to overcome an unconscious sense of emptiness and meaninglessness we get hooked on emotions; crises and panics are a great occasion for emotion and life seems "juicier" [msg #147].
- **Accumulated trauma.** Personal and collective trauma remains; traumatised people constantly live in fear, consciously or not, and frightening events easily push them into trauma — a hard mental state where one must survive. In trauma, stress or accumulated fear we make wrong decisions, slide into crude and even wild behaviour and collectively make ourselves worse [msg #147].

### Mobilisation and anxiety contagion
Under the heading "Не заразитесь тревогой" (*Don't catch anxiety*), the author said a state of emergency in Russia was likely and that he considered this good because people in Moscow and other cities seemed insufficiently mobilised; safety measures in the small Vietnamese village where he was staying were many times higher than in Moscow. The anxiety risk comes from news, uncertainty and the move to remote work [msg #155].

### Crisis response as contribution
Having chosen "opening", the author's company launched four crisis initiatives, which illustrate the "helping movement" stance: a community of "new-wave helping specialists" (practitioners, trainers, teachers, coaches, organisational therapists working with leaders and teams) to coordinate efforts more powerfully than individually; a "board of directors" of self-made business owners and top managers to think together "without statuses" about strategy and joint initiatives; a consulting project on reassembling businesses for "how to find yourself in the new world"; and a supporting course-space on emotional resilience where the mindfulness-program team gathers best practices for fear, stress and anxiety, aiming to help people keep their sanity and resourceful state — "это самое важное" (*that is the most important*) [msg #160].

### Analyst report on the March 2020 correction (forwarded PDF)
The author forwarded (from Max Rodin) a Freedom Finance report dated 11 March 2020 by Yerlan Abdikarimov titled "Коррекция февраля-марта как “strong buying opportunity“" (*The February–March correction as a "strong buying opportunity"*). It is third-party analyst opinion, not the author's own, and the report's own disclaimer says it reflects only the analyst's view and cannot serve as a basis for investment decisions [file #149].

**Thesis.** Despite the depth and drama of the US correction, the analysts see factors pointing to its local character, opening an attractive opportunity for an active investor aiming at the second half of 2020 [file #149].

**What happened (catalysts).** From the 19 February S&P 500 high the market lost 19%. On 24 February it broke the growth channel formed since October 2019; daily and 4-hour divergences had formed and on 26 February S&P futures crossed the 23.6% Fibonacci retracement. On 28 February a local bottom formed after Powell's statement, an emergency 0.5% Fed cut and Joe Biden's primary win; growth lasted 4 sessions, then the market fell again on record-low bond yields and virus spread. On Monday 9 March US indices fell over 5% with a trading halt amid an oil price war; oil fell over 30% and credit markets sagged [file #149].

**Driver 1 — the virus.** The outbreak began in late December 2019 in Wuhan; Wuhan was quarantined from 22 January; the first US case was 26 January 2020 in Arizona. WHO figures cited: 116,166 cases, 4,088 deaths, 64 thousand recovered worldwide; in the US 761 cases, 27 deaths, 8 recovered. Markets feared the first truly destructive pandemic of globalisation [file #149].

**Driver 2 — oil.** OPEC failed to agree with allies led by Russia on output cuts; Saudi Arabia announced big discounts and reportedly prepared to raise output to 10 million barrels/day; oil fell 22% on Monday [file #149].

**Credit stress and algorithmic trading.** The high-yield bond ETF HYG fell 4.7% on Monday, the sharpest drop since 2009; investment-grade LQD fell almost 4% in the week. Bloomberg's US financial stress indicator worsened at the fastest rate since 2008; Markit CDX North America investment grade rose 42 bp to 125 bp, the highest since February 2016; the FRA/OIS spread hit its highest since 2011. When the market crosses key support levels and VIX rises, algorithmic systems amplify volatility (the May 2010 "flash crash" cut US market cap by 860 billion in 30 minutes). Trend-following CTA funds, using momentum and breakout models, moved from long in mid-February to fairly short at month's end (JPMorgan estimate), with CTA equity sales near $400 billion in the last week of February [file #149].

**Growth drivers the analysts listed.**
1. Virus and oil are global themes with local effects; single problems did not reverse markets in 2012 or 2015–2016. Signs of improvement: Wuhan closed its last temporary hospitals (14 built), 38 of 42 Apple stores in mainland China reopened, Deutsche Post confirmed its forecast [file #149].
2. Oil support over 3–6 months: Saudi and Gulf producers and Russia have limited ability to raise output quickly; contango will fill land and floating storage, softening pressure. JPMorgan forecast Brent at $37 in Q2, $42 in Q3, $44 average for the year (base case Saudi output 10.2 vs 9.7 million barrels/day); low oil and rates (including 30-year mortgages) may lift US consumer sentiment [file #149].
3. Government stimulus and Fed action: the market expected 50 bp cuts at both the 17–18 March and 28–29 April meetings (Bank of America forecast 25 bp each); probability of a March cut over 70%; possible de facto new QE (treasury bill purchases of $60 billion a month after June; overnight repo raised from $100 to $150 billion). Ian Shepherdson (Pantheon Macroeconomics) suggested rates to zero and a 1 trillion stimulus; Germany 12.4 billion euros (2021–2024), Japan a $4 billion package and about 270 billion yen of reserve; Trump wanted payroll tax holidays until the November elections. When the Fed started growing its portfolio in September 2019 volatility was surprisingly low, while the pause in February caused turbulence [file #149].
4. Bottom signals: the put/call ratio jumped to almost 1.12 on 10 March (highest since late 2018) from a nine-year low under 0.5 a month earlier; AAII survey showed 39.6% bearish, which the report reads as a good indicator of a mature bull market (global tops come at peaks of optimism, as in 2001 and 2007); Goldman Sachs Asset Management bought fundamentally interesting companies ("эта дислокация предоставляет активным менеджерам прекрасную возможность" — *this dislocation gives active managers an excellent opportunity*, Katie Koch); Deutsche Bank positioning fell from "extremely overweight" to "very underweight" — 5th percentile on 5 March vs 95th a few weeks earlier [file #149].
5. Long-term drivers intact: no recession (which accompanied all bear markets), GDP growth stable at 3.5% y/y, credit spreads below 2007 levels, the market still above the 20% standard bull-market correction, strong US consumption; top-50 S&P companies trade with average upside of 16%, top-10 with 25%, forward multiples project 12–20%; forward P/E fell below 17x (2018 level); an average analyst upside above 20% is described as anomalous and matching market lows; Bank of America: only 53% of recession-predicting indicators signal danger vs over 80% needed since 1960 [file #149].

**Technical picture.** The long-term trend since 2009 holds: the formal 20%-from-high criterion is not breached, with support at 2700; the index came close to it in 2011 (−21%), 2015–2016 (−15%) and autumn 2018 (−20%); the channel through the October 2011 and December 2018 lows is not broken. A rare sharp daily divergence formed, similar to October 2018, after which the market rose 6% [file #149].

**Two-wave pattern of corrections over 10%.** The report describes a common pattern taking up to 2–3 months:
1. A first fast wave breaks the local trend, usually within 1–2 weeks, and sets a serious low and a VIX high (9 sessions in February 2018, 21 sessions in autumn 2018).
2. A short bounce on low volume of 30–50% of the decline, lasting days to weeks (26 sessions Feb–Mar 2018, 28 sessions Oct–Dec 2018, 52 sessions Aug–Oct 2015).
3. A second, final but weaker wave with falling selling intensity: at a new index low fewer stocks make new lows and VIX does not make a new high.
4. After this final sell-off, maximally negative sentiment and serious daily divergences form.
Two scenarios were expected: (1) moderate — bounce to 3040–3100, a new wave down to 2800–2600, bottom, then new highs; (2) corrective — continued fall testing 2640, then a bottom with wide volatility and growth to highs on the December 2019 model [file #149].

**Three-tranche position building.** Do not try to "catch the bottom" and buy the full equity limit now; build positions in stages on the way down — a new portfolio of quality stocks with strong balance sheets, low debt, fundamental growth potential and industry leadership, in 3 tranches:
1. At current levels if S&P 500 futures break the 2900–3000 range.
2. Either if the market keeps rising and breaks 3060–3100, or if it makes a new low around 2700–2640.
3. If 3160 is broken, or if after falling to 2640 the index forms a daily divergence, VIX fails to make a new high and fundamental triggers fire (falling coronavirus statistics, Fed cuts, a new Fed asset-expansion programme).
Selling a portfolio built at higher levels is also not effective: per Bank of America, an average investor who since 1930 missed the 10 best S&P 500 days each decade earned significantly less than one who simply stayed invested; since 1928 there is on average one correction a year, with losses usually recovered within 3 months [file #149].

**Sectors named.** Low virus sensitivity: tech (FB, NFLX, AMD, AMZN, GOOGL), cloud (SKYY), gold miners (rise in uncertainty), fundamentally strong Chinese names (BABA) as China exits the epidemic cycle. Healthcare/biotech could benefit from a moderate Democratic candidate (JNJ, PFE, LLY); homebuilders benefit from Fed cuts. ### Depression from layered circumstances (the author's case, winter 2020–2021)
At the end of December 2020, Covid and the quarantine "finished off" the author. After a hard year he began sinking to a bottom he calls moderate depression. The cause was not one event but several circumstances layered "как пирог" (*like a pie*): [msg #404]
- the Covid and quarantine year demanded that everyone adapt to change;
- demand for deep mind's services grew fast, and the team began working a great deal;
- he lost an important opportunity: travelling and resting while travelling;
- several difficult family processes required his emotional involvement;
- people in his close circles fell seriously ill, and some died.

He treats his state as normal given these events. It seems to follow naturally when outside circumstances demand inner resources that were already exhausted. He says things did seem to get better. Even so, he does not try hard to assess his state: "Состояния приходят и уходят, нет смысла цепляться за такие изменчивые вещи" (*states come and go, there's no point clinging to such changeable things*). The principle is to observe and do the best you can. [msg #404]

### Hindsight bias and the illusion of control
Many people have strong anxious thoughts that they have wasted their time and that there is no future. In hindsight the picture always looks clear. It seems you knew long ago where things were heading and what to do, and you "просто" (*simply*) didn't do it. The author says beginner investors know these simplifications and cognitive errors well, because during a crisis everything suddenly becomes "clear" to them. [msg #750]

In reality, nothing is clear or predetermined at any given moment. With complex, multi-factor systems, all we can do is play with probabilities and manage risks. Nobody controls the situation. Otherwise investment funds would not go bankrupt, Titanics would not sink, and stable companies and totalitarian regimes would not collapse. Conspiracy theories would then be a reliable forecasting method, which the author says ironically. [msg #750]

### How collective catastrophe becomes inner conflict, PTSD and depression
People with a relatively healthy psyche can easily end up in strong inner conflict and post-traumatic disorder. The exception is people who are deep in defence against reality right now. Events that split our inner world into "before and after" often lead, after some time, to depressive feelings and a depressive picture of the world. [msg #750]

The author cites research from the University of Liverpool. It found that traumatic events are the strongest factor in anxiety and depression in adulthood. He adds that people under long, intense stress can develop PTSD symptoms that lead to depression, which he defines as a prolonged feeling of sadness that affects a person's ability to function normally. [msg #750]

**What the conflict looks like now (2022):**
- Every day we witness senseless and prolonged cruelty.
- It is increasingly clear that this happens at our expense. Each month of the current situation seems to take a year of normal life away from future generations.
- Feeling all this is very hard, and we cannot stay in constant contact with these feelings.
- We cannot ignore them either, because we feel the consequences and keep receiving information from outside. [msg #750]

**The energy drain.** All of this takes energy. The energy keeps leaking because of very real uncertainty, which triggers a physiological stress response. It is harder than usual to restore that energy because dialogue in society is being blocked. Talking with others about our experience is "терапевтическая социальная медитация" (*therapeutic social meditation*): it lets us live through emotions and move forward. In the author's view, official policy does not support this healthy process in society and actively blocks it, again at the expense of the future. [msg #750]

**Normalisation.** Given this situation, it would be strange **not** to feel anxiety or have depressive thoughts or some neurotic symptoms, for example insomnia. At the same time, "никто не знает будущего" (*nobody knows the future*). Our perception and actions can be distorted by our state, and the author says this is usually what happens. Depressive thoughts are fully justified, but it can help to keep the wider picture in mind. [msg #750]

**We are not sovereign individuals.** We are not "индивидуальными суверенами, надежно укрытыми в собственной отдельной скорлупе" (*individual sovereigns safely sheltered in our own separate shell*). We are connected to thousands of other people and included in hundreds of collective systems and processes. Something on the political level, which is large and important for all of us, has broken and is in crisis. The crisis shows up in our bodies, emotions and thoughts. This happens whether we lived under the illusion that politics did not concern us, or had long explored and acted on our agency as political actors. [msg #750]

**Adaptation before action.** This outer conflict becomes inner, and everyone adapts as best they can. At some point, resolving the conflict will require clear action in the outside world. Before that, adaptation needs support. See *Self-support statements in collective crisis*. [msg #750]

### Permacrisis and the mental-health data
The world was never predictable. But in a globally connected world, interdependence is so strong that crises multiply each other's effects and increase the load on our psyche. Collins Dictionary named "permacrisis" its word of the year. It defines the word as an extended crisis combining political instability, war and the constant rise in the cost of living. [msg #758]

Instability peaking everywhere has been a trend for at least the last decade, starting long before the pandemic. According to a recent UN report the author cites, this instability is reversing human development for the first time in history and setting up negative consequences for future generations. [msg #758]

Uncertainty reduces our ability to act together and affects every level of a system. At the individual level it shows up as a feeling of being unsafe and as stress. This is why the UN report puts mental health at the centre. [msg #758]

**What the author predicts.** Harmful stress turns into trauma and mental disorders over time. On that basis, he expects the trends described by the Institute of Psychology of the Russian Academy of Sciences to intensify, meaning a rise in mental illness. Everyone will keep working with stress, anxiety and depression, and with aggression in society. [msg #758]

### The "snowflake" myth: distress versus weakness
The author analysed data because there is so much talk that young people have become weak and sensitive. He gives five points: [msg #1859]

**1. The rise in suffering is real, but it is not weakness.** He cites the US data shown under *Evidence and numbers*. The cause is still an open question, but the rise in symptoms is definitely real. [msg #1859]

**2. Anxiety and depression are not the same as weakness.** Sensitivity to stimuli and resilience to difficulty are different psychological processes. A person who cries at a touching scene may adapt better in life and take a hit normally in a crisis. For the three claims hidden in "youth is weak", see *Unbundling the "youth is weak" thesis*. [msg #1859]

**3. The strength of older generations is often numbness.** Generations that watch violence on TV more calmly, and in practice cannot recognise emotional abuse in real life, often see this as a sign of toughness. The author says it is more likely desensitisation, a reduced sensitivity that has nothing to do with strength of character. He adds that science points the other way: this kind of "emotional anaemia" correlates with lower adaptability, and adaptability is the real condition for resilience in today's world.
- **Male alexithymia.** Boys are systematically taught to suppress emotions. By age 6 their expressiveness drops, even though they are born as expressive as girls. Culture wrongly treats this as strength. In the scientific data it is linked to later depression, PTSD and bodily symptoms, which in fact make a person fragile.
- **The Russian context.** Lyudmila Petranovskaya describes how war children with untreated PTSD shut off their feelings as a defence. Because of this they could not give their own children full-quality contact. That raised a generation of hyper-responsible children, the "детей с ключом на шее" (*children with a key around their neck*). The author concludes that the "терпи и молчи" (*endure and keep quiet*) culture in Russia was not real resilience but coping, a way to survive a catastrophic century.
- **The place from which older people judge.** Older generations often judge the young from learned emotional anaemia: "я не чувствую — значит, справляюсь" (*I don't feel — so I'm coping*). This stance goes with chronic emotional discomfort, which gets projected onto others who "allowed" themselves not to suppress and not to suffer. It often provokes contempt, because the young person is a living example of a different strategy and points to the older person's own pain. [msg #1859]

**4. Young people letting themselves feel is not heightened sensitivity.** The author calls it "восстановление ампутированного органа" (*the restoration of an amputated organ*). A young viewer who flinches at blows on screen or at rude words at work seems simply to live with less numbness than several generations before. In the author's aside, the state is also trying to put them in their place for this. [msg #1859]

**5. For balance: hardening and the cultural influence of psychology are real.** Moderate difficulties do train resilience, and today's parents' overprotection sometimes works against the young. Also, when anxiety is called a disorder in the culture and any discomfort is called trauma, people become more vulnerable to things they would once have lived through without noticing. [msg #1859]

**Conclusion.** Complaining about the young is nothing new; this dynamic is thousands of years old. Protzko's studies, which the author cites, showed that adults systematically misjudge the young because of distortions in perception and memory, so the comparison is not valid. "Если бы каждое поколение было слабее предыдущего, мы бы давно не стояли на ногах" (*If every generation were weaker than the previous one, we would have stopped standing on our feet long ago*). [msg #1859]

## Practical playbook

- **When you feel the urge to judge:** judge each other less overall ("Не суди — древняя истина", *Judge not is an ancient truth*). When you want to point at weakness in someone else, ask yourself instead: "какие чувства я сам запрещаю и подавляю, что меня так бесит их выражение у других?" (*which feelings do I myself forbid and suppress, that their expression in others infuriates me so much?*). [msg #1859]
- **Seeking outside help:** the author reframes getting outside help as a sign of responsibility, strength and self-care. [msg #404]

# Measuring and managing stress load

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's teaching on how badly people judge their own state, how to measure overall stress load objectively (HRV, a monthly questionnaire, team-level physiological measurements), why habituation to chronic stress is a false adaptation, what helps people adapt to big changes, how to think about state management as a realistic skill set, and how to handle "open loops" that silently drain the nervous system. The author's reason it matters: people (and leaders especially) believe they are fine while chronic stress degrades their decisions for months and years, and the consequences — burnout, apathy at 30, serious illness at 35–40, staff loss — arrive whether or not stress is acknowledged [msg #364, #865, #1106].

## Core ideas

- **Subjective feeling does not track real psychophysiological state.** Research at Moscow State University (MSU) found no correlation between HRV measurements and how people rate their state; people orient on mood and emotion instead (detail in *Why self-assessment fails*) [msg #364].
- **"I'm used to pressure" is usually burnout, not adaptation.** Long cortisol overload leads the body to reduce cortisol output; the resulting numbness is mistaken for resilience (see *Chronic false adaptation*) [msg #865].
- **Measure regularly and match practices to load.** The same practice (e.g. an hour of strength training) helps at low load and finishes you off at high load, so stress load should be measured monthly and practices chosen accordingly (see *Overall stress load questionnaire*) [msg #865, #1790].
- **Team state is the cornerstone and blind spot of management.** Pulse surveys and the Maslach test do not show it; physiology-based measurement does, and business plans must adapt to it (see *Team stress-load measurement*) [msg #1106].
- **Adaptation runs on resource and focus.** Continuously assess and accumulate psycho-physical resource, and focus ruthlessly with clear boundaries (see *Two factors of adaptability to change*) [msg #1048].
- **State management is not feeling good all the time.** It is a skill set with four realistic goals: know what is with me, know what affects and helps me, enter the red zone less and leave it faster, and have access to peak performance (see *Realistic goals of state management*) [msg #1383].
- **Open loops drain the nervous system even while you lie on the sofa.** They are closed not only by doing but by a concrete plan; manage their number and status with a five-step method (see *Open loops*) [msg #1813].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Overall stress load questionnaire (monthly self-measurement)
Purpose: because self-assessment is unreliable (MSU research), the author recommends a "fresh" questionnaire of the overall level of stress load that puts strong emphasis on the physiology of the process, so that one does not mislead oneself and knows one's real situation [msg #865].
1. **Take the test.** It is a test of overall stress load "с рекомендациями для стадий" (with recommendations for stages); the result places you in a stage and gives stage-specific recommendations. It was translated into Russian, with its recommendations, by Daria Kutuzova [msg #865]. In 2026 the author says it remains available free of charge on the deep mind world site [msg #1790].
2. **Repeat it every month.** Monthly repetition shows the dynamics: "Тест классно проходить раз в месяц и смотреть динамику" (It's great to take the test once a month and watch the dynamics) [msg #1790, #865].
3. **Choose practices that fit the current load level.** The author's example: hour-long strength workouts are good when load is small "и лишь добивают, если высокая" (and only finish you off if it is high) [msg #865]. For different levels of stress load "нам нужны совершенно разные спорт, питание и вообще подход" (we need completely different sport, nutrition and overall approach), and we ourselves almost never take this into account [msg #1790].
4. **Feed the result to your AI health agent (2026 addition).** If you use an AI chat/agent that deals with your health and state, after the test give it both the results and the recommendations and ask it to take them into account; the author expects the trajectory of its work with you to adjust accordingly [msg #1790].

### Team stress-load measurement (physiology-based)
Purpose: make the team's state — "краеугольный камень и слепое пятно сегодняшнего управления" (the cornerstone and blind spot of today's management) — visible; it stays invisible in most teams even when pulse surveys exist [msg #1106].
Why conventional tools miss it (two reasons the author lists):
1. **People cannot give an honest picture.** We simply cannot assess our state objectively; the author cites a study showing that people often underestimate or overestimate their stress level because of cognitive biases [msg #1106].
2. **Pulse surveys are either rare or methodologically doubtful.** Most companies use the classic Maslach test, which the author calls respectable and valid, but it only shows stages of burnout — and a person in high stress may not (yet) have burnout. deep mind instead looks at physiological reactions and the corresponding stress-load level, i.e. sees the picture in real time [msg #1106].
How it is used: after a measurement meeting, a visual of stress distribution is shown to leaders and to the team itself, and the author analyses it together with the situation on the ground — e.g. in one company HR came out best, which raises the question what that department does differently [msg #1106]. Earlier (2020), the team ran assessments with personal recommendations for each employee and, from the interviews, showed who had already burned out and who was heading there "на всех парах" (at full steam) [msg #364].
Case and what to do: in the example company, the company and deep mind discovered that more than half of employees were in extremely high stress. Ordinary tasks then require extra effort and results are worse because the prefrontal cortex already works worse; the team's overall prefrontal resource has dropped and "Точно можно не ждать великих проектов в этот период" (You definitely shouldn't expect great projects in this period). The author's prescription: do not draw "воздушных замков" (castles in the air) destined to fail; adapt business plans and processes faster to the real state of the team implementing them; and work directly on stress and fatigue. Such a state does not disappear easily, because it is usually a consequence of systemic problems — toxic communication, a culture of perfectionism or poor process organization. With understanding and step-by-step work one can leave the vicious circle of constant stress and average results, regain access to collective intelligence and build a team people want to be in [msg #1106]. High-load periods often lead to staff loss — people drop out of the company or work at half strength; deep mind originally started with formats that prevent reaching this state at all [msg #1106].

### Two factors of adaptability to change
Purpose: what helps adaptation under serious change, "будь то смена страны или менеджмента в компании" (whether a change of country or of management in a company). The author derives it by combining a years-long personal "living experiment" in adaptability (constant travel and moving) with the team's analysis of 20 scientific papers on change management and adaptability in companies, used in a workshop for 150 managers of one holding [msg #1048].
1. **Constant assessment of psycho-physical resource and accumulating it.** Resource is everything that supports a good state — from sleep and movement to meditation, massage, ethical colleagues and meaning in life. Resource is the fuel for adaptation and flexibility, but it burns very fast in moments of stress and re-traumatization (which the author says periodically happens to him when task complexity is too high). If resource is low and much adaptation is required, we fall into the downward loop (below). Hence resource during change is "наш главный друг" (our main friend), and everything that loads the nervous system and thus depletes resource should, where possible, be banned [msg #1048].
2. **Ruthless focus on what matters and clear boundaries.** Adaptation adds new tasks even when not obvious — "Переварить новую комнату в отеле — задача для нервной системы" (Digesting a new hotel room is a task for the nervous system). Under overload, not scattering oneself becomes essential, otherwise factor 1 suffers and we lose resource and enter the downward loop faster. Rank the priority both of the changes themselves (so as not to react to everything at once) and of ordinary activities and tasks — declining, re-prioritizing and drawing clearer boundaries [msg #1048].

### Downward loop of resource depletion
The cycle the author warns against: "нет ресурса — плохие решения — еще меньше ресурса — еще более плохие решения" (no resource — bad decisions — even less resource — even worse decisions). It starts when resource is low while the demand for adaptation is high [msg #1048]. The same logic appears at company level in the "vicious circle of constant stress and average results" [msg #1106].

### Realistic goals of state management
Purpose: a realistic target for state management, which the author calls "важнейшую линию интеллекта" (a most important line of intelligence) and a key set of skills and knowledge of our time [msg #1383].
1. **Знать, что со мной** (Know what is going on with me) — awareness of one's current state [msg #1383].
2. **Знать что и как на меня влияет и что помогает** (Know what affects me, how, and what helps) — and thereby get into the red zone less often [msg #1383].
3. **Быстрее из нее выходить, не застревая надолго** (Get out of it faster, without getting stuck for long) [msg #1383].
4. **Иметь доступ к состоянию пикового перформанса** (Have access to a state of peak performance) [msg #1383].
The point is not to feel a certain way — "не стоит играть в господа бога, контролирующего жизнь" (don't play God controlling life) — but not to harm oneself, to help oneself, and to make this a genuine and reasonably effective habit [msg #1383]. The author's own illustration: a weekly WHOOP-style "Weekly Trends — RECOVERY" screenshot showing daily recovery values across Sun 18 to Sat 24 of 87%, 88%, 65%, 57%, 56%, 40% and 20% (the exact mapping of values to days is not recoverable from the transcription); he presents it as an example of results he likes — red happens, under objectively large stress load, but he recovers quickly by keeping state in priority and focus [msg #1383, img #1383].

### Open loops of the nervous system — five-step method
Definition: "Открытая петля — это любая незавершенность с эмоциональным зарядом, которую нервная система держит в статусе нерешено" (An open loop is any incompleteness with an emotional charge that the nervous system keeps in the status "unresolved"). The concept comes from what the author calls the nervous-system lens for interpreting one's state and life processes. The charge can come from three time directions [msg #1813]:
- **Future:** risk, uncertainty — "что будет?" (what will happen?).
- **Present:** an undone action, a hanging fork — "делать или нет?" (do it or not?).
- **Past:** the unlived — what already happened but is not integrated.
Loops are often confused with ordinary tasks or with important anxious topics, but the concept is broader: an unfinished conversation where nothing is at stake any more drains resource just like a looming threat. One can have 100 tasks, but we usually find structure for them and they do not load the nervous system. "Купить билеты к пятнице — не петля" (Buying tickets by Friday is not a loop). A loop creates inner forks, and thoughts about them return on their own, uninvited: "Не мы о них думаем, а они думают нас" (We don't think about them — they think us) [msg #1813].
Mechanism: the nervous system cannot pause a loop; it services it constantly, spending resource, which lowers productivity and can damage health. Per Zeigarnik, unfinished tasks stay in active memory and crowd out everything else; the unfinished pulls toward completion, and the accompanying rumination prolongs physiological stress activation far beyond the stressor (a study the author links). "Событие длится час — петля вокруг него работает неделями" (The event lasts an hour — the loop around it runs for weeks), and the whole time the body pays: cardiovascular, endocrine and immune systems; HRV drops [msg #1813].
Dose: one or two open loops are a normal mode of life; five can easily lead to a collapse of the nervous system in which a single evening conversation is enough to keep you awake until morning [msg #1813].
Principle: the task is not to close all loops — impossible and unnecessary. A loop is closed not always by doing the task but by a concrete plan: the brain needs clarity on "что и когда" (what and when) to take the topic off duty. So we must manage the number and status of loops [msg #1813].
The author's scheme, used in dialogue with an AI when he feels overloaded:
1. **Talk it all out.** Tell the AI everything happening now, what presses, what spins in your head — without structure or editing, as it is. Voice is even better: less inner censor. Then ask it to pose questions about possible loops for the nervous system; you will be surprised how many loops surface that you did not consider loops, because "У нервной системы своя логика приоритетов" (The nervous system has its own logic of priorities) [msg #1813].
2. **Sort each loop with it into one of three statuses:**
   - *Actually closed but not acknowledged* — decisions long made by circumstances that you keep "cooking" every day; they close with one line, e.g. "до сентября так, пересмотр тогда-то" (it stays like this until September, review on such-and-such date).
   - *Closable with one action* — a letter, a conversation, a specialist consultation that turns background horror into an assessed risk; "час неприятного против недель фонового слива" (an hour of unpleasantness versus weeks of background drain).
   - *Truly open, not solvable today* — often long processes such as a cash gap, relationships or emigration [msg #1813].
3. **Build containers for the truly open ones.** Bounded windows when the topic has the right to you — for example 25 minutes a day: facts, one action, close until tomorrow. Outside the window the topic does not exist. This is the concrete plan that takes the topic off duty: "нервная система отпускает не когда проблема решена, а когда у нее есть назначенная встреча" (the nervous system lets go not when the problem is solved but when it has a scheduled appointment) [msg #1813].
4. **Have a capacity rule.** 2–3 truly open loops is the working maximum for any nervous system, even a very trained one. If there are more, something is closed by an act of will, even imperfectly: "Неидеально закрытая петля дешевле идеально открытой" (An imperfectly closed loop is cheaper than a perfectly open one) [msg #1813].
5. **After 19:00 loops are not opened.** Not in conversations, not in messages, not in your head before sleep. If a thought arises — one short answer: "завтра в 13:45" (tomorrow at 13:45). This is not ignoring but created boundaries that help one actually be effective [msg #1813].

## Detailed insights

### Why self-assessment fails
The author calls it a very important idea that "Люди в основном не понимают как они себя чувствуют и насколько эффективны" (People mostly don't understand how they feel and how effective they are), confirmed by MSU research (his alma mater). The researchers compared psychophysiological measurements — heart rate variability, which the Welltory app is built on — with people's subjective state: "данные никак не соотносятся. Люди очень плохо оценивают свое состояние" (the data do not correlate at all. People assess their state very poorly). People orient on emotions or mood rather than on their real overall psychophysiological state, which is apparently harder to feel [msg #364].
A researcher's field observation quoted in the post: after emergencies, the Ministry of Emergency Situations (МЧС) ran interviews and psychophysiological examinations; people said "все нормально" (everything's fine) while the devices showed "ужас" (horror), and vice versa. People also misjudge their work capacity and attentiveness — e.g. they believe that if their mood is bad (anxiety/depression) they are cognitively ineffective, "А это не всегда так" (And that is not always so) [msg #364].
Consequence for leaders: "Нам кажется, что мы ок, но мы не ок" (We think we're OK, but we're not OK). That is why many people lead teams and projects badly for months and years — they do not realize they already feel really bad and, because of chronic stress, make poor-quality decisions every day [msg #364].
Market response the author notes: the fitness tracker OURA launched in 2020 a team assessment feature based on HRV to see people's state in real time [msg #364].

### Chronic false adaptation and cortisol addiction
The author writes from months (or years) of high stress load, capped by fundraising for deep mind, which entails 100+ important decisions — and decisions, especially important ones, make up a substantial part of our overall stress load [msg #865].
Mechanism as stated: when cortisol runs too high for too long, the body reduces its production, we literally burn out, and a feeling arises that nothing much touches us anymore. From work with teams the author knows that many professionals living under pressure take this for successful adaptation — the ironic self-portrait: "Я хорошо справляюсь со стрессом, такой весь привык быть под давлением, только волосы вот что-то лезут и ору на своих сотрудников" (I cope with stress well, I'm so used to being under pressure, only my hair is somehow falling out and I yell at my employees) [msg #865].
Real coping criterion: we handle pressure well when our physiology does not fail and our relationships and work do not suffer from impulsive amygdala reactions [msg #865].
Habituation to stress and "cortisol addiction" (stressful events become enlivening and everything else bland), which we consider adaptation, "всегда плохо заканчивается" (always ends badly); the author has seen many sad stories of people who no longer want anything at 30 or start falling seriously ill at 35–40 [msg #865].
Ignoring is no defence: after covid, war and modern life nobody wants to hear about stress, but it does not disappear when ignored and we live with the consequences. Research the author links shows that even masters and top performers make terrible decisions and fail if they have not learned to self-regulate and actually reduce their stress response [msg #865].

### Mind, adrenaline and perspective under pressure
Olympic champion Kelly Holmes, asked about the weight of physical vs mental skills for winning, immediately answered 20% to 80%. Her account: at the 2004 Olympic final the top four were separated by only 0.2 seconds; all were equally talented and fast, and the difference was how the mind reacted to pressure and anxiety. You need adrenaline to race, but "если его чуть больше, чем надо — ты проиграл" (if there is a little more of it than needed — you've lost). In 1995, when she lost at the Olympics, she did not know this; when she won gold in 2004, she did [msg #865].
Richard Lazarus, described as one of the most influential psychologists studying how thoughts affect emotions, holds that a sense of perspective is the most important factor in staying calm during intense stress; our ancient response systems feel great vulnerability when we are trapped in circumstances. The author applies it to himself: hope of reduced "entropy" (long tasks getting resolved, fewer moves, clearer investments) helps, but recovery after a period of high stress load always needs rest and a sense of perspective [msg #865].

### What rest is after long, intense pressure
The author's definition: rest in his case, or after long intense pressure, is not a change of activity. "Отдых — это пространство без долгов или ответственности" (Rest is a space without debts or responsibility) — in which one manages to switch off from the habitual narrative and the nervous system is not attacked by abstract information [msg #865]. Constant need to adapt to change also wears one down — "от этого точно надо отдыхать" (you definitely need rest from it) [msg #1048].

### Adaptation to changing places — the author's experiment
Years of constant travel have made the author's life a living experiment in adaptability (he plans a book). He says he reached "boss level" in adapting to different housing: he deeply understands his body-mind stress reaction and the actions needed to complete it on entering a new room where he will sleep [msg #1048].
Moving from Bali to the Indian Himalayas took 1 month to feel "normal" — a lot by his standard — because of strong differences in habitual comfort and physical conditions: food, temperature, altitude, noise and cleanliness; India is also not his place overall. He meets much discomfort daily and tries to make it a practice, hatching from the cocoon of his ideas about life [msg #1048].
Mechanism: the mismatch between the habitual and the real external environment creates more stress and negative emotion in the body; they fill the emotional container, and resilience and flexibility drop. This happens to everyone when changes are too strong or too long — when we experience unmoderated, intolerable or unpredictable stress [msg #1048].
Upside: surfing the wave of change gives contact with fundamental reality in which everything is constantly moving, and builds mastery in adaptability — "если мы не коллапсируем при этом" (if we don't collapse in the process) [msg #1048].
Next stage he announced: adapting to the US, which he expects to be complicated by a big time-zone difference and a work schedule shifting to the morning (he hates plugging into the external agenda in the morning) and eased by access to favourite places and important values [msg #1048].

### State management is not "always feeling good"
Just as mindfulness is not about being calm, state management is not about always feeling good and never feeling stress [msg #1383]. "Управление состоянием" (state management) is the term deep mind uses in corporate programmes on stress reduction, peak performance or basic mindfulness; "self-care" or "wellbeing" would be more familiar but carry different shades of meaning. It is "безусловно важнейшая задача современности" (unquestionably the most important task of our time), approachable not abstractly but through a skill set — a set of skills one can develop. These programmes have run in large well-known companies for 6 years [msg #1383].
The expert trap: being an ambassador of stress resilience creates the incorrect expectation (including in himself) that he must always be in shape, never stressed, always in a good mood. He calls this unrealistic and sometimes harmful — "Я же не персонаж мультика" (I'm not a cartoon character) — and smiles at people who are "always cheerful, energetic, wonderful". He often feels lousy, his team always knows it, yet he believes many in his situation would have broken down long ago and could not do as much [msg #1383].
Why the red zone is unavoidable: however well you plan logistics, there may be a child screaming the whole flight, lost luggage, a hotel refusing entry (as once at night in New Delhi), or someone starts a war and exchanges freeze your assets. Sometimes it is manageable load and stress; sometimes even relatively simple events stack up into difficulty. Then we enter the red zone, sometimes get stuck, start experiencing chronic stress, get exhausted and burn out [msg #1383]. His own example: in April, logistics and overlapping projects meant he never had 2 consecutive days off, despite a dedicated person helping with this — "это прям плохо для состояния, и я про это прям знаю" (this is really bad for the state, and I really know it) [msg #1383].
"Состояние без стресса доступно, только для этого нам надо перестать жить" (A stress-free state is available — only for that we have to stop living) [msg #1383].

# Politics, power and authoritarianism

## What this covers

This part collects the channel author's political essays from late 2019 to late 2024: assessments of authoritarian leaders (Lee Kuan Yew, Putin, Lukashenko) and of "young authoritarian democracies"; a critique of leadership based on fear (written around the 2020 Russian constitutional vote); the Belarus protests as a case of an authoritarian regime's decline and of a population's values shift; forecasts about Russia's resource economy; a question of how ordinary people can regain political agency after 2022; an essay on why people wage war; a forwarded protocol for staying calm when detained at a protest; and reflections on the 2024 US election and on whether mindfulness is compatible with political judgment. According to the author, these matter because inner change (values, beliefs, narratives) is the foundation of outer political change, and because political passivity and the belief that "nothing depends on me" are themselves what keeps fear-based systems in place [msg #326, #752, #220].

The author's stance is openly personal: he calls his view of Belarus "весьма диванный взгляд" (a rather armchair view) despite his diploma from the political science department of Moscow State University, and his US-election post a "поверхностном обзоре" (superficial overview) [msg #322, #1231].

Three essays by the channel author. The first (#1306) asks whether the rise of the right in the West is a lasting "right turn" or a temporary "bump", explains the psychology and economics behind support for authoritarian populism, and ends with a personal question about tolerating complexity. The second (#1636) reviews a book on Russian anarchists ("Убить в себе государство") and presents Grigory Maksimov's account of how Russian history turned from a humanist line to a totalitarian one. The third (#1741), written amid news of a possible Telegram block in Russia, explains from the author's university training and experience in government how authoritarian decisions actually unfold, and why fear rather than real control is the system's main instrument. According to the author, this matters because simplified thinking, unreflected history and collective resignation are what keep societies stuck — while the ability to hold complexity, to reflect on history and to refuse fear keeps a path to a healthy future open [msg #1306, #1636, #1741].

This part holds three essays by the channel author from August–November 2026. They add to the existing topic file. (1) A review of Rustam Alexander's book «Секс был» (*There Was Sex*) argues that authoritarian power always tries to control intimacy. The reason given is that it divides humans into "people" and a "resource", and this control is morally wrong and also technically bound to fail [msg #1895]. (2) «Говорить правду» (*Telling the truth*) takes apart the comfortable belief that "nothing depends on me". It explains how closed regimes actually end, what preference falsification is, and why the main job of everyone else is to protect the people who speak publicly [msg #1922]. (3) «Хороним Трампа и правый разворот?» (*Are we burying Trump and the right turn?*) reads the 2026 US midterm results as confirming the author's 2024 forecast that the right turn was a "bump". It then compares the American and Russian feedback loops [msg #1971]. According to the author, all three matter for the same reason: authoritarianism is a losing strategy in the long run, but how fast and at what cost it ends depends on whether ordinary people keep their sense of responsibility and their ability to hold complexity [msg #1895, #1922, #1971].

**Relation to the existing topic file.** #1922 extends *Empowerment of the narrative* (#752) and the "self-fulfilling doom" point of the Telegram-block essay (#1741). It does so with an explicit mechanism: elite split, preference falsification, Havel's greengrocer. It also adds data, and it concedes more pessimism about individual influence than #752 did (see Caveats). #1971 updates the "right turn or bump" question of #1306 and the populism reading of #1231. The author now says events have confirmed the "bump" view. He also says his hope from 2024, that inclusivity would be made understandable and beneficial for the majority, seems to have come true. And he changes his criterion for judging progress from election results to society's capacity to hold complexity. #1895 adds a new angle on the authoritarian worldview, sexuality, which connects to the "resource" logic of earlier posts on authoritarian leadership [msg #1922, #1971, #1895].

## Core ideas

- **Leadership must be judged in context and over time.** A leader's record looks different from different time points; authoritarian rule can bring order after chaos but then causes ossification [msg #30, #136]. See *Assessing authoritarian leaders*.
- **Fear-based leadership is a product of inner weakness; the alternative is openness and flexibility.** Strong leaders create conditions in which life unfolds by itself [msg #220]. See *Five features of fear-based leadership thinking*.
- **Authoritarian regimes sell stability and act through fear, but become the cause of destabilization when they refuse to develop** [msg #269].
- **Countries live poorly because they are badly governed, not because of enemies** [msg #269].
- **Violence against protesters starts a downward spiral of defeat for the regime; the population becomes an actor of politics** [msg #322, #269].
- **Inner change is the foundation of outer change:** Belarus's values jump preceded its street protests [msg #326].
- **Fight for conditions, not for surnames** — and be political through conscious leadership in one's own life, home and organization [msg #150].
- **The narrative "my actions don't matter" is itself the most important acupuncture point** for change [msg #752].
- **War is a social pathology, not a natural necessity;** its main drivers are power-seeking groups and exclusive group identity [msg #923, #924].
- **Evaluation and ranking are compatible with mindfulness;** "считать все равным" (to consider everything equal) is a wrong nihilistic view [msg #1233].
- **Developing is harder than degrading** [msg #1231].

- **The right turn is a bump, not a reversal (author's view).** Each new generation moves society toward values of freedom and development rather than security and power; rollbacks happen, but long-term, more complex values become the base of social structure [msg #1306].
- **Stress breeds demand for protection and simple solutions.** The burden of the "new ethics" plus inflation, wars and AI threats makes people seek protection, and crises raise support for authoritarian populism [msg #1306].
- **The real question is personal capacity for complexity.** Not "turn or bump" or which conspiracy theory is real, but whether I personally can withstand, understand and accept complexity instead of simplifying [msg #1306].
- **Russian cultural foundation is humanist; totalitarianism is a superstructure.** The author separates a humanist, freedom-loving, dignity-based cultural foundation from political-ideological superstructures that sometimes appear "like a cancerous tumour", and finds support for this in the Russian anarchists and Maksimov [msg #1636].
- **Authoritarian systems are not monolithic or omnipotent.** Orders pass through many actors and floors, decisions swing like a pendulum, resources are finite, so the system governs through fear and expectation management — and resignation is the worst outcome [msg #1741].

- **Authoritarianism is sex-phobic because it treats people as a resource.** The stronger a state's authoritarian ambitions, the more it intrudes into citizens' beds. Intimacy is the one sphere it cannot fully control, and a "totality machine" reads anything it doesn't control as a threat [msg #1895].
- **Authoritarian control of life is technically doomed.** Desire can be suppressed but not managed. Bans produce not obedience but the "underside" of the same life: underground abortions, ignorance, violence, disease [msg #1895].
- **"Nothing depends on me" is a bet, not a fact.** The individual's influence on big politics is close to zero. But closed systems fall when elites split, and elites watch what ordinary people really think. Nobody knows that real distribution, including the regime. So the belief in one's own powerlessness is a self-fulfilling, responsibility-removing bet [msg #1922].
- **Public truth-telling is the key lever, and protecting truth-tellers is everyone's job** [msg #1922].
- **The right turn was a bump (confirmed, in the author's view, by the 2026 midterms).** Still, one should not "bury" the opponent: what is repressed comes back, and left and right swing because each one's excess produces the other [msg #1971].
- **Where feedback doesn't work, tension goes inward.** America responds to stress with hysteria, Russia with freezing. Both are losses of stability [msg #1971].
- **New criterion of progress:** not who wins elections, but how much complexity people and countries can hold without lurching from extreme to extreme [msg #1971].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Five features of fear-based leadership thinking (vs three features of leaders who give the future room)
Context and purpose: written as "Голосую против" (I vote against) for the 2020 vote fixing the system in a new Constitution "до 2036 года" (until 2036). The author's premise: there have always been leaders who govern through fear; "Из-за внутренней слабости они обладают стремлением доказать свою полноценность, подчиняя себе силой" (Because of inner weakness they have a drive to prove their worth by subjugating by force). Under fear and "в заморозке" (in a freeze) people forget they have a choice — which leaders to buy from, which to work for, in which leaders' country to live. That choice did not always exist, but "у нас есть" (we have it). The system fixed by the Constitution is "продукт мышления ее лидера" (a product of its leader's thinking) [msg #220].

The five features of that thinking [msg #220]:
1. **Excludes healthy conflict and diversity.** It beats with batons and locks up everyone who disagrees.
2. **Discriminates against and humiliates groups of its own citizens (male and female) using budget money**, ignoring basic human freedoms and rights.
3. **Is unable to generate meaning.** "Единственный предлагаемый нам смысл: вера в саму систему, называемая патриотизмом" (The only meaning offered to us: faith in the system itself, called patriotism).
4. **Lives in the past, not the future.** It humiliates the memory of ancestors by making it an object of propaganda.
5. **Builds an economy that grows only when energy prices grow**, with no development plan and no future.

The alternative: "Сильные лидеры и лидерки развили силу смирения" (strong leaders, male and female, have developed the strength of humility) and know that the greatest task is to create conditions where life unfolds by itself — without pushing, war or coercion. "Альтернатива страху - открытость и гибкость" (The alternative to fear is openness and flexibility). With such thinking, leaders solve entirely different tasks: how to give free internet access all over the world, how to eradicate poverty, how to make people happy in their work [msg #220].

"Будущее есть, когда лидеры и лидерки не бояться [sic] жизни и дают место другим" (There is a future when leaders are not afraid of life and give room to others). Such leaders [msg #220]:
1. **Use diversity and difference as a source of development.**
2. **Create future-oriented meanings** that fill people's hearts.
3. **Look for ways to distribute resources evenly** and let not only the group of energy extractors and builders earn, but artists, taxi drivers and teachers of both sexes.

The author's conclusion: there are plenty of such leaders, "но их место занято" (but their place is occupied). Both abstaining and voting "for" mean choosing the system of fear; to avoid handing one's choice to others "надо проголосовать против. Это имеет значение" (one must vote against. It matters) [msg #220].

### Movchan's conditions-not-surnames approach to changing power
The author says Andrei Movchan wrote well about how "we" can change the situation in the country, and states his own identical position: "сегодня мы должны стать гораздо политичнее" (today we must become much more political), and that this politicalness should first of all be expressed "в сознательном лидерстве в своих жизнях, домах и организациях" (in conscious leadership in our lives, homes and organizations) [msg #150].

Movchan's argument, as quoted [msg #150]:
- A fight against power is either a fight for power (which Movchan rejects as "борьбы жабы с гадюкой" — a fight of a toad with a viper) or a fight for an unrealizable dream of a different, good power ("а я фантазиями не увлекаюсь" — and I don't indulge in fantasies).
- "Бороться надо не за одни фамилии против других фамилий, а за условия, при которых самые плохие фамилии будут вынуждены вести себя прилично" (One must fight not for some surnames against other surnames, but for conditions under which the worst surnames are forced to behave decently).
- These conditions are determined not by laws but by **institutions, the openness of the country, the level of education of society, and the mobility of the population**.
- What to do if you want better power (Movchan's list): contribute to science and education; create jobs that do not depend on the authorities; help students study and work abroad; be engaged with the Russian-speaking diaspora worldwide (it is free in its views and their expression, and an active core for creating a democratic Russia will certainly form from it, "если такое случится когда-нибудь" — if that ever happens); be active in your small world — home, work collective, village.
- On the constitution: let them rewrite it every day, put a raspberry jam recipe in it or write that Putin will rule even from the other world (Lenin was already "живее всех живых" — more alive than all the living, and nothing happened) — it changes nothing while they are in power, and when their power ends no one will remember that constitution.

### Fage's resource-curse diagnosis and scenario for Russia
The author reposts a forecast by tech entrepreneur Sergei Fage, saying that when Fage writes about technology and the world (not about consciousness and meditation) he mostly agrees with him. The author's own addition: the destruction of the resource economy may happen much faster than we think, because we think of development linearly while today, thanks to technology, it proceeds exponentially — "изменения возникают резкими взрывами вверх, а не спокойным подъемом по лестнице" (changes come as sharp explosions upward, not a calm climb up a staircase) [msg #272].

Fage's argument, step by step [msg #272]:
1. **Prediction:** Russia faces the collapse of its resource economy — very painful in the short term, very good in the medium term.
2. **Strengths:** Russia is the cheapest country in the world for highly qualified labour; years of sanctions led to PPP/Nominal GDP of ~3x (higher only in Indonesia, Turkey, Central Asia, India and Africa). Russia is No. 1 in the world by share of population aged 25–35 with higher education (Fage cites the Wikipedia "List of Countries by Tertiary Education"). There is a culture and love of hacking systems, i.e. creative thinking — hence many Russian names among hackers, crypto people, high-frequency traders, mathematicians and programmers. This does not help build roads, but helps create what is most valuable in the future world: IP, volatility trading and assets hidden from expropriation by "менее продуктивной и более многочисленной частью общества" (the less productive, more numerous part of society). There is also a culture, if not of love for chaos and uncertainty, then of experience with them.
3. **The root problem — natural resources** — produces three problems:
   1. They can be pumped by "глупые люди с оружием" (stupid people with guns), who thereby get power.
   2. They are easy to seize, which creates an ecosystem in which one does not want to invest capital.
   3. They lead to supporting huge inefficient spaces and a large number of inefficient people.
4. **Resolution:** with the fall of Western economies and the shift to more efficient technologies (e.g. electric cars), the state and "чуваки с оружием" (dudes with guns) will rapidly lose income; it cannot be replaced by squeezing crypto people, traders and other creators of future economic value, because the most valuable assets will be the most obscure and little-known ("Пытаться конечно будут" — they will try, of course).
5. **Outcome:** popular discontent grows while the armed men become harder to pay. This leads either to a peaceful change of power (no economic sense in holding on) or to collapse, during which the value creators temporarily leave for Singapore or Israel — then return and rebuild a country no one is left to resist. Or they never return, and "Россия" becomes a word for a cloud diaspora of people who came from here; in the future world physical countries will be irrelevant.
6. **Fage's own hedge:** "никто не знает, что будет в будущем. Просто это рассказ, который нравится и который имхо мы сейчас реализовываем" (nobody knows what will happen; it is just a story we like and which, IMHO, we are now realizing).

The author ends by asking readers what they think [msg #272].

### Lessons of the Belarus protests
Context (November 2020): Lukashenko's actions — getting rid of his closest circle and banning international diplomas — were becoming "все более безумной" (ever more insane). Stepping back from the violent content, the author proposes to look with interest: "мы впервые так близко и подробно наблюдаем кончину авторитарного режима" (for the first time we are watching the death of an authoritarian regime so closely and in such detail), made possible by technology unavailable before [msg #322].

Even if we are tired of the events and have switched off, we keep the knowledge [msg #322]:
1. **Protests by the population can have a very strong effect.**
2. **Using violence against protests launches a downward-spiralling defeat for the authorities.**
3. **Elites end up in a desperate meat grinder** and lose a great deal in the turbulence that follows violence against protests.

Result: within a few months, across the whole post-Soviet space, "население стран резко и удивительно становится актором политики, а не просто ее субъектом" (the population of the countries sharply and surprisingly becomes an actor of politics, not merely its subject) — probably a new, frightening and incomprehensible phenomenon for the worldview of current, but no longer contemporary, rulers of personalist autocracies [msg #322].

Further points from the same post [msg #322]:
- **Why Russia would help suppress the protests even while disliking Lukashenko.** The Belarusians were unlucky to play on the same field as heavy external actors, notably a large neighbouring country whose post-Soviet structure, regime and relations with the population are very similar. Its rulers cannot afford a fast, loud collapse of an authoritarian neighbour: it would set too negative an example and damage their own main myth "о сильной, контролирующей и нерушимой власти" (of strong, controlling and indestructible power). So it would be no surprise to later learn of that country's equipment, security forces or other resources involved in suppression — even though the author quite believes its authorities do not want to support Lukashenko personally and wish him gone. "Политика - часто процесс парадоксальный и нелинейный" (Politics is often a paradoxical and nonlinear process).
- **Interconnection:** "Изменяя Беларусь, изменится и Россия" (Changing Belarus, Russia too will change). The field of the possible in people's heads has already transformed; the author senses new notes in Russian political rhetoric that had seemed impossible.

### Explanations of war — evolutionary, biological, psychological
Motivation: when we see death and suffering we naturally want to protect ourselves and close off; even some people from the "осознанной тусовки" (mindfulness crowd) in February–March 2022 tried to rationalize what was happening by taking a quasi-adult position declaring war something natural. The author is deeply moved by ideas that entrench violence: they "могут быть более опасными, чем само насилие, так как готовят для него почву, фактически оправдывают и обессиливают борющихся с ним" (can be more dangerous than violence itself because they prepare the ground for it, effectively justify it and disempower those fighting it). The idea of war as normal is such an idea, and he wants to counter it [msg #923].

His ally is Steve Taylor, PhD, senior lecturer in psychology at Leeds University, who studies wars and considers them a **social pathology** — a very widespread one; human history looks literally like a chronicle of violence. Per research, in the most conflict-saturated period, Europe 1740–1897, there were 230 wars and revolutions [msg #923].

The explanations, in the order presented [msg #923, #924]:
1. **Evolutionary explanation.** Some evolutionary psychologists suggest wars are natural because humans consist of "selfish genes" demanding reproduction; so it is natural to try to seize survival resources and fight other groups for them.
2. **Biological explanation.** Men are biologically predisposed to conflict because of more testosterone (thought to be linked to aggression); violence may also be linked to low serotonin, since there is evidence that animals given serotonin become less aggressive.
   - Verdict: "Эти теории выглядят неуверенно" (These theories look shaky). They cannot explain the absence of war in early history and in most hunter-gatherer societies. Anthropologist R. Brian Ferguson gathered convincing evidence that wars have existed only about 10,000 years and became frequent only about 6,000 years ago [msg #923].
3. **Psychological explanations** — judged stronger.
   - **William James** (the first psychologist to study the subject): wars are so common because of their positive effect on individual and society. War became a very effective tool for creating unity: it binds people together, giving their community a direction; at the individual level it lets one rise above the routine of ordinary life, creating meaning for an ordinary person's existence [msg #923].
   - **Modern psychologists** give more weight to other factors, which "мы должны думать в первую очередь, когда видим агрессию" (we should think of first when we see aggression):
     - **Main motive — power.** "Основным мотивом войн является желание одной группы людей — обычно властных группировок, увеличить свою власть и силу" (The main motive of wars is the desire of one group — usually power factions — to increase its power and strength), pursued by conquering and subjugating other groups and seizing their territory and resources [msg #923].
     - **Second — group identity.** The universal human need for belonging and identity easily takes the form of ethnic nationalism or religious dogmatism, where we feel special pride in group belonging [msg #923]. The problem is less the pride itself than the attitude it creates toward other groups: identifying exclusively with one group automatically creates rivalry and hostility, the destructive mentality of "мы - наши" (we — ours) and "они - чужие" (they — strangers), which easily leads to conflict. Empathy matters here: one of the most dangerous aspects of group identity is what psychologists call **moral exclusion** — denying other groups human rights, respect and justice; moral standards apply only to members of one's own group [msg #924].

Conclusion: "Войны не являются чем-то, что нам просто необходимо принять" (Wars are not something we simply have to accept). As the strength of group identity decreases and empathy develops — which fits modern trends — humanity may be starting to overcome the pathology of war. Steven Pinker, in his fundamental work, also points out that however frightening the media agenda, violence in the world has decreased both long-term and short-term; paradoxically, our perception of violence has not tracked this decline, possibly because of the growth of communication. Further decline is not inevitable but depends on forces interacting with our better impulses, such as empathy and common sense [msg #924].

The author: yes, people often fight, but humans are beings with the most developed consciousness and the ability to consciously determine behaviour — to choose how to live rather than replay animal or historical patterns. Every crisis and war should make us multiply personal and collective efforts to cultivate empathy and common sense: "Это точно в наших силах" (This is certainly within our power). He expresses deep compassion for all who suffer in wars today, "И даже к террористам и убийцам, разум которых настолько замутнен, что они не ведают что творят" (and even for terrorists and murderers whose minds are so clouded that they know not what they do) [msg #924].

### Arithmetic of hope — how closed regimes end and why truth-telling matters
Purpose: to answer "Is it true that nothing depends on me?" The author's argument from #1922, step by step.
1. **Notice why the thought is comfortable.** The author says the thought "nothing depends on me" came to him not as a conclusion but like weather: he found it already settled. The first thing he felt was not despair but bodily relief, "то расслабление между лопатками, с каким опускают на землю чужой чемодан" (that relaxation between the shoulder blades with which one puts down someone else's suitcase). That relief made him look closer, because thoughts that settle in us so comfortably are rarely disinterested. The self-interest was obvious right away. Despair and indifference demand no risk, no choice, not even arguing with those who are "still twitching" for some reason. Yet they look not like pitiful capitulation but like adult sobriety. You hand over hope, and even the simple wish to see unadorned reality and understand that change is needed. In return you get release from responsibility and from the need to fight and change anything. "А сдачу тебе отсчитывают уважением к твоему" (And your change is counted out in respect for your) "realism" (in scare quotes in the source) [msg #1922].
2. **Accept the hard data.** The individual's influence on big politics really is around zero. The author says this is not a pessimist's pose but data he would prefer were more inspiring. Gilens and Page analysed almost 2,000 American policy decisions. They found that the wishes of the bottom 90% of the population barely influence the laws adopted, while the wishes of the rich influence them quite clearly. The author thinks the situation is hardly different "in our case". The harder truth from political science goes further: closed, near-totalitarian systems almost never end because people went out into the street. They end "когда раскалывается элита, когда часть находящихся внутри вдруг обнаруживает, что снаружи сделалось безопаснее" (when the elite splits, when some of those inside suddenly find that outside has become safer) [msg #1922].
3. **Understand why elites split.** An elite that violates its population is constantly calculating what will be swallowed and what won't. So it matters a great deal how many people actually think differently, not on the square but "на кухне, в курилке, в машине с поднятыми стеклами" (in the kitchen, in the smoking room, in a car with the windows up). The elite counts not the opposition or the activists, but ordinary people, each of whom is quite sure that he decides nothing. Evidence of how much this opinion matters to the regime: to make people "swallow", the federal media budget this year allocates 148 billion rubles to the opinion of the person "who decides nothing", and in reality the figure is far higher. The author calls this another record, twice the spending on physical culture and sport [msg #1922].
4. **See the hidden assumption: nobody knows the real distribution of opinions.** "Вся арифметика отчаяния и равнодушия держится на допущении, будто реальное распределение мнений кому-то известно" (The whole arithmetic of despair and indifference rests on the assumption that the real distribution of opinions is known to someone). But it is, first, dynamic and, second, known to no one. It gets worse the more repressive, loyalist and personalist the regime becomes. The further and more repressive things get, the more we self-censor and lie to each other about what we think. Timur Kuran called this preference falsification, and in the end everyone loses from it, including those at the top. They have the same data problem: "социологический опрос в стране, где за ответ случаются последствия, измеряет не мнение, а очень примерную температуру" (a poll in a country where answers have consequences measures not opinion but a very approximate temperature) [msg #1922].
5. **Draw the consequence: monoliths crumble suddenly.** This is why such systems look like a monolith until the last day and then crumble in 3 weeks. It explains why nobody predicted 1989, 1991 or the Arab Spring: it is impossible in principle, since the needed data is hidden in our heads. "Монолитность, стало быть, не свойство системы, а свойство нашего неведения друг о друге" (Monolithicity, then, is not a property of the system but a property of our ignorance about each other) [msg #1922].
6. **Turn it around: literally everything depends on us.** This holds only when our opinion actually adds up to visible weight. In this "battle for the head", nobody matters as much as people able to tell the truth publicly, for example the obvious truth that we don't need war. A person saying aloud what they think out-argues no one and changes no one's beliefs. But nothing else changes the estimate of "how many of us there are", because each such statement adds a point to someone else's picture of the world. The work looks pointless, yet it is exactly what repression targets first [msg #1922].
7. **Reframe the belief.** "Сколько нас, я не знаю. Не знаете и вы. И они, вопреки всему своему аппарату, тоже не знают" (How many of us there are, I don't know. Neither do you. And they, despite all their apparatus, don't know either). The author says this is perhaps the only thing all of us know for certain. While this remains so, the statement "nothing depends on me" should be treated not as a fact but as a bet. It is a "rotten" bet that comes true exactly to the extent people believe in it: "единственное убеждение на свете, которое одновременно снимает с человека ответственность и само себя исполняет" (the only belief in the world that at the same time relieves a person of responsibility and fulfils itself) [msg #1922].
8. **Why the author keeps speaking.** The people who turned out to matter in 1989 had spent decades before that doing things that looked completely pointless. "Отличить заранее «бессмысленно» от «рано» не удавалось еще никому" (Nobody has ever managed to tell "pointless" from "too early" in advance), which is why he keeps talking [msg #1922].

### Havel's greengrocer
Václav Havel explained the mechanism in 1978 through a greengrocer. Every morning the greengrocer puts the slogan "Proletarians of all countries…" in his shop window, though neither he nor anyone else believes it. By the mere act of displaying it he tells his neighbours: the system is solid, I'm in the game, you play too. On the morning the slogan doesn't appear, the greengrocer overthrows nothing. He just stops lying, and so shows everyone else that it is a lie. The author uses this as the model of how public truth-telling works: it does not persuade anyone, it changes everyone's estimate of how many people share the view [msg #1922].

### Division of labour of truth-telling (protect the one who speaks)
- **The asymmetry.** Everyone benefits from someone else's honesty. But the costs of truth, "в виде разных налогов" (in the form of various taxes), fall on a specific person with a surname, a family and a mortgage [msg #1922].
- **What follows.** The author says the conclusion is not that everything is pointless but the exact opposite. Since the contribution is disproportionate and one person pays, protecting that one person is the work that falls to everyone else. That means knowing their names and supporting those who can speak aloud with attention, a donation, a letter, a nod of agreement: "вот наша главная работа" (that is our main job) [msg #1922].
- **Sharing the work.** Sometimes we can share this work ourselves and show others our disagreement, telling the truth if we feel it differently from what television says. The author says now is exactly such a case, and that working out how specifically "I" can do it doesn't seem so hard. But first one has to "вернуть себе ответственность там, где у нас ее забрали обманом или силой" (take back responsibility where it was taken from us by deceit or force) [msg #1922].

### Political thermostat — feedback vs freezing
- **The rule.** "Термостат работает, только если недовольство доходит до власти и что-то меняет" (The thermostat works only if discontent reaches power and changes something) [msg #1971].

- **Russia.** In September "Единая Россия" (United Russia) got a record 349 of 450 seats, and many readers read this text through a VPN. ### Why authoritarian models fail: Belarus, August 2020

### The Russian anarchist tradition and why the author reads it
As part of his ongoing study of the idea of freedom, the author read the book "Убить в себе государство" (Kill the State in Yourself), which he calls a fresh, very well written overview of specific figures in Russian history who developed ideas of freedom from the state. ### Grigory Maksimov's account of the turn to totalitarianism
Grigory Maksimov (1893–1950) was one of the last major Russian anarchists of the 20th century: he went through the revolution from inside, refused to cooperate with the Bolsheviks, survived repression and emigrated first to Europe, then to the US. In his writings he describes and names the moment when Russian history turned from the humanist line to the totalitarian one; the author wants readers to hear him [msg #1636].
Maksimov's claims as presented:
- Large-scale violence was not characteristic of Russia. In the imperial period, court-ordered executions were mainly of political criminals; left radicals mainly did propaganda; revolutionary terror was not massive [msg #1636].
- All Russian culture was based on humanist ideals [msg #1636].
- Conspiracy theories became popular only with the penetration of Marxism; Marxism challenged the ideas of humanism, freedom and human rights [msg #1636].
- Lenin became a hostage of political ideas that inevitably led to dictatorship; shootings became routine rather than an exceptional way of solving political questions [msg #1636].
- The definition of totalitarianism and the Jacobin lesson are under *Maksimov's definition of totalitarianism* [msg #1636].
The author's conclusion: if one speaks of the great social catastrophes of the 20th century, possibly the biggest was the Bolsheviks — and totalitarian regimes in general, which destroyed human dignity. Without reflecting on history it is very hard to move toward a healthy future, and we have to live through many sad consequences of the absence of such analysis. So it matters less whether he personally shares Maksimov's view or that of other figures in the book; what matters more is that we can think about and discuss such things at all — which, "увы, в наше время не просто" (alas, is not easy in our time) [msg #1636].

### The Telegram block and how authoritarian power really works
- **Practical stakes.** The author had not planned to comment and says one must look at the facts. A full block would be the collapse of the business model for many projects and would disrupt working infrastructure of many large businesses whose communication and processes are set up in Telegram with no alternative — "Была бы — уже переехали бы" (if there were one, they would already have moved) [msg #1741].
- **Emotional reaction and stance on the state.** The news brings growing anxiety to media acquaintances and sadness and anger to him. Technologies matter more than ever, not their restrictions; "Бесит вредительство" (the sabotage infuriates). He asks rhetorically when the state ever helped people rather than hindering them from all sides, and whether it was ever truly effective in implementing its intentions — or always just "тупотопорным" (blunt-axed, crude) [msg #1741].
- **Checking beyond the news.** He wrote to several people to learn what is happening not from the news, while reflecting on his MGU course and government experience (see *Pendulum theory of state decisions*) [msg #1741].
- **The system is not omnipotent.** It very much wants to seem so; as soon as everyone sees its resources are extremely limited, it is finished (expletive censored in the source), because fear of resisting will drop sharply [msg #1741].
- **Why fear substitutes for control.** Carrying out harsh restrictive decisions needs huge resources, which are always lacking and always finite, plus layered systemic inefficiency at all levels. Therefore what is needed is not so much real control as "управление ожиданием, запугивание и страх" (management of expectation, intimidation and fear) [msg #1741].
- **Self-fulfilling doom.** The more people fear and the more it seems everything is doomed, the more it becomes so. Hence analyzing whether Telegram will be blocked is not very productive: what they can, they will block; "А все остальное на нашей стороне" (everything else is on our side). The author believes reality will in the end be *largely* the way we all accept it [msg #1741].
- **Disproportionate but real influence.** Influence is completely disproportionate among people, but it exists. The worst that can happen is not internet shutdown or a Telegram block but "что мы все сложим лапки и пойдем на эшафот" (that we all give up and walk to the scaffold) [msg #1741].
- **What is worth holding onto.** Access to free information, the ability to think together with other people and to stay in contact with different opinions may be costly, but it is possibly the most important thing we have and the most important thing to hold on to. The author says he will stay and hopes readers will too: "ищите возможность дышать свежим воздухом" (look for the chance to breathe fresh air) [msg #1741].

### Why a book about Soviet sex is about the present
At breakfast a friend asked the author why he was reading «Секс был». He realized that from the outside it might look like rather specific reading, and he answered that it is not about the past but about the most current present [msg #1895].

The historical arc he draws from the book: the Soviet state started out for health and freedom, but the authoritarianism that emerged quickly threw the country back a century. It brought state control and anti-scientific propaganda into sexuality. Stalinism created the preconditions for there never being any sex education in the Union, and this harmed millions. Milestones by decade:
- In the 1920s people rode naked in Moscow trolleybuses.
- In the 1930s abortions were already banned.
- In the 1940s girls from Arkhangelsk were sent to camps for love affairs with foreigners.
- In the 1950s there was a mini sexual revolution: Moscow lived for a week like a completely different city, and then paid for it with human fates.

Rustam Alexander assembled these stories bit by bit from archives, medical records and other people's memoirs into one gripping and at times sad narrative. In the author's reading, the result is a book not about sex, though sex is in it, but about the patterns that we, "вынужденные наследники пост-советского пространства" (forced heirs of the post-Soviet space), still carry today. It is about how our society and power are built. The author also sees the book as a story of ignorance and crimes that has not ended [msg #1895, img #1895].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- **The author's view of Putin shifted over time:** from "rather a good thing" (less blood and chaos than the 1990s) to closer to the liberal opposition in 2019, to the explicit 2020 "vote against" a fear-based system; in early 2020 he still asked whether the Putin era might one day be viewed with gratitude, as Singaporeans view Lee [msg #30, #136, #220]. (!) CONTRADICTION (in tension, as positions over time): the hypothesis that "a young country needs an authoritarian style" (#136) sits uneasily with the later thesis that in the 21st century no one needs a wise ruler controlling their freedom (#269).
- **Unverified forecasts:** the author predicted in November 2020 that Lukashenko's departure was "a matter of months"; he wrote "Лукашенко ушел" (Lukashenko has gone) as an anticipated framing. (!) UNCLEAR: the sentence is phrased in the past tense while describing an expected event; the source does not report it as having happened [msg #322].

- **Fage's forecast is explicitly speculative** ("никто не знает, что будет в будущем"), and the author distances himself from Fage's views on consciousness and meditation [msg #272].
- **Self-acknowledged limits:** "весьма диванный взгляд" on Belarus despite a political-science diploma [msg #322]; a "поверхностном обзоре" (superficial overview) of the US election; field observations from ~30 conversations, with his own evaluations "искажающими или нет" [msg #1231, #1233]. The author also admits, about the war in Ukraine context, that "все решается на поле боя" — political agency is offered as a positive factor, not a decisive one [msg #752].
- **Biological/evolutionary theories of war** are presented and then judged weak by the author following Taylor and Ferguson; Pinker's decline-of-violence claim is accompanied by Pinker's own caveat that further decline is not inevitable [msg #923, #924].
- **Reader pushback** on the US-election post (most dislikes since posts criticizing boomers) centred on perceived arrogance, ranking incompatible with mindfulness, and a superficial picture; the author's replies are in *Author's four answers on evaluation, ranking and mindfulness* [msg #1233].

- (!) UNCLEAR: the author calls the generational shift toward freedom values a "scientific fact" and cites vertical-development theories and a video, but gives no data in the post itself; this is his stated view [msg #1306].
- The link between right-populist values and "less educated and less intellectual" strata of US society rests on a link to an earlier post of the author's, not on data presented here [msg #1306].

- **(!) CONTRADICTION (nuance with #752).** In #752 the author urged starting from the position that nothing will change "until I specifically change it". The two posts are therefore compatible, but the 2026 essay is notably more pessimistic about direct influence [msg #1922].

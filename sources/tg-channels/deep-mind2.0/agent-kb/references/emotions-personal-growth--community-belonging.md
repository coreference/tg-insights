# Community, belonging and loneliness

## What this covers

This topic gathers the author's (deep mind channel) views on why social isolation is dangerous, why small communities built on shared values and honesty are the most "alive" and effective structure of society, and how to tell such communities apart from status-driven "alpha" or narcissistic groups that look like spaces of development but trap people. It also covers a personal practice of generosity between peers (the "Silicon Valley question") and why the author prefers the company of people who have done real inner work. According to the source, this matters for health (isolation affects mortality more than smoking), for employees' ability to work, for personal growth and maturity, and — after 2022 — for the emotional survival and future identity of emigrants from Russia, Ukraine and Belarus [msg #192, #537, #613, #1291].

## Core ideas

- **Isolation kills; solitude does not.** Social isolation affects mortality more strongly than smoking, but what harms is the subjective feeling of being cut off, not living alone (see *Isolation vs solitude*) [msg #192].
- **Shared values create trust.** Social health is improved through micro-communities with expressed values, because values filter people and raise trust inside the group [msg #192].
- **Small communities (10–100 people) are the living core of society**, because they contain knowing each other, trust and real mutual help; totalitarian societies, by contrast, seek to atomize people [msg #613].
- **Status communities block growth.** Success and business communities run on the evolutionary "alpha" pattern and force members to keep masks of success, which rules out the genuine experiences that growth needs [msg #537].
- **Narcissistic communities are a trap disguised as development**: their informal priority is inflating ego and status; they can give a feeling of superiority and access to resources, but not real freedom [msg #1291].
- **The antidote is honesty and openness** — and egalitarian groups where power is distributed and people matter for authenticity rather than achievements [msg #537, #1291].
- **Living from abundance**: proactively offering support ("How can I support you?") builds strong connections and new projects [msg #123].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### The Silicon Valley question — "How can I support you?"
**Origin / when to use.** The author had lunch with his friend Mike from San Francisco, who researches consciousness at Stanford (the influence of meditation, trauma and psychedelics on the brain and human activity). They had not seen each other for a year and a half; the author briefly described what deep mind does — working through leaders' outdated ideas about business, helping establish communication and teaching teams to manage their state. Mike listened attentively, said he wanted to ask a "Silicon Valley question", and after a pause asked: «**Как я могу тебя поддержать?**» (*"How can I support you?"*) [msg #123].

**Effect on the author.** He felt confused and then a warm emotional response. He realized he had become somewhat stuck in a "Russian or Moscow mentality": a sale only happens if you are actively selling; you have to ask others to help you and actively do the "work" yourself even at a friendly meeting. The question broke that frame [msg #123].

**Why ask it — Mike's two reasons** (the author asked him why he had asked):
1. **"Это приятно" (*It is pleasant*).** Mike feels meaning in such acts. The two of them share the same values, so by helping the author he also helps himself realize those values [msg #123].
2. **"Это развивает" (*It develops*).** "Please use my network and knowledge." By opening access to one's resources and frequently asking such questions, people create new projects and strong connections [msg #123].

**The author's takeaway.** «Классно жить из ощущения достатка и быть проактивно открытым человеком.» (*"It's great to live from a sense of abundance and be a proactively open person."*) [msg #123]

**Scaling idea.** The author reflected on what strong changes could be achieved by gathering 10–20 people with values of creation (созидание) and helping them ask each other their Silicon Valley question in an atmosphere of supportive dialogues and practices — an idea for a roughly 5-day support retreat [msg #123].

### Isolation vs solitude
- **Isolation is not the same as being alone.** «Изоляция не равна уединению.» (*"Isolation is not equal to solitude."*) Many people live alone and meet a small number of friends, yet are quite healthy and content [msg #192].
- **The deciding factor is subjective**: «Разницу определяет субъективное чувство отрезанности от других.» (*"The difference is determined by the subjective feeling of being cut off from others."*) [msg #192]
- **Workplace relevance.** From the standpoint of employees' state and ability to work normally, under conditions of physical separation (the post is from May 2020, during lockdown), it is important to pay attention to this feeling and question [msg #192].
- **Remedy.** Globally, social health can be improved precisely through micro-communities with expressed values: values filter people and increase trust within the group [msg #192].
- The author ran a poll to very roughly measure the level of social isolation in a narrow sample of people interested in development, mindfulness and new trends in management; he notes that sociological surveys for society as a whole exist without him. ### Small communities as the structure of society
**Context.** Written in March 2022: around 300+ thousand Russians, 3+ million Ukrainians and some number of Belarusians are "as if spat out into open space" from their own countries. The author calls the unprecedented outflow of the most qualified, talented professionals from Russia the second most significant tragedy of the situation after the fighting itself — a large personal and collective tragedy that requires a period of grieving and depression [msg #613].

**Argument, step by step:**
1. **Think about tomorrow.** At some point the evil will end, and we will be left with the results of the decisions and actions taken now; so the question is where to apply effort to create a happy life for future generations [msg #613].
2. **More effective systems win.** From management theory and business history, more effective systems ultimately defeat less effective ones. Combined with Martin Luther King's line «Those who love peace must learn to organize as effectively as those who love war», the author concludes that the future depends not on the ability to assimilate and dissolve into Armenian, Turkish, British or American environments, but on the ability to find in oneself genuine leadership, meanings and ways of organizing that far surpass the "low discourse" that has captured modern Russia [msg #613].
3. **Totalitarianism atomizes.** Citing Hannah Arendt's claim that a totalitarian society strives to atomize people, the author sees small communities — **from 10 to 100 people** — as playing a key role in the struggle for self-awareness and as spaces where leadership and meaning are created [msg #613].
4. **Why small communities work: three ingredients** — they have already "taken off" as the most significant and living structure of society because they contain *знание* (*knowing* each other), *доверие* (*trust*) and *реальная взаимопомощь* (*real mutual help*) [msg #613].
5. **New identity will come from them.** Not now, but eventually people will grieve their losses and find strength to build a new identity. It will come from the experience of supporting each other and the connections in small communities — "precisely there, and not in families or some offices" — that an understanding of oneself as an important group of people on this planet will arise [msg #613].
6. **Most effective way of organizing.** For a de-Putinized, decommunized and de-Stalinized Russia these connections and meanings will be very important, because they are created in the most alive places, out of kindness, intelligence and care for each other. «Кажется, это и есть самый эффективный способ организации.» (*"It seems this is the most effective way of organizing."*) The call: create, join and support communities [msg #613].

**The author's own case.** In November 2021 deep mind launched two communities on themes key to them — conscious human development (mindfulness) and the crypto world — first gathering about 60 people in each, then aiming at around 100 each. In the first weeks after February 2022, the author says that for him personally and for many of "us" communities played "almost the most important role" in adapting to the new reality: there people discussed a lot, went through things together, argued and conflicted, but still helped each other a great deal [msg #537, #613].

**What solidarity looked like in practice.** Out of solidarity the communities publicly opened or jointly prepared materials: a practice of emotional regulation (EFT); a list of useful resources for mental stability ("let's keep our heads in place"); how not to harm yourself with mindfulness practices; why "they" cannot be talked out of it, or how totalitarian sects work; supports in dark times — a meeting with a Buddhist nun; and, from the crypto community, a widely shared guide on how to move rubles into crypto. Community members, under deep mind supervision, launched a group of supportive practices in Yerevan for emigrants and organized a small local deep mind community on Koh Phangan for coordination and help in Thailand, with Russians, Ukrainians and Belarusians in it. ### Alpha-pattern communities vs communities of honest growth
**Diagnosis.** «Сообщества успеха и бизнеса плоские и душные.» (*"Success and business communities are flat and stuffy."*) They are built on the automatic evolutionary pattern of *альфовость* (alphaness): the strongest in the pack gets more resources and attention. In most communities, even young ones, it has stayed the same — the norm is to show that everything is fine with us in order to get even more from others [msg #537].

**Why growth is impossible there (mechanism):**
1. Growth means deepening the understanding of oneself and of other people [msg #537].
2. To deepen, one must consciously meet the emotions, thoughts and sensations present in one's experience and integrate them into self-knowledge [msg #537].
3. In a community this looks like meeting together with real pain, problems, fears and anxieties — and, of course, jointly experiencing joy, pleasure, connectedness and love [msg #537].
4. Such genuine experiences are extremely rare in places where we must keep masks of success, or be anxiously and painfully on guard in case someone suspects we don't measure up [msg #537].

**Two traps.** Worse still for growth: getting used to the rules of such communities, people over-play them and start sincerely believing in their own chosenness and superiority. The author says this inevitably leads to one of two traps for development: (a) dependence on power and external approval, or (b) boredom and the need to keep entertaining oneself [msg #537].

**Antidote.** The author believes that in 2021, with today's level of technology and the spread of psychology and mindfulness, it is possible to go beyond old models of social life and build an environment of real development and support. «Противоядие от автоматических схем и проекций я вижу в честности и открытости.» (*"I see the antidote to automatic schemes and projections in honesty and openness."*) This is the main thing he wants to create in both of his communities [msg #537].

### Narcissistic community — definition, functions, warning signs, alternative
**Background.** The author argues that capitalism gave people an unprecedented degree of freedom but had side effects. One of the main problems of modern culture is a distorted interpretation of individualism: instead of real self-realization it often turns into narcissism — a "neurotic need" to build oneself and one's status through external attributes of success, which include not only houses but also subtle things like a network or reputation [msg #1291].

**Definition.** In group dynamics this produces *narcissistic communities*, which look like spaces of development but are in fact a trap that hinders real movement toward the birth of one's adult personality. A narcissistic community is a group whose main informal priority — possibly not obvious at first — is inflating ego and social status rather than the real development of participants. The author says he has long preferred to leave such circles or watch them from a distance [msg #1291].

**Disguise.** Such groups are often formally built around constructive themes — entrepreneurship, personal growth, spiritual practices — but these themes are from the start, or become over time, just a screen for solving quite different tasks [msg #1291].

**The three hidden functions:**
1. **Strengthening hierarchy** (Укреплять иерархию). Resources, attention and influence flow upward, and the leaders' status becomes ever more significant. Mere membership creates a feeling of significance, especially before those who are not in the group [msg #1291].
2. **Creating exclusivity** (Создавать эксклюзивность). Access is limited by money, connections and other external markers of success, even if they are falsely phrased as inner qualities of a person [msg #1291].
3. **Maintaining functional relationships** (Поддерживать функциональность отношений). Instead of real support and authentic contact, people use each other as a resource [msg #1291].

**The four warning signs ("маячки"):**
1. **Constant competition** (Постоянная конкуренция). Members compare themselves to each other; relationships are built on demonstrating success [msg #1291].
2. **Rigid hierarchy** (Жесткая иерархия). Power is concentrated in a small circle and is "felt in the air". Conversations have low transparency; it is hard to constructively discuss power in the group or the principles of its existence. Someone determines levels of access to events or information [msg #1291].
3. **Illusion of chosenness** (Иллюзия избранности). "We are the best, the rest are losers." What matters is not the process of development but the chance to belong to the elite [msg #1291].
4. **Toxic retention and communication mechanisms** (Токсичные механизмы удержания и коммуникации внутри). Members seem to have to constantly prove their worth, including through participation in expensive rituals — programs, courses, membership fees [msg #1291].

**Bottom line.** «Такие сообщества могут давать чувство превосходства и обеспечивать доступ к ресурсам, но не ведут к подлинной свободе» (*"Such communities can give a feeling of superiority and provide access to resources, but they do not lead to real freedom"*) [msg #1291].

**Psychoanalytic grounding (as cited by the author):**
- **Otto Kernberg**, one of the main researchers of narcissism, described *pathological narcissism* — on which such groups are built — as the inability to create authentic relationships based on vulnerability. In narcissistic groups, instead of seeking a genuine identity, a person increasingly hangs in a world of external attributes, in a three-part pattern: «я должен вписываться / чувствую собственную значимость от того, что я вписался / свысока отношусь к тем, кто в подобные группы не вписался» (*"I must fit in / I feel my own significance from having fitted in / I look down on those who did not fit into such groups"*) [msg #1291].
- **Heinz Kohut** claims a healthy personality is built on self-worth, not external recognition. Narcissistic communities, in the author's view, constantly heat up dependence on external evaluations, replacing inner work with an endless race for status. That worldview has little in common with the realistic and complex picture of the personality we actually are. It seems to give a person a sense of strength, but simultaneously places them in a hierarchy of constant competition and insecurity through exclusivity. Worse, such an environment pulls attention away from the values and ways of being with others that actually help people become mature personalities — "this is harmful to health" [msg #1291].

**Healthy alternative.** There are always healthy alternatives: groups built on equality rather than hierarchy. Power is distributed, real values move away from statuses, and participants matter not for achievements but for authenticity [msg #1291].

### Interaction as a "dance of ranges" — why the author prefers people who have done inner work
(Forwarded from the author's personal channel.) Other things being equal, the author finds it more interesting to meet people experienced in inner work, though he tries to stay open to all kinds of people, including marginalized groups (he mentions wanting to talk with a homeless man who was crying in San Francisco, but not feeling confident enough yet in the new city, where it may also be dangerous). In terms of comfort and joy in communication he observes a pattern: it is more interesting with those who are going through some coherent path of inner work [msg #1120].

**Two explanations he rejects (as the main reason):**
1. *A shared field for talking* — it is always easier to interact with someone who shares your interests and experience [msg #1120].
2. *A wish to discuss inner-work experiences* — ayahuasca, therapies, retreats, coaching. In practice he finds few people interesting to discuss therapy or retreats with, although many try to talk to him precisely about this since he writes about it and runs a business on it. Only a few people work on development seriously and systematically, and they no longer chatter about it; the rest usually greatly exaggerate the significance of their second vipassana for others — though he sincerely rejoices at any achievement and stage of the path [msg #1120].
He grants that both have some sense in them, but they are not the real reason [msg #1120].

**The real reason.** With such people it usually becomes possible to move a bit faster into a conversation «по-чеснаку» (*"for real, honestly"*), in which you don't have to build anything of yourself — partly because the other person doesn't switch on strong "evaluation radars" and isn't actively trying to sell an image of themselves [msg #1120].

**Why people evaluate and filter.** The author understands these evaluations and efforts: the importance of survival/prosperity, which many, out of ancestral historical habit, pursue through constant filtering of people — sorting "ours vs. others", creating groups, cliques and gangs, which "nobody has legislatively abolished yet" [msg #1120].

**Choosing to be oneself.** Increasingly, he wants to play this pattern through himself less and simply be himself. His concrete examples: at a meeting of successful founders he says he is having a hard month, if that's true; he wears to an evening what matches his mood, not the occasion; he doesn't eat when he doesn't want to while everyone around is having dinner. In short he behaves "a bit like a marginal" who is easy to misunderstand [msg #1120].

**Mechanism — the dance of ranges.** With people who have gone through their own path and dropped some personal clamps, becoming more honest inside with themselves, there is immediately more lightness, authenticity and freedom. However free he himself is (he adds that he has much room to grow), «любое взаимодействие это все-таки танец диапазонов» (*"any interaction is still a dance of ranges"*): if the other person is actively playing roles rather than living themselves in the moment, it pulls him down into roles and masks too [msg #1120].

**Conclusion.** These preferences are about neither safety nor attachment to experiences and inner work. «Опыты — всего лишь опыты и не представляют особой ценности, если человек не растет и не меняется.» (*"Experiences are just experiences and have no special value if a person does not grow and change."*) Rather, they reflect a love of freedom and honesty, "above which there is no pleasure in this world" — where two "marginals" can meet and discover they are not marginals at all [msg #1120].

## Evidence and numbers

- The author states, without naming a study, that social isolation affects mortality more strongly than smoking and is harder for men to bear than for women [msg #192].
- Social trust levels as given by the author (source not named): Russia 20–25%; Moscow worse, about 5%; Scandinavian "Euro-socialist" countries 60–65%; Central Europe 40–45%. On this basis he calls Russia a very "atomized" country where people are isolated from each other [msg #192].
- Emigration figures (March 2022, the author's estimate): about 300+ thousand Russians, 3+ million Ukrainians and some number of Belarusians had left their countries [msg #613].
- Optimal community size per the author: 10–100 people; his own communities started at about 60 members each with a target around 100; the retreat idea was for 10–20 people over about 5 days [msg #123, #537, #613].

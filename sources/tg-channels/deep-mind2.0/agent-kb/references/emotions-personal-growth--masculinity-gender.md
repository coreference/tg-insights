# Masculinity, gender and leadership

## What this covers

This topic collects the channel author's posts on men's psychology and gender: the Hollis-based model of male fear and compensation, the critique of hegemonic ("real man") masculinity and its poor fit with the digital economy and modern leadership, the observation that men use supportive practices far less than women, the critique of gender holidays and the "survival mindset", the author's reflections on mature male figures and wholeness, and a 2026 essay on "new masculinity" supported by dating and relationship data. It also covers the parallel argument that leadership is turning toward feminine qualities, integration of diversity and empathy. According to the source this matters because unhealed compensatory masculinity costs men (rage, loneliness, sadness, suicide, failed relationships, falling market value as leaders) and limits society's capacity for creativity and development [msg #103, #146, #389, #1716].

## Core ideas

- **Much of men's lives is run by unacknowledged fear, compensated by power.** Following James Hollis, the author holds that the fear of not matching the image of a man and the fear of physical/psychological trial are men's hidden life drivers, and that the striving for status and power is their compensation (full model in *Hollis' model* below) [msg #103].
- **Traditional masculine "strength" is often a reaction to fear of being insufficient**, not inner stability; under the lens of science, psychotherapy and attention to power structures, most of what is traditionally considered masculine falls apart [msg #1716].
- **Hegemonic masculinity adapts poorly to the new economy and to modern leadership**, which expects flexibility, empathy and communication skills [msg #389].
- **Leadership is turning toward feminine qualities and toward integrating diversity**, because patriarchy is not effective in an interconnected world [msg #146, #51].
- **Healing starts with a man admitting fear and vulnerability, first of all to himself**; wholeness means being strong thanks to vulnerability, not despite it [msg #103, #1471].
- **Men use external support and practices much less than women**, which the author sees as making women more adaptive under stress [msg #174].
- **Attractiveness and relationships are shifting from structural role to personal qualities**, so masculinity must move from the function of power to authentic personhood [msg #1716].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Hollis' model of male fear and compensation
Purpose: explains what drives men, why they compensate, what it costs and how they can heal. The author wrote it after waking up "in a terrible state" with sharpened fears "о которых большинство мужчин не в силах публично признаться" (which most men are unable to admit publicly), deciding to protect himself from decay "через контакт с мудростью старейшин" (through contact with the wisdom of elders) by reading the book on men's path by his favourite Jungian analyst James Hollis. He frames it as a thought about men's reality whose awareness is necessary for "оздоровления культуры" (the healing of culture). He also states that the ability to solve important questions is, for a man, a question of his self-realization and his "состоятельности" (solvency / being a man of substance) [msg #103].

1. **Fear governs a significant part of men's lives.** There are two fundamental male fears:
   - *Fear of not matching the image of a man* ("не соответствовать образу мужчины"). The defence against it is rivalry and the attitude that a man of substance is a results-producing man ("состоятельный мужчина - это результативный мужчина").
   - *Fear of a physical or psychological trial* ("страх физического или психологического испытания"). It shows itself as doubts about one's ability to protect oneself and one's family [msg #103].
2. **Men fear admitting these fears so much that the fears condition them maximally.** Under the influence of culture, the fears become men's main, unconscious life drivers. "Для нас страшнее не пройти «испытание» и не состояться, чем заболеть и умереть." (For us it is scarier to fail the "trial" and not to make something of ourselves than to fall ill and die.) Any form of powerlessness turns out to be worse than leaving life [msg #103].
3. **Fear that cannot be shared leads to compensation.** Driven by fear and unable to share it (he would be shamed), a man comes to compensation: a man who grabs at showy masculinity, fashion trends and expensive things is, to some degree, compensating a sense of his own small significance. The most common form of compensation is power. "За демонстрацией власти скрывается комплекс, за комплексом - страх." (Behind a display of power hides a complex, behind the complex — fear.) "Самые опасные животные в природе - это напуганные животные." (The most dangerous animals in nature are frightened animals.) The complex of striving for status and power is, in the author's words, the main driving force in men's lives: it makes men act and in the end traumatizes them. Giving rein to rage, some men wound others and then feel regret, shame and sadness, growing distant from each other [msg #103].
4. **Unawareness of fears and of culturally imposed role ideas about how strong a man must be is costly.** The world of men is largely a world of rage, loneliness and sadness. Men commit suicide 4 times more often than women (figure as stated by the author). Driven by complexes and fears, men compete instead of partnering, co-building and co-creating [msg #103].
5. **Unhealed men will not be able to lead modern organizations.** The author predicts that men not healed of these complexes "не смогут быть лидерами в современных формах организаций" (will not be able to be leaders in modern forms of organizations) and that their market value will fall rapidly in the new decade. Mechanism: compensatory pseudo-masculinity locks a man inside his own complexes and completely deprives him of the ability to listen without bias, to see, and to positively influence others [msg #103].
6. **Healing requires that a man stop being ashamed of his fear** (numbered as a second "5." in the source). This is very difficult: admitting fear means risking no longer feeling like a man and waiting to be shamed by others. A vicious circle forms: fear deepens loneliness and leads to new fears. The author's prescription has two levels:
   - *Collective*: create collective free spaces where men gather for development — build self-awareness, conduct modern forms of initiation, find elders and share their experience of life.
   - *Individual* (per Hollis, final changes happen individually): "Мужчины должны найти силы и признаться в первую очередь самим себе в слабости, уязвимости и страхах. Это позволит им действительно стать мужчинами и обрести силу." (Men must find the strength to admit, first of all to themselves, their weakness, vulnerability and fears. This will allow them to truly become men and gain strength.) [msg #103]

### Five reasons for the turn toward feminine qualities in leadership
Purpose: the author's explanation (written for 8 March, explicitly "не попытка задобрить женщин в праздник" — not an attempt to placate women on the holiday) of why masculine leadership models, dominant for many years, are giving way. He grants that under masculine models humanity made great progress, but also saw "ужасающими однобокостями, перегибами и проблемами" (horrifying one-sidedness, excesses and problems). He believes the turn toward more femininity and balance has already happened in many places: men express their own femininity or "female qualities" more actively and healthily, and women leaders occupy more and more leading positions. The accumulated reasons [msg #146]:
1. **Psychotherapists have shown the machismo complex** as a person's striving to compensate their own vulnerability [msg #146].
2. **We have seen how psychic trauma arises**, how it is passed from generation to generation "на генетическом уровне" (at the genetic level, as the author states), and have begun to understand its key role in producing the aggression and violence characteristic of hierarchies; we have learned to heal it and reduce violence in the world [msg #146].
3. **We have realized the colossal role of emotional and bodily intelligence**, which the dominant male culture denied [msg #146].
4. **Society is maturing** and becoming more sensitive to violence and direct displays of power; young people refuse to work with traditional leaders [msg #146].
5. **Flexible forms of management have established themselves** in the organizational world and proved more effective than conventional, originally army-derived hierarchies [msg #146].

Conclusion drawn by the author: patriarchy is not effective in an interconnected world and is under pressure from all sides. He expects growing demand for feminine qualities in leadership: more socio- and world-centricity, flexibility, the ability to listen, empathy, compassion and love. These qualities provide integration — connecting people into one in their diversity — and "только через интеграцию разнообразия можно обрести устойчтивость [sic] оргнизаций [sic] и сообществ в современном мире" (only through integrating diversity can organizations and communities gain sustainability in the modern world) [msg #146].

### Five hidden meanings of traditional masculine traits
Purpose: from the 2026 "Новая мужественность" (New masculinity) essay; a deconstruction of what traditional male traits turn out to be "под лупой науки, психотерапии и внимательности к структурам власти" (under the magnifying glass of science, psychotherapy and attention to power structures). The author says most of what is traditionally masculine falls apart under this lens — it "не просто плохо пахнет насилием, но и очевидно хрупко в основании" (not only smells badly of violence but is obviously fragile at its foundation) [msg #1716]:
1. **Demonstrative hypersexuality** looks like a way to prove one's normality [msg #1716].
2. **Homophobia** turns out to be fear of one's own vulnerability, and also simply a concrete rejected sexual desire [msg #1716].
3. **Toughness** is a consequence of trauma that distances one from one's own feelings, or a cover for anxiety [msg #1716].
4. **Emotional distance** is a form of self-defence [msg #1716].
5. **Constant competition** is an attempt to hold on to a shaky identity [msg #1716].

The author's verdict: "Как мужчина скажу — во всем этом нет никакой мужественности." (As a man I will say — there is no masculinity in any of this.) With a hedge ("Конечно, не всегда" — of course, not always), he states that too often traditional male "strength" is not inner stability but a reaction to the all-pervading fear of being insufficient, and asks: if you remove this fear, plus power and the need to conform, what remains? He frames examining the data below as getting at least close to "шагу номер 0 — осознать свое положение" (step number 0 — becoming aware of one's position) [msg #1716].

### Survival mindset vs creative mindset (critique of gender holidays)
Purpose: the author's explanation of why Soviet gender holidays (23 February and 8 March) persist and why he rejects them [msg #395].
- **Mechanism — cultural ritualization:** Soviet gender holidays are an example of cultural ritualization, when an action starts being performed automatically while its meaning gradually washes out. "Основной смысл в повторении этих праздников сейчас — делать то, что делали всегда." (The main meaning in repeating these holidays now is to do what has always been done.) This creates a feeling that everything is stable and life is under control, and unites people around hegemonic masculinity or habitual models of gender in a patriarchal society [msg #395].
- **Harm:** packing men and women into someone's strange expectations and assigning them specific role models causes much pain and suffering and greatly limits the whole society's capacity for creativity and development [msg #395].
- **Survival mindset (Russian: выживанческого майндсета, genitive in source):** the author suggests he may not grasp the holidays' meaning because he and all following generations no longer have a survival mindset — a set of attitudes formed by unprocessed traumas and violence, which sees the world as an unsafe place surrounded by enemies. State propaganda relies on it and tries to strengthen it, including through gender holidays and other ritualization of culture that is no longer adaptive. Young people also want safety, but safety together with the striving for basic physiological comfort is not the main driver they act from [msg #395].
- **Four theses the author offers instead:**
  1. Instead of crossing themselves before diving into an ice hole, men had better think about how they harass their women and how much that makes them "real" men [msg #395].
  2. The most dangerous enemies sit inside the country, not outside it [msg #395].
  3. In the modern connected world one must learn to create and negotiate, not dig in and defend [msg #395].
  4. "Ничего хорошего из страха создать никогда не получится. Для созидания нужны смелость, открытость и любовь." (Nothing good can ever be created out of fear. Creation needs courage, openness and love.) [msg #395]
- The author's proposal: instead of 23 February and 8 March, celebrate a day of the diversity of genders and of love [msg #395].

### Shift from power function to authentic personhood in relationships
Purpose: the author's answer to "if masculinity can no longer rest on a hierarchy of dominance, what remains?" [msg #1716].
1. **Notice the mismatch.** The data (see *Evidence and numbers*) signal a mismatch between the habitual form of male self-presentation and what is perceived as attractive once systemic pressure decreases. That self-presentation is often built on an identity traditionally supported by the system of power and prescribed gender roles — marriage, economic hierarchy, cultural norm. Today men increasingly must prove their worth "без *возможности принудить к выбору*" (without the possibility to compel the choice), even subtly. The author stresses this is "не приговор конкретным людям" (not a verdict on specific people) [msg #1716].
2. **Recognize what status does and does not do.** Money and power increase visibility and widen access to partnership — "было бы глупо это отрицать" (it would be stupid to deny this) — and a watch still attracts girls' attention; but by themselves they do not create stability, do not guarantee relationship quality and are not what women want most [msg #1716].
3. **Move from structural role to personal qualities.** The data together point to a possible change in criteria of male attractiveness — from structural role to personal qualities [msg #1716].
4. **Become an authentic person who builds genuine relationships.** Perhaps the answer lies in moving from the function of power to the ability to be an authentic person and create genuine relationships — not through suppression and superiority, but through the ability to take responsibility for one's expressions and to really see one's partner. In traditional cultures people rarely could see each other this way, because they met first of all as roles [msg #1716].
5. **Treat the crisis as an opportunity.** The current crisis can change this millennia-old norm, and the author wants only to accelerate the transition: it does not merely "threaten" male identity but creates the basis for relationships that can give freedom, love, joy and support immeasurably greater than before [msg #1716].

## Detailed insights

### Hegemonic masculinity and the new economy
The author asks whether hegemonic masculinity prevents adaptation to the new economy and answers that habitual models of masculinity, or of "real" men, show lower adaptivity in the new digital world [msg #389].

He quotes sociologist and political strategist Mark Penn: behaviours and habits suited to men in more dangerous eras are not very useful in the information era. Readiness for risky acts, pride (self-love) and a tendency to switch attention helped men in all times, from the Stone Age to the end of World War II; today they rather hinder. "Преследование дикого кабана и изучение статей в Википедии требуют совершенно разных навыков." (Chasing a wild boar and studying Wikipedia articles require completely different skills.) Society expects men to be providers, and expects efficiency, practicality and reliability from them rather than sociability; but with social media, culture became oriented toward communication, which makes some men feel like superfluous people. Penn's forecast: if the microtrend of men's poor adaptivity grows, women will continue to move into leading positions in society [msg #389].

Marianne Legato, a specialist in gender aspects of medicine, reaches a similar conclusion: despite the many advantages of their position in the past, today "classical" men are in great trouble and are more vulnerable than ever before to biological and social threats [msg #389].

The author adds his own observation: in modern leadership and effective flexible teams this trend is also evident; hegemonic masculinity is quickly read as a toxic phenomenon there, all the more so because leaders today are expected to have more flexibility, empathy and skills that "real" men rarely possess [msg #389].

### Women politicians and the desacralization of politics (Finland example)
The author quotes a Forbes piece on how women came to power in Finland: over the last 10 years women politicians rejected the "male" model closed on work and efficiency. It turned out they have personal lives, children, psychological traumas, dreams and failures, they may even look awkward (paparazzi once caught Li Andersson drunk), but this does not stop them being politicians. To some extent women desacralized politics by refusing to make the usual sacrifices to it, and instead brought their life experience into it. They all grew up in the 1990s — years of economic crisis and loss of social guideposts, when those in power maniacally believed in an "efficient economy", mainly in cutting social spending [msg #51].

According to the quoted text, the new wave of women politicians is characterized by thinking based on "целостности восприятия и эмпатии" (wholeness of perception and empathy). This resonates with voters, who are ready to vote for their female peers, support them and link their personal future with them, because it is easy to entrust one's future to a person who is like you and understands what worries you [msg #51].

### Diversity as real strength
The author argues that differences between people can no longer be denied: science has shown how strongly our chronotypes, nervous systems, ways of processing information and types of thinking differ. In this biological reality, any attempt to make people "march in formation" is doomed to instability and conflict [msg #146].

For well-being, he writes, it is not enough to strive to squeeze the whole world oil market; one must learn to create an integrated culture — a culture of entrepreneurship in which our differences serve as the basis for innovation and creativity. That is real strength, and it is gained when diversity is seen not as a threat to stability and to our domination but as the most valuable wealth. Accepting difference, we encourage our own growth and heal from one-sided compensatory pictures of the world: "Перестаем бояться и начинаем любить." (We stop fearing and start loving.) The post's headline thesis: "Мы разные и это сильно." (We are different, and that is strong.) [msg #146]

### How men cope (or don't) with stress
In April 2020 (isolation, uncertainty, loss of assets, increased police presence), the author asked men how they cope. He had led a mindfulness session at an "embodiment circle" — an open international initiative of weekly events with supportive practices — where there were almost 50 women and he was the only man [msg #174].

His observation: women far more actively turn to external support and to keeping themselves sane through constructive tools — bodily, psychotherapeutic and meditative practices. He describes his own "possibly extreme" view: for about five years he has not understood city life without regular work on his psychic resource through mindfulness practice and psychotherapy — "Зачем плохо жить, если можно хорошо?" (Why live badly if you can live well?) — except perhaps when there are truly no resources for it [msg #174].

He cites polls putting anxiety in Russian society at 68%; in this situation women look more adaptive and, he predicts, "Через месяц будут лучше перформить" (in a month they will perform better) — "Это просто смарт" (it is simply smart). He asks whether men don't need such support because they are "strong and will endure everything", and lists the likely male substitutes: sport; alcohol, carbohydrates and workaholism. His warning: if you distract yourself from emotions and uncertainty by working 15 hours a day, what will happen when you burn out? He explicitly says he is not moralizing but genuinely curious [msg #174].

### Mature male figures and wholeness (personal reflection with a lesson)
On a trip to the Caucasus mountains, the author recalls his grandfather, head of a regional Soviet organization who electrified remote mountain areas by building power lines, earning the titles of Honoured Builder and Honorary Power Engineer of the Union. Always sporty, he died early, at 59, of a heart attack right in his office. The lesson the author draws: perhaps his grandfather left an important and very sad reminder "относиться к жизни проще, меньше работать и вообще следить за собой" (to take life more simply, work less and generally take care of oneself) — a lesson addressed to most men. The author says he has learned part of it and is still learning the rest, especially "умение не повторять судьбу внешне сильных, но внутри истощённых фигур" (the ability not to repeat the fate of outwardly strong but inwardly exhausted figures) [msg #1471].

He longs for mature male figures in society — "Тех, кто соединяет силу с уважением жизни" (those who combine strength with respect for life) and who have values. He distinguishes this from "лжи о традиционных ценностях" (lies about traditional values) by people who are the first to trample them — not keeping their word, lying, cheating, abusing the weak; none of this has anything to do with masculinity. Three years earlier, discussing the typical "male initiations" of the author's childhood and youth, Holger said something like "это катастрофа, а не инициации" (this is a catastrophe, not initiations) [msg #1471].

The author is not a fan of rigid roles or gender stereotyping ("a man must be such, a woman such"). Such thinking is part of the reason people find it hard to become whole: weakness has to be hidden behind control and suppression. "Когда мы внутри цельны, мы признаём разные части себя и становимся сильными не вопреки уязвимости, а благодаря ей." (When we are whole inside, we acknowledge different parts of ourselves and become strong not despite vulnerability but thanks to it.) This produces a man who can write poems and sing, show tenderness, cry when crying is needed, and maintain the dignity of being present in his own life — taking responsibility for his personality, choices and actions [msg #1471].

He notes that his own initiations were varied — otherwise he would not hold these ideas — and that he did once experience "мужские теплые руки, нежность и любовь" (warm male hands, tenderness and love) that stay with him forever. His conclusion: what matters is not so much searching for examples of masculinity or femininity outside, but becoming a bearer of wholeness oneself and creating a space where it becomes possible [msg #1471].

### Symbols of gender today
The "New masculinity" essay opens with a scene: two female friends admired that instead of the now-usual digital watch the author wears a mechanical watch ("котлы, как батя" — slang: a watch, like dad's). This led him to think about through which symbols we perceive gender differences today and what it means to be a man in the modern world; he later notes that the watch still attracts girls' attention, but such status markers do not create stability by themselves [msg #1716].

## Evidence and numbers

- **Male suicide:** men commit suicide 4 times more often than women — figure stated by the author without a source [msg #103].
- **Anxiety in Russia (spring 2020):** according to polls cited by the author, the level of anxiety in Russian society rose to 68% [msg #174].
- **OkCupid dating platform analysis:** showed a stable asymmetry of perception — under free choice women rate men significantly more strictly than men rate women; 30% of men evoke in women not a neutral but a distinctly negative reaction [msg #1716].
- **Long-term partner preferences (David Buss, "Preferences in Human Mate Selection", as linked by the author):** for long-term relationships women value reliability, kindness/goodwill and emotional stability more highly than status symbols. The author stresses this is "не мой вывод… а данные" (not my conclusion… but data) [msg #1716].
- **Predictors of relationship quality (an APA PsycNet study the author cites):** status symbols are not among the predictors of long-term relationship quality at all [msg #1716].
- **Pew Research:** 63% of men under 30 in the US are not in a relationship; among women of the same age the figure is half as high. The author interprets this as the gap between classical masculinity and what is actually needed affecting men's ability to build relationships [msg #1716].

# AI and the human transition

## What this covers

This part collects what the deep mind author (Max, a leadership and mindfulness consultant) wrote between 2020 and 2026 about living through the AI transition: the economic and employment pressure of AI, the virtualization trend and Web3, ChatGPT and the reinvention of expertise, the psychology of the "AI tsunami", the four intelligences he believes AI cannot replace, the future of work as discussed at San Francisco Tech Week, his own experience of AI as a thinking partner and the resulting crisis of cognitive capital, and his calm, practice-based approach to adoption. The recurring claim is that the AI shift is above all a transition in human consciousness, identity and adaptation capacity — and that the leadership of this change has to start inside oneself [msg #317, #1390, #1739].

This part has four posts by the deep mind author on the psychology of the AI transition. They cover the rise of the "AI-native" professional and the inner barriers to becoming one; the hunger for meaning at work and Hannah Arendt's labour/action distinction as the answer to "what will you do with the time AI frees?"; awareness as the capacity that stays uniquely human, with three concrete ways to use AI to develop it; and how AI is transforming therapy and the helping professions. The author argues that the transition is mainly a matter of psyche, nervous system and meaning, not only of technology. He therefore sees inner resilience, awareness and meaning as more relevant in the AI era, not less [msg #1761, #1793, #1802, #1819].

## Core ideas

- **AI is the defining technology and a paradigm change, not ordinary progress.** Unlike earlier technology waves, the usual techno-optimist law that progress eventually raises welfare and absorbs temporary unemployment may not hold for AI; later the author says AI is spreading faster than the internet because it yields tangible value from the first implementation (see *Detailed insights → AI and employment*) [msg #317, #1519].
- **The technological transition goes together with a transition of consciousness** — from materialist to post-materialist values ("orange to green" in Spiral Dynamics) — and AI can become a tool for a more humanistic society only if inner human growth is taken into account [msg #317].
- **The most personal is the most systemic.** Personal anxiety, powerlessness and inspiration about the AI future are expressions of systemic conditions; since the future is unpredictable, change has to be led first within oneself (see *The van conversation*) [msg #1390].
- **Human value shifts to what cannot be automated.** As IQ-type tasks are automated, ethical, spiritual/existential, philosophical and presence intelligences become decisive (see *Four human intelligences of the future*) [msg #1483].
- **The danger is a quiet "soft loss", not a catastrophe**, and people react to the AI wave with three typical reactions that are normal unless one gets stuck (see *Three psychological reactions*) [msg #1519].
- **Cognitive capital is being devalued.** When algorithms reproduce cognition faster and cheaper, thinking does not disappear but loses its rarity, shaking the status logic of post-industrial society [msg #1712].
- **AI adaptation is a human-transition problem, not a technological one**: tool-setup courses address the surface; what matters is human speed of adaptation, skills and resources [msg #1739].
- **Adopt calmly but actually adopt**: neither frighten, dramatize and FOMO, nor ignore; practice daily and cross-pollinate (see *Calm AI-adoption approach* and *No Drama / No FOMO strategy*) [msg #1707, #1724].

- **The AI shift is anthropological, not just technological.** The type of professional subject is changing, from a self-reliant individual to a human–AI "centaur". Value now comes from skilled co-authorship with AI. Full detail is under *AI-native / centaur professional subject* [msg #1761].
- **Resistance to AI is psychological, not lazy or unintelligent.** The transition hits professional identity, which triggers shame, anxiety and overload, and it activates protective parts. Full detail is under *Psychological barriers* [msg #1761].
- **AI raises an existential question about work.** When AI takes over routine, people face "what will you do with this time?" The author says the neurotic answer, doing even more, is a dead end. Machines can take over labour but never *action*. Full detail is under *Labour vs action* [msg #1793].
- **Awareness becomes more valuable and, at the same time, harder.** AI cannot be aware. That makes awareness the uniquely human asset, but fast AI-assisted work erodes the slow feedback loops that awareness depends on. Used correctly, AI can still help develop awareness in the broad sense [msg #1802].
- **Helping professions are being reshaped now.** The first RCT of a generative AI therapy chatbot exists. Practitioners already use AI in their work, and clients are developing new AI-related problems. The author says that for therapists, adapting is a necessity, not an option [msg #1819].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Four human intelligences of the future
**Purpose.** A "pre-sensing futurological frame" for deciding which of one's lines of development to prioritize when basic cognitive tasks — analysis, search, synthesis — are increasingly solved by AI. Its foundation: Howard Gardner's 1983 theory of multiple intelligences, which holds that a person is not an IQ but an ensemble of cognitive, bodily, emotional and social skills developing along different trajectories; Ken Wilber called such intelligences "lines of development". The author expects IQ not to lose significance as a whole but to drop far down the scale of demand, and asks: what if the main human abilities become not those that can be automated, but those that cannot be handed to a machine? He presents the four forms as his own conviction ("по моему убеждению" — "in my conviction") about what will be defining in the coming decades [msg #1483].

1. **Ethical intelligence (Этический интеллект).** The ability to orient oneself in the consequences of decisions and actions of different complexity and range — and often not to calculate them but to *feel* them. Why it matters: when algorithms begin making decisions, the question becomes who takes responsibility. "AI умеет отличать кошку от собаки, но по-настоящему не понимает, почему нельзя использовать чужую боль ради прибыли" (AI can tell a cat from a dog, but does not truly understand why one must not use another's pain for profit) — the ethical compass remains a human task. Grounding: Lawrence Kohlberg's research on stages of moral development; Carol Gilligan's ethics of care as an alternative to abstract morality; philosophers like Michael Sandel stress that ethics is not a department of jurisprudence but the foundation of public existence. Russian-language authors who write on this, per the author: Lobsang Tenpa and Sasha Malakhov [msg #1483].
2. **Spiritual / existential intelligence (Духовный / экзистенциальный интеллект).** The ability to bear the absence of answers, to sense meaning, to be in contact with the whole. "AI не знает, зачем жить. Он не просыпается ночью от страха смерти." (AI does not know why to live. It does not wake up at night from fear of death.) Grounding: Viktor Frankl described it as a basis of survival under conditions of the absurd; William James as a form of knowing not reducible to the rational; John Vervaeke as an answer to the modern meaning crisis. The author stresses it is less about religious dogma than about the capacities to be aware, to interpret and to be in relationship with something larger. Russian-language authors: Dasha Zolotukhina and Sasha Malakhov [msg #1483].
3. **Philosophical intelligence (Философский интеллект).** The ability to reflect on thinking itself, to see frames, to hold contradiction. "ИИ отлично отвечает на вопросы, но не может почувствовать откуда они взялись." (AI answers questions excellently but cannot feel where they came from.) It does not notice which assumptions are built into a system and who benefits from them. Grounding: Michel Foucault, who taught to take apart hidden systems of power; Hannah Arendt, for whom thinking is a form of ethical action; Byung-Chul Han, who shows how modern society loses depth under the pressure of productivity. Russian-language author: Dima Solovyov [msg #1483].
4. **Intelligence of presence and awareness (Интеллект присутствия и осознанности).** The ability to manage attention, notice one's own patterns and not get lost in a hyper-stimulated environment. In a world where attention has become a currency, the ability to consciously choose where to look and what to feel is "новый тип грамотности" (a new type of literacy). It is not only self-regulation but also the art of being — engaged in one's life at different levels of depth. Grounding: Francisco Varela and Evan Thompson through the idea of embodied mind; Daniel Siegel's *mindsight* — the capacity to observe one's own system; Otto Scharmer's notion of sensing presence — an inner point able to transform any action of a leader. The author says he himself tries to write about this, and Alexandra Vilvovskaya in the context of bodily intelligence [msg #1483].

**Status and summary.** The author explicitly says this is not yet a canonized scientific classification, but not fantasy either — a futurological frame relying on understandable thinkers and scientists; a vector of inner evolution already launched against the background of burnout, loss of meaning and extreme technocratization. "AI научит нас учиться быстрее, сделает еще эффективнее. Но не научит чувствовать, брать ответственность и быть [достойным человеком]." (AI will teach us to learn faster, will make us even more efficient. But it will not teach us to feel, to take responsibility and to be [a decent human being].) [msg #1483]

### Three psychological reactions to the AI tsunami
**Purpose.** A map of how the psyche responds to the approaching "AI tsunami", so one can recognize one's own reaction. Every reaction is normal, but getting stuck in any of them costs the ability to act soundly and adaptively [msg #1519].
1. **Anxiety and contraction (Тревога и сжатие).** We feel ourselves becoming obsolete; even successful professionals keep thinking something like "Мой навык может исчезнуть за ночь" (My skill may disappear overnight) [msg #1519].
2. **Euphoria and overheating (Эйфория и перегрев).** Others throw themselves into the race, hoping to "catch the wave", and burn out quickly [msg #1519].
3. **Numbness (Оцепенение).** Some people — and even countries — pretend nothing is happening and pay for it by falling behind [msg #1519].

### Weinstein's "put the board in the water" program
Eric Weinstein repeats: "Пора ставить доску в воду и грести" (It's time to put the board in the water and paddle). The author interprets this not as an urgent startup launch but as inner work with three components [msg #1519]:
1. **Develop personal resilience** — presence practices, bodily intelligence, work with traumas and emotions.
2. **Collect your "capital of meanings" (капитал смыслов)** — personal experience, vision and methods: what cannot be downloaded from a model.
3. **Build communities** where support and knowledge exchange accelerate adaptation.

### Calm AI-adoption approach
**Context.** Because in the AI world education no longer guarantees growth, tech Twitter discusses a "permanent underclass" — a sad place a person risks ending up in if they do not start mastering AI tools now (the post opens with a meme: "— У нас есть 1-2 года, чтобы избежать попадания в permanent underclass, AI будет... — Питер, я выхожу замуж" — "We have 1–2 years to avoid falling into the permanent underclass, AI will… — Peter, I'm getting married"). Such statements create harsh FOMO, which by itself does not help, because under stress and anxiety the ability to learn and see the strategic horizon declines; yet simply brushing them off (like the bride brushing off Peter) is no longer possible either. So the author chooses "спокойный подход, но все-таки подход" (a calm approach, but still an approach) [msg #1707]:
1. **Allocate at least 30 minutes a day to AI automation** — a small, regular dose instead of panicked bursts [msg #1707].
2. **Look at what others *really* do** — real practice rather than hype [msg #1707].
3. **Apply AI more actively not only to work but to the body and psyche.** Work will keep changing so fast that we will not keep up; the body and psyche determine whether we can process all these changes and ourselves within them at all. Example: in December deep mind ran a workshop for employees of a large ("yellow") company on AI for state management — feeling better by using an AI assistant together with data from Whoop and other gadgets. The author's observation: this theme spreads AI among employees faster and at the same time gives overloaded people a new resource and support. His own two main examples of AI for inner tasks: daily management of the state of the nervous system, and vertical development [msg #1707].

The author also describes the value of a *collaborative* format: in a raw, chaotic reality nobody keeps up with, it makes sense to gather a shared field and look at different speakers' cases together rather than listening to one lecturer [msg #1707].

### No Drama / No FOMO strategy
**Diagnosis.** Public responses to AI resemble the Covid era, when humanity slid into black-and-white automatic strategies. The information field has formed around two extremes — **techno-horror stories** and **techno-evangelism** — and outside that bubble there is no constructive answer either, only "я в домике" ("I'm in my little house", i.e. hiding) of varying deafness: from simple ignorance and misunderstanding to sometimes aggressive defenses spewing hatred or contempt toward AI. Humans generally struggle with a balanced position toward technology: either we project a magic pill onto it, pushing outward the need to grow and take responsibility inside, or we experience almost primal fears and distort reality. The author rejects the extremes and proposes a space for "No Drama/No Fomo" sense-making of these intense, frightening and inspiring changes. Three things he considers strategically important and takes on himself [msg #1724]:
1. **Do not frighten, dramatize or FOMO — but do not ignore either.** Make sense of it from a meta-position, relying on science and real cases.
2. **Interact with AI regularly** — daily, gradually expanding one's experience and the depth of AI's penetration.
3. **Cross-pollinate** — talk, help each other, look at other people's real cases.

He also states he deliberately does not teach vibe-coding, how to install OpenClaw, or scare people with the death of their profession: he interacts with these tools but is not deep on the technical side, "Да и сомнительно шарить в том, что ежедневно устаревает" (and it is questionable to master what becomes obsolete daily) [msg #1724].

### Four reasons to watch Web 3.0
Written in 2022 to explain why a leader- and team-development practice launched a separate community on blockchain/crypto/Web3 [msg #669]:
1. **Blockchain has passed the point of no return** and has begun to really affect the economy and politics; this is noted in reports of leading Valley venture funds such as Andreessen Horowitz. "Islands" of new infrastructure already exist that will fully change how people interact and how society is structured.
2. **Web 3.0 enables a collective future rather than a corporate or state one** — the second part of the puzzle deep mind works on: a more transparent, healthy and meaningful future in which each person has much greater freedom from large systems and authoritarian rulers. Consciousness development is non-material, but it needs material infrastructure. Example the author gives: most modern organizations are an archaic form of collaboration whose foundations were laid in the industrial era centuries ago; we mostly refresh our ancestors' experience without evolutionary change. Most organizations still cannot unlock human potential or make work a place of authentic development — they were not created for that — and cannot act from a systemic, all-stakeholder logic to overcome rather than aggravate global challenges (hunger, poverty, climate change, the epidemic of mental illness, authoritarianism and totalitarianism). Changing this requires a shift in culture and skills, but these go hand in hand with new structure, goals and business processes.
3. **The next evolutionary step in management practice will probably be made in Web 3.0** — decentralized, blockchain-based organizations. Blockchain-built DAOs are becoming the evolutionary edge of experiments in governance, effective interaction and resource distribution in groups, creating infrastructure for truly open, transparent and engaging organizations; decentralized technologies "already" create the economic basis for the most freedom-loving, creative and effective forms of social life. "We can fight it or use it for good."
4. **Stay in constant contact with the field of Web3 projects** to understand how the future is being created, if we want to participate in the largest social-technological-business-political transformation since the internet. The author personally found no better way than gathering people who see the situation similarly, are already immersed in Web3 and are ready to develop together.
Recommended further study in the post: an open lecture on DAO from the community, the Andreessen Horowitz State of Crypto report, and Vitalik Buterin's paper "Decentralized Society: Finding Web3's Soul" (with a Russian translation) [msg #669].

### Three role options in a world of cognitive abundance
Facing the existential question "какую роль хочу занимать в мире когнитивного изобилия?" (what role do I want to occupy in a world of cognitive abundance?), the author lists his options [msg #1712]:
1. Become an architect of Human+AI processes.
2. Go deeper into highly qualified therapy and facilitation.
3. Do a hybrid — create environments where people learn to think with AI but do not lose the subject (their own agency).
He considers such an identity crisis "in the era of cognitive singularity" inevitable soon for everyone who has invested heavily in intellect, and wants to go through it together with others [msg #1712].

### Hierarchical vs network model of access to careers
From the SF Tech Week panel: the big transition is from a **hierarchical model of access** (diploma → career ladder) to a **network model** (competencies, creativity, speed of adaptation → visibility and influence). AI accelerates this, because a person now has tools to create value without the "permission" of elite institutions [msg #1550].

### AI-native / centaur professional subject
Purpose: this describes the new kind of professional the author and a founder friend see emerging. It is the person employers will increasingly want to bet on.
1. **The old model of the professional.** For a long time, modern work was built around a stable structure: you, your mind, experience, skills and network. Even when digital tools were around, the centre of action stayed in the person. You were the main carrier of thinking, and everything else was a supporting environment [msg #1761].
2. **The new figure: a hybrid.** What is emerging is neither a pure human nor a pure machine but a hybrid. The author uses Ethan Mollick's term *centaur*: someone who "не ищет как делегировать все AI, а умеет все лучше опираться на него" (does not look for how to delegate everything to AI, but is getting better at leaning on it). The centaur uses AI to deepen their own thinking and expand their results. They know AI's weak spots and understand their own responsibility and position as the human in the pair [msg #1761].
3. **Value comes from co-authorship plus a stance.** Value increasingly arises from skilled co-authorship, which requires new skills and also a *stance* (позиция). This stance is not about knowing many AI services. It is not about wanting to hand every task to agents either [msg #1761].
4. **Definition.** An AI-native person is first of all someone who has already inwardly accepted that "ткань работы изменилась" (the fabric of work has changed). Professional maturity now includes skills plus the ability to be in a mode of co-creation with technology [msg #1761].

The example the author gives: a founder friend runs an unusual event company that is well known among very wealthy people. He told the author he is less and less willing to bet, when hiring, on people who do not become AI-native, meaning people who do not seriously enter this environment and do not learn to work in a constant hybrid with AI. The friend put it much more harshly. The author softened the wording but finds the position hard to disagree with. He says this is not because he likes technological Darwinism or thinks slower adapters are less valuable. It is because a new work environment is forming right now and the shift cannot be ignored. He expects a growing number of founders to share this sense of the present moment. He also says the idea of an anthropological turn is something he has repeated before and now sees increasingly often "в тенхотвиттере" [sic] (on tech Twitter) [msg #1761].

### Psychological barriers to becoming AI-native
Purpose: this explains why people resist AI and what they need in order to become the new subject.
1. **The cause.** The author says resistance to AI is probably not about laziness or a lack of intelligence. It is more likely about the psyche: our patterns and our resources [msg #1761].
2. **Identity threat.** The AI transition hits more than specific skills and habitual actions. It hits identity. It calls into question the old image of the professional who relies only on themselves [msg #1761].
3. **Forced incompetence.** The transition makes people clumsy again, "сырыми и не до конца компетентным" (raw and not fully competent). This produces shame, anxiety or a feeling of overload [msg #1761].
4. **Protective parts get activated.** Common defensive reactions are contempt, the wish to brush AI off, or the opposite: feverishly trying to catch up [msg #1761].
5. **The load is lasting.** Seen through the author's lenses, the new AI-native subjecthood is very much a story about the psyche and the nervous system. ### Broad vs narrow awareness
- **Narrow sense:** a specific skill of noticing your immediate, direct experience. At its heart is direct perception of reality, not information or insight [msg #1802].
- **Broad sense:** the general ability to see clearly what usually stays below the radar of consciousness. ### 3 ways to use AI to develop awareness (in the broad sense)
1. **AI Proxy of yourself.** Create an agent as a copy of yourself and learn about your usual reactions and strategies by interacting with it. How: load a wide layer of context into the AI, such as your test results and history, your texts, and your live conversations and calls. You then have your own Proxy, and you give it cases and questions. Why: it lets you see yourself from the outside. As a bonus you get a like-minded assistant [msg #1802].

How: build an agent that gives developmental feedback, and feed it daily voice notes about your life, or all the calls and meetings you take part in [msg #1802].
3. **AI as a helper in exploring your social location.** This is an exercise deep mind runs at the start of its Resonance programme. ### The AI tsunami and the risk of soft loss (2025)
Writing from San Francisco, where OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind are based, the author argues the AI revolution will not spread in waves over years as the internet did: AI gives tangible benefit from the first implementation, so the world connects faster — from Latin America to Siberia. Eric Weinstein (mathematician, managing director of Thiel Capital) calls it the tsunami of our lifetime. His thought, as quoted: those who fear world domination by a superintelligent AI are getting a bit ahead of themselves — "Мы можем оказаться переиграны куда раньше — и вовсе не гениальным, а довольно «тупым» искусственным интеллектом" (We may be outplayed much earlier — and not by a brilliant but by a rather "dumb" AI) [msg #1519].

Mechanism: what matters is not the exciting singularity but that even simple AI can already replicate itself and change by learning on real data. Intellectual functions are now copied and scaled like electricity; any cognitive skill can become a replicable service [msg #1519].

**Soft loss (мягкий проигрыш).** The most dangerous trajectory is not catastrophe but a calm erosion of one's role: you are still busy, but the real value "flows" into others' models and your experience is packaged into someone else's agent. Weinstein says many underestimate not dramatic but cumulative effects: small daily AI gains add up and suddenly turn into a chasm between players. This is not another round of progress but a change of the whole human way of life — a shift of the foundation: social institutions and personal strategies — diplomas, career ladders, even ideas of the value of labor — lose their usual stability [msg #1519].

**Copyable intelligence as capital.** Weinstein speaks of the birth of "copyable intelligence as capital": whoever has access to data, their own processes and communities can turn their experience into an asset; the rest risk competing with endless digital twins. Psychologically this requires a different identity: value stops being defined by position or tenure; what matters is the ability to learn fast, assemble unique skill combinations and build one's own field of data and practices [msg #1519].

**The tsunami as a chance.** Like Covid, the AI tsunami will expose what was always fragile — in the material world and the inner one: dependence on status, fear of the new, the habit of relying on others' scripts. There is liberation in the challenge: with enough resource and the right focus of attention, we can redefine what it means to be a living human and stop measuring ourselves by "usefulness" in old coordinates. In the spirit of Theory U (we can always sense the future in the present), the author feels the real revolution will happen not in code or servers but in how we interact with the world in body and consciousness. His questions: are we open or closed to the new? Can we change? Do we have a foundation without the familiar statuses and attributes of the old world? [msg #1519]

### Awareness: what AI is not, and how fast AI work erodes awareness
- **Why awareness is a human–machine distinction.** The ability to be aware is one of the most fundamental differences between humans and machines. Language models have no subjective experience, no interoception, no bodily signals and no continuous memory of themselves. They do not notice their own patterns. They "просто воспроизводят статистику по токенам" (simply reproduce token statistics). The author calls this a fundamental limitation that is unlikely to change quickly, if at all, and warns that "we will forget this" [msg #1802].
- **Value inversion.** The more powerful AI becomes at processing data, the more valuable becomes what it fundamentally cannot do. So awareness is not just another useful skill. It is what remains exclusively human in an AI world: "Не скорость и не продуктивность, а живое присутствие в контакте с реальностью" (not speed and not productivity, but living presence in contact with reality) [msg #1802].
- **The mechanism of harm.** When AI makes work many times faster, we overload the brain's executive centres. The brain gets fewer of the slow feedback loops that sustain awareness. The author's analogy: just as the internet killed our attention, AI will very likely harm people's capacity for awareness, which requires slowing down, not speeding up [msg #1802].
- **The paradox.** A world with powerful AI demands more awareness from us and at the same time creates conditions in which it is harder to develop [msg #1802].
- **Threat and opportunity.** Technologies usually bring both. Used correctly, AI can become a tool for developing awareness, as long as you understand its limits and lean on it where we are weak. The author says your skill here decides your personal answer to whether AI becomes a tool of liberation or just another distraction and addiction [msg #1802].

He expects the whole landscape of this practice to change quite radically very soon [msg #1819].
- **New client problems.** The author lists dependence on AI companions, disorders related to trackers and data, and nervous-system dysregulation from being constantly connected to a screen [msg #1819].
- **For practitioners.** Helping practitioners have to reinvent themselves and their profession quite fundamentally to adapt to large-scale change. The author acknowledges this can trigger people and cause stress, but he is "absolutely sure" that adaptation is not an option but a necessity. His forecast: therapists who integrate AI will see demand grow, and those who refuse will face economic pressure in the near future [msg #1819].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The four intelligences are explicitly the author's conviction and "not yet a canonized scientific classification" — a futurological frame [msg #1483].
- (!) CONTRADICTION (tension between sources): the GenSpark founder and the SF Tech Week audience treat "AI will take my job" as noise and expect everyone to be lifted ("we will all work like the boss") [msg #1550], whereas Weinstein's "soft loss", the "permanent underclass" discourse and the author's own "crisis of devaluation of cognitive capital" describe gradual displacement and a chasm between players [msg #1519, #1707, #1712]. The author reports both without fully reconciling them; his own position moved toward concern by 2026.
- (!) UNCLEAR: the GenSpark founder's share of work done by AI is given both as 80–90% of routine and 60–90% overall in the same post [msg #1550].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: the sources for the 16% AI-usage, 0.01–0.1% agent-usage and 79–85% enterprise agentic-AI figures are not named; the author himself hedges that companies are "trying", not succeeding [msg #1739].
- The author's 2023 claim that AI lacks ethics, transpersonal knowledge and spirituality "in any form" is his assessment at the time [msg #811].
- The author admits he is not deep on the technical side of AI tools [msg #1724] and that his 2026 plans are made by "a consciousness that does not yet understand" the new world [msg #1712].
- Conflict of interest: several posts promote deep mind formats (crypto community, workshops, Resonance); the author's bet on high-presence human processes coincides with his own business [msg #669, #1707, #1712].

- **Insight is not presence.** The author says AI can help you see patterns and hidden dynamics and widen your perspectives, which in the broad sense is real awareness, but in the narrow sense it is not: "В сердце этого навыка лежит не информация и не инсайт, а прямой опыт восприятия реальности" (at the heart of this skill lies not information and not insight, but the direct experience of perceiving reality). He already sees a risk that people will confuse understanding with the phenomenological capacity to be present in life. He stresses these are different things [msg #1802].
- The author frames the AI-native hiring stance as harsh and says he softened his friend's wording. He explicitly distances himself from technological Darwinism and from judging slower adapters as less valuable [msg #1761].
- The author does not ask readers to trust Amodei's forecast ("whether we believe such claims or not"). His own claims about AI improving therapy rest on what he personally observes [msg #1819].
- #1819 also promotes deep mind's own workshop, so the author has a commercial interest in his message that adaptation is necessary. #1761 similarly links to deep mind's AI adaptation offerings [msg #1761, #1819].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: #1761 promises a separate post about helping specialists who support clients' nervous systems through their own. #1793 continues in a comment that is not in the source. The three future scenarios in #1819 are named but not described [msg #1761, #1793, #1819].

- Silence is a position. When a powerful person does not respond to serious accusations, non-response itself becomes a public gesture, and society responds through bottom-up regulation (cancel culture) [#1651].
- Russia does not suffer from "new ethics" — it lacks basic ethics. Panic about "ethical terror" imports a US debate; the local problem is legitimized violence at home, work, prisons and the army [#1331].
- Influence equals power and creates responsibility. Anyone with an audience, even a small one, holds power and must handle it as an adult; see *11 principles of ethical media presence* [#961, #1629, #1634].
- The attention economy rewards simplification and status games. It produces imitation of expertise and narcissistically infected content; the reader's defence is trust hygiene and asking "who benefits from my feeling of deficit?" [#1727, #1750].

- One phrase was inflated into a false thesis. In three days a single phrase from an interview became the claim that one of the country's main HR leaders hires "by the vibe" instead of data, followed by personal insults and remarks about her appearance; the video got about a million views. She never said a company should be managed by intuition alone [#1979].
- What she described is the ordinary stance of a mature leader. Attention to body, emotions and intuition as a source of information that *complements* analysis is, according to the author, mainstream in American leadership culture; the reaction is the rejection a society that expects bad things from change always gives to the new [#1979].
- Fear of novelty does not explain the strength of the reaction — displaced aggression does. Pain from long-suppressed systemic violence seeks a visible but safe target; see *Displaced-aggression mechanism of public harassment* [#1979].
- In a pile-on, the rationality invoked by attackers stops working. See *Collapse of rationality in a pile-on* [#1979].
- Public harassment is violence, and paying a price is the path of genuine leadership. Saying what you consider true before the environment is ready is costly; many great leaders paid a huge price for truth [#1979].
- New culture needs active, conscious care; the old one does not. The author calls on the community to support Zolotukhina and one another, to show new leadership more boldly, to handle their privilege with care, and to offer the attackers compassion as well as resistance [#1979].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Survival-driven victim-blaming mechanism
Purpose: explains why, "even today, after all the explanations on the internet", people often accuse victims of violence of being guilty themselves, and why this directly concerns each of us [#941].

1. Survival as the deepest drive. Humans strive to survive; winning over others and achieving success increases resources and the chance not to die. Survival happens not only in the wild but in the community: historically the fate of an individual depended heavily on the people around [#941].
2. Violence splits participants into aggressor and victim, strong and weak. In the logic of animal life this split "naturally" makes the aggressor right. Witnesses find it easier to join and admire the strong, dominant — hence "winning and successful" — party [#941].
3. Joining force reduces one's own risk. By siding with power, people historically raised their survival chances and lowered the risk of problems [#941].
4. Stigmatization of the victim is the cheapest way to join. The formulas are "это с ней что-то не так" (something is wrong with her) and "сама виновата" (she's to blame herself) [#941].
5. Exploitation by power-seekers. Because the pattern is ancient and tied to the most important need, it is highly significant and easily activated to form communities of force/violence. Most people do not work on themselves and intuitively strive toward strength out of unconscious fear and a focus on their own safety. There are always people who use these deep fears for selfish ends; given access to instruments of violence or power, they create raider or gangster groups, aggressive organizations, autocracies and dictatorships [#941].
6. Subtle form in the workplace. The same pattern shows up as fear of showing negative emotions and states in a competitive work environment: in the office we do not talk about our depression because we intuitively sense it will lead to problems rather than to support and to multiplying the team's or company's shared resilience [#941].
7. Cost. In organizations and communities of force, resources are used extremely inefficiently because the "weak" are oppressed rather than supported; people compete for pieces more than they learn to grow the whole pie [#941].

How to get past the pattern (author's prescription): it is crucial to outgrow this fear, otherwise "we will be ruled not by the smart and just, but by those ready to intimidate and beat when the occasion arises". It is hard to "hack" until people get stable experience of integrating marginalized ("weak") groups into their family, community and society — i.e. until we see many times that supporting the "weak" yields far more results and shared safety together. This is exactly what real democracies and any forms of inclusivity (D&I in organizations) aim for. To build a higher-order community, we support everything culturally considered weak, stop projecting our own fears into it and discourage others from doing so. Hence any marginalized group, emotion or manifestation is maximally important for anyone wanting an effective, resilient society. Supporting the depressed, the ill, women, LGBTQ+, children and people with disabilities, "we fight dictatorships and create a truly sustainable environment for future generations". The author concludes: "Это и есть подлинные традиционные ценности." (This is what genuine traditional values are.) [#941]

#### 11 principles of ethical media presence ("Этичная медийность — манифест")
Purpose: a manifesto for authors and readers [#1634].

Why it is useful. For authors: ethics in media is "definitely not about some subjective morality" but about creating truly durable, strong relationships with the audience; it enables real connection, trust and community, and helps avoid a ruined reputation. For readers: by choosing ethical authors whose content we let pass through us, we create a healthier information environment with fewer manipulations and distortions [#1634].

How ethics is defined here. Ethical media presence begins with acknowledging power. If we have an audience — even a small one — influence and the phenomenon of power appear, and with them always the risk of abuse: through emotions, opacity, building a vertical hierarchy, or creating a narcissistic or quasi-religious space around oneself. The ethics of publicity rests on an adult, honest attitude to that power — without the illusion "«я просто делюсь—хотите читайте, хотите нет»" (I'm just sharing — read it or don't) and without romanticizing one's own sincerity. It helps one take an adult position sensitive to consequences [#1634].

1. Do not use the blog as an emotional container. People did not subscribe to be our therapist or parent. Public vulnerability is a gift only when it is integrated, structured and does not turn the audience into a system for regulating our emotions. Vulnerability is useful when it gives space and clarity to all sides, not when the blog becomes a way of coping with one's feelings [#1634].
2. Do not play on emotions. Media often creates power through drama. Posts in the style of "стыдно признаться" (ashamed to admit) give a quick emotional spike but form audience dependence and sustain a narcissistic dynamic. Ethical media means refusing emotional manipulation "even if they don't seem like that to us". Human emotions are not a sales tool or a way to hold attention [#1634].
3. Do not distort the picture of life. Toxic positivity and "успешный успех" ("successful success") create a distorted reality in which people grow more anxious and feel something is wrong with them. Ethics means giving reality its place rather than building a cult of the ideal image — talking about life's difficulty but without broadcasting despair [#1634].
4. Safe vulnerability. The ability to speak about one's experience without romanticizing suffering, without dramatizing, without turning pain into a show. "Уязвимость — не перформанс." (Vulnerability is not a performance.) It is a form of mature openness that leaves the reader free, respects their boundaries, and creates a space of humanity for humanity's sake — not so that we are loved or followed [#1634].
5. Transparency of intentions. Where it is analysis, personal experience, advertising, or a request — the difference must be explicit. Opacity of intentions is one of the most toxic forms of media influence: it creates a feeling of trust and then uses it for one's own aims [#1634].
6. Responsibility for consequences. Every word is an action. In the era of post-truth and a vulnerable information environment, any post can raise anxiety or push someone toward a dangerous decision. Ethics is the ability to ask oneself: "«Как мой текст может повлиять? Не причиню ли я вреда?»" (How might my text affect people? Won't I cause harm?) [#1634].
7. Trauma-informedness. The audience is alive and heterogeneous: it includes people with neurodivergence, PTSD, depression, anxiety disorders, fresh losses. An ethical author takes this into account and knows that certain images, phrases and intonations can retraumatize [#1634].
8. Respect for the audience's subjectivity (agency). The reader is not a resource for reach growth nor "глина" (clay) for forming our identity. The ethical stance is "«Я вижу в читателе самостоятельного, взрослого, знающего человека»" (I see the reader as an independent, adult, knowledgeable person). This means refusing the cult of personality, constant self-idealization and the inner attitude "«я знаю, как вам жить»" (I know how you should live) [#1634].

#### Trust hygiene against imitation of expertise
Purpose: a reader's defence against popular texts that "look like knowledge" [#1727].

The phenomenon. The author describes reading yet another post by a blogger who daily produces popular analytics about the world: seemingly smart, neatly assembled, with references to research, creating a feeling of understanding and an urge to save and forward it. On closer analysis "everything breaks": generalizations, not-quite-correct conclusions, important doubts omitted. Not because the author lies or consciously manipulates, but because in the attention economy everything that helps hold attention is exploited ever harder — for example scientific style and simplicity [#1727].

Why it happens ("Ваши лайки — их работа" — Your likes are their job). For many people, likeable, forwardable texts are now daily work, and the wider the audience, the more money. Pace and depth rarely go together, yet one has to post more and faster. Even a sincere author who does read research physically cannot each time dive into context, unpack limitations, and hold contradictions and uncertainty in mind — especially without, for example, basic psychological education, when their job is writing scripts but the blog must be about the human being. Complex, probabilistic, nonlinear processes become a neat story with a beginning, middle and conclusion. Research that actually says "в среднем" (on average), "при прочих равных" (all else being equal) and "с большой вариативностью" (with large variability) starts sounding like universal rules in retelling. "Это не ложь, но и не вся правда." (It's not a lie, but not the whole truth either) — just the part that best holds attention [#1727].

Real expertise looks unimpressive. Social networks barely tolerate the doubts, caveats and uncertainty typical of real expertise; a text spreads more easily if it gives the feeling that everything is clear and predictable, that there is a key and a mechanism. Real expertise has more pauses, more "it depends", various kinds of caution and far fewer promises [#1727].

What to do — steps:
1. Don't wage war on bloggers or expose them from a moral position. Feedback is still legitimate (the author sometimes writes it in comments himself), but it is more constructive to develop trust hygiene — learning to notice where a text gives a feeling of understanding but does not withstand careful checking [#1727].
2. Check the author's credentials. Look into and know whether the author has real experience, what their expertise is, and where their competence ends [#1727].
3. Verify important claims, including with AI — "не как оракула, а как инструмента" (not as an oracle but as a tool) that helps reveal limitations, generalizations and oversimplifications [#1727].
4. Notice your own need. We consume clear, quasi-scientific texts so easily not because we are naive or cannot think, but because we are tired and overloaded. In a crazy world we want simple supports; scientific language and clear schemes create authority for the blogger and confidence for the reader. This lands especially well on topics of body, aging, health and the future — where there is much anxiety and little real understanding. It may be worth noticing what we really seek in such texts and what we actually find [#1727].

#### Four payoffs of author-elevating (narcissistic) content
Purpose: explains why authors write "narcissistically infected" content — not only from a probable structural predisposition to harmful narcissism, but simply "because it works. It's unethical, but who cares?" Content that elevates the author gives these benefits [#1750]:
1. Sense of own significance — "I'm not just a participant, I'm the one who got there earlier/better than others" [#1750].
2. Higher conversion — people subscribe, buy and repost better when their fear of falling behind is touched [#1750].
3. Symbolic power — the author becomes not just an expert but a gatekeeper figure granting access to something "better" [#1750].
4. Author's self-regulation — sometimes a way for the author to convince himself he is in control, needed, ahead, exceptional; then the audience is used as a mirror [#1750].

#### Healthy question after reading status-driven content
Purpose: a self-check when a post leaves you anxious or feeling deficient [#1750].
1. Notice the state. The author repeatedly catches himself in the state "автор сверху, а я снизу" (the author above, me below): he has understood, I can't keep up; he is on a new level, I'm not fast/advanced/cool enough [#1750].
2. Don't stop at your own feelings. This can be attributed to envy, narcissistic vulnerability, fear of falling behind — but also ask: what exactly is happening here? How are these statements and the relationship with the audience built? "Кому выгодно, чтобы я чувствовал себя отстающим?" (Who benefits from my feeling behind?) [#1750].
3. Trust the signal. "если мне кажется, то мне не кажется" (if it seems so to me, it isn't just seeming) — the author says he hears narcissistic infection in content fairly accurately, which means someone is trying to use him [#1750].
4. Slow down and remember simplification. If you notice yourself falling into anxiety and a sense of deficit, remember that "superior" rhetoric almost always radically simplifies life [#1750].
5. Ask the healthy question: not only why I envy or am triggered, but what exactly in me was touched and who benefits from the deficit in this relationship being on my side. Very often the answer: my emotions are activated in contact with content deliberately organized around superiority, lack and status struggle [#1750].

#### Six signs of "ethical terror" (from the linked article by Daniel Ost.)
Purpose: the linked Telegraph longread «Террор этики и этика террора» (author signed "Daniel Ost.") — the article the channel author critiques in #1331 — defines ethical terror and lists how it looks. These are the article author's claims, not the channel author's; the channel author's rebuttal is in *"Terror of ethics and ethics of terror": critique of the anti-new-ethics article* [#1331].

Definition. "Этический террор — систематическое использование моральных аргументов не для улучшения этической ситуации в обществе, а для подавления инакомыслия и установления контроля над дискурсом." (Ethical terror is the systematic use of moral arguments not to improve the ethical situation in society but to suppress dissent and establish control over discourse.) Applied to psychology and psychotherapy specifically, it is when rules created for the psychologist's office (which frame the therapeutic process) suddenly become general rules for the life and interaction of all people [#1331].

1. Absolutization of morality. The "correct" position is presented as the only possible moral point of view; any disagreement is automatically labelled an ethical crime [#1331].
2. Blurring of distinctions. Minor violations or even merely unfortunate wording are equated with serious violations. The article's example: "Его неудачная шутка — это такое же насилие, как физическое нападение" (His failed joke is the same violence as a physical assault) [#1331].
3. Collective guilt. Responsibility is extended to all members of the group the "violator" belongs to — for example, all men are declared accomplices of one man's actions [#1331].
4. Immunity to context. Any contextual factors, historical circumstances or cultural specifics are ignored; the same standards are applied to events of different eras [#1331].
5. Demand for ritual repentance. "Violators" are required not simply to apologize but to perform public self-abasement according to a set script [#1331].
6. The "стрелочка не поворачивается" principle (the arrow doesn't turn around). A fundamental hypocrisy in which actions condemned in opponents are considered permissible and even virtuous when committed by the "right" people: insults, dehumanization, aggression and bullying become "righteous anger" or "necessary resistance" when aimed at the "wrong" side [#1331].

The article's overall reading: ethical terror uses the same mechanisms as totalitarian systems — searching for enemies, public accusations, forced confessions of guilt ("Только что на партсобрания не вызывают" — only they don't summon you to party meetings), now under the cover of protecting vulnerable groups or fighting for justice. Instead of dialogue, mutual understanding and gradual improvement of society comes a striving for ideological purity and punishment of the "wrong-thinking". Like any terror it leads to fear, self-censorship and degradation of public discourse — "К страху сказать "Небо синее, а трава зеленая"" (to fear of saying "the sky is blue and the grass is green") [#1331].

#### Seven therapeutic principles and their incorrect transfer to public life (linked article)
Purpose: the article's core argument (section 1, "on mixing life, social processes and therapeutic ethics"): principles that are professional, trained and necessary inside therapy are transferred into public ethics. Methods developed for the controlled environment of the therapy room — trained specialists working with clients who came voluntarily — are forcibly introduced into the broad social context. This "терапевтизации" (therapeutization, as the article puts it in the genitive) of public morality turns healing instruments into weapons of ideological struggle; the article finds it especially alarming that the transfer is often carried out by mental-health professionals themselves, who should understand the contextual limits of their methods [#1331].

1. Unconditional acceptance and validation. In therapy: recognizing all the client's experiences, words and assessments as meaningful, without moral judgment. Incorrect transfer: "Вы обязаны верить мне на слово и не имеете права сомневаться в моей интерпретации событий" (You are obliged to take my word and have no right to doubt my interpretation of events) [#1331].
2. Priority of subjective experience. In therapy: "what matters is not what objectively happened but how you experienced it". Incorrect transfer: "my subjective trauma cancels others' right to an objective investigation of the facts" [#1331].
3. The "safe space" concept. In therapy: creating an environment where the client can express any thoughts without fear of judgment. Incorrect transfer: demanding that the whole public space be turned into a zone free of triggers and uncomfortable opinions [#1331].
4. Taboo on retraumatization. In therapy: careful handling of the client's traumatic experience. Incorrect transfer: "any challenge to my position is retraumatization and violence" [#1331].
5. Therapeutic neutrality. In therapy: refraining from moral assessments for the sake of the therapeutic process. Incorrect transfer: demanding that society give up moral judgments of destructive behaviour [#1331].
6. Working with splitting. In therapy: temporarily accepting black-and-white thinking as a stage of healing. Incorrect transfer: encouraging a Manichaean division of the world into "good us" and "evil others" in public discourse [#1331].
7. Replacing legal mechanisms with a therapeutic position. In therapy: focus on psychological aspects and subjective perception rather than legal responsibility. Incorrect transfer: replacing legal procedures based on the presumption of innocence and requiring evidence with emotionally coloured public accusations — "Не важно, что говорит закон — он чувствует себя жертвой, значит, преступление было совершено" (It doesn't matter what the law says — he feels like a victim, so a crime was committed) [#1331].

Why acceptance in therapy is a tactic, not agreement. The therapist's "unconditional acceptance" includes all of the above but does not mean genuine agreement with destructive beliefs, explosive affect, manic delusion or the endless arsenal of distortions and defence mechanisms — it is a conscious therapeutic tactic. If the therapist temporarily refrains from confronting, for example, the client's splitting ("everyone is bad" vs "I'm the victim"), idealization or projection, it is to strengthen the therapeutic alliance and create a safe environment for exploration. This benevolent-neutral position lets the client fully reveal their inner picture of the world without fear of judgment, so that later, once enough trust has formed, careful restructuring of dysfunctional patterns of thinking, behaviour or affect regulation can begin. Therapeutic ethics was made for the office: strict boundaries in which the specialist helps the client cope with trauma without imposing their own values [#1331].

What happens outside the office. Taken into the public field, these rules produce "катастрофа" (catastrophe): aggression instead of healing, moral terror instead of dialogue. Example: demanding that "triggering" topics be avoided in public discussions (as in therapy) kills the possibility of honest conversation about problems. In the office you gradually learn to meet pain; in the real world you are offered not to meet it but to flee — ban, hush up, erase. The article's analogies: "Это как лечить аллергию, вырубая все деревья в городе" (it's like treating an allergy by cutting down all the trees in the city); and sterility — a surgical operation needs sterility, but sterility in everyday life is not only unattainable but harmful; "Стерильно в вакууме — но там и жизни нет" (It's sterile in a vacuum — but there's no life there either). Life outside therapy is a non-sterile zone [#1331].

#### Countermeasures to "ethical terror" (linked article, section 6)
Purpose: the article's answer to "what to oppose to this devilish machine". Article author's prescriptions [#1331]:
1. Clear separation. Therapy is for personal healing; justice is for objective legal process. "Нельзя лечить общество как пациента." (You can't treat society like a patient.) [#1331]
2. Demand evidence. If someone accuses another of "psychological violence", they must show concrete harm rather than hide behind a vague "I feel that way" [#1331].
3. Refuse to reward hysterics. When rudeness toward you is disguised as "sincere expression of emotions", call it rudeness [#1331].
4. Return words to their original meaning. In a world where anyone can declare themselves a victim and another an executioner: yes, trauma exists; yes, support is needed; but sick ideas must not be allowed to infect everyone, turning humanity into "сборище обиженных тиранов" (a gathering of offended tyrants) [#1331].
5. Rely on rational grounds of discussion — a return to the fundamental principles of critical thinking and open dialogue as the way to resist distributed "ethical" censorship [#1331].
6. Systematically expose double standards. Document cases where identical actions get radically different assessments depending on the ideological affiliation of the actor; this reveals the selectivity of moral outrage and makes visible the hidden ideological frame posing as universal ethical principles [#1331].
7. Use historical memory. Draw parallels between modern censorship and its historical predecessors — e.g. show the methodological similarity between Soviet indexes of banned literature and today's lists of "problematic" expressions — to break the illusion of uniqueness and moral impeccability of current practices and return the discussion to the eternal opposition of free thought and dogmatic control [#1331].
8. Psychological steadfastness. Develop the ability to withstand social pressure and emotional attacks while staying loyal to rational discussion; where conformism has become a guarantee of safety, independent thinking and readiness to defend the right to doubt, even against an imagined moral consensus, become an act of civic courage [#1331].
9. Revive personal responsibility. A balance between compassion and demandingness, between recognizing others' pain and refusing to weaponize it; learning again to distinguish real threats from the inevitable discomfort of growth; putting the exchange of ideas, not a competition of traumas, at the centre of public discourse. Genuine care for mental health means not avoiding everything potentially "triggering" but developing inner resilience to meet life in all its complexity and contradiction [#1331].

#### Power inflation model of elite abuse
Purpose: the author's psychological reading of the Epstein case — what happens to the psyche of people ready to use children as objects of sexual action and hide it for years relying on their position [#1699].
1. Selection. Citing psychological research, the author states that big money and power disproportionately often end up with people with pronounced narcissistic and psychopathic traits. This does not mean all influential people are like this, but systems of power and capital select and amplify these qualities, forming a "bubble" of lowered empathy, instrumental treatment of others and a sense of one's own exceptionality [#1699].
2. Power as goal. It is a world of competition and dominance where power is not a side effect but the goal; as it expands, one wants to get rid of any limits — moral or legal [#1699].
3. Inflation. Like any stimulus, power, when there is a lot of it, stops feeling as good as before; a search begins for more absolute forms. Some want a Nobel Prize, some want to redraw the geopolitical map [#1699].
4. Omnipotence through dependent eyes. Most of them want to be looked at by eyes that are fully dependent, lacking subjectivity and unable to resist. It is less about sex than about the experience of omnipotence "так, между делом" (just in passing). Sex becomes the language of power, and the victim's age is not an accidental detail but the key element of the whole construction of pleasure [#1699].

#### Displaced-aggression mechanism of public harassment (травля)
Purpose: the author's explanation of why the reaction to an innocuous statement about intuition was so violent — "Одним страхом новизны силу этой реакции, однако, не объяснить." (The strength of this reaction, however, cannot be explained by fear of novelty alone.) It adds to the earlier *Survival-driven victim-blaming mechanism* (msg #941), which explains siding with a strong aggressor; this one explains how a group chooses a target and why the target is often someone embodying what the attackers are denied [#1979].

1. Accumulated aggression. The author states that "В русскоязычном обществе накоплено колоссальное количество агрессии" (a colossal amount of aggression has accumulated in Russian-speaking society) [#1979].
2. Its origin is suppressed pain from systemic violence. The aggression comes from pain which people living for a long time inside all-pervading systemic violence are forced to suppress in themselves for years, because they have no way of presenting it to those who actually cause it [#1979].
3. The psyche looks for a detour. Under such conditions the psyche seeks a workaround and finds a figure that is noticeable and at the same time safe to strike ("фигуру заметную и при этом безопасную для удара") [#1979].
4. Target selection. A woman, a mother of two, who speaks openly about feelings from a high position fits this role "почти идеально" (almost perfectly): she is visible because of her status, yet attacking her carries no risk, unlike confronting the real sources of violence [#1979].
5. Envy-like trigger: she embodies what others cannot allow themselves. Zolotukhina becomes a trigger also because she embodies bodily presence, sensuality, intuition, openness and compassion — things many people cannot permit themselves. When someone nearby lives that openly, one's own constriction ("зажатость") becomes more noticeable and hurts more, and the attack is a way to make that pain stop [#1979].

Relation to the existing file: like the earlier victim-blaming mechanism, the author places the cause in collective conditions (systemic violence, a society used to expecting bad things from change) rather than in individual malice — which is why he ends by asking for compassion toward the attackers [#1979].

#### Collapse of rationality in a pile-on
Purpose: describes what happens to argument once harassment is under way. The paradox, according to the author, is that "перестает работать как раз та рациональность, от имени которой ведется атака" (the very rationality in whose name the attack is conducted stops working) — the attack on Zolotukhina was framed as defending data against "vibes", yet it abandons evidence itself. Its three features [#1979]:
1. Words are torn from context — a single phrase about what she does when there is no clear answer in her head becomes a general management doctrine [#1979].
2. The person is credited with things they did not say — e.g. that a company should be run on intuition alone, which she nowhere stated [#1979].
3. No clarification can be heard any more — because "спор давно идет не о фактах" (the dispute has long since stopped being about facts); correcting the record does not work when the energy behind the attack is displaced pain [#1979].

This links to the existing file's observation about the Oxxxymiron case that calling an investigation "yellow press" devalues the possibility of dialogue (msg #1651): in both cases the author describes a move away from substance, here performed by a crowd rather than by a powerful individual [#1979].

#### The author's call to the community

### Detailed insights

#### Oxxxymiron and Gordon: consent, silence and defending the abuser
- Societies without consent. The author wrote about Oxxxymiron and Gordon after reading comments. In some societies the concept of consent is completely absent: the average person does not understand how to be in sexual relationships so that it is not, or does not "suddenly" become, violence. When the "suddenly" happens, it is very hard for him to accept responsibility for the pain, because those around will always say "Какое насилие?! Все нормально, это она сама виновата" (What violence?! Everything's fine, she's to blame herself), and the state will chime in about enemies plotting to destroy centuries-old traditions of patriarchy [#1325].
- Narcissistic defence vs. empathy. When situations repeatedly poke this ignorance, the author suggests pausing to consider that someone on the other side may be in pain — that we don't understand something but won't look into it because of our ego — instead of immediately falling into narcissistic defence and shouting about a "new ethics" imposed artificially, as if "we are the centre of the planet" [#1325].
- Why obvious things become non-obvious. In a society of rigid hierarchies, "которые всегда основаны на насилии" (which are always based on violence), where one collective trauma follows another and healing public dialogue is obstructed by politics, it is unsurprising that clear things become unclear: that it is not normal to yell at employees, beat your wife, or have sex with a 16-year-old when you are 50. "*Всегда же нормально было.*" (It was always normal, wasn't it.) The author's answer: no, it was always abnormal; there was simply even less strength to speak about it. Every case voiced aloud and every text written returns that strength and draws clearer boundaries between black and white [#1325].
- Why the axiom holds. A child is under the power of authority and not fully free before an adult; a child cannot fully understand the consequences of their actions and take responsibility for them. "Это не придуманная где-то мораль, а базовая детская психология." (This is not morality invented somewhere, but basic child psychology.) [#1325]
- Others are not objects. At home, at work and in sex there are living people before us, not objects of our manipulation — objects of family, labour or sexual relationships. They are as alive as we are, but statuses, positions and opportunities are not always equal. Seeing this is "никакая не новая этика, а просто этика здорового человека" (no new ethics at all, just the ethics of a healthy person) — needed if we want violence to be seen as violence and war not to turn out to be peace [#1325].
- Desired norm. The author hopes apologies and at least attempts to restore losses caused by violence will one day become the norm — instead of silence in response to serious accusations (Oxxxymiron) or dull attempts to explain everything by politics (Gordon) [#1325].
- Defending the artist = defending blurring the line. Krasilnikova's investigation had several women describe grooming and sexualized violence by Oxxxymiron when they were teenagers and he was an adult with power, status and cultural influence. When defence of the artist starts — citing talent, era or cultural significance — it in fact defends the opposite thesis: that this boundary can be blurred for some reason [#1651].
- Non-response as position. After the investigation there was no substantive answer; Miron rather disappeared from public view. Half a year later a new album came out — formally a music release, factually a public gesture. Where the conversation has already happened, absence of a response becomes a position [#1651].
- The "yellow press" dismissal. The album contains no direct comment, only an ironic mention of "желтуха" (jaundice), read as a hint at the yellow press. The author calls this the key moment: Krasilnikova's investigation is certainly not yellow press — repeated unsuccessful attempts to get comment, openness to dialogue, professional methodology; "Так жёлтая пресса не работает." (That's not how the yellow press works.) Calling it yellowness means "не вступать в разговор по существу, а обесценить саму возможность диалога" (not entering a substantive conversation but devaluing the very possibility of dialogue) — a move the author says is very familiar [#1651].
- Women defending the figure = defending their own experience. A significant part of the defensive rhetoric came from women: "со мной так было, и в этом не было ничего страшного" (it happened to me and there was nothing terrible about it). Talk of sex in stairwells, being used, blurred boundaries and adolescence was presented as ordinary. Here defence of the figure turned into defence of one's own experience, even if it was in essence an experience of violence. The author calls this painful: it is no longer about one person but a deeply embedded cultural norm in which female vulnerability and being used are considered natural. Acknowledging violence means acknowledging not only someone else's guilt but one's own trauma and one's own lack of choice in the past — for many almost psychologically unbearable. So defence of violence often masks as defence of agency — "я сама так решила, мне было нормально" (I decided it myself, it was fine for me) — but this logic reproduces the systemic order in which responsibility again shifts from the figure of power onto the more vulnerable [#1651].
- A related example in the ethics-terror post: someone writes a post *in support of* Oxxxymiron because "а что такого, я сама так делала!" (so what, I did that myself!), then is sincerely surprised that commenters explain their "normal" need not be the same for everyone [#1331].

#### Cancel culture as hopeful bottom-up regulation
Summing up: an adult man with power does not enter dialogue, does not articulate boundaries and takes no responsibility, demonstrating instead well-being, distance and indifference. Although the author says he generally pays more attention to system and culture than to the person, in 2025 this strategy "выглядит откровенно слабо" (looks frankly weak). Where an adult reaction is absent and institutions don't work, a different mechanism appears — social regulation from below. The album's non-acceptance is "не истерия и не заговор, а коллективный ответ на отсутствие ясной рефлексии" (not hysteria or a conspiracy, but a collective response to the absence of clear reflection). After the Dolina case it is no longer isolated: despite an authoritarian background and shrinking political space, a modern ethical discourse and civic agency are forming in the Russian-speaking environment. At least part of society takes leadership and says: "если ты не готов вступать в разговор об ответственности, мы не готовы делать вид, что ничего не произошло" (if you're not ready to talk about responsibility, we're not ready to pretend nothing happened) — possibly the most important outcome [#1651].

#### Why the Epstein story surfaced at all
The author is interested not in the list of names but in what the case reveals about society: why the rich and influential get drawn into such practices, and why such stories come out despite showing the uncomfortable truth of "worlds where everything is allowed". The same system that for decades let such practices exist through money, status and untouchability at some point can no longer fully hide them — not because it becomes just, but because the balance of forces shifts, protection disappears, and information starts living its own life. American society and its power system are built so they sometimes cannot keep such things under the rug: power is fragmented, and media live in a logic of struggle rather than broadcasting a single ideology. The very logic of the story's appearance indicates that at its centre is not sexuality but power — how it is experienced and how it distorts a person. When elites war publicly, dragging out dirty laundry, their psychological portrait becomes more visible, along with the real problems of modern society rather than an attention-scattering agenda. Remove the noise of culture wars, identity debates and images of an external enemy, and the question of distribution of power and its limits comes to the fore — and how power, as the ability to influence and avoid consequences, gradually changes human appearance so that violence becomes routine. Attention returns, if briefly, to why power is so unevenly distributed, so little constrains it, and the distortions this almost inevitably produces [#1699].

#### "Terror of ethics and ethics of terror": critique of the anti-new-ethics article
The author responds to a widely shared article claiming new ethics and trauma-informedness create oppression and dictatorship; under Trump this is the official new discourse, so such things can now be said aloud joyfully [#1331]. The article's six theses, as the author summarizes them:
1. Ethical terror is the suppression of dissent through moral arguments for ideological control, not for improving society [#1331].
2. A dangerous substitution: principles of therapeutic ethics are transferred into the broad public field, creating absurd standards of behaviour [#1331].
3. The modern "ethics of trauma" turns trauma into an instrument of power rather than an object of careful attention and healing [#1331].
4. Substituting ideology for therapy breeds a society of infantile, vulnerable and intolerant people incapable of healthy dialogue [#1331].
5. Modern mechanisms for suppressing dissent resemble Soviet methods, only instead of a repressive apparatus there is "public morality" and "cancel culture" [#1331].
6. "Ethical terror" makes people weaker, depriving them of psychological resilience and the ability to cope with real difficulties [#1331].

The author's response:
- Wrong context. He understands the relief a New Yorker or San Franciscan might feel, but asks what people in St Petersburg and Saratov are celebrating: "где именно вы сталкиваетесь с этими тенденциями, кроме интернета?" (where exactly do you encounter these trends, besides the internet?) There is no articulate, influential group in Russia seriously promoting new ethics. Domestic violence or sexual exploitation of secretaries outside the Garden Ring and not involving celebrities interests few people, let alone gender-neutral pronouns or discrimination of small peoples. Influential groups rather legitimize violence at home and at work — not to mention prisons and the army [#1331].
- Basic ethics first. "Нам бы хотя бы базовую, конвенциональную этику поднять" (We'd do well to raise at least basic, conventional ethics) — the commonplaces of human wisdom: respect each other, don't steal, don't kill, don't rape. Most people practically never encounter new ethics in real life enough for it to "get on their nerves" [#1331].
- Partial agreement. The article has important and fair theses, and also generalizations and misunderstanding of new ethics, deliberate or accidental. He offers to analyze it as a representative of the helping professions and of a culture for raising ethical standards — if someone really needs that, rather than just wanting to act out fear and anger that someone questioned habitual hierarchies of violence [#1331].
- The article commits what it accuses. When the author of the article calls any manifestation of care for safety "ethical terror", he erases contexts and nuances, turning isolated extremes into a supposed common practice — "это тоже террор, только антиэтический" (this is also terror, only anti-ethical). Such generalizations and tilting at windmills are fuel for hatred and division [#1331].
- How to seek truth. Don't limit yourself to articles with only one point of view, often based on local cases; come to living representatives and discuss in debates. He says it is hard to comment on new ethics and trauma-informedness until you have properly understood them [#1331].

#### The linked article in full: further arguments of «Террор этики и этика террора»
The channel author's post links to this Telegraph longread by Daniel Ost.; its frameworks are written out above (Six signs of "ethical terror"*, *Seven therapeutic principles and their incorrect transfer*, *Countermeasures to "ethical terror"). Its remaining arguments, all the article author's claims, are below [#1331].

"Ethics of trauma" (section 2). The article calls the manipulation of the concept of trauma especially telling. In the therapy room the client's subjective experience is taken seriously, but in public discussion one cannot forbid critical analysis by appealing to the indisputability of someone's trauma. When "trauma" becomes a shield against all criticism and professionals use their status to suppress rather than improve dialogue, that is toxic "therapeutization" of public space — not protection of victims but instrumentalization and generalization of therapeutic concepts to strengthen certain groups' power. Psychologists, who should understand the difference between therapeutic and public ethics, end up in the vanguard of this mixing, becoming "новых инквизиторов" (new inquisitors) of a fashionable humanitarian church [#1331].
- The paradox: "чем активнее мы говорим о психическом здоровье, тем патологичнее становится общественное состояние" (the more actively we talk about mental health, the more pathological the state of society becomes). Reason given: real therapy helps accept life in all its complexity, including pain and disappointment, but popular culture (partly via activist therapists) has turned it into a cult of safety whose main goal is avoiding discomfort at any cost [#1331].
- Three resulting distortions: (1) "инфантильный активизм" (infantile activism) — people demand that the world solve their personal problems; (2) "коллективный нарциссизм" (collective narcissism) — groups compete for the status of the most vulnerable, claiming the right to special treatment; (3) "параноидальная бдительность" (paranoid vigilance) — constant searching for hidden insults in neutral words [#1331].
- Consequence for the psyche. The system creates not a healthy society but one of dependent, unhappy people obsessed with revenge and rage, where one learns to avoid difficulties rather than overcome them, to cut off "wrong" people rather than build relationships through dialogue, to destroy rather than create, to shout and accuse rather than argue. A psyche raised in the cult of safety becomes fragile, "как иммунитет ребёнка, выросшего в стерильной комнате" (like the immunity of a child raised in a sterile room) who falls ill at the first contact with the "reality" of a dirty stairwell [#1331].
- Digital executions. Under slogans such as "Мы боремся за психологическую безопасность!" (We fight for psychological safety!) real digital executions unfold. The article calls this a vivid sign of left ideology ("the left hand doesn't know what the right is doing": a lecture against violence is followed by explosive insults and sweeping accusations against whole groups). A person who made a careless (or unwise) statement is declared an "abuser", "narcissist", "rapist" or "-phobe" not because they caused real harm but because their opinion did not fit the new morality in which feelings (more often victims') matter more than facts, reality or common sense; they are bullied, insulted and required to perform "public repentance", all framed as "restoring justice" [#1331].
- Trauma as currency. "Травма превращается в валюту: чем уникальнее ваша боль, тем выше социальный статус." (Trauma turns into currency: the more unique your pain, the higher your social status.) People increasingly collect diagnoses like trophies [#1331].
- Rudeness justified as "emotional discharge". Example: a stranger online writes "Сдохни, мудак!" (Drop dead, jerk!) and, when reproached, answers "Это моя реакция на вашу токсичность!" (This is my reaction to your toxicity!) or "Don't tell me what to feel and how to speak!" If a person declares a trauma — real or imagined — they get carte blanche for any statement, even calls for destroying individuals or groups [#1331].
- Discomfort is needed. Progress requires disputes and even discomfort; if everything that "wounds" someone is banned, dialogue itself is strangled, "Потому что "всех" ранит "все" и каждого — свое" (because "everything" wounds "everyone", and each person by their own thing) [#1331].

Soviet parallels and the panopticon (section 3). The article argues that mechanisms for suppressing dissent have transformed over the last century and are especially alarming in post-Soviet countries, where decades taught people to obey rather than think. In the USSR it was a centralized state machine with physical coercion — from shootings and the Gulag to psychiatric clinics and professional marginalization; the system was "transparent in its opacity": everyone knew which topics to keep quiet about ("kitchen jokes"). Today it is replaced by distributed ideological control through "cancellation" and social isolation: "Не нужен Главлит, когда есть армия активистов" (No need for Glavlit [the Soviet censorship body] when there's an army of activists), no need for the KGB when there are vigilant activists of the new left [#1331].
- In the USSR dissent was "treated" in psychiatric clinics (example: Novodvorskaya and the notorious "sluggish schizophrenia" diagnosis); now the methods are softer but the logic is the same — declare inconvenient thoughts a "dangerous illness" and their holders contagious and abnormal. Instead of punitive psychiatry, the language of "traumas" and "triggers"; instead of forced treatment, mass cancel culture; instead of censorship by "lists", censorship by "best intentions" — e.g. a ban on discussing problems because they "stigmatize", or refusing to criticize ideas on grounds of "emotional safety" [#1331].
- Shared techniques with Soviet censorship: lists of forbidden words and concepts, demands for ritual self-condemnation, public "debriefings", a cult of ideological purity. Difference: in the USSR one at least knew one was resisting an external repressive force; today cancellation masquerades as a spontaneous impulse of public morality. Then the intelligentsia resisted state pressure; today it often becomes its conductor, voluntarily patrolling the boundaries of acceptable discourse. A person who lost a job or reputation over a "wrong" joke or a decade-old statement cannot even identify their executioner, because the decision was taken not in offices or Central Committee meetings but by the collective action of "the public" [#1331].
- Panopticon. The article invokes Michel Foucault's analysis of the panopticon — a prison where inmates are under constant potential observation without knowing when they are watched, so the external guard becomes unnecessary as the prisoner takes over the function of surveillance. Today's culture of ideological vigilance realizes this at unprecedented scale: everyone is potentially visible, every word can be taken out of context, every old statement can resurface. "Внутренний тюремщик ликует!" (The inner jailer rejoices!) [#1331]
- Internalized censorship is called the main triumph: self-censorship reflexes fire before a thought becomes a word. Unlike Soviet or Nazi systems, where thought was driven underground but inner freedom survived, the new regime colonizes the very process of thinking; ritual politically-correct formulas and avoidance of "problematic" topics are only visible symptoms of thinking pre-adapted to presumed ideological limits. It is more efficient to make a person produce an inner overseer than to build camps and maintain an army of censors. Result: society hushes up real conflicts instead of resolving them — fear of speaking, thinking, being oneself. The article quotes E. Shulman: "иметь и высказывать свое мнение — очень дорогое удовольствие" (having and voicing your own opinion is a very expensive pleasure); not everyone is ready to lose resources, influence, recognition, shelter or family for the truth [#1331].

Paradoxes and metamorphoses (section 4).
- Ethical terror does not make people stronger: the more society shields itself from the slightest discomfort, the more helpless it becomes before real crises; the more activism protects us from "psychological threats", the weaker our psyche. The article claims depression and anxiety statistics grow precisely in countries where the fight for "psychological safety" has peaked, and hedges the cause with "возможно" (perhaps): a person taught from childhood to see "trauma" in every uncomfortable conversation cannot adapt to a life where conflict and disappointment are inevitable. No figures or sources are given [#1331].
- Swimming analogy: imagine everyone stopped teaching children to swim so they "wouldn't be scared of water" — a generation that drowns in a puddle. Likewise, declaring "harmful" any ideas that cause doubt or discomfort raises people unable to tell truth from lies or rationality from manipulation. "Проблемы не исчезают от запрета на их обсуждение." (Problems don't disappear when discussing them is banned.) Seeing "psychological violence" behind every dispute about politics or morality infantilizes everyone; an adult, healthy person can survive offence and unpleasant criticism, and depriving them of this "for their own good" steals the dignity of independent choice [#1331].
- Spill-over into justice: someone fired without evidence because another "felt unsafe" around them, or an activist jailed for texts called "psychological violence against society" — the article calls this a new form of totalitarianism where it is enough to declare dissenters "sources of collective trauma" and the crowd tears them apart "in the name of healing" [#1331].
- Cargo cult of activism. Part of the distortion comes from mechanically transferring Western activist practices and terminology into different socio-cultural contexts — like Pacific islanders building bamboo radio towers to attract cargo planes, activists reproduce the external attributes and vocabulary of American social movements without understanding their roots, applying concepts grown from the specific postcolonial and racial history of the US to societies with radically different histories of trauma and conflict. In trying to decolonize thinking they are colonized by foreign categories: talking about "microaggression" in countries that lived through the Gulag, about "privilege" where memory of mass terror is alive, applying US-centric race concepts to different structures of social division. The article calls this a crisis of intellectual independence, where the borrowed toolkit replaces analysis instead of serving it [#1331].
- Social networks as the ideal environment. Algorithms reward scandal (louder outrage, more likes and reposts); hashtags like #cancelculture or #shame become "цифровыми вилами" (digital pitchforks); anonymity gives a sense of impunity to insult, threaten and spread lies under the mask of "fighting for justice". Example given: the harassment of J. K. Rowling and attempts to break into her home after her Twitter posts — "at home, as a crowd, with weapons"; the article sarcastically suggests some psychologists would defend this as letting the oppressed seek justice. Main problem: substitution of reality — no half-tones, you are an "angel" or a "demon"; the digital court denies the right to defence because screens do not convey intonation, context or sincere apology; "Человек превращается в мем — плоский и однозначный." (A person turns into a meme — flat and unambiguous.) [#1331]
- Distorted scale. When 100 accounts on Twitter or Facebook shout about a "gender apocalypse", it seems the whole world is burning, but it is often a handful of activists amplified by algorithms; the media take this virtual noise for "the voice of the people" and act as amplifiers. Platforms profit from bullying by selling ads to an aroused, engaged audience and then say they are "just a neutral tool" — "like selling petrol and matches in a forest during a drought and then being surprised by fires" [#1331].

Why people do it and why it works (section 5).
- Why people join: ethical terror gives an illusion of righteousness; attacking others as a defender, the aggressor feels like a hero, thinking hate is "therapy for the oppressed" and bullying is "restoring justice". The article calls it ordinary thirst for power and stupidity: "Такие люди не хотят диалога — они хотят трибунала." (Such people don't want dialogue — they want a tribunal.) They don't want to understand but to punish, enjoying the moment they can force someone to their knees and apologize [#1331].
- Why it works: it gives simple answers in a complex world — mock "it's not so clear-cut" instead of dealing with contradictions; call everyone who earns more "toxic" or "oppressors" instead of analyzing capitalism; blame "white heterosexual men" or WASPs instead of analyzing causes of inequality. "Это религия для нежелающих думать: не нужно менять систему — достаточно изменить язык." (It's a religion for those unwilling to think: no need to change the system — changing the language is enough.) Like any religion it breeds hypocrites who condemn "oppressors" by day and bully colleagues by night, since "emotional discharge" permits everything [#1331].
- Real victims lose. "Самое циничное, что настоящие жертвы реального насилия в этой системе только проигрывают." (The most cynical thing is that real victims of real violence only lose in this system.) When everyone shouts "I'm traumatized!", the voices of those who suffered real pain are lost in the noise; a society tired of hysterics starts doubting even objective crimes; reasonable people learn to stay silent [#1331].
- Root error: replacing humanism with "левой гуманитарщиной" (left-wing humanitarianism). Left activism that has captured psychotherapy imposes the idea that any discomfort is "violence of the system": sad or dislike work — capitalism's fault; quarrel with a male partner — "patriarchy"; dissatisfied with yourself — an "inner oppressor formed by white men". This denies personal responsibility, reducing everything to abstract "oppression"; since the bounds of "oppression" depend only on imagination, lists of enemies keep growing. Therapy that searches for external enemies instead of working with the client's real "I" betrays its purpose: it teaches complaining, blaming and demanding that the world adjust to your fears instead of giving tools for life [#1331].

Epilogue "in defence of reason" (section 7). Human history is a pendulum between freedom of thought and attempts to control it; today ideological walls are built of words, taboos and social algorithms rather than concrete and barbed wire, so technological freedom of communication combines with unprecedented pressure on its content. Genuine thinking has always found ways through — through the cracks of the Soviet system, medieval religious bans, inquisitors of all eras. Rational thinking becomes an act of moral resistance, "формой интеллектуального диссидентства" (a form of intellectual dissidence): commitment to facts in a post-truth world, logical consistency amid emotional manipulation, readiness to follow the argument wherever it leads — an ethics of intellectual honesty. The struggle is framed not as a conflict of political ideologies but between a dogmatic impulse that freezes thought and the living flame of reason that melts it. "Ибо мысль, как вода, всегда находит дорогу к морю свободы." (For thought, like water, always finds its way to the sea of freedom.) [#1331]

How the two texts relate. The channel author's six-thesis summary in #1331 is a condensation of this article. He agrees it has "important and fair theses" but objects that it generalizes isolated extremes (largely from US contexts) into a common practice and calls any care for safety "ethical terror" — i.e. the article's own sign 2 (blurring of distinctions) and sign 4 (immunity to context) are what he accuses it of. The article itself also attributes the problem partly to importing US activist frames into post-Soviet contexts (cargo cult), which overlaps with the channel author's point that Russia's problem is a lack of basic ethics rather than an excess of new ethics; the two disagree on whether the phenomenon is actually present at scale in the Russian-speaking world [#1331, article #1331].

#### Responsibility of public figures and bloggers to speak up
Written in the context of repression of LGBT people ("fighting one's own people", trying to instil fear in youth), when channels and people changed avatars to rainbows [#961]:
- Solidarity as a by-product. Manifested savagery leads not only to depression and fear; against everything it lets solidarity, support and visibility appear. The author does not believe most Russians are homophobic, though he knows sad cases, and is glad to see healthy involvement in neighbours' problems [#961].
- Non-participation escalates violence. Postponing statements, reposts, going into the streets eventually leads to ever more violence. Even if we are "in the little house" away from it all, the spiral works so that it eventually comes to everyone [#961].
- Influencers' responsibility. Mistakes are not scary; not drawing conclusions and not learning from them is bad. Popular figures who keep running Instagram and Telegram for thousands while fully ignoring violence and social problems — posting themselves in beautiful outfits, advertising goods — seem to the author too infantile and incongruent with reality; he is not against that life as such but unsubscribes from such accounts. Every person with a thousand followers is responsible for how they affect the information field: if whole groups are repressed and I post hotel photos and stay silent yesterday, today and tomorrow, I contribute to the situation continuing and worsening [#961].
- Understanding silence. Taking a position is always costly; when all is well you don't want to get involved, and audiences don't like reading the frightening and unpleasant. The author doesn't like it either but prefers to see and know, to stay in contact with reality and develop truly broad compassion. Activism is inconvenient for public figures on average; yet it should be made transparent that popular figures broadcasting only smiles, memes and a beautiful normal life "продают нам инфантилизм и создают неосознанное общество" (sell us infantilism and create an unconscious society) [#961].
- Deliberate moralizing. He acknowledges modern psychologized culture dislikes moralizing (no one is without sin; everyone is an adult), but explicitly chooses to moralize for the sake of a clear position: an adult is defined not by age but by behaving like an adult — here, taking responsibility for what happens in the society that lets us have friends, reputation and earn money, rather than pretending it doesn't concern you because you left, are above politics, below politics or anything else [#961].
- Collective problems need collective action. Common problems are solved exactly when we start solving them together — including when speaking up and coordinating toward social participation and civic governance becomes beneficial and necessary [#961].
- Consumer responsibility. As content consumers it matters which information field we support and which information we amplify; the author unsubscribes from "only pretty pictures" and from those who don't see their responsibility to show a social position exactly when it is most needed. "Каждое действие имеет значение." (Every action matters.) [#961]

#### Power and ethics in relationships of influence (the author's self-reflection)
Disclaimer from the author: the text is not an attempt to clarify a private situation but reflections on contact, power and professional disagreements in the field of inner work, where any word can become an act of influence and any unspoken move can lead to unexpected consequences [#1629].
- The case. It became clear the person had formed rather harsh conclusions about him from external manifestations and texts, without asking about his motivation and without letting him know the relationship had changed. When a person occupies a position of influence, their conclusions stop being mere opinion and become a gesture that sets an attitude — even if they don't think about it or don't want this role. "у слова в одной точке есть вес, которого нет в другой" (a word at one point carries weight it doesn't have at another). One-sided conclusions become not a private decision but an action that creates a certain pattern of reality for both sides [#1629].

#### Narcissistically infected content: definition and dynamics
- Context. The deeper the author goes into AI transformation, the more he thinks one must be attentive to information sources; in AI and entrepreneurship, speculation on emotions seems especially heated [#1750].
- Definition. Content "заражен логикой грандиозности, исключительности и скрытого обесценивания читателя" (infected with the logic of grandiosity, exclusivity and hidden devaluation of the reader). Sometimes a person really is just sharing useful information, but the form is built so that it becomes a hidden message: he is already at the next level, while you are still floundering somewhere down there [#1750].
- How it holds attention. Not through calm trust but through a mix of admiration, anxiety, shame and hope — micro-doses of hierarchical arousal. Such rhetoric presses on basic needs and wounds: the fear of being nobody, of missing the historical moment, shame of one's ordinariness, a deficit of inner support, and hope that somewhere there is a figure who has figured it out and can lead. Hence such channels evoke a strange mix of disgust and attraction: you dislike the energy but can't tear yourself away, because the psyche whispers that if you watch a little more you'll catch up and stop being insufficient. "Но чаще всего догоняешь не реальность, а чужую игру на сцене." (But most often you're catching up not with reality but with someone else's stage play.) [#1750]
- Symbols vs statistics. When someone writes they are 100 times faster, have already built an AI business, followed by a list of further achievements, it is usually a genre of self-presentation, not an accurate description of reality. Left out of the brackets: experience, team, failures, the psychological price of that pace, quality of results and long-term consequences. Only the symbol is broadcast publicly — I'm super-efficient, special, cool. "А символы становятся токсичными, когда, мы принимаем их за статистику." (And symbols become toxic when we take them for statistics.) People are sometimes so deceived by a character's popularity they don't notice the dynamic [#1750].
- What is offered is a relationship. Such content offers not just information but a relationship where the author is above and you below, with the promised chance to get closer; many blogs, personal brands and whole businesses are built on this hook [#1750].

#### The case: what happened and how it escalated
On 9 November an IT blogger, Digital Ninja, released a video about an interview with Yandex HR director Dasha Zolotukhina, in which she says that at moments when there is no clear answer in her head she listens to her body and notices what she feels. The author says he did not want to write about it at all — "зачем поддерживать вниманием булшит" (why feed bullshit with attention) — but changed his mind because of the escalation: within three days the single phrase was turned into the claim that one of the country's main HR leaders hires "по вайбику вместо данных" (by the vibe instead of data); then came "секта" (cult), "эйчарка" (a dismissive word for a female HR person), comparisons with Blinovskaya, and discussion of her appearance. The video gathered about a million views. The lesson the author draws from the sequence: distortion of a statement, then gendered and personal devaluation, then mass reach — the typical shape of a pile-on [#1979].

#### Intuition and the body in leadership: the author's position
The author insists that Zolotukhina never said a company should be run on intuition alone. What she describes is, in his words, "обычной современной позицией зрелого руководителя" (the ordinary modern stance of a mature leader): attention to the body, emotions and intuition as a *source of information* that *complements* analysis — not a replacement for data. In American leadership culture this has long been mainstream. The author frames the episode as the leading edge of a cultural shift that has reached the Russian-speaking world and met what the new always meets in a society accustomed to expecting bad things from change: rejection ("отторжение") [#1979].

#### Public harassment as violence and the price of leadership
The author says he personally worries for Zolotukhina: however much resilience one has, public harassment is violence that wounds ("публичная травля — это насилие, которое ранит"). At the same time he frames it as the nature of genuine leadership: you do and say what you consider true earlier than the environment is ready to accept it, and many great leaders paid an enormous price for truth. This is the author's opinion, not a cited finding [#1979].

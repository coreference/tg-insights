## Meaning, purpose and life direction

### What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's teaching on how a person finds and lives a purpose: moving from "must" to "want" motivation by consulting bodily sensations, seeing oneself as the author of one's life, recognising that external objects, money, status and even "meaningful" projects cannot fill an inner lack, answering nihilism, orienting by values and direction instead of yearly goals, rethinking the post-50 "third age", redefining the "big game", and finding transcendent tasks. It also includes a guest essay on art as a practice of self-remembering. According to the author, purpose is a matter of responsibility and adult attitude to life — without searching for one's right application, life is spent in vain [#6].

This part adds one reflective post (2026) about seeing one's present, relatively trouble-free time as rare luck and privilege, about the inevitability of illness, death and changing circumstances, and about what this finite time should be spent on. It extends the existing topic file's material on facing death and transcendent tasks (the birthday post on transcendent tasks and the "against nihilism" post) with a new angle: gratitude for one's current freedom and solidarity with those in hardship as the trigger for meaningful action. According to the author, without this awareness the time when we can really choose, grow and help is easily "slept through" [#1976].

### Core ideas

- Purpose is a responsibility, not a luxury. The author treats life purpose as part of an adult, responsible relationship to life; not thinking in terms of purpose is, in his view, a Soviet legacy (see *Purpose as adult responsibility*) [#6].
- Act from "want", checked in the body. Long-term outstanding results are questioned under "must" motivation; inner motivation grounded in pleasure and bodily feedback, deepened by meditation, gradually reveals boundaries, deep values and finally purpose (see *Want-vs-must self-inquiry*) [#20].
- We are the authors of our lives. Power holds us like a rope on an elephant only because we allow it; the fight is not for success or survival but against one's fear of changing the world and closedness to feelings [#96].
- Pleasure-chasing never ends dissatisfaction. Behind objects of desire lies a wish for fullness and connection with something bigger; that search must come first (see *Chris Wallis's seven points*) [#99].
- External achievements ≠ significance. Both money and "business with meaning" fail when driven by compensatory discomfort; what is needed is revising the concept "external achievements = my significance" and inner work on one's unique configuration (see *The cocktail of unique configuration*) [#458].
- The main question must be answered inside. Career switches into coaching or shamanism can be new projections; the real path is individuation — answering "who am I without the expectations of society or parents?" (see *Path of individuation*) [#568].
- Nihilism is a cover for fear and ego. Meaninglessness relieves us of responsibility; meaning is found empirically, not conceptually, by finding oneself and bearing the suffering of going against others' expectations [#569].
- Direction and values beat yearly goals. Goals ignore means, add stress, create rigidity, reinforce the achievement/deficit paradigm and rest on the past; a clear direction plus constant experiments is evolution's way of moving in uncertainty [#788, #790].
- After 50 comes a new maturation, not decline. The third age (50–75) is a new life period requiring self-redefinition and autonomous choice of meanings [#1402].
- The big game is about how, not what. Money, power and success are legitimate, but the real big game begins with deciding to live by one's own truth, free from the neurosis of compensating fear [#1446].
- Transcendent tasks give life significance. Facing death invites tasks that take us beyond the separate "I"; no transcendent task is universal [#1747].

- A calm period is a privilege, not a given. Being free of serious illness, deaths, dependants and urgent money emergencies is "a blessing" and great luck; it will not last forever, which makes this time precious [#1976].
- Finitude is universal. Humanity has defeated neither disease nor death nor the simple changeability of circumstances, from which a huge number of misfortunes grow; everyone will meet these in some form, whether we want it or not [#1976].
- Unaware of privilege and finitude, we spend time on ego. The default use of free time is the pursuit of safety, comfort, pleasure and ego gratifications, which the author doubts can make such fleeting time truly meaningful [#1976].
- Meaning comes from creating, growing and helping. The limited, unguaranteed time should be used to create, build, grow and help others, especially those who have it harder, thereby finding one's unique meaningful form of contribution to the great flow of life [#1976].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Want-vs-must self-inquiry through bodily sensation
Purpose / when to use: the question arose in the author's In Focus meditation group — how to do the practice, and live in general, from the motivation "хочу" (I want) rather than "надо" (I must). For the author this is the key question on the way to the last step of the path "работа - карьера - предназначение" (work - career - calling). He claims that if one properly works through this theme, life will strongly and positively change [#20].

1. Find what you already do from "want". Ask: are there activities I do from "want", not "must"? If yes, what exactly creates that "want"? Is it pleasure and joy? If so, what exactly is the pleasure in — which meanings and sensations in these activities cause pleasure, and how does it show up in the body? [#20]
2. Examine how motivation affects results. Reflect on how the result changes depending on whether you act from "must" or "want". The author asks pointedly whether one can get long-term outstanding results from "надо" (must) motivation at all. [#20]
3. Select anchors for the next practice. After careful reflection, identify which meanings and bodily sensations can help you do your next practice from "want", and lean on these meanings and sensations before the next practice [#20].
4. Feel the goal of your current track in the body. Think about your goal for the current track: what concretely will change in your day if you achieve it? Will there be more pleasure in it? From what? Try to feel in the body as fully as possible all the pleasant sensations while asking these questions and visualising the future [#20].
5. Check every action against bodily feedback. Each time you perform a task, sense the body's response to it. Check yourself «не с мыслями, а с телесными ощущениями» (not with thoughts but with bodily sensations): are there pleasant sensations in the process? Is there pleasure and joy? Do you tense or relax? [#20]
6. Deepen the skill with meditation. For deepening this skill, the author says there is nothing more useful than meditation, because it helps us become aware of ourselves more often [#20].

Outcome and why: over time this work can reveal your boundaries and deep values, and eventually lead to awareness of life's goal — purpose. The author stresses it is «постепенный, реальный и органический процесс» (a gradual, real and organic process). Discovering inner motivation from pleasure and "want" can, he says, give more energy than you ever dreamed of asking for [#20].

#### Chris Wallis's seven points on motivation and life strategy
Source: the author attended a lecture by Christopher Wallis, PhD of UC Berkeley, described as one of the leading Western researchers and philosophers of yoga and tantra, and passes on his idea about motivation and life strategies [#99].

1. Everyone lives by a belief system, few check it. All people live by their own system of beliefs, but most do not have a system of self-reflection sufficient to at least periodically check whether those beliefs are true [#99].
2. The majority belief: external objects bring happiness. We strive for whatever is pleasant most of the time and avoid the unpleasant as much as possible; our strategy is to chase our own desires — «Это сделает меня счастливым. Нет это сделает меня счастливым» (This will make me happy. No, this will make me happy). That is all [#99].
3. Unchecked, this strategy never arrives. If these beliefs are not examined and the strategy is not conscious, a person chases happiness all their life and never catches it: «Никогда не будет достаточно удовольствия для того, чтобы прекратить неудовлетворенность» (There will never be enough pleasure to end dissatisfaction) [#99].
4. Desire itself is not the problem. Problems and suffering come from not understanding the limits of happiness from satisfying desires. Pleasure is completely normal as long as you know exactly what it can and cannot give you [#99].
5. What we really want is fullness. Behind success, tasty food or sex lies a sense of fullness; we want not the objects of desire but that feeling. The main goal we all seek, often without admitting it, is «ощущение наполненности и соединенности с чем-то большим» (a sense of fullness and connectedness with something bigger) [#99].
6. Put the search for connection first. To end dissatisfaction, do not primarily chase pleasure nor avoid the unpleasant; explore and work with what connects us with something bigger and creates sustainable fullness. This search must come first, otherwise we stay stuck chasing pleasure and trying to avoid dissatisfaction [#99].
7. The search cannot be forced — be honest. The search either exists or not yet. «Если все, чего ты хочешь - это комфорт, будь честным с собой и признайся в этом» (If all you want is comfort, be honest with yourself and admit it). Honesty about your real goal is already the start of the path to change [#99].

#### The cocktail of unique configuration
Purpose: the alternative to "business with meaning". The author argues it is not enough to do something cool, right or advanced (whatever is a good deed, trendy or liked by one's crowd). Required instead are (a) revising the false concept we live from — «внешние достижения = моя значимость» (external achievements = my significance) — and (b) painful inner work on understanding one's «конкретно своей уникальной конфигурации» (specifically one's own unique configuration). We must free ourselves from reflexively living life on someone else's rails and pursue courageous growth of consciousness, carrying for years the answers to "who am I?" and "why am I here?". Without this it is extremely hard to find work that may sharply diverge from what parents, friends and society consider success and meaning, but that leads to a feeling of deep rightness for ourselves [#458].

The result of such a search (with readiness to really fight for one's true self) is the discovery and re-discovery of a unique cocktail of our:
1. Talents [#458]
2. Values [#458]
3. Pleasures [#458]
4. Demands of the historical time («требований исторического времени») [#458]
5. Experience and resources [#458]

Then, the author says, we can create something professionally that can astonish ourselves with the beauty of unfolding our own potential: «бизнес за пределами травмы и компенсации» (business beyond trauma and compensation), which becomes our daily practice of growth and unfolding — «Это бизнес из сердца, а не со смыслом» (It is a business from the heart, not "with meaning") [#458].

#### Path of individuation
Purpose: the author's answer to the mid-career crisis of successful professionals (see *Career disillusionment and new projections*). He suggests that all along we may not have solved the main question, which often stays unconscious and smears across daily life as a basic anxiety and restlessness until it is addressed directly. It certainly arises for everyone in childhood or adolescence; then it depends on us whether we answer it ourselves or take someone else's ready answer. The question: «кто я без ожиданий социума или родителей?» (who am I without the expectations of society or parents?), or why do we live, or what is death? These questions help pass the main life process that alone will truly fill us with meaning — «Возможно, он и есть смысл жизни» (Perhaps it is the meaning of life) — the process of individuation [#568].

To set out on the path of individuation one must take responsibility «родиться в собственной правде» (to be born in one's own truth). It is not mainly about reaching heights, cycling through identities, changing professions or adding personas to one's arsenal. Far more, we must:
1. Deal with our traumas [#568].
2. Build a healthy ego with clear boundaries [#568].
3. Restore bodily sensitivity and attentiveness to ourselves, so as to understand on a deep sensory level what we are here for [#568].
4. Carry the responsibility of such being («нести ответственность такого бытия») [#568].

Further reading recommended: James Hollis on individuation, and a channel post on what is needed for qualified work with the psyche and body [#568].

#### Four limitations of yearly goals ("Goals man → Values and direction man")
Purpose: the author notes every method has natural limitations, and it is important to remember them so as not to be trapped by a development tool. Since many people plan yearly goals, he reflects on the limitations of that tool [#788].

Background: in his corporate career he always set yearly goals — carefully analysing all life areas, structuring opportunities and checking progress every month. Such planning probably helped him out of many difficulties, to raise his salary several times and to understand how problem-solving works. About five years before the post he dropped this paradigm: he kept goals as certain tasks within the year, but direction and values became the main reference points [#788].

Why goals as a tool for planning the whole year can lead the wrong way:
1. Goals do not account for means. It is easy to look at your education area and decide to improve English this year; much harder to predict what challenges the year will bring — whether you will have resources even for one lesson a week, and what you must sacrifice for it [#788].
2. Goals increase stress and burnout. Once you fix on paper which job, body or apartment you must acquire, you start feeling stress from the need to constantly do something to fulfil these wishes. You also form expectations and get upset when they break against the reality of covid, war or a global recession [#788].
3. Goals frame and create rigidity. One can call it focus, but in the blinkers of what we must achieve it is easy to miss strange opportunities and unexpected scenarios that life is rich in. When the situation changes sharply and we lack a deep foundation of values and direction, goals can become destructive — we keep doing what is no longer needed in the new reality [#788].
4. Goals entrench the achievement paradigm and block further growth. Goal-setting presupposes moving from what you lack to what must appear; it is built around material or non-material accumulation and acquisition, and so pulls toward the future, lowering the value of the present and of the process itself. Goals without a foundation can therefore reinforce the deficit paradigm that makes us unhappy, in which «мы обмениваем время жизни на вещи, без которых верим, что окажемся несчастными» (we exchange our lifetime for things without which we believe we will be unhappy) [#788].
5. (Added in the follow-up) Goals are based on the past. The author calls this the most terrible drawback he had not mentioned: we always plan and set goals based on past experience and yesterday's identity, while tomorrow we will be different people in an ever-changing world [#790].

Where goals still belong: the author stresses he is not simply against goal-setting. He chooses a direction, remembers what kind of person he wants to be and opens himself to the unknown; goals exist within work or personal tasks and projects that do not define his whole life, do not create an illusion of control and must be extremely flexible. Goals are definitely important in problem zones — to pay off a debt, get citizenship in a new country or support a team, analysis and discipline in following a plan are necessary. In the current situation (2023), goals can also help structure chaos a little and organise actions. He shares a yearly goal-setting framework translated and extended by community member Pasha Sikorsky. Conclusion: «Осмысленность и наполненность возникает не от приобретений, а от приведения жизни в соответствие с тем, что правда приносит радость» (Meaningfulness and fullness come not from acquisitions but from aligning life with what truly brings joy) [#788].

#### Values and direction instead of yearly goals
Purpose: the promised "more ecological and flexible" alternative to yearly goals for increasing meaningfulness and clarity [#788, #790]. Metaphor: «Если ты хочешь встретить рассвет, нужно повернуть голову на восток» (If you want to meet the sunrise, you need to turn your head to the east). Defining a key direction for the year creates focus, simplicity and leaves flexibility [#790].

1. Identify your values by noticing your states. Values are fairly simple to understand if you notice your states in everyday life: what truly fills me, what gives energy, what makes me forget about difficulties? Further good questions: What is critically important to me? Without what do I not want to live my life? The author's own values as examples: a high degree of flexibility and autonomy; saying openly what he thinks and considers true; creating the new and incredible; reducing suffering and multiplying health [#790].
2. Check whether your current life lets you realise them. His corporate career did not allow him to realise even half of these values, so the break was "absolutely inevitable". Regardless of whom he could associate with, what he drove and how much money arrived, he felt a dissatisfaction that no attributes of a good life compensate: «Нельзя утолить жажду едой» (You cannot quench thirst with food) [#790].
3. Set a simple direction rather than a target outcome. When he left the position of department director at OZK five years earlier to end his corporate career, he did not set a goal like "in a year have a mental-health company with a team of 15 and 15 million revenue". The direction was simple: move from a career in organisations to working for himself while realising his most important values [#790].
4. Run constant experiments toward the direction. «Для движение в условиях неопределенности эволюция уже давно нашла самый оптимальный путь — ясное направление и постоянные эксперименты в его сторону» (For movement under uncertainty evolution long ago found the optimal way — a clear direction and constant experiments toward it). With values and direction clear, he allowed himself to respond to any opportunities and experiments that let him realise values right now and move toward the next life stage [#790].
5. Let the form of the next stage emerge from the trials. He did not want to be a mindfulness teacher, leadership coach or speaker, but went to talk about new forms of organisations at the Black Cooperative and began running personal facilitation sessions on embodiment and awareness for McKinsey workers. These were accessible steps in his direction that realised values now rather than tomorrow; they gave energy, and over time it was from such trials that the shape of the next stage — deep mind — became clear (the company appeared only a year later). By the time of writing there were over 100 such experiments, often without a clear goal but always with a sense of energy and pleasure [#790].
6. Ask what life is calling you to this year. The future is unknown, but by attentively observing yourself and the world around you can always feel where everything flows — that is the direction important to you now. The author says his life fills with a special feeling of rightness when he stays in contact with it [#790].

Why: the author suggests life does not need to be pushed forward by constantly trying to become bigger, higher and stronger — our time will run out anyway, we will inevitably age and die, unable to take achievements with us [#790]. He links the whole approach to Otto Scharmer's practice of "leading from the future", which presupposes a special openness to reality in the present moment, as opposed to living in concepts and thoughts about oneself and the world that are usually not authentic: we took them from others without careful checking against our actual experience, so these thoughts cruelly determine decisions and constrain the path toward the socially approved rather than toward what genuinely creates eudaimonia [#790].

#### Peter Laslett's four ages and the third age space
Purpose: frame for a trend the author highlights — in high-living-standard countries old age is being postponed and a new 15–20 years of active, rich mature life is appearing [#1402].

British sociologist Peter Laslett, probably the visionary of this trend, described it in *A Fresh Map of Life* as the emergence of the "third age space". He divided life into four ages:
1. First age — learning and socialisation [#1402].
2. Second age — maturity, work and parenthood [#1402].
3. Third age (usually 50–75) — the period after the main social obligations, when a person can reformulate identity, reinvent themselves and contribute «не по обязанности, а по смыслу» (not out of duty but out of meaning). It resembles neither youth nor old age; it is an independent life field with time for oneself and for postponed interests. Sometimes here a person can ask for the first time: «А как я хочу жить для себя, не обуславливаясь прежними ролями и сценариями?» (And how do I want to live for myself, not conditioned by former roles and scenarios?) [#1402]
4. Fourth age — (!) INCOMPLETE: the source says Laslett proposes four ages but describes only the first three [#1402].

Four causes of the third age space:
1. Medicine and health — improvements in cardiology, oncology, diagnostics and lifestyle have slowed ageing [#1402].
2. Rising emotional well-being with age — Carstensen's Stanford research showed that people from 50 to ~75 experience more positive emotions, cope with stress more steadily and are less prone to anxiety [#1402].
3. Culture and economics — late parenthood, career change at 40–50, new openness to learning and relocation are becoming mass phenomena; there is no longer a "normal path" that ends at 60 [#1402].
4. Technology — access to information, work, social ties and learning is already less limited by age, erasing old ideas of "inability to do new things" after 50 [#1402].

Four opportunities if one works on adaptability skills and treats the third age as a new maturation rather than prolonged old age:
1. Learning again — not because you "must" but because it is "interesting" [#1402].
2. Building relationships — not to "make it in time" but to be "finally for real" [#1402].
3. Working — not out of fear but out of the wish to bring value [#1402].
4. Healing — not only past wounds but also the lifestyle, so that it has more tenderness, time and presence [#1402].

#### Practices of self-remembering vs self-forgetting (guest: Valeriy Karavaev)
Purpose: a guest essay by Valeriy Karavaev (founder of Karavaev.Lab, acting teacher, mindfulness instructor, member of the deep mind community), offered explicitly not as truth or expertise but "as a true amateur, from the word *to love*", on why art matters [#799].

What he does NOT mean by art: cultural leisure; going to museums; discussing new TV series; reading fiction like *Atlas Shrugged*; political actionism like Pussy Riot or Pavlensky; art in the applied sense — important ideas in a beautiful package, like Irvin Yalom's novels. He loves Ivan Vyrypaev's work, but not for the topical themes he discusses. He is not against any of these, he only clarifies [#799].

The problem he answers: some of his smart, respected friends see art as optional, an unnecessary decoration — why spend time on thick novels, three-hour films, dusty paintings and boring symphonies, not to mention contemporary art that looks like mockery or madness? Why read poems if the same ideas can be stated directly? [#799]

The classification — all life practices fall into two categories:
1. Practices of self-remembering (самовоспоминание). Art, for him, is such a practice [#799].
2. Practices of self-forgetting (самозабвение). TV series are "blissful self-forgetting"; he practises this too, and says it is fine [#799].

The classification is not evaluative: he believes that for a modern city dweller continuous awareness is in some sense simply unbearable (half-joking, but with some truth) [#799].

Why art is self-remembering: its value and even benefit lie not in what it tells us but in how it says it, and what it does to us meanwhile. Vygotsky in *Psychology of Art*: «в настоящем произведении форма должна уничтожить содержание» (in a true work form must destroy content). Vygotsky's example: Krylov's fable *The Dragonfly and the Ant* — research found that children often side with the dragonfly, even though the moral («так поди же попляши», "so go and dance") clearly favours the ant. Vygotsky distinguishes the didactic fable from the poetic one and shows that Krylov is a skilled artist, not a moralist: he deliberately collides content (the moral) and form (sympathy for the dragonfly) in the same text. The reader experiences a short circuit, because a poetic fable is more like a Zen koan than a moral tale (as in Aesop). Conclusion: a true work of art does not simplify our relationship with reality but complicates it — «Но это цветущая сложность, которая не усыпляет, а пробуждает» (But it is a blossoming complexity that does not lull but awakens). That is why art is a practice of self-remembering [#799].

#### Transcendent tasks
Purpose: the author's concept for what makes existence not meaningless, prompted by birthday messages and the confrontation with passing time [#1747].

1. Definition. «Трансцендентность — это такое свойство явлений, которое позволяет нам буквально пережить себя, как нечто большее, чем отдельное "Я"» (Transcendence is a property of phenomena that lets us literally experience ourselves as something bigger than a separate "I"). There is very little room for it in a world obsessed with metrics, achievements and material objects [#1747].
2. Why they matter. If amid the bustle for safety and pleasantness we do not make room for transcendent tasks, our presence in life will remain literally unnoticed — «Был Максим, да и хер с ним. Всю жизнь про себя.» (There was Maxim, and to hell with him. All his life about himself.) Transcendent tasks take a person beyond self-focused bustle, which is somewhat important but ultimately centred on oneself; they offer a way in a sense to outlive or reach immortality — through them we go beyond this separate person who will die and invest ourselves in something bigger [#1747].
3. Why people miss them. Many today lack understanding, inner supports and personal maturity to meet the fact of death without being destroyed by it, so they spend life distracting themselves — in alcohol, anxiety or bustle — and use up all their allotted time without approaching their transcendent tasks. The author calls this sad and compassionate to observe [#1747].
4. Death gives birth to life. The existence of death also generates life itself; on a birthday one can also recall the miracle of being in the world of people, which gives the chance to find and even solve some transcendent tasks, contributing to the common flow of life and making existence not meaningless [#1747].
5. An obvious example — raising children. One obvious transcendent task is the birth and sincere upbringing of children, when it happens not by accident and we pass on not only pain but something that will truly live in the children after us [#1747].
6. No task is universal. No transcendent task fits everyone by default, whatever anyone says; this is a meaning that cannot be dictated by someone — only we ourselves can discover and realise it, making it completely authentic [#1747].
7. The author's own task. For him one such task lies in human growth and development: helping many people (or few, but truly) turn attention to their inner world, and then living through moments when people notice and even befriend the inner space from which all their decisions, actions and words arise. He is curious which other tasks he will find [#1747].
8. Staying focused. It is easy to forget and lose focus, because many appearances divert attention to what seems pleasant to this "I". To resist even a little, the author pragmatically tries to think about others, and wishes readers to get off their phones more often so as to find their transcendent tasks sooner and solve them in this life [#1747].

#### Privilege-and-finitude reflection
Purpose: a way of reframing the present that the author models in his own morning reflection; it reorients time use from ego goals to meaning. Components in the order he moves through them [#1976]:
1. Notice how good things actually are right now. The author woke from a dream in which a long-dead friend came to him and immediately thought how good everything really is: for a long while neither he nor his loved ones have been seriously ill, nobody is dying, nobody depends on him, and he does not urgently need to find millions because of a disaster. He calls being this free «благословение и… большая удача и привилегия» (a blessing and … great luck and privilege) [#1976].
2. Turn attention to those for whom it is different now. He thinks of people sitting at a loved one's hospital bed, saying goodbye to relatives or living through their death; those carrying children, sick or ageing parents with no day of respite; those searching for "those very millions" because they have no choice. His stance toward them: «Между нами нет стены — только время и обстоятельства» (There is no wall between us — only time and circumstances), i.e. their situation is one he may yet face [#1976].
3. Rescale your own difficulties. He has plenty of difficulties, like everyone, but against this background many habitual difficulties become simply tasks, and everything that happens gains a broader perspective [#1976].
4. Recognise finitude. Once you see this time as luck and privilege, it becomes precious, because it will not always be so; it may end tomorrow or even today. Without meeting this fact, the time when we can choose, grow and help is easy to sleep through [#1976].
5. Test what you spend time on with three questions. The author names what time slips into when privilege and finitude are not realised: safety, comfort and pleasures, and what pleases the ego — pleasant things, power, reputation, useful connections, money. He then asks: (a) can this make such fleeting time truly meaningful? (b) will it bring deep satisfaction and peace at the moment we realise our position has changed? (c) will today's actions be a reliable support in tomorrow's difficult situation, or will they, on the contrary, greatly increase our suffering? [#1976]
6. Redirect time toward creating, building, growing and helping. The answer he offers: use the limited, fleeting and by no means guaranteed time to create, build, grow and develop, and help others, especially those who have it harder — and so find one's unique meaningful form to contribute to the great flow of life of which, "for some unknown reasons", we turned out to be a part [#1976].

### Detailed insights

#### Purpose as adult responsibility
Writer Richard Leider asks audiences: what are the two most important days of your life? The first is obviously your birthday. The second is not the day of death, since that is the completion of the story, not its climax. Leider's answer: the day you understood why you were born [#6].

The author observes that in his (Russian-speaking) context it is still not customary to think in the category of life purpose. He believes this is a legacy of the Soviet mindset: there was a common state goal, so why have your own; individualism was perceived as egoism and was even punished. Today, he argues, purpose looks like an element of responsibility and an adult attitude to life: «Мы обязаны искать свое самое правильное применение, иначе расходуем жизнь впустую» (We are obliged to seek our most right application, otherwise we waste our life) [#6].

#### Authorship and inner power
The author states that everything he does is aimed at each of us seeing more power in ourselves. «Власть - это же веревка на слоне» (Power is the rope on the elephant): it holds us in place only because we allow it, and it seems we cannot change anything — but we can. He is sure a freer and happier life is possible for everyone. Ultimately he does not believe we want careers and things; we want love and friendship, to dance together and support each other, to create the new and respect the old [#96].

If we feel this and allow one small thought — «что мы авторы наших жизней» (that we are the authors of our lives) — everything starts to change. The world is not the USA and Iran, not Putin and Trump; the world is our souls, families, communities and companies. Stagnation is us; change is also us; we are the conflicts; we are our companies and countries [#96].

What to fight: «Бороться нужно не за успех и не за выживание, а со своим страхом менять мир вокруг себя и закрытостью к чувствам» (One must fight not for success or survival, but against one's fear of changing the world around and closedness to feelings). How: by doing bodily practices, being aware of sensations, allowing deep reflection and open dialogue we change the situation. We fight, but not through force and violence — through depth and openness, which the author considers much more effective. This creates a culture in which one can feel, and therefore there is ever less room to suppress, divide and disempower life itself [#96].

#### Business with meaning vs business as practice
In entrepreneurial circles, the image of a "business with meaning" has become a mainstream ideal of success. Money and power alone are not enough, since many people have them, often not particularly outstanding people (the author adds: in Putin's Russia often especially unoutstanding but loyal ones) [#458].

Material values do not by default bring fullness and deep satisfaction. They add pleasure, create an illusory sense of safety and a certain degree of freedom — but even this freedom must be filled with something essential. And deep-rooted insecurity and a feeling of lack often do not pass with money or status [#458].

So finding work that creates meaning becomes a logical step. Entrepreneurs who made money on often prosaic things start looking for business ideas that not only make a profit but become a statement — affirming the significance of their life in eternity. They use accumulated resources, aim to become more public, build communities around themselves, run festivals and forums, create consulting companies. Sometimes it becomes just a new way to make money, sometimes it is genuinely driven by a motivation to create something high-quality and meaningful [#458].

But in both cases, when the striving to fill life with meaning comes «от компенсаторного дискомфорта с тем, что уже в ней есть» (from compensatory discomfort with what is already in it), from outside it often resembles the sports cars that men used to buy in mid-life. Only the form of the gesture has changed — an attempt to say to oneself and others "Look what I can do!". In that case the second round, where fun or something socially good is bolted onto money, also does not lead to peace. Moreover, once you have taken on a meaningful business you cannot escape a showy "flow" and satisfaction, which the author finds even sadder than honest money-grabbing without acting. The positive alternative is *The cocktail of unique configuration* above [#458].

#### Career disillusionment and new projections
The author notes (2022) a wave of disillusionment with the leading professions of recent years: product managers, UX designers, entrepreneurs. Having reached certain heights — social recognition, a load of cool projects, money, high freedom and mobility — people realise something essential is still off-screen. External achievements, even in interesting intellectual and advanced professions, cannot by themselves answer a person's main question, and at some point this produces a crisis [#568].

The frustration, not always conscious, pours into searching for an activity that from the new vantage point seems natural, alive and cool. So entrepreneurs and product managers become coaches, psychotherapists, or "even more fun" — vipassana teachers, shamans, bathhouse masters, all kinds of healers and psychedelic guides. The author ironically predicts a next wave: organisers of extreme retreats, tantra teachers and sex/psychedelic psychotherapists — where again someone will suddenly feel that everything is already clear and it is time to do good to people [#568].

His evaluation: there is nothing bad in changing profession or expanding one's role repertoire; often it genuinely deepens us as persons and enriches activity and life. But there is nothing good, for our development process, in generating new projections that do not bring us closer to the essence of the life challenge and merely repeat an already-travelled scenario of solving outside what must be solved inside. Once we believed an interesting profession would make us happy and projected the question of inner satisfaction onto external success; having reached a milestone, we achieved a lot but again did not find deep calm and satisfaction. The way out is the *Path of individuation* above [#568].

#### Against nihilism
Context: readers wrote to the author that life is meaningless and he misleads people by claiming one must reflect and search. He traces this nihilistic position to postmodernism and to often out-of-context ideas of Sartre, Deleuze, Nietzsche and other "comrades" (товарищей) [#569].

- Humanity has outgrown postmodernism. In his view we have learned not only to deconstruct any meanings to zero but also to rank black and white well: «Убивать животных объективно хуже, чем выращивать цветы» (Killing animals is objectively worse than growing flowers). He points to Ken Wilber's work from twenty years earlier as help here [#569].
- Pop-nihilism distorts Sartre. Sartre proclaimed that the world and the self have no purpose, but also pointed out: «через сознание и выбор Я может придать миру смысл» (through consciousness and choice the self can give the world meaning) [#569].
- Meaninglessness covers the fear of becoming oneself. It instantly removes responsibility for living a brave and candid life the way one truly wants, even if it contradicts others' expectations — going into creativity, play, caring for others, searching or any other calling. That calling was always with us and calling us, but we pushed it down out of fear of rejection, ridicule and disapproval. One can lie to oneself and to the author that there is nothing, but deep down we always know the truth — something is lost and requires searching [#569].
- Nihilism suits the ego. If everything is meaningless, no effort or zeal is needed; meaninglessness opens the way to any egoism — after us the flood, since life is meaningless and ends with us. «Тогда все что имеет значение — Я и мое удовольствие» (Then all that matters is me and my pleasure). Such a personal philosophy defines egoists and can lead them only to a meaningless, egoistic and narcissistic life of constant hedonism-seeking, and finally to constant dissatisfaction and boredom [#569].
- Lessons from contact with death (personal stories). Long ago the author's wife worked as a clinical psychologist in a nursing home; everything there was saturated with death, and this experience strongly corrected both their views, because the value and meaningfulness of life at its end becomes literally tangible. Once he found a sick kitten on Bolshaya Dmitrovka; while they drove it to the hospital it died in his hands. It was an incredibly touching and mysterious moment, and the day was filled with deeper meaning «чем от чтения всех книг Ницше» (than from reading all of Nietzsche's books) [#569].
- What to do if life seems meaningless. One must urgently pull oneself out of the trauma of fear that closes us off from the fullness of experience and from connection with others through suffering and joy. Concretely: go and look at death and suffering — volunteer in a hospice, help a homeless person get a job, bring home an abandoned dog — and always look all these beings in the eyes. Perhaps this will open our traumatised heart and let in connection with something bigger: not a silly philosophical or mental concept, but the real mystery of being [#569].
- Why the author will not state "the meaning". Nihilists ironically ask him to say directly what the meaning is. His answer: for others, his meaning would be only someone else's social construct — exactly the problem he writes about. Nobody else has your meaning, but that does not mean they lack tools for searching [#569].
- How meaning is found. Meaning can be found when we decide to find ourselves and bear the sacrifice of suffering from going against others' expectations. But mere denial of expectations is not enough — it is important to arrive at something of one's own, «эмпирически, а не концептуально» (empirically, not conceptually). That is how personality is born from the abyss of unconscious automatic existence. Nihilists, he says, are stuck in denial, using it to avoid responsibility, and have given birth to nothing [#569].

#### The new old age: why the third age demands self-redefinition
Data the author cites: according to WHO data, a person's biological and psychological age increasingly diverges from calendar age; people 50+ are becoming active participants in the economy, culture and politics — not as an exception but as a new norm. Average life expectancy since the 1950s has grown by more than 20 years globally and by almost 30 years in developed economies. This is not just years added to old age but a new life period that previously had no cultural frame and did not exist as a stage of development [#1402].

Quality matters as much as quantity: quality of life in these years is higher than ever, changing the very sense of age; for the first time in history, after 50 we face not "winding down" but a new period of development requiring new skills and searches. Earlier the script was simple — study → work → family → pension → departure. Now there is more freedom and possibility, but also more uncertainty and anxiety: one must again, with much greater autonomy, choose who to be in the period of second maturity, where to direct energy and which meanings to serve. The author is sure this will be a big challenge for many, especially those not used to redefining themselves and changing identities at a younger age. If we are 45–55 now, we are probably just entering the longest and richest stage of life, which may last until 75 or even 80 and become not a waiting for the end but a period of amazing unfolding [#1402].

#### The big game
Setting: late at night, exhausted after three days of intense socialising, the author keeps a meeting with Sasha; they skip small talk within three minutes and speak "not from the mind but from the heart". Sasha asks: «Макс, что такое по-настоящему большая игра для тебя?» (Max, what is a truly big game for you?) [#1446]

- Redefinition. The author has thought about this for years, if not decades, when facing what does not work for him. For him the big game is interesting not in the context of power, success and money but as an internally consonant path — where material stability meets a clearly found mission to honestly serve something bigger. He stresses this is not the result of an ideology or special moral stance, but «опыт помноженный на внимательность» (experience multiplied by attentiveness) [#1446].
- Two failure modes he knows from experience. When he starts sliding into mere money-making or other inflating of his ego, the essence of the process that fills him is instantly lost. Conversely, any impact without a firm material base quickly turns into spiritual bypassing, which cannot become truly sustainable and in which he starts to suffer from lack [#1446].
- His conventional big game. By the usual paradigm he got into the big game early: right after university he guided Klaus Schwab around SPIEF, then did early political internet projects for the Presidential Administration, and soon was in fairly high circles of power for the next five years — which, he notes, led him to a position of deep criticism of what is happening [#1446].
- The price of the conventional path. Every path has a price. Motivation to move forward purely through money, success and power looks extremely neurotic; its price is «постепенное пожирание личности, вместо ее подлинного возникновение» (the gradual devouring of personality instead of its genuine emergence). These are not shadow entities for him, but being okay with them is one thing and being driven by them is another. Outwardly such a path may look like a big game but actually be only a prelude to being a Human — or lead us off that path entirely [#1446].
- Where the real big game started. The unmistakable taste of the big game arose not after entering big offices but after the decision to live by his own truth, free from the neurosis of compensating fear. This is only the beginning; further on one learns better to embody this truth, making mistakes and gaining more freedom to do what is right [#1446].
- Criteria of the subtler big game. Power, money or success are legitimate, even important things. The key question is not *what* you have but *how* you got it and how you live in general. Can you not dodge the world but also not abuse it? Is your status a consequence of honesty and of creating sincere, accessible and adequate benefit for the world? And ultimately — however pompous it sounds — is it a reflection of love that only you can express in this way, and no one else? [#1446]
- No neurotic modesty. So today the big game exists for him; he does not renounce it «в невротической же самообесценивающей скромности» (in an equally neurotic self-devaluing modesty). He tries to accept this challenge of life, in which alone a free personality can emerge [#1446].

#### Birthdays, presence and meaning returned by others
On the morning after his birthday the author managed, for the first time in two weeks, not to pick up his phone in the first 15 minutes after waking. He notes that the constructive strategy here is not prohibition but replacement — in his privileged case, replacing the morning phone with swimming alone in a clean warm sea [#1747].

Over breakfast he spent an hour going through messages and video notes from friends and close ones sent for his birthday, and reflected that through such responses «мы возвращаем друг другу смысл и ценности» (we return to each other the meaning and values) that are manifested in the world through our particular existence. These things sometimes sound abstract, but perhaps they are what matters and remain in the flow of life as the taste of our stay in this world [#1747].

Whether you want to lose yourself in revelry or detachedly pretend nothing special is happening, such days still create islands of presence — presence not as a pleasant contact with present-moment sensations, but the kind that confronts you with reality, with a truth that is far from always pleasant: part of you will still remember and shudder at the fact that time is passing. People's responses and this encounter with death invite reflection on transcendent tasks (see *Transcendent tasks*) [#1747].

### Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The Soviet-mindset explanation for not thinking in terms of purpose is explicitly the author's own impression («Мне кажется», "It seems to me") [#6].
- The claim that "must" motivation cannot produce long-term outstanding results is posed as a rhetorical question, not supported with evidence [#20].
- The author is not against goals: he keeps them for tasks, projects and problem zones (debts, citizenship, supporting a team) and in chaotic times; the critique targets goals as the structure of the whole year [#788].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: Laslett's fourth age is mentioned (four ages) but not described [#1402].
- The WHO and Carstensen findings are cited by the author without figures beyond those given; the life-expectancy numbers (+20 years globally, almost +30 in developed economies since the 1950s) are as stated in the post [#1402].
- Valeriy Karavaev explicitly disclaims expertise and truth («Ни в кой мере не претендуя на истину» — "in no way claiming to be the truth"; he speaks as a lover of art), and calls his self-remembering/self-forgetting classification non-evaluative [#799].
- The author's privileged replacement for the morning phone (sea swimming) is acknowledged by him as a privilege; the transferable rule is replacement rather than prohibition [#1747].
- Tension worth noting: #20 grounds direction in pleasure and "want", while #99 (Wallis) warns that chasing pleasure never ends dissatisfaction and that fullness/connection must come first. The sources reconcile this partially — pleasure is fine if one knows its limits [#20, #99].

- This is a personal reflection written in the author's voice, not a tested method; the step order above follows the sequence of his thoughts in the post. The post ends with links to the author's ecosystem, which are dropped as promo [#1976].

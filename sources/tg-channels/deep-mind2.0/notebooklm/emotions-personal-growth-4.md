## Vertical development and the integral approach

### What this covers

This file collects what the deep mind channel author taught about adult (vertical) development and Ken Wilber's integral approach between 2020 and 2025: process thinking versus picture thinking, why experience does not by itself produce growth, how neurotic patterns harden with age, the four (later five) zones of integral practice with the author's own routine, the boosters of vertical growth named by Dustin DiPerna, purposeless spaces as a growth condition, inner work metaphors, and spiral-dynamics readings of culture and leadership. The author presents vertical development as the response to an environment so complex that "something must change in essence" in management, and as "one of the most subtle pleasures of life" that makes people see more perspectives and become much more effective [#671, #1513].

This part collects the author's posts on vertical (stage) development of adults: spiral dynamics colours (blue, orange, green, yellow) mapped onto Bill Torbert's action logics and Robert Kegan's stages, the claim that the yellow/Strategist level is the scarcest and most needed capacity in the AI era, three kinds of learning and why only transformational learning changes the "subject", inner work as a "psychological pension" for old age, and the author's picture of real adulthood as taking responsibility for one's own pain. The author argues it matters because the AI wave makes knowledge, skills and even achievement-based identity insufficient, and only the growth of the person themselves keeps its value — "не только для эффективного лидерства… но и в целом для счастливой жизни" ("not only for effective leadership… but for a happy life in general") [#1823, #1547].

### Core ideas

- Think in processes, not pictures. Energy crises come less from real failures than from the question "can I?"; seeing self, team and product as developing processes turns doubt into productive questions (see *Process thinking vs picture thinking*) [#140].
- Vertical development is the growth of the structures of consciousness, not the accumulation of skills; modern business challenges cannot be solved just by mastering skills, and a second wave of interest in adult development theories is arriving in the post-Soviet space [#671, #1513].
- Experience is not a predictor of success — without external learning and reflection, experience gives "nothing but a story" (see *Experience plus reflection model*) [#890].
- Without inner work people grow more rigid with age; meditation, therapy and trauma healing keep neural paths fresh (see *Classic vs non-classic ageing scenario*) [#880].
- Integral practice combines developmental trainings across zones so that development is balanced, inclusive and effective; Wilber's latest book frames five foci leading to Radical Wholeness [#905, #1095].
- Vertical growth can be boosted by knowledge of stages, meditation, exposure to other cultures, inner journeys and experiences beyond the familiar [#1513].
- Spaces without goals are needed to integrate experience and to escape solving problems with an "old version of self", but must be entered with an ethic of care and with facilitation [#1466].
- Cultures and societies also move through levels: the hippie/1960s shift to pluralistic values now dominates developed countries and has reached its edge (nihilism, narcissism); the author expects an integrative update rather than a rollback [#987].
- The integral approach is a meta-map: no perspective holds the full truth but each holds part of it [#1544].

- Vertical development is the main task of the epoch. Orange optimization and green empathy each give partial answers and lose to the real complexity of the world; yellow is the main deficit. Details in *Spiral dynamics levels* below [#1823].
- Yellow does not come through understanding. It is a structure of perception formed through real experience; many who collect integral concepts are actually green or orange with a richer vocabulary [#1823].
- Only transformational learning changes the subject. Knowledge is relatively useless, upskilling is the gold standard but insufficient for the key dead ends; the author recommends a combination of upskilling and transformation [#1748].
- Inner work is inner capital. The longer one lives without it, the more neurotic patterns dominate as compensating resources decline with age [#1630].
- Maturity = taking responsibility for one's own pain. Not enduring, but answering for one's feelings; this is what transforms lenses of lack into lenses of love [#1817].
- Processuality is what keeps value in the AI era. Long, methodical, love-driven inner work, not knowledge, turns an ordinary person into a master [#1547].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Process thinking vs picture thinking (four reframing questions)
Purpose: to stop the energy drain caused by doubt and redirect it into action. The author's post is titled "Лучше мыслить процессами, а не картинками." (It is better to think in processes than in pictures.) [#140]

1. Notice where the crisis really comes from. The author says that in recent years he caught the sense of crisis, unbearable strain and a big loss of strength not on actual failures but in moments when the question "а могу ли я?" (can I?) crept into the mind. Variations: do I have enough abilities? Is this a working team configuration? Is this possible in this country? Is my idea realistic? [#140]
2. Understand the mechanism. Such questions open the road to doubt and painful inner states. Instead of an unbiased analysis of the situation many cognitive distortions arise: tying self-esteem to the results of activity, anxiety and anger, fears. They cause a "colossal outflow of energy" that ends either in giving up ("опущенными руками", *hands dropped*) or in a lower speed of movement [#140].
3. Redirect the energy. The energy spent on doubts is more constructively spent on the work itself: developing strategy, dialogues with smart people, prototypes and testing hypotheses [#140].
4. Ask the right questions, which come from process thinking. "Я, команда, продукт- не фиксированная данность, не картинка. А развивающийся процесс, на который можно влиять." (I, the team, the product are not a fixed given, not a picture, but a developing process that can be influenced.) Each doubt question is replaced with a process question:
   - instead of "do I have the abilities?" — What do I need to learn and how do I need to change to make it work for me?
   - instead of "is this a working team?" — How must the team configuration or communication within it change so that everything works?
   - instead of "is this possible in this country?" — How to launch this product in this country?
   - instead of "is my idea realistic?" — What needs to be let go of and what steps implemented for the goal to become real? [#140]
5. Result. Process thinking and a growth mindset open access to energy and creativity and, most importantly, the possibility to move and to make the necessary mistakes [#140].
6. Diagnostic for picture thinking. If we have only one right answer to all questions, the coolest framework or one best theory, we most likely have picture thinking and are beginners or fanatics — we try to fit the whole world into our correct picture instead of finding answers to the right questions [#140].

The author cites the book *Mindset* (Russian title "Гибкое сознание", *Flexible Mind*) as a generalisation of scientific research confirming this logic and also showing greater success and life satisfaction in people with growth mindsets [#140].

#### Experience plus reflection model of skill growth
Purpose: to explain why years in a role do not make someone good at it and what does. [#890]

- The belief being challenged. The author used to hold that experience means growth. On inspection he calls it a patriarchal idea: it asserts only the power of age or managerial position rather than supporting meritocracy [#890].
- The taxi example. Learning to drive, he expected taxi drivers who spend 10x more time at the wheel than he does to become professionals; in practice they often drive inattentively, don't know the rules and create accident-prone situations [#890].
- The rule. Experience by itself gives "ровно ничего, кроме истории" (exactly nothing except a story) unless one adds external learning and reflection. Conversely, going beyond the average level of mastery is fairly easy when we explore our experience through the meaningful, formalised experience of others and become aware of ourselves and our patterns [#890].
- The author's result. He already drives a car and a motorbike better than the average taxi driver though he drives rarely, because he read 2 books about driving, watched a couple of videos and constantly analyses himself and the traffic in the process, changing his behaviour on the road [#890].
- Application to managers. The idea that managing people requires a separate skill set, education and constant study of oneself and others has still not spread. People manage teams and companies for decades without figuring it out, falling ever more into the bias "*я уже 10 лет руководитель, значит должен делать это хорошо*" (I've been a manager for 10 years, so I must be doing it well) [#890].
- How to reflect correctly (from the research the author cites, see *Evidence and numbers*): merely remembering and replaying past situations is not enough; people who successfully change themselves try to understand the causes of a difficult experience, focus on their own role in it and on the changeable qualities of their personality, and then try to change something in themselves — behaviour, skills, goals and ways of perceiving [#890].
- Conclusion: "без рефлексии и самоосознанности можно и до седых волос дожить, но так ничего про жизнь и не понять" (without reflection and self-awareness you can live to grey hair and still understand nothing about life) [#890].

#### Classic vs non-classic ageing scenario (rigidity vs inner work)
Purpose: to frame inner work as the choice that keeps a person flexible over a lifetime. Post title: "Р — ригидность" (R is for rigidity) [#880].

1. Classic scenario. With age we behave less and less flexibly. Neurotic patterns condition us more each year and we are less able to surprise ourselves and others [#880].
2. Biological mechanism. The more often we walk one neural path, the more it *physically* turns into a road and even a highway, which at some point we will almost inevitably drive down again [#880].
3. Time acceleration as a sign. The more habitual the roads, the faster time flows. Instead of the real world we see its stable images. Life starts flying by — a sure sign of reduced freshness of perception, i.e. reduced awareness [#880].
4. What remains of a person. We end up knowing and remembering our parents and grandparents mostly by these neurotic patterns: grandmother always worried, dad complained. In the second half of life many people's defining traits become not their authenticity — "свобода действовать в соответствие с потребностями и моментом" (freedom to act in accordance with needs and the moment) — but neurosis, "индивидуальный способ адаптации к миру и поиска любви в нем" (an individual way of adapting to the world and seeking love in it). Over time we try to remake the world to fit our neurosis, often causing much pain to children and those around [#880].
5. Non-classic scenario. We add inner work to life — meditation, therapy or healing traumas. This brings freshness to neural paths we built ourselves or acquired from ancestors and helps create novelty in behaviour and perception [#880].
6. The choice. Either gradually and inevitably turn into dad or mum, surprised at how it happens, watching life accelerate; or seek a way out of automatism by training letting go of the past, letting go of ideas about oneself and the world, and direct perception of them. This way we stay *physically* more flexible and open to novelty [#880].
7. Historical note. Our grandmothers did not have this choice — "никакой тебе медитации и психотерапии в 37ом" (no meditation or psychotherapy for you in '37). We do, and the author wonders how we will use it and how the world will change when such a choice becomes the classic scenario [#880].

#### Integral practice — four zones of development
Purpose: to structure one's development methods so development is balanced, inclusive and maximally effective. The author says integral practice is the idea around which he has built his development for about the last 10 years; it arose naturally from Ken Wilber's integral theory ("одной из передовых и гениальных ментальных моделей 20 века", *one of the advanced and brilliant mental models of the 20th century*) and was formalised by Wilber and his associate Terry Patten [#905].

Core principle (simplified by the author): "мы комбинируем развивающие процессы [тренировки] из разных сфер, чтобы они покрывали основные задачи развития" (we combine developmental processes [trainings] from different spheres so that they cover the main tasks of development), which include unfolding our potential in this particular life — personally, with others and with the planet as a whole [#905].

The zones as listed by the author:
1. Wake Up — awakening of perception [#905].
2. Grow Up — clearing personal history and traumas [#905].
3. Clean Up — integrating the shadow and clearing projections [#905].
4. Show Up — embodying oneself in relationships, work and service to others [#905].

(!) UNCLEAR: the author's glosses of Grow Up ("расчищение личной истории и травм", clearing personal history and traumas) and Clean Up (shadow and projections) overlap; in his own routine he files psychotherapy and shadow work jointly under "Clean Up / Grow Up". In #1513 vertical growth (moving up stages/structures) is the subject of a separate discussion, but the author does not explicitly map it to Grow Up [#905, #1513].

Why it works, per the author: the person gets not only a synergy effect but also pulls up the "dropping out" zones that often strongly slow us on the path. "Это самый продуктивный способ развития, который я знаю, если слово продуктивность тут вообще употребимо." (This is the most productive way of development I know, if the word productivity is applicable here at all.) [#905]

Variant: in Terry Patten's approach, described in the book *Integral Life Practice* (Russian "Практика интегральной жизни"), a slightly different classification of zones is used: Shadow, Mind, Body, Spirit [#905].

His base practice, given as a worked example of building an ILP [#905]:
1. Wake Up — gym 3 times a week with strength training and sauna.
2. Wake Up — daily meditation, 30 minutes.
3. Clean Up / Grow Up — psychotherapy once a week, shadow work in a group and through the 3-2-1 practice. (!) INCOMPLETE: the 3-2-1 practice is named but its steps are not explained.
4. Show Up — coaching on request, usually once every few months, plus developing a mission-based project, which is perceived as and is a separate practice.

He sometimes adds extra practices, each of which fits into one of these zones. He also notes in a later post that because only one structured material for integral practice existed (and that many years ago), much has to be tuned and invented independently, as he did with his own practice [#905, #1095].

#### Wilber's five foci of development toward Radical Wholeness
Source: Wilber's book *Finding Radical Wholeness: A Practical Guide to Integral Theory, Psychology, Spiritual Practice and Shadow Work* (released a few weeks before the June 2024 post, English only at the time). The author frames it as Wilber finally giving fresh practical recommendations for whole development after a fundamental review of the theory in his previous book [#1095].

Thesis (Wilber's view as reported): the constant human search for growth and satisfaction is often incomplete. Wilber integrates the wisdom of different approaches to offer a path to radical and complete wholeness through 5 foci:
1. Waking Up
2. Growing Up
3. Opening Up
4. Cleaning Up
5. Showing Up
(!) INCOMPLETE: the post lists the five names but does not define them; Opening Up is new relative to the four zones in #905 and is not explained [#1095].

Claims reported from the book: no other growth path includes all five categories, each of which is a unique path to wholeness. By combining and integrating them we come to what Wilber calls Big Wholeness ("Большой Целостностью") — a fullness in which we feel connected not only to all important aspects of ourselves but to everything in our world; thus we truly understand wholeness and can make room for everything that brings us life [#1095].

Additional content of the book as described: how to develop our multiple intelligences; how to deepen and widen the sense of love through the practice of integral tantric sex (the author's aside: "wow, нечто новенькое и горячее!" — *wow, something new and hot!*); and several integrally adapted practices — developing the witness position, the non-dual practice of One Taste ("единого вкуса"), where we learn to abide in a state without division into object and subject, and shadow work practice. These lead to direct experiences rather than only cognitive understanding of development and unfolding [#1095].

#### Boosters of vertical growth (after Dustin DiPerna)
Definition first. Vertical growth = "Внутренняя эволюция структур, через которые мы ориентируемся в реальности и ее же конструируем" (the inner evolution of the structures through which we orient in reality and construct it), i.e. developing the complexity of the lenses through which we see, interpret and shape reality. The author calls it an extremely valuable process, reflected also in the outer evolution of human civilisation; individually, the number of perspectives we can hold grows, skills grow, the depth of life grows [#1513].

Context: DiPerna (a vertical development specialist) was asked the question that interests adult development specialists and the community of entrepreneurs, coaches and vertical development fans: what can promote our movement up the stages of development or structures of consciousness? The factors [#1513]:

1. Information about stages of development / structures of consciousness. Simply knowing that an adult can keep developing, and what such steps can look like in one's inner world, can promote movement along them. Here the author places studying the models of Wilber, Torbert, Cook-Greuter, Kegan and Graves [#1513].
2. Meditation / systematic development of awareness. Effective meditation helps a person see the patterns of their own thinking and behaviour. As Kegan showed, it makes object what is usually the invisible subject through which we act and live; noticing such things and bringing the unconscious into attention *is* vertical development. Caveat: meditation does not lead to it directly but can "lubricate" the evolution of the psyche's structures [#1513].
3. Exposing oneself to other cultures. Observing other cultures makes one's own culture visible — and culture is largely an expression of the centre of gravity of the level of consciousness in a society. So all travel, if we are open to contact with otherness rather than collapsing into deaf defences, can help us see the hidden structures of our current level and move further [#1513].
4. Inner journeys. Psychotherapy, dream yoga, psychedelics and other transpersonal experience — exploration of inner spaces, properties of consciousness and altered states. Condition: if it does not traumatise and we can integrate it well, it helps growth [#1513].
5. Experience exceeding everything familiar to us (named by DiPerna last in his list) — everything that is normality and everyday life for us. The author's illustration: he was always drawn to "strange" experience, the better the further from his ordinary life — living as if in the Middle Ages at Boudha in Kathmandu, watching state decisions being made with a tie tight around his throat, walking tensely through the Tenderloin in San Francisco, sitting in a monastery near the Chinese border eating the strange food of Karen villagers. He sensed a subtle value he couldn't formulate; such experiences "словно делали меня свободнее в моменте и расширяли диапазон возможного" (as if made me freer in the moment and widened the range of the possible). DiPerna's answer clarified it. He stresses this is not about luxury [#1513].

Closing stance: it would be wrong to see vertical development theories as segregating or evaluating people, but investing in one's own movement through structures of consciousness is "одно из самых тонких удовольствий в жизни" (one of the most subtle pleasures in life): as structures grow more complex we see more perspectives, are freed from a huge amount of inner difficulties and become many times more effective [#1513].

#### Purposeless spaces — two reasons and two safeguards
Post subtitle: why they are needed for growth and how to set them up like a master [#1466].

Problem. In urban life, states without directedness become a new luxury. We are directed when searching for or consuming information, holding meetings, deciding where to eat; a person today may spend 99% of waking time in this mode. It feels effective because we are busy with tasks, but in fact it can hinder both progress on truly important matters and growth. The author says he periodically suffers from constant directedness (an event-oversaturated life) and is going on a solo trip to the mountains to create a space with a different structure of attention — where attention and he "as a system" have no rigid direction toward solving tasks; "Где я скорее просто есть, чем есть для чего-то" (where I rather simply am than am for something) — a space of relaxed inclusion, simple presence [#1466].

Why such a space is needed:
1. It allows integrating lived experience and growing. Without conditions where we can change the structure of attention and stop grasping objects through tension, experience struggles to become knowledge: it piles up and becomes background. Then it is hard both to realise changes that have already happened and to creatively synthesise new inner structures. This lowers realisation of potential and over time generates inner conflicts, frustration and a sense of being "не в своей жизни" (not in one's own life). Research support cited: when a person is not solving a specific task but constructively "doing nothing" — not in a dulled mind — the Default Mode Network activates, a network associated with self-awareness, memories, integration of experience and reflection [#1466].
2. It helps solve truly important matters — sometimes better than head-on effort. The work of Harvard psychologists Kegan and Lahey, per the author, confirms that people often get stuck not for lack of will or resources but because they try to solve problems with an "old version of themselves" — models that no longer match current reality. Reproducing the past, we cannot escape dead ends, which require novelty. For such breakthroughs it is vital to "zero out" the habitual; purposeless spaces tear us out of it and switch on direct perception of lived experience, yielding insights and answers we may have ignored for years [#1466].

The nuance (risks). Leaving the habitual brings vulnerability as well as benefit. One can open up and get stuck; feel freedom and get scared — "А кто я без всех этих задач?" (And who am I without all these tasks?); or be so stunned that reality seems even greyer — "Хочу теперь всегда только так!" (Now I want it like this always!) — and again directedness and contraction [#1466].

Two safeguards ("2 achievements of modern civilisation" to bring into purposeless spaces):
1. Modern ethics of inner work. Example: someone opened up at tantra and then "flew off" somewhere where effectiveness in ordinary affairs became many times lower. So we learn not to go over the edge without resources, not to chase breakthroughs, not to torment body and psyche, but to create a careful, safe environment [#1466].
2. Attention and awareness to the process itself. Example: you walked in the mountains for a week, and a day later at work you are running on the usual rails "как вагон РЖД" (like a Russian Railways carriage). So add facilitation — external (specially trained people) or internal. Then we don't just reboot and have something happen to us; we understand in detail what changes our state, learn to enter it faster and more safely, and take it with us instead of dissolving it after 2 days, as often happens without good integration [#1466].

Summary line: spaces in which our state differs radically from the usual but do not overload us are "живая вода нашего роста" (the living water of our growth) and of solving the main life challenges [#1466].

#### Inner work as archaeology, architecture, and natural nature
Asked at a lecture whether inner work resembles archaeology or architecture, the author answered that his metaphor is closer to a dance or a path. Path/journey is a classic description of deep exploration or personality development in serious inner-work systems; dividing it into archaeology or architecture makes no sense to him, because "станцевать придется и с тем и другим, а может даже с чем-то третьим" (you'll have to dance with both, and maybe even with something third). He notes the question applies a metaphor to a complex, contradictory, non-linear process that "everyone now tries to measure with rulers and package into a product approach"; nothing especially bad in that, but inner work is "поле любви и смысла" (a field of love and meaning) — we can take far more from it than an x% productivity increase. The integral view is very useful for understanding such processes [#1044].

Three faces of inner work:
1. Archaeology. Every person's history has many knots that tangle and restrain growth — both the inner sense of freedom and quite external opportunities to achieve desired results. Going to therapy to untie them, we may within 5 minutes of the therapist's questions fall into long-forgotten times and do real archaeology [#1044].
2. Architecture. When searching to go beyond the ordinary we may land in systems that do such exploration; some contemplative traditions focus not on a person's history but on potential. Anger, fear and attachment are inherent in all of us, but we need not be conditioned only by them and can develop something else — neural and bodily habits that change us as a person, "ведь гнев и любовь на практике не могут существовать одновременно" (since anger and love in practice cannot exist simultaneously). Cultivation practices have helped produce incredible people; results of the construction can reach miracles — like Gaudí with the Sagrada Família, who perhaps also tried to show that more is available to a human than it seems [#1044].
3. Our deep, natural nature. Some methods and teachers point beyond any histories, conditioning or liberating qualities to something "удивительно простое и неусложненное никакими концепциями" (amazingly simple and uncomplicated by any concepts) — discoverable as a healing and already perfect Self beyond the need to heal or develop anything; it includes both archaeology and architecture and wholly transcends them, as the indescribable simplicity of sunlight on a child's face or a warm wind smelling of fresh flowers transcends any stories, ideas and problems. "Тут остается только танцевать" (Here all that remains is to dance) [#1044].

#### Spiral dynamics levels as used by the author (blue → orange → green → yellow)
The author uses spiral dynamics colours together with Torbert's action logics and Kegan's stages of adult development; he calls it "карта" (a map) built by Kegan at Harvard over forty years of academic research and by Torbert at Yale as "логики действия" (action logics) [#1823].

1. Blue — named only as "традиционных *синих* структурах" (traditional blue structures); disappointment in them is one of the experiences that forms yellow. (!) INCOMPLETE: the source does not describe blue further [#1823].
2. Orange — rational-efficient, the achievers. The logic of optimization, productivity and status. It believes complex systemic problems are solved by "правильными метриками и достаточно умными людьми" (the right metrics and smart enough people). In the AI era orange competences — analysis, optimization, expertise — are being automated, so orange "теряет монополию на успех" (loses its monopoly on success). The author calls the current moment a "crisis of achievement-ism" (кризис достигаторства) and says orange ambitions are becoming less fashionable. Per his field observations, Russian-speaking entrepreneurs in the US are mostly "крепко застряли на *рационально-эффективном*" (firmly stuck on the rational-efficient), which is why many welcomed Musk's and Thiel's manners and the right turn they initiated by supporting Trump — a turn the author judges failed, because politics, society and economy are more complex than what can easily be fixed by a worldview of infinite optimization; he says he predicted this ending a year and a half earlier but did not expect the turn to prove "всего лишь кочкой" (just a bump) so fast [#1798, #1823].
3. Green — post-hierarchical. A person builds a worldview on values of consensus, equality, anti-hierarchy and diversity; "Чувства становятся важнее правил, все точки зрения принимаются" (feelings become more important than rules, all points of view are accepted). What starts to matter is universal human nature rather than correctness and role in the hierarchy, productivity and status. Green believes systemic problems are solved by "правильными ценностями и достаточным состраданием" (the right values and enough compassion). Green is "not optimal" either, but "дает фору рациональному достигаторству" (gives a head start over rational achievement-ism). In the AI era green competences — empathy, attention, care — are "убедительно имитируются" (convincingly imitated). Its weakness: paralysis by the diversity of perspectives it notices. Its emergence foreshadows a mainstream shift toward "пост-продуктивности" (post-productivity), which the crisis of achievement-ism may accelerate [#1798, #1823].
4. Yellow — the scarce stage. It arises "не сразу, а через шаг после *оранжевой*" (not immediately but one step after orange), i.e. after green. It is "структура восприятия" (a structure of perception) formed through real experience: disappointment in traditional blue structures, orange ambitions and green ideals, "без потери уважения к тому, что каждый из нам дает" (without losing respect for what each gives us). It is a sense of the world as constantly interconnected and interdependent processes, where it becomes literally impossible to give the simple answers that used to satisfy. Yellow people hold complexity "в уме-теле" (in mind-body) without promising simple solutions like orange and without being paralysed by the diversity of perspectives like green. What stays genuinely scarce in the AI era is exactly what neither automation nor imitation reaches: seeing whole systems and their blind spots at once, working with contradictions without simplifying them, and acting where there is no right answer, "только более или менее зрелый выбор" (only a more or less mature choice) [#1823].

#### Three kinds of learning (what to choose and avoid in the coming years)
Purpose: choose a development environment in a time when reality changes so intensely that the right choice of environment decides whether one adapts or falls behind. Based on the author's 7 years in deep mind developing people in companies with top IT teams; he states "все обучение бывает трех видов" (all learning is of three kinds) [#1748].
1. Knowledge and frameworks — "relatively useless". Not necessarily bad, but it does not lead to change, so participants' time is spent very poorly: a person gets new facts, maybe interesting frameworks or theories, "И пошел жить прежную [sic] жизнь" (and went on living the old life). Most programmes are like this — not because bad people make them, but because creating real experience and insight is much harder than transmitting information [#1748].
2. Upskilling. Real skills appear, behaviour and results change; the current gold standard of good corporate (and other) learning. It can be applied to subtle skills too: deep mind breaks topics like state management or team communication into concrete skills developed through specific processes and practices, explains them in plain language rather than metaphors, and checks whether the skill level changed. Limitation: it is often insufficient for the key dead ends and most important challenges, which require not upskilling but a change of the subject who approaches them — a change of lens and of the complexity of the worldview, because our current complexity does not let us see some causal links or aspects of reality we do not notice or understand [#1748].
3. Transformational. The person approaching the tasks is reassembled; what changes is less the content of the mind or behaviour than the angle from which one sees reality. Especially needed in leadership roles with more novelty and complexity, in non-standard situations and in periods of high uncertainty — which, the author says, is now; and leadership is needed not only as CEO but in one's own life [#1748].

Author's observation from practice: learning built around meta-skills cannot stay only at level two. One can try to ignore the transformational level (as some providers and corporate learning leads do), but then not only is perhaps the most important part of the effect lost, the person risks being "словно подвешенным в воздухе" (as if suspended in the air): transformation starts but stays in a blind spot, and its incompleteness often leads to painful problems. Non-traumatizing, sustainable transformation needs a competent environment — "safe and brave space или профессионально удерживаемый контейнер" (a safe and brave space or a professionally held container) [#1748].

Recommendation: look this year at least not for the first type, and better for a combo of the second and third, "Ибо сейчас становится слишком дорого тратить время просто на знания или только на навыки, не затрагивающие самого субъекта" (because it is now too expensive to spend time on mere knowledge or only on skills that do not touch the subject himself) [#1748].

#### Inner capital / psychological pension — the age equation
Thesis (from developmental/age psychology, per the author): "Чем дольше человек живёт без внутренней работы, тем чаще его невротические паттерны начинают доминировать над всей системой" (the longer a person lives without inner work, the more often neurotic patterns dominate the whole system) [#1630].
- Mechanism: not mysticism but natural dynamics — with age cognitive flexibility drops, neuroplasticity decreases, and defensive strategies and habitual reactions strengthen [#1630].
- The equation: on one side, years in which a person only reinforced habits and patterns without ever working on them directly; on the other, the gradual decline of resources that used to compensate. The author's four examples of failing compensations: anxiety used to be ploughed under by active work, but demand for you may no longer be the same; steam was let off with alcohol, but the body now tolerates it terribly; sport made you feel good, but it no longer comes easily; emotions were escaped in endless motion, but energy is significantly lower [#1630].
- Symptoms at 55, 60, 65: fear of flying suddenly stronger than the wish to travel; attachment to home or a pet becomes almost phobic; outbursts of anger, anxiety and irrational fears seem strange to others; relationships become an endless cycle of mutual claims. On the surface it looks like it came "на пустом месте" (out of nowhere), but it is entirely lawful [#1630].
- Missed crises surface: unlived age crises skipped earlier did not vanish; they waited until old defences stopped holding them [#1630].
- Narrowing window: the saddest part — by then a person may have almost no psychological space left to turn toward himself, not because he is bad, but because the window for change narrows with age: beliefs harden, strategies become more automatic, openness to the new drops [#1630].
- Conclusion: inner work, if based on genuinely effective and proven tools, is not a trend for momentary relief but a key factor shaping life in maturity — an important, "пусть и не всегда надёжной" (if not always reliable), insurance against the "разбитого корыта" (broken trough). It is "стратегическое преимущество" (a strategic advantage) determining whether at 50, 60, 70 you stay alive, flexible, warm, capable of relationships and interest in life, or become hostage to defensive strategies that once helped survive but now cause terrible suffering to you and others [#1630].
- Irony noted: inner work is most actively rejected ("это всё чушь / для слабаков / со мной-то всё в порядке" — "it's all nonsense / for the weak / I'm fine") by precisely those who are hit hardest by its absence. Good news: meditation and psychotherapy work at any age — maybe less effectively than before, but they always leave a chance for change and growth [#1630].

#### The integral approach: definition and value
- Definition: "Интегральный подход — это мета-картина человеческого опыта и знания, которая стремится собрать воедино то, что разные культуры, науки и духовные традиции поняли о развитии человека." (The integral approach is a meta-picture of human experience and knowledge that seeks to bring together what different cultures, sciences and spiritual traditions have understood about human development.) [#1544]
- Main idea: "никакая перспектива не обладает полной истиной, но каждая содержит часть истины" (no perspective possesses the full truth, but each contains part of the truth). The integral view does not replace specific disciplines (psychology, sociology, neurobiology, spiritual practices) but creates a "map" that helps reconcile them and see the whole; it helps look at a person systemically and holistically rather than get stuck in a one-sided view [#1544].
- It is not a new school or set of techniques. Its value is that it:
  1. gives a meta-language in which different disciplines can talk to each other;
  2. helps avoid reductionism (e.g. reducing everything to biology only or to spirituality only);
  3. opens a perspective of holistic development of the person and society [#1544].
- Who benefits from adding the integral lens, per the author:
  1. Helping practitioners — to see not only the individual psyche but cultural context, embodiment and spiritual aspects;
  2. Leaders and organisations — to design development systems for people and companies that account not only for results but also for values, culture and people's states, and to understand why employees or groups act so differently;
  3. Personal seekers and practitioners — to orient better among the many approaches, from psychotherapy to meditation, from philosophy to body techniques [#1544].
- Wilber is described as the founding father of the integral approach and probably of integral psychology, who has written about 25 books and now rarely appears in public due to age and health; DiPerna is a student of Wilber and Daniel Brown. The author considers Wilber and DiPerna key living experts on vertical development [#1544].

#### Responsibility for one's own pain as real adulthood
From facilitating intensive group and individual therapy processes with Dasha, the author sees people leave habitual neurotic patterns and see the same events "словно из другой точки" (as if from another point): "Линзы нехватки сменяются линзами любви и наполненности" (lenses of lack are replaced by lenses of love and fullness). When transformation is truly deep, compassion, acceptance and a sense of connectedness arise — not as beautiful words but phenomenologically, as direct, maximally real experience; the unity of wisdom and compassion becomes available, letting one see the world from maximal fullness [#1817].

This shift is felt every time as maturity, a real adult position, and its source is always one: when a person has enough resource and courage to take responsibility for their pain and dissatisfaction "*именно на себя*" (on oneself specifically) — to stop presenting it to parents, children or partners. Caveat from the author: this does not mean accepting any behaviour of others toward you, but answering for your own feelings [#1817].

This vulnerability and kindness contradict the usual image of adulthood: "*детям в оболочке взрослых тел, привыкшим терпеть. И считающих это отвественностью.*" (children in adult bodies, used to enduring, and considering this responsibility). Such an inner architecture looks like years of inability to address the source of problems directly. Most people never take responsibility for their feelings and lose to their weaknesses for decades, turning this into tyranny at home, inability to build vulnerable trusting relationships, into wars and violence. Mechanism: unlived, unintegrated pain and nervous-system dysregulation become external — transmitted to people around and harming their wellbeing; the author calls this one of the strongest mechanics shaping our world [#1817].

The alternative: people doing the hardest work requiring courage and vulnerability, often in tears, looking straight at pain and finding outer and inner strength to be with it instead of fleeing. The author suggests Richard Schwartz would probably say such changes come from access to Self — the place in us from which one can relate with compassion ("со страданием" [sic] in the source) and kindness to the most unsightly parts we usually deny, hate and fear [#1817].

#### Bodhicitta and the selfish start of inner work
We often approach inner work with a rather selfish motive — to feel better. The author believes its results spread far beyond us, changing families and society: having healed pain and changed a pattern, we stop passing it on and multiplying needless unhappiness in the flow of life after us; "Действие, направленное на себя, на деле исцеляет бескрайнее количество существ" (an action directed at oneself actually heals countless beings). In Buddhism this is bodhicitta — an unselfish intention and way of living to be useful to others, free them from suffering and lead them to happiness, an intention leading beyond our perpetual self-fixation from which we ourselves suffer. Bodhicitta is "не техника" (not a technique) or tool to make inner work easier, but "самораспаковывающееся видение мира" (a self-unpacking vision of the world) that becomes available in genuine growth and healing. Still, at any stage we gain greatly by approaching our inner path with this motivation: going to the most painful places "без закрытого забрала" (with visor up), embracing them, remembering it is done not only for oneself but for an unimaginable number of people [#1817].

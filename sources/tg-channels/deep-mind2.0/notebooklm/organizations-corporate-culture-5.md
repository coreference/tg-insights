## Self-management and new organization models

### What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's posts on moving away from machine-like, control-heavy companies toward organic, self-managing organizations: the mechanistic vs organic distinction, Frederic Laloux's Teal organizations and how they are misunderstood, Arnold Mindell's deep democracy with its Open Forums, Netflix's management by context, the psychological obstacle of transference in self-organizing teams, and Sociocracy 3.0. A thread running through all of them is the channel's insistence that structure and procedures are not enough: the transition requires the inner maturation of leaders and team members, plus special supportive spaces and processes. According to the source, organic companies are more effective, easier to manage and "will win" on complex, fast markets [#116, #205, #418, #875].

### Core ideas

- Organic beats mechanistic. A mechanistic company breaks like a chair with a broken leg; an organic one keeps working like a hand that lost a finger, because its parts are interconnected and interchangeable. Companies, unlike objects, can make this transition. Full detail in *Mechanistic vs organic company* [#116].
- Teal is about the leader's maturity, not structure. Copying the structure and procedures of Teal companies misses the point; the decisive and hardest step is the leader's growing up and change of worldview. Full detail in *Two key misunderstandings about Teal* [#205].
- Power does not disappear in self-management. In Teal, power becomes distributed and transparent but does not vanish; nothing is wrong with power itself, even in hierarchy — the problem is immature people who abuse it [#205].
- Structures without the "living component" suppress life. Rules give stability, but ignoring emotions, feelings, trust and openness makes organizations depressive, aggressive and unproductive; deep democracy adds awareness of feelings and atmosphere to existing rules [#383].
- Self-organization fails because of transference. People confuse present relationships with past ones (bosses, parents); declaring "no hierarchy" is insufficient without long experience in a new environment and work on awareness of transference [#418].
- Context instead of control, if talent is dense. Management by context gives far more autonomy and trains decision-making, but is harder; which model to choose depends first on the concentration of talent in the team [#442].
- Exponential organizations follow different rules. In the information economy old barriers, USPs and business models become a burden; success rests on seven components — a transformative purpose, community, a balanced founding team, a 10x technology, Lean iteration, outsourcing infrastructure to the community and maximum flexibility. Full detail in *Seven key components of an exponential organization's success* [#226, article #226].
- The Teal wave was a false start. Many founders were disappointed, but possibly because few had the personal integrity, skills and support for the transition; three trends may bring a stronger return of self-management [#875].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Mechanistic vs organic company (chair vs hand metaphor)
Purpose: a five-point diagnostic and argument posed as the question "Are we building a mechanistic or an organic company?" ("Мы строим механистическую или органическую компанию?") [#116].

1. The mechanism. A chair stops performing its function when its leg breaks; a car can explode because of an engine fault. A mechanism breaks when one of its parts fails. There is no sense of inner interconnectedness and no ability of some parts to perform the functions of others. In organizations with a mechanistic consciousness, sales staff think only about sales and accountants only about accounting. Only managers can take care of the organization's work as a whole — and employees often find those managers inconvenient and resist their efforts [#116].
2. The organism. A hand does not stop grasping when one finger breaks. Each finger can substitute for another and works in the interest of the whole organism. Organic systems are built interconnected and interchangeable, and they know how to integrate the interests of all stakeholders [#116].
3. The transition is possible for companies. Unlike objects, companies can move from the mechanical type to the organic. If every leg of the chair understood and accepted the benefits of belonging to the system, the chair would acquire an organic consciousness: it would stop being merely a broken chair, because each element would take on part of the consequences of the breakage and do everything possible to keep it functioning. "Стул бы стал подобен руке" (The chair would become like a hand) [#116].
4. Why organic matters. For a company to work effectively in the long run on a complex and fast market, teamwork must be organic — where everyone supports everyone, nobody is irreplaceable, and everyone thinks in the interest of the whole system. Organic consciousness gives proactivity and the ability to act like an entrepreneur, i.e. to anticipate customers' needs and position oneself to meet them. For this the company must be creative and ready to take risks. Such companies have real values and living goals. The author claims they have proven more effective than mechanistic ones, are easier to manage, and "Они победят" (They will win) (the source gives no data for this claim) [#116].
5. The leader must grow up. For a company's leader to decide to build an organic company, he has to mature: learn to see interconnections, learn to manage his emotions, become aware of his deep goals and values. According to the author, nobody has ever managed to grow up without inner work, acknowledging that the outer depends on the inner, and personal crises. Psychotherapeutic and meditative practices accelerate maturation and make it less painful [#116].

#### Two key misunderstandings about Teal
Context: Andrey Grigoriev, founder of the modern marketing lab Deep Up and someone the author calls close to him in values, asked the author to expand on Frederic Laloux and his concept of living Teal organizations, which, in the author's view, many have understood not quite correctly ("многие поняли не совсем верно") [#205].

1. Misunderstanding: "To build a living company you need to change the structure and introduce the procedures typical of such companies." The author's answer: "Это нифига не так" (*That's not so at all*, colloquial) — Teal requires the leader's growing up, a change in his thinking and view of the world. This is the most key and most difficult part, and a really big evolutionary step [#205].
2. Misunderstanding: "Teal is a way to get rid of power in the company." Power as a phenomenon has caused many people pain, and some want universal equality and the absence of power; such people often strive to create cooperatives, self-organizations and Teal. But this cannot be done: in Teal, power becomes distributed and transparent but does not disappear. "В самой власти нет ничего плохого даже в иерархии" (There is nothing bad in power itself, even in hierarchy). What is bad is the immaturity of people who abuse power and are unaware of the violence they inflict on others [#205].

#### Deep democracy: from hidden problems to a productive atmosphere
Author: Inna Laletina (deep mind; process facilitator, Portland process work center). Subtitle: how to move from hidden problems to a productive atmosphere [#383].

1. Rules vs feelings (the problem). Social norms and rules help society function: they give a sense of stability and safety, help people survive, interact and solve the tasks they face. Such structures are necessary for both society and organizations. But without the living component — emotions, feelings, trust and openness — organizations and communities start to work worse. People often experience states and emotions that do not fit current structures and norms. If this living component is ignored, structures begin to suppress life itself: a depressive state appears, the atmosphere becomes aggressive and unproductive. When *facts* become more important than *feelings and dreams*, people get bored or sad: they do not want to attend general meetings, relationship problems are avoided, and interest in work and communication is lost [#383].
2. The philosophy of deep democracy. Created by Arnold Mindell, a physicist and world-level conflict facilitator, it helps heal a society or organization. First of all it declares the importance of all people and all feelings ("важности всех людей и всех чувств"). To the organization's existing orders, rules and procedures it adds awareness of feelings and atmosphere in the moment [#383].
3. Raise the level of awareness in the organization. Create a culture in which it is normal to notice and share inner experiences [#383].
4. Work continuously on Diversity and Inclusion (D&I). That is, on the organization's openness and flexibility toward the diversity of its members: their culture, age, gender, nationality, religion, economic status, abilities, experience and emotions [#383].
5. Open Forums as the place for deep democracy. Many have understood that personal work requires special processes and spaces — meditation practices, coaching, psychotherapy, retreats. Organizations need the same to solve a complex challenge of modernity: integrating emotions and diverse cultures into an effective work process; hence special spaces and processes are created at the collective level, in organizations and communities. Deep democracy proposes Open Forums ("Открытые Форумы") — meetings where every participant feels heard. Why it works: when a space is created where every voice is heard and it is safe, the natural flow and diversity of life are not blocked; this creates a favorable and productive atmosphere and, above all, a more sustainable one, because the organization stops generating aggression by ignoring others' opinions and feelings [#383].

#### Special work required for self-organization (transference)
Purpose: explains "why self-organization does not work" and what must be done when building new collective systems [#418].

The psychological fact. "Человеческое сознание часто очень плохо разделяет реальные текущие отношения с опытом отношений в прошлом" (Human consciousness often separates real current relationships from the experience of past relationships very poorly). This is the phenomenon of transference (перенос), known to psychologists, which implies that no relationship is new to us; all are colored by previous connections. At work it means an employee perceives a manager through all of his previous experience with bosses, and even deeper through the prism of relationships with the earliest and longest caregivers — the parents. "Часто, неосознанно мы путаем людей, время и место" (Often, unconsciously, we confuse people, time and place) [#418].

Consequence. If a person had many authoritarian, suppressing relationships in the past, difficulties with being at ease in democratic leadership models are likely. The author offers this as the explanation of why the Russian people could not simply take the freedom Gorbachev was handing out at the time. Therefore, when building a flat/agile/self-organizing team, it is not enough to proclaim the absence of hierarchy or an equal distribution of power; one also has to work on overcoming the habitual way of being in work relationships. People simply cannot switch out of the blue ("с бухты барахты") to fundamentally different power relationships without special work — especially in Russia, with its sad experience of traumatizing authoritarian and totalitarian systems [#418].

The special work must include:
1. Long and stable experience of living in the new environment. Creating this environment requires supportive transitional spaces — communities, labs, trainings — where a person can safely try new models of behavior. The author notes deep mind does this in Russia via community courses, retreats and in-company projects [#418].
2. Work on awareness of transferences. The person becomes aware of the link between past and present and understands how he or she sometimes overlays not-quite-adequate patterns from the past onto present relationships. Both leaders and other team members must do this work, and it is done within psychoanalytic psychotherapy. The author adds a hedge: he does not know of another therapy aimed this concretely and directly at the task, "но может она и есть" (but maybe there is one) [#418].

Example and recommendation. The author knows startups in Moscow whose key team, with all founders, undergo psychoanalytic therapy. He suggests that probably everyone building fundamentally new ways of doing things together should take care of this approach together with the mandatory creation of supportive spaces — at minimum, professional facilitation of open communication in teams. For going deeper, he points to the clinical approach to leadership and organizations of Manfred Kets de Vries, INSEAD professor [#418].

#### Management by control vs management by context (Netflix)
Source: a summary of a Forbes article the team made for themselves and shared; the post says Netflix prioritizes innovation over efficiency and does not burden its team with control mechanisms. This culture, with its relentless attention to talent and readiness for situational rather than normative decisions, is what lets Netflix keep growing and changing [#442].

1. Control. The leader approves and directs his team's initiatives, projects and decisions. Sometimes he intervenes directly in the work process. In other cases he can give subordinates broader authority, avoiding direct intervention, but in exchange establishes various control procedures [#442].
2. Context. Managing with context is much harder, but employees get far more autonomy. The leader's task is to provide the team with all necessary information so that everyone can make balanced decisions and do their job without constant supervision. The main advantage: the employee "прокачивает навык" (levels up the skill) of decision-making and later can act independently [#442].

Example — a 16-year-old son who started going to parties with older friends, and you worry every time. Two ways to solve it:
- *Control mechanism:* you decide yourself which parties he attends, monitor his behavior, control everything about the party; if you say yes, you still activate location tracking on his phone to make sure he went exactly where he said [#442].
- *Management by context:* carefully talk through the context so you are on the same wave; once sure he understands how great the danger is, you let him go to any party without restricting or tracking his movements. If the son already messed up in the past and you do not trust him, you will most likely prefer strict control; but if you know he is reasonable and reliable, you can outline the context and count on responsible behavior. That way you teach him to make adequate decisions not only at parties but under pressure in general and in the face of any future temptation [#442].

#### Choosing a management model by talent density
Rule from the same post: when choosing a management model, the first question to answer is the concentration of talent in the team. If your staff struggle to solve assigned tasks, you will have to watch the work process vigilantly and take key decisions yourself. If you have assembled a team of real virtuosos, they surely want freedom, and such a team is better managed with context [#442].

#### Three reasons self-management may return
Context: a few years ago, new horizontal management models boomed; Frederic Laloux contributed significantly to the hype with the model of Teal organizations. In his book he presented an organization where interaction between employees, decision-making and company structure are built on more flexible and collective principles; he studied various organizational cultures and concluded that Teal organizations are the next stage in the evolution of corporate structures. Since then the hype has subsided, and many founders became disappointed: they tried to implement the new paradigm and something went wrong. The post suggests this was possibly a false start ("фальстарт"): not many had enough personal integrity, skills and support for such an important transition [#875].

The post names at least three fundamental reasons why a more pronounced trend may lie ahead (hedged with "возможно" — *possibly*):
1. Growing awareness. The general consciousness of leaders and employees is rising, which "безусловна" [sic] is a condition for self-management [#875].
2. Changing work culture. The demand for inclusion in decision-making and for autonomy only increases [#875].
3. AI. The development of AI may influence the popularity and spread of Teal models, because it can help structure and systematize their implementation individually for each company [#875].

#### Logic of simplification/division/reduction vs logic of living systems
The slide attached to the Sociocracy 3.0 post (from Ivan Molchanov's lecture) contrasts two pictures connected by an arrow pointing from left to right [#875]:
- Left — "ЛОГИКА УПРОЩЕНИЯ/ ДЕЛЕНИЯ/РЕДУКЦИИ" (logic of simplification / division / reduction): drawn as a classic tree hierarchy — one node at the top, three below it, each with subordinates, several levels deep, all the same blue color [#875].
- Right — "ЛОГИКА ЖИВЫХ СИСТЕМ" (logic of living systems): drawn as overlapping circles of different sizes and colors, nested and interconnected by lines in many directions rather than a single chain of command [#875].
The arrow indicates the intended movement from the first logic to the second [#875].

#### Exponential organizations: the book and why the author rates it
The channel recommends the book *Взрывной рост. Почему экспоненциальные организации в 100 раз эффективнее вашей (и что с эти [sic] делать)* (Explosive growth: why exponential organizations are 100 times more effective than yours, and what to do about it*; the linked longread gives the subtitle as "в десятки раз продуктивнее вашей" — *tens of times more productive than yours) by Salim Ismail, head of Singularity University, and several entrepreneurs including Peter Diamandis. It came out in Russian five years late, like much other business literature. The author nevertheless believes it will become a management classic and is absolutely mandatory reading for all entrepreneurs and leaders who want to be part of the new world [#226].

The longread, written by Maxim Timofeev (Максим Тимофеев), frames the book personally: "Есть книги, которые меня сильно торкают" (There are books that really hit me). Last year such a book for him was Otto Scharmer's *Theory U*, after which he went to study with Scharmer in Germany at the Presencing Institute (spelled "Presenceing" in the source); this year *Взрывной рост* "однозначно претендует" (clearly claims) that status, and he now aims to go study at Singularity University [#226].

Why he considers it important [#226]:
- An upgrade of thinking. The book is "важный апгрейд нашей системы мышления" (an important upgrade of our system of thinking): it sheds light on many hidden processes of fast-growing startups — exponential organizations [#226].
- The contours of a new organizational world. The authors explain why many barriers and unique selling propositions (УТП) stop working in the information economy. More than that, many business models and USPs of the past no longer provide an advantage but become a "killing load" ("убивающим грузом") under the changed conditions [#226].
- Practical, not just theoretical. The book contains concrete recommendations for building an organization for the new economy — for startups, for large corporations and for leaders [#226].

#### Seven key components of an exponential organization's success
Purpose: the longread's list of "Ключевые поинты- составляющие успеха организации в новой экономике" (key points — components of an organization's success in the new economy), each with the book's example and, for several, how deep mind applies it to itself [#226].

1. Massive Transformative Purpose (Большая трансформирующая цель, MTP). A large purpose that transforms the field. Example: Google's goal is "Организовать всю имеющуюся в мире информацию" (To organize all the information available in the world). deep mind's own purpose "for now" is "Создать здоровое и осмысленное общество" (To create a healthy and meaningful society) [#226].
2. Building and actively involving a community in the company's development. Example: Chris Anderson's community DIY Drones, devoted to building drones, with more than 50,000 participants. The author says deep mind is moving in this direction "slowly but surely" [#226].
3. A balanced founding team of four roles: a visionary, an expert in customer interaction, a chief technologist and an experienced financier [#226].
4. A breakthrough idea or technology that improves the situation in some area at least tenfold ("в 10 раз и более"). For deep mind this is technologies for working with a person's state, based on mindfulness and body practices, which the author believes can greatly improve people's state and communication in companies many times over [#226].
5. A flexible business model of fast iterations that welcomes mistakes (Lean). In the fast economy, strategies and long-term planning will work worse and worse; what matters more is live and constant customer feedback. deep mind's practice: they analyze feedback after every contact with a client and adapt all products for each new launch [#226].
6. Shifting important infrastructure processes to the community, customers or external staff. Example: at Uber, matching a driver to a passenger happens outside the core structure [#226].
7. Maximum flexibility. The fewer employees on staff and the more non-key operations outsourced, the faster and the more flexible the company [#226].

### Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The claim that organic companies "have proven" more effective, are easier to manage and "will win" is stated without evidence in the post [#116].
- The Gorbachev explanation and the special difficulty attributed to Russia are the author's interpretation via transference, not a cited study [#418].
- The author hedges that he knows no therapy other than psychoanalytic psychotherapy aimed directly at transference, but allows one may exist; the recommendation for teams is prefaced with "Вероятно" (probably) [#418].
- The management-by-context material is a second-hand summary of a Forbes article about Netflix, not the channel's own research [#442].
- The forecast of a return of self-management is explicitly tentative ("возможно") [#875].
- The deep mind posts promote deep mind's own community courses, retreats and company projects as transitional spaces; readers should note this interest [#418].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: the Open Forum procedure and the Sociocracy 3.0 content are not in the provided source [#383, #875].
- (!) CONTRADICTION: the post's book title says exponential organizations are "в 100 раз эффективнее" (100 times more effective), while the longread gives the subtitle as "в десятки раз продуктивнее" (tens of times more productive) [#226, article #226].
- (!) CONTRADICTION: the ranking is captioned "ТОП 50" but the attached images list 100 companies; the source of the ranking is written "WIERD" (possibly a misspelling of Wired — (!) UNCLEAR) [#226].
- The applications of the seven components to deep mind (its MTP, mindfulness/body-practice technology as a 10x idea, feedback analysis after every client contact) are the author's statements about his own company [#226].

## Team trust, feedback and conflict

### What this covers

This file collects what the deep mind channel (a mindful-leadership consultancy that facilitates teams) teaches about the foundations of teamwork: trust and psychological safety, how to give and receive feedback, how to handle conflict (cognitively, culturally and through the body), how a culture of perfectionism damages teams and what replaces it, and the conditions for shared "team flow". According to the source, communication quality is what decides whether a group is a team at all: without functioning communication, a group's productivity equals or is even lower than the sum of its members working separately, and without synergy "it is most likely not a team at all" [#92]. In modern companies that move from hierarchy to flexible cross-functional teams, the author says communication quality "obviously comes to the forefront" [#342].

### Core ideas

- Trust is the glue of synergy. Truly effective information exchange happens only when people can say honestly and directly, in the moment, what they like, dislike and worry about; trust is the simpler, more human route to this than complex business processes. See *Trust: definition, economics and how to start building it* [#92, #405].
- All quality-communication definitions reduce to two things: safety and trust, which are 100% interconnected. See *Two pillars of quality communication* [#342].
- Feedback is the engine of organisational evolution (action - error - feedback - correction), but the brain perceives negative feedback as an attack, so feedback must be designed around human nature. See *Simple 3-step feedback frame* [#128].
- Both delicate silence and unconstructive criticism are evils. The goal is a culture of constructive openness, honesty and directness that neither harms the person nor withholds important information; teams that achieve it win through speed of learning [#420, #439].
- Conflict resolution is physiology, not philosophy. Conflict triggers fight-or-flight, which perpetuates the conflict; embodied strength and softness have to be trained [#437].
- Perfectionism culture is a bias that harms business, creating fear and risk avoidance; antidotes are appreciation, learning from mistakes and separating the person from the mistake [#1074].
- Team flow is a real performance factor with seven preconditions, a bright and a dark side [#1543].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### The evolutionary loop: action - error - feedback - correction
Purpose: explains why feedback matters more than any "super method". The author states that nobody possesses a super method or knowledge that will guarantee development and sustainability in the long run; what matters more is the speed of testing reality and the quality of feedback rather than "some cool knowledge". An organisation's resilience in the global economy rests on the constant use of the key principle of evolution: «действия - ошибка - обратной связь - коррекция» (actions - error - feedback - correction). People cannot be excluded from this process, so "we will have to learn to give each other timely feedback" [#128].

#### Why negative feedback fails: the defensive reaction
When we hear negative feedback about our work, it is perceived as criticism. The amygdala perceives it as an attack; a defensive reaction starts and the ability to accept arguments impartially decreases. To be "part of evolution, not its barrier", one has to handle this challenge and start accounting for human nature — stress reactions, defence mechanisms and emotions [#128]. The post supports this with a quotation from Ray Dalio: most people have no idea how others perceive a situation and do not even try to understand another's way of thinking, because they are too busy proving they are right; people are closed and rely solely on their own beliefs, and this bias costs them dearly — they miss opportunities and ignore threats [#128].

#### Simple 3-step feedback frame
Purpose: a simple frame for delivering feedback that, according to the author, bypasses ego barriers. The author describes it as "one of the simple feedback frames" [#128].
1. Feedback is given with permission. Ask first: «Мне надо дать тебе обратную связь. Могу?» (I need to give you feedback. May I?). The person may refuse. Why: it reduces the "attack" perception described above [#128].
2. First, what is going / turning out well. Start with what works [#128].
3. Then, what can be improved [#128].

Why it works, per the author: it is very simple and therefore applicable in practice. If the frame is held «от души» (from the heart), i.e. with a sincere desire to understand the problem and help, "we fully bypass ego barriers and can transmit information with quality". This is how people develop together and move organisations forward, instead of getting stuck in "crossed ego positions" that leave only anger and disunity [#128].

#### Two pillars of quality communication: safety and trust
The author says that when trying to describe truly quality communication, deep mind uses many definitions, but "honestly" they can all be reduced to two points: 1. Safety (Безопасность) and 2. Trust (Доверие). They are 100% interconnected: there will never be trust in a team if members do not feel complete freedom to openly voice their points of view and discuss any tension or joys. Therefore, to create modern forms of communication in teams, one must start first of all with safety and trust [#342].

The author defines the target communication: modern teams must learn to communicate openly, quickly, clearly and at the same time empathically. Effective communication transmits information clearly and quickly without distortion and simultaneously performs a seemingly opposite task — uniting and binding people into a team [#342].

#### Principles of effective feedback in teams (deep mind workshop principles)
Purpose: the principles deep mind relies on when running communication and feedback workshops in companies [#439].

Two foundational principles:
1. To build a culture of candour, employees must learn to express their opinion honestly and directly. To do so they must act with the best intentions and in a constructive form. Feedback is given not to hurt the other but to help productively sort out all processes, thoughts, feelings and behaviour [#439].
2. Focus not on problems but on the goals of quality communication. Teams often have problems with psychological safety and toxic communication, and one can over-focus on that problem because it is significant and painful. But it is more important to focus on the desired future possible in a culture of candour — a future where the team creates a brilliant product. To steer there, people must speak faster and more clearly up, down and horizontally about what is good and what is bad [#439].

General principles of effective communication (numbered as in the source, including two "zero" rules):
- 0. Delicate silence is lame — «Деликатное молчание — ацтой» [#439].
- 0. Unconstructive, non-actionable criticism is lame — «Неконструктивная неприменимая критика — ацтой» [#439].
1. Don't like it — say so [#439].
2. Like it — say so [#439].
3. Speak immediately, or when it brings maximum benefit [#439].
4. Speak often [#439].
5. Understand whether the person is ready to receive feedback now and in what form it is most useful [#439].
6. Speak from the intention to help and from kindness [#439].
7. Speak extremely concretely and actionably, with examples and without generalisations [#439].
8. It is crucially important that criticism flows bottom-up [#439].
9. For that, leaders must every time encourage, verbally and non-verbally, the candid behaviour of their employees [#439].

Why, per the source: the more employees hear about what needs fixing, the better they handle their tasks; there are fewer behind-the-scenes ("under the carpet") discussions and work speeds up considerably. Most importantly, freely flowing information helps the team reflect on its experience and learn faster than others [#439]. Recommended book: see *Tools and resources*.

#### Three deficits that become sources of conflict (Asmolov)
From an article by psychologist Alexander Asmolov (a professor at the author's university) about deficits in society and conflicts of our time, the author lists three key deficits that become sources of conflicts [#386]:
1. Deficit of meaning (дефицит смысла) [#386].
2. Deficit of trust (дефицит доверия) [#386].
3. Deficit of understanding (дефицит понимания) [#386].

(!) INCOMPLETE: the post names the three deficits but does not explain each one.

Asmolov's proposed resolution of deep modern conflicts is a change in the very optics through which we look at the world: in various civilised countries there is a slow transition away from the conflict model, in which history of different eras is perceived through the optics of wars and battles. The alternative is the «оптика толерантности» (optics of tolerance), where tolerance is understood as support for the norms of life's diversity, not as mere endurance ("терпимость"). Tolerance is the opposite of xenophobia understood as elimination, i.e. destruction of any otherness [#386].

Example of productive leadership communication quoted from the article: during the pandemic, Canada's prime minister was not afraid, almost every day, to answer questions from the country's residents; he went out to the streets and talked with fellow citizens to defuse conflict-prone situations and minimise panic. If he did not know how to help a person, he said: «Давайте вернемся к вашему вопросу завтра. Пришлите информацию, и мы сделаем все, чтобы вам помочь» (Let's return to your question tomorrow. Send the information and we will do everything to help you). The article calls these examples of a worthy exit beyond functional and role programmes and standard ways of making decisions. The author invites comparing this with Putin's actions today, and adds his own opinion that it is doubtful "our siloviki" (security officials) will ever have access to such optics [#386].

#### Embodied intelligence skills for masterful conflict resolution (Andrey Kazakov, after Paul Linden)
Purpose: a guest post by Andrey Kazakov (entrepreneur, business trainer, embodiment facilitator, head of the My.Dojo project) for the Deep mind community. Kazakov's premise: conflict resolution usually focuses on information between the parties — what we think and say. That matters, but one element is always present and usually ignored: our body [#437].

Mechanism, quoting Paul Linden (Aikido master and trauma therapist, book *Embodied Peacemaking*): conflict causes a physiological fight-or-flight reaction, and this physiological state makes us think and behave in a way that perpetuates and escalates the conflict. However peaceful a person wants to be, their ability to think, speak and act peacefully will be undermined if their body is not in a calm state. Kazakov's conclusion: peacemaking, or effective conflict resolution, «это не философия, а физиология и навыки телесного интеллекта» (is not philosophy but physiology and skills of embodied intelligence) [#437].

The four skills:
1. Strength / sturdiness (Прочность). Sturdiness is often associated with rigidity, which is considered a source of power and readiness to confront threats. Rigidity arises when we tense our muscles; acting with that tension in the body is like driving a car with the handbrake on. It does not help us hear the other side or be flexible in resolving the situation. Real sturdiness increases flexibility. It is created by attention to the body's structure — weight distribution, straightness of the spine. By directing attention to this structure we can bring it into balance and maintain it that way. From this arises power (сила) for action [#437].
2. Softness (Мягкость). Softness is often associated with powerlessness ("struck on one cheek — turn the other"). From the point of view of physiology or embodied intelligence, the processes that lead to powerlessness are shrinking of body size, constricted breathing, muscle tension, stiffness and narrowing of posture. Real softness in the body is above all relaxation and warmth. In such a body we can more fully accept the other's point of view, and from acceptance and warmth in our actions arises love (любовь) [#437].
3. Integrated application of softness and sturdiness. In peaceful — and therefore effective — conflict resolution we cannot rely on only one of these skills. Linden: «Любовь без силы слабая и неэффективная» (Love without power is weak and ineffective), «а сила без любви грубая и жесткая» (and power without love is crude and harsh); «сила – это основа способности любить, а любовь – основа мудрого использования силы» (power is the basis of the ability to love, and love is the basis of the wise use of power). Therefore we must learn to combine love and power — not just cognitively, but by bodily embodying sturdiness and softness [#437].
4. Training bodily awareness. Tense moments are called that because of our natural automatic reaction: physiologically we tense and contract muscles. To apply love and power in practice in a conflict, one must learn to act freely and creatively. Regular mindfulness training helps avoid falling into automatism: we pay attention to our state, checking it, and then consciously learn to relax and expand in a stress situation [#437].

#### Quick body-based conflict reframing exercise ("try it right now")
From the same post [#437]:
1. Mentally look at an average conflict situation of yours (not the worst one — «среднюю конфликтную ситуацию»).
2. Become aware of your body, posture and muscle tone.
3. Relax your tongue and belly.
4. Recall something or someone that makes you smile.
5. Look around, noticing the space around you.
6. Notice what has changed in your view of the conflict.

#### Manifestations of perfectionism culture in the workplace (7 signs)
The author's position: perfectionism culture seems necessary and important in companies, but he is "sure" that it is in many ways a bias that obstructs the path to a business where people flourish. Perfectionism creates a culture of fear and risk avoidance, disrupts teamwork, is harmful to health and, he says with a linked study, "provably" leads to reduced productivity. Best solutions come from a strong mind, not from inflated demands on reality and attempts to squeeze something out of it; glorifying perfectionism hinders everything from which the best version of us — individually and together — grows [#1074].

How it shows up at work:
1. Little appreciation is expressed for ordinary work, and the recognition that is expressed is unevenly skewed toward those who already get most of the credit [#1074].
2. People frequently point out that a person or their work is insufficient [#1074].
3. A person's or their work's shortcomings are often discussed with others without saying it to them directly [#1074].
4. Mistakes are taken personally: they shape how others perceive the person who made them, instead of being seen as what they are — mistakes [#1074].
5. Making a mistake is confused with being a mistake; doing something wrong is confused with being wrong [#1074].
6. Little time, energy or money is invested in reflection or identifying lessons learned that could improve work practice; little or no learning from mistakes [#1074].
7. An active tendency to point out what is wrong; no culture of seeing, naming and appreciating what is right [#1074].

#### Antidotes to perfectionism culture (6 practices leaders and companies already use)
1. Develop a culture of appreciation, where the organisation spends time making sure all work and effort of people is valued [#1074].
2. Develop learning organisations, in which everyone is expected to make mistakes and can grow from them instead [#1074].
3. Create an environment where people can acknowledge that mistakes sometimes lead to positive results [#1074].
4. Learn to separate the person from the mistake [#1074].
5. When giving feedback, always talk about what went well, because that is "always an existing part of reality" [#1074].
6. When offering criticism, ask people to give concrete suggestions on how to do things differently [#1074].

The author's conclusion: from an environment that is even slightly aware of its established culture and is freeing itself from blind submission to it, clinging to perfectionism as the main strength of business looks like a rather weak idea; he calls for a work environment where effort is valued, people learn from mistakes and grow together [#1074].

#### Seven preconditions of team flow
Definition: team flow is a flow experience shared by all participants, arising from optimal team dynamics while performing interdependent personal tasks for a common goal. "Shared" means several team members simultaneously experience flow, each in their own subtask, but within one common design and rhythm. Group flow emerges where several people suddenly find themselves in one rhythm, one dynamic, a single movement of thought and action [#1543].

The seven preconditions (English name with the original Russian gloss):
1. Collective ambition | общая амбиция — a shared "why" and internal motivation to be together as exactly this team; a feeling that working together is a value in itself [#1543].
2. Common goal | общая цель — a clear, shared, sufficiently challenging goal, understood by everyone and supported by all [#1543].
3. Aligned personal goals | согласованность с личными целями — personal goals are built into the common one: each person understands how their contribution leads to the common result and vice versa [#1543].
4. High skill integration | высокая интеграция навыков — knowing each other's strengths, complementary roles, distributing tasks so that each person's challenge-skill balance fits [#1543].
5. Open communication | открытая коммуникация — a constant "short loop" of feedback that is clear, timely and safe; empathic listening in the team and mutual attunement [#1543].
6. Safety | психологическая безопасность — one can take reasonable risks, err and learn; there is no fear of judgement or punishment, but respect and trust in intention and effort [#1543].
7. Mutual commitment | взаимная включённость — jointly holding focus: each on their own subtask and on the big picture; concentration and engagement in the process [#1543].

Example: Professor Jani Romanoff described how he and colleagues created a new research group. At first there was a lot of chaos and nothing was clear. But they regularly celebrated small successes and supported each other, and gradually this created an environment where everyone felt more confident. Then flow in the group began to grow and difficulties diminished. Lesson: small celebrated wins and mutual support build the confidence from which group flow grows [#1543].

### Detailed insights

#### Trust: definition, economics and how to start building it
- Why trust rather than processes. To reach in-the-moment, honest and direct communication (so the team does not stall, does not hide information, surfaces errors and can work with them), one can use complex business processes — or, simpler and more humanely, work on trust. "Trust is the most important glue that will give us synergy" [#92].
- First step for a leader. The first thing to do to create trust is to invite facilitators or personally hold a frank conversation with team members, showing leadership vulnerability — talking about one's own experiences and fears. For a systematic approach the author recommends studying Patrick Lencioni's model "5 dysfunctions of a team" («5 пороков команды»), which he calls a simple and effective framework for team building [#92]. (!) INCOMPLETE: the post recommends the Lencioni model but does not describe its five components.
- Trust requires a future. Ben Horowitz, entrepreneur and investor, writes: «Если я верю, что будущего нет, то доверие невозможно» (If I believe there is no future, trust is impossible). His example: slaves have no property and can lose everything at any moment without warning, which produces remarkable short-sightedness and a chase after momentary interests [#405].
- The author's extension. Understanding that everything can be taken away at any moment (or one can be fired on a whim) throws us back to the "beige level" of Spiral Dynamics — existing fixated on survival and fear. In that state we are prone to corruption and do not build sustainable economies and products [#405].
- Societal diagnosis (author's opinion). The author says that in Russian society trust outside local communities is currently extremely low, mainly due to the lack of real private property rights and independent courts, and that this requires enormous spending on propaganda [#405].
- Working definition. «Доверие в организациях и обществе = я верю, что отношения в будущем принесут мне больше выгоды, чем если предам вас сегодня» (Trust in organisations and society = I believe the relationship in the future will bring me more benefit than if I betray you today) [#405].
- Trust lowers transaction costs. Investing in building trust means lowering transaction costs and creating the foundation for managing an effective organisation or society, because «в любом человеческом взаимодействии объем необходимого общения обратно пропорционален уровню доверия» (in any human interaction the amount of necessary communication is inversely proportional to the level of trust) [#405].
- Absence of trust breeds control. If trust is absent, we have to introduce control mechanisms and strengthen bureaucracy at all levels. This obstructs creativity and innovation, lowers productivity and reduces the ability of an organisation or a country to adapt to market trends [#342].

#### Why communication is the core of the consultancy's work
The author realised the significance of communication for companies when he was responsible for all external communications at a large Russian exporter (ОЗК): mere words said to an Interfax or RBC journalist could cause difficulties for company executives and strain producers across the whole industry. Three years after leaving employment to create deep mind, he again mainly works on communication in corporate environments: in the consulting line (not educational programmes, but solving specific team and company tasks), communication is what they most often work on. The reason: they help create corporate cultures for the new economy, and when companies move away from rapidly obsolescent hierarchy toward flexible cross-functional teams, communication quality comes first [#342].

#### Feedback culture: the goal is candour, not just comfort
- Feedback is described as the "cornerstone" of team communication. The author cites research that feedback is one of the most effective ways to raise work quality and effectiveness, but few people can give or receive it without it becoming wounding criticism that toxically affects the micro-climate [#420].
- Critique of the psychologist/coach approach (author's opinion). Psychologists and coaches invited to help with a company's feedback culture will most likely focus on this "toxicity". Working with them makes people in the team feel morally easier and safer, "but in reality the team's goals are not achieved" [#420].
- Both product and humanity. Teams must make a quality product — comfortably and humanely. The first without the second means turnover and hell; the second without the first is pleasant and nice, "but the company will die" [#420].
- deep mind's stated goal. Not merely teaching employees not to traumatise each other with incorrect feedback (which the author calls definitely important, right and needed), but helping build «культуру конструктивной открытости, честности и прямолинейности» (a culture of constructive openness, honesty and directness) — communication that neither harms the person nor suppresses important information. In such a culture two things are simultaneously evil: unconstructive non-actionable criticism, and delicate silence [#420].
- Competitive mechanism. When information flows through all levels quickly and clearly, the team starts winning in the market in a "trickier" way than in classical business: by getting ahead through speed of learning and adaptation, not through a unique selling proposition, scale or efficiency [#420].

#### Feedback through self-regulation and mindfulness
- Unconstructive feedback, or the wrong amount of it (too rare or too frequent — the author links a study), negatively affects both team work and the whole company, from productivity to employer brand. Conversely, research shows feedback is one of the most effective ways to raise work quality; small adjustments in communication can be a strong game-changer in a team [#820].
- deep mind, facilitating teams, works on feedback culture primarily through developing self-regulation and mindfulness skills. The author calls this a science-backed approach, "though practically nobody uses it yet". He says: "we know for sure" that effective communication and learning cannot happen in a person's dysregulated state, and "toxicity" is always a manifestation of trauma, neurosis or strong stress [#820].
- Inner plus outer change. Truly strong change in an organisation is possible when internal and external processes work together: employees' skills and attitudes change, and organisational processes change too. Tools automating feedback address the second, process side (see *Tools and resources*) [#820].
- The author reports personally feeling "pain" from colliding with communication flows in many even advanced teams — the level of culture here is often very low — and holds that anything improving this area reduces stress and suffering of living people in companies [#820].

#### Destructive conflict patterns in the culture and the alternative
- Two extremes. The author observes that Russian society seems to have only two extremes: avoiding conflicts, or immediately jumping to the stage of war — attack ("говномет") or deaf defence, with resentments, violence and manipulation, depending on the level of emotional maturity. «Все кулуарно гниет и тухнет под столом, либо мгновенно превращается в срач» (Everything rots behind the scenes under the table, or instantly turns into a flame war). Neither option helps development [#840].
- Role models. In his opinion, high-ranking bosses (начальники), who inevitably act as role models for the bureaucracy and their electorate, badly influence the culture of communication in the country with their red lines and populism — a role model "not of dialogue but of bullying" («бычки»). He believes a new time is ahead and it is time to work on alternative conflict behaviour, at least in oneself [#840].
- Case — his own critique of a conference. After he criticised a Skolkovo conference, some advised him to practise more, since others' behaviour "hooks" him — mindfulness supposedly leads to accepting the world. Students of some meditation teachers from the panel wrote to him privately that their teachers are wonderful and enlightened and that he must urgently fix the situation. He notes he writes such critical posts only about once a year, partly because they fall into the conflict pattern: instead of "thanks, we've thought about it" they lead to "ugh, toxicity" or "you're the fool yourself", which he feels as the extreme of hushing up or fear of conflict. Similarly, a community discussion of a secular guru's promotion, he suspects, may trigger many people as if it were digging in someone else's laundry or gossip [#840].
- What healthy dialogue includes. He finds it hard to see how a field — and oneself as a client — can develop without healthy dialogue, which includes direct honest opinions, often unpleasant for someone and conflictual, and which helps growth — provided people speak constructively, not from emotional entanglement and not as a personal evaluation without clear argumentation. What matters is not whether a promotion is "approved", but the very fact and possibility of such discussions: "How else to figure things out? How to get important feedback?" [#840].
- Contrast example. Gabor Maté openly talks about Peterson's problems with anger and suggests he work on his shadow. If the author wrote something similar about a well-known Russian coach or psychotherapist, he says, "a rise of the machines and eternal personal war" would begin. He leaves open: what this says about us, where this pattern comes from, and what to do with it [#840].
- Personal stance. He asks readers to tell deep mind and him personally if they do something immature or unethical: everyone tries to do the best possible, nobody is insured against mistakes, and it is great to know about them. «Я приучаю себя любить неприятный фидбек, если он конструктивный» (I am training myself to love unpleasant feedback if it is constructive), and he tries not to see war and personal scores in everything. And so that wars really do not happen, it is also important not to stay silent about what one considers wrong, "as most of us did for 24 years" [#840].

#### Team flow: background, benefits and the dark side
- Background. In art and sport, group flow has long been recognised as an important factor for results, but in the work environment it is almost unstudied — though precisely there it may significantly affect effectiveness, and models describing its prerequisites and effects are emerging [#1543].
- Individual flow. The flow state was researched and described by psychologist Csikszentmihalyi: time disappears, thoughts flow freely, you fully dissolve in what you do; the work comes together by itself and the body seems to forget itself — you may not notice you haven't eaten for a long time or that several hours have passed. The author cautions it is wrong to confuse flow with the state of full presence cultivated in meditation, though flow has many benefits [#1543].
- Evidence of benefit. Research cited by the author shows that shared flow not only strengthens creativity in a group but also improves the quality of the common result — it is not just subjective enjoyment but a real factor making the outcome stronger and more valuable in the eyes of both group members and external experts [#1543].
- The dark side. Flow is usually described as positive — it makes work joyful and brings a sense of power and meaning. But in flow we work at the limit; this is exhausting and recovery may take time. Musicians, for example, get injured more often when playing in long flow states. So it is important not only to enter but also to exit flow: take pauses, do not promise more than you can deliver, accept that the rhythm sometimes breaks — "otherwise emptiness may follow euphoria" [#1543].
- What is special about shared flow. It differs from solitary flow: it has more warmth and belonging — "we stop being separate islands and become part of a common movement" [#1543]. The attached image only carries the "DEEP MIND" logo [#1543].

### Evidence and numbers

- Google, 180 teams (as cited by the author, referencing a New York Times article): team composition matters much less for performance than the way members interact. The key performance factors were quality of presence, trust and psychological safety. "Teams with a high level of trust bring Google the greatest profit" [#342].
- Trust and countries (author citing an OECD-hosted paper): research shows trust improves the functioning of organisations in every sense and even positively affects the socio-economic development of entire countries [#342].
- Feedback effectiveness (author citing a Wired article): feedback is one of the most effective ways to raise work quality and effectiveness [#420, #820].
- Feedback frequency (author citing a ScienceDirect paper): both too-rare and too-frequent feedback have negative effects [#820].
- Perfectionism (author citing a ResearchGate paper on perfectionism, work engagement, workaholism and burnout in employees): perfectionism provably reduces productivity [#1074].
- Team flow (author citing a PsycNET-listed model): shared flow raises group creativity and the quality of the result as judged by members and external experts [#1543].

### Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- (!) INCOMPLETE: Lencioni's "5 dysfunctions of a team" is recommended but its components are not described [#92].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: Asmolov's three deficits (meaning, trust, understanding) are named but not explained individually [#386].
- (!) CONTRADICTION (tension, not resolved by the source): the 3-step frame in #128 and antidote 5 in #1074 recommend always starting with/including what went well, while #420 and #439 warn against delicate silence and push for direct, frequent criticism; the source presents these as compatible (directness delivered with kindness and constructive form) but does not reconcile them explicitly [#128, #420, #439, #1074].
- The claims that "toxicity" is always a manifestation of trauma, neurosis or strong stress, and that the self-regulation approach is science-backed but almost unused, are the author's assertions; the second is partly self-promotional for deep mind's method [#820].
- The AppRaise recommendation is given without having tried the product, and the post carries tracking links typical of a recommendation [#820].
- Several statements are the author's political and social opinions (low trust in Russia due to absent property rights and independent courts, propaganda spending, leaders as "bullying" role models, siloviki lacking the optics of tolerance) rather than research findings [#386, #405, #840].
- The author notes that the flow state should not be confused with meditative full presence, and that flow has a dark side (exhaustion, injury risk in musicians) [#1543].
- Open questions the author leaves explicitly unanswered: what the extreme reaction to criticism of public figures says about "us", where this pattern comes from, and what to do with it [#840].

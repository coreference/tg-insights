# Theory U, presencing and co-sensing

## What this covers

This part covers how the deep mind channel (author Max Rodin and guest Olga Gurova) introduces Otto Scharmer's Theory U: its origin and core theses, the U-process with its three openings (mind, heart, will), the "meta-position" built from them, the diagram of the social grammar of destruction (absencing, trauma, fundamentalism) versus creation (presencing), and the group formats deep mind runs — company workshops, case clinics, co-sensing circles and facilitative founder interviews. It also includes the author's field observations: a company where body work was possible, the international Presencing Institute community at the start of COVID, a critique of the post-Soviet "overcoming" leadership pattern, and six observations from interviews with post-Soviet founders in New York and Silicon Valley [msg #39, #152, #320, #432, #465, #606, #1013, #1114, #1129, #1156, #1157, #1178, #1274].

According to the source this matters because Theory U lets people who practise nothing in particular access deep states, which makes it possible to work on a problem at the level of its cause rather than its symptom and to transform organisations and large social systems; the author calls it "one of the most important tools for change today" [msg #320].

This part covers how Max Rodin, founder of deep mind, teaches and applies Otto Scharmer's Theory U between early 2025 and mid-2026: felt presence as humanity's "main technology", the blind spot of leadership (the inner source of action), the open mind/heart/will, leading from the emerging future, the shift from ego-system to eco-system awareness, co-sensing, Arawana Hayashi's Social Presencing Theater (SPT) and The Village, case clinics run on a Himalayan trek, and why repeating one practice beats chasing new ones. According to the source, it matters because the world has become nonlinear and unpredictable (COVID, AI). In such a world, tools that extrapolate past data stop working, and the quality of a leader's attention and presence decides the quality of results [msg #1278, #1403, #1728, #1779].

This part adds three 2026 posts by Max Rodin (deep mind). They show how he applies Theory U outside formal sessions and how he connects it with neighbouring ideas. The first is a first-person story of practising deep empathic listening with a barber in Moscow. The second reviews Strebulaev and Dang's *The Venture Mindset* and compares it with Theory U. The third reports a co-sensing session and its collective map of what is leaving and what is emerging, and contrasts Theory U with foresight. According to the source, listening is one of the most important leadership skills of our time and can be trained. The venture logic of innovation is becoming basic for every leader. And a group shift of attention gives access to far more information than usual [msg #1879, #1928, #1946].

Relation to the existing knowledge file: the existing file already covers the levels of listening as a step of the Theory U facilitative interview, co-sensing circles, and the thesis "lead from the emerging future, not from past data". The new posts **extend** these ideas. Listening is now practised as a daily skill with strangers, not only in sessions [msg #1879]. ## Core ideas

- **Systems fail because their parts do not see themselves or the whole.** Problems are solved deeply only by understanding and redirecting collective attention so the parts open up to feelings, thoughts and information and become aware of the whole system (the *5 noble truths*) [msg #432].
- **The U-process rests on three openings of the leader — open mind, open heart, open will.** Through practices of managing attention one can sense "the future present in the present" even when no exact answers exist [msg #465, #606].
- **Presence is the antidote to absence.** Fundamentalism and trauma, bound together, come from absence in experience and closedness to reality; openness and presence counter this destructive social process [msg #1274, #1178].
- **Automatic thinking ("downloading") blocks anything new.** Relying only on past knowledge and experience, one cannot create anything truly new — an app or a move in a relationship; Theory U formats are designed to switch it off layer by layer [msg #1114, #1129].
- **Leadership means living with challenges; the way you meet them is a pattern to make conscious.** The post-Soviet "overcoming" pattern produces short-term results at an excessive cost and spreads a culture of violence [msg #1013].
- **Inside-out change.** A social paradigm shift is under way that takes the inner causes of external crises into account, possibly the biggest since the Enlightenment [msg #816].

- **Presence is the main "technology" of humanity and cannot be owned or transmitted.** Felt presence, experienced first-hand, is in the author's view the most valuable discovery in human history. No AI or other invention will compare with it. All anyone can do is create conditions that greatly raise the chances of actually *knowing* it, not just understanding it (see *Felt presence* below) [msg #1278].
- **The quality of results depends on the state you act from.** Card thesis: Theory U says the outcome depends not only on skills, strategies and decisions but on "из какого состояния человек действует" ("from what state a person acts") [img #1779]. This inner state is the *blind spot of leadership* [msg #1728].
- **We act the way we perceive.** A leader who looks through habitual schemas, judgments and old conclusions will reproduce the past again and again. So leadership of the future starts with a new quality of attention [img #1780].
- **Lead from the emerging future, not from past data.** "Данные — это описание прошлого. А ковида в нем, например, не было." ("Data is a description of the past. And COVID, for example, wasn't in it.") In nonlinear times, sensing the future that is already emerging is the only leadership that can help [msg #1728, #1403, img #1781].
- **Open mind, heart and will** together produce a different leadership reality: more stability and adaptability, faster sensing of change and of opportunities that are not yet in charts or plans [msg #1769, img #1781].
- **Real change requires an inner shift.** Changing processes, roles or control only treats symptoms. You have to change the *source* of action, sometimes by letting go of the leader identity of "the one who always knows" [img #1782].
- **From ego to eco.** The leader is not a lone hero but someone who creates space where others see, think and act better. When a system is filled with attention and presence, care, kindness and love arise naturally [img #1783, msg #1844].
- **Theory U is an evolutionary practice, not one more framework.** It changes the optics of leadership itself and transforms a person or group [msg #1307, img #1784].
- **Repetition, not novelty, deepens practice.** A known form frees attention to perceive what is actually happening. Novelty gives stimulation (and stress); repetition gives sensitivity [msg #1842].

- **Listening is a skill, so it can be trained, and you can train it anywhere.** The author deliberately practises deep empathic listening with random people. This is more useful the further the person is from his own bubble [msg #1879].
- **The common denominator between people lies in feelings, not facts.** Real listening turns two "functions" (a haircut machine and a wallet) into living people who can feel and even trust each other [msg #1879].
- **Openness requires resource but also adds resource.** When he listens this way, the author says, he gets a better haircut, people work better with him in a team and value him more as a partner. Solutions to problems such as migration would also be of a different quality [msg #1879].
- **The venture mindset and Theory U arrive at largely the same picture from two directions.** The venture mindset comes from Stanford research on the venture industry. Theory U comes from MIT work on the deep processes of innovator-leaders. Both are worth mastering [msg #1928].
- **Innovative logic rests not on intellect but on the bodily capacity to tolerate not-knowing.** The author says this used to set apart founders and rare corporate innovators but is becoming basic for every leader [msg #1928].
- **Theory U is not foresight.** It is a technology for creating group resonance. It shifts the structure of personal and collective attention and so gives access to much more information, which helps each person see the right actions for their own context [msg #1946].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### 5 noble truths of Theory U
Purpose: Rodin's condensed "squeeze" of Theory U, formulated while explaining it to the founder of Varlamov.Est, as a first introduction to the approach. He notes that like any philosophical system and method of inner work, Theory U is hard to grasp from description and becomes clearer after practice with a qualified trainer [msg #432].
1. **Parts of systems are unaware.** Systems have deep problems and contradictions because their parts do not recognise themselves as parts or do not see the system as a whole. Therefore they often act on the level of personal interests without considering second-, third- and further-order consequences [msg #432].
2. **Disconnection from feelings and thoughts distorts the picture.** The more these parts are at odds with their own feelings and thoughts, the less they understand the real picture of the world and the more they tend to act in defence of their own point of view — often to the detriment of the whole system and its other parts [msg #432].
3. **Deep solutions require understanding collective attention.** To solve system problems at the essential level and not at the level of symptoms (as is commonly offered), we need to understand how collective attention works [msg #432].
4. **Redirect attention in two ways.** Individual and collective attention must then be directed so that the parts of the system, first, open up to feelings, thoughts and other present information, and second, become aware of themselves as parts and of the system as a whole [msg #432].
5. **Theory U is the toolkit for this.** For this process there is a community, a language, a theory and a set of practices for the different stages of opening and awareness and the qualitative changes that follow; together they make up Theory U [msg #432].

### U-process and the three states of the leader
As described by guest author Olga Gurova, the U-process underlies the u.lab course and the whole of Theory U; it describes the **stages of innovation work** on any project — a personal career, social activism or a business. Its core is developing three states of the leader [msg #465]:
1. **Open mind (открытый ум)** — the ability to find the new even in a familiar field and to keep learning [msg #465].
2. **Open heart (открытое сердце)** — the ability to put yourself in another's place and feel the situation from different positions [msg #465].
3. **Open will (открытая воля)** — the courage to implement change even without knowing the exact answer [msg #465].

Why it works (Gurova): when trying to catch the future present in the present there are no exact answers yet, but through practices of managing attention we can open up and sense more accurately where to go and what to do. Scharmer's methods gradually immerse people in these states of openness at different levels; learning happens through experience, continuously deepening one's understanding of the world and one's own practice [msg #465]. Rodin adds that Scharmer discovered this natural U-process — based on presence and authenticity — in his original research, when he looked for the principles behind change and transformation; part of the leaders he interviewed were from Silicon Valley, and Gurova notes he interviewed hundreds of innovator leaders [msg #1129, #465].

### Stages of the meta-position (based on Theory U and deep mind's experience)
Purpose: the meta-position is useful in the most difficult circumstances; it lets one think more broadly and make quality decisions [msg #606].

Why it is needed: under stress or strong emotion, consciousness narrows into an emotional subjective position from which we act mediocrely without realising it in the moment. Many therefore think that effective action requires cutting off emotions — but that does not lead to a meta-position; it only strengthens the shadow action of suppressed emotions, and we again think very narrowly. In the meta-position, by contrast, emotions are **included in the map of awareness**: we notice where they came from and what they appeared for, and at the same time keep the whole picture. "Мета-позиция – это не состояние, а скорее процесс" (*The meta-position is not a state but rather a process*) with successive stages [msg #606]:

1. **Open mind — awareness and curiosity.** Work with one's own attention to let go of judgement, see the situation with an unclouded eye and let in a fuller factual picture of reality. How: observe; try to notice what is happening without instant automatic evaluation; see and hear, again and again; do not turn away from reality but try to discern facts, separate them from noise and understand them. If reality triggers an evaluation, notice it as a striving for control and safety and let it go. If anxiety, fear or pain arise, notice them as your own reaction to events and treat them, too, without evaluation or condemnation — simply as facts [msg #606].
2. **Open heart — compassion.** Work on letting go of one's own cynicism or contempt towards others. In practice this is impossible without letting go of contempt towards oneself, so we try to notice and loosen the habitual clinging to self-criticism, guilt and a sense of inadequacy, to pain over past mistakes or over the impossibility of having foreseen a future that has already happened. Thus we understand that we ourselves often act wrongly out of pain, misunderstanding of others' feelings, general ignorance and anger — and this lets us see ourselves and **all** actors in the situation from compassion. Every participant acts from their own pain and is suffering right now. This does not justify violence, but it draws attention to its causes and gives a real possibility to end violence rather than multiply it. The author states that compassion is a 100% necessary element of the meta-position: it opens access to feelings, which carry far more information about reality than the thinking system, and it helps find something in common with others where all commonality seems lost forever [msg #606].
3. **Open will — courage.** Reaching it is the hardest step for many, because it requires letting go not of judgement of others but of fear within oneself. "Страх – последнее, что держит нас в ограниченной эго-позиции." (*Fear is the last thing that holds us in the limited ego-position.*) To access the broad picture one must unclench the fist of this fear and open access to free action. Helpful questions / foci of attention [msg #606]:
   - "Что я защищаю на самом деле?" (*What am I really protecting?*)
   - If I allow for the possibility of losing it and assume it is not so important — what happens after?
   - "Какое будущее хочет проявиться прямо сейчас?" (*What future wants to emerge right now?*)
   - What is my role in the current historical moment and in the emergence of this future? [msg #606]

Integration: at some point all stages may suddenly come together — we see reality without bias, feel from compassion and recognise our own historical role without throwing off responsibility. The author calls this the fullest picture of reality available to a human, coming from connection to the widest field of information. From it, it is vital to keep acting — at least as a prototype or small steps — otherwise we slide back into narrow ego-perception and change nothing [msg #606].

### Theory U facilitative interview (for founders, leaders, teams)
Purpose: a research/facilitation interview in a "new paradigm" that differs both from standard customer-development interviews and from functional, often one-sided strategy sessions; it follows Scharmer's Theory U frame, where communication is based on presence and spontaneity, so the participants together walk through the U-process natural to all innovators and create a deep, fresh understanding of reality. Rodin used it in New York with four Russian-speaking leaders/teams, and later in the Valley [msg #1114, #1156].

Why: to start a U-process instead of the usual "treading water", the participants must stop reloading automatic thinking — the constant narrative of already absorbed ideas and past experience that a leader or team tells themselves daily, which is hard to bypass in any classic meeting that pays no attention to the way of thinking and the state from which participants think and talk [msg #1114].

How (Rodin's components):
1. **Use a liberating structure.** To change the modality of the dialogue he uses a liberating structure developed in the Theory U approach [msg #1114].
2. **Ask questions that arise as a response.** The interview runs through questions that emerge in response to what is said [msg #1114].
3. **No hypotheses, no solving.** He creates no hypotheses and does not try to solve anything [msg #1114].
4. **Work "with yourself".** He actively works on his own state to bring presence and spontaneity into the shared state — meditating more and resting on the day of the meeting [msg #1114].
5. **Go down the levels of listening.** Together these help move deeper through the levels of listening and understand the situation much better [msg #1114].

Pitfalls: it is hard work, because one must constantly and carefully work with people's ingrained habits of devaluing everything that does not fit their established picture of the world. Presence, authenticity and deep listening are a field almost completely ignored as unimportant, "although we already have all the scientific data that this is ignorance" (author's claim; no data cited) [msg #1114]. Outcome claimed: such interviews help founders understand their situation more deeply and see growth points [msg #1156].

### Felt presence and authentic action
Purpose: this is the experiential foundation the author puts under all Theory U work. He describes it as a personal commitment "быть живым и настоящим" ("to be alive and real"). The commitment did not come from a clever idea in a book. It grew out of living through, "в собственной шкуре" ("in one's own skin"), a perception of the world in full contact with it [msg #1278].
1. **What presence is.** You are not *interpreting* the world through cognitive filters of ideas passed on by ancestors or surroundings. You are resting in "неусложненном всем этим чувствующем присутствии" ("sensing presence uncomplicated by all this"). You do not restrain or shape experience in any way; you allow yourself simply to be in it as it is [msg #1278].
2. **Why it matters (mechanism).** When a person replaces direct life with thinking about it, they create a heap of delusions, which the author calls the main source of all problems. Cut off from reality, it is easy to build mental constructions that justify anything: one's grandiosity, entitlement, cynicism, violence, wars, theft. All this becomes far easier when, instead of seeing reality, you act as its "creator" in your own interests. Full presence opens one's eyes to these delusions [msg #1278].
3. **What presence is not.** "присутствие — не фреймфорк [sic] и не разработка" ("presence is not a framework and not a development"). No one owns it, creates it or can hand it over. The best minds can only design conditions that multiply the chances of knowing its power for sure [msg #1278].
4. **Where it is being explored.** According to the author, presence is now studied at leading universities. This has produced an unprecedented wave of popularity for mindfulness, which is one way of experiencing presence. At MIT, which he calls the world's leading business school, a theory and set of practices (Theory U) is being developed to integrate this experience into leadership in the broadest sense [msg #1278].
5. **Authentic action as the outcome.** "Аутентичное действие — единственное что позволяет человеку быть лидером." ("Authentic action is the only thing that allows a person to be a leader.") It means not hiding from fear but living life. This matters especially in times of change, when acting automatically leads to failure. Authentic action is truly fresh, so it arises only non-reactively, not out of habit, automatism or past experience. It is born from the "I" when the I exists and is present rather than turning away from reality, "Когда Я готово станцевать с ней" ("when the I is ready to dance with it"), showing itself and responding. "В таком присутствии мы по-настоящему рождаемся на свет и видим куда стоит идти." ("In such presence we are truly born into the world and see where to go.") [msg #1278].

### Blind spot of leadership (source of action)
Purpose: this is the concept the author credits with letting Theory U give footing in uncertainty and complexity. Earlier stages of management evolution missed it entirely [msg #1728].
- **Definition.** It is the state from which a leader acts, which we usually do not account for at all in leadership and change. Actions from automatism, fear or anger lead to completely different results than actions from presence, attentiveness and openness [msg #1728].
- **Mechanism.** Presence makes it possible to take in the widest available number of perspectives and to start interacting with the world "буквально на ином уровне" ("literally on a different level"). The author calls this kind of leadership the heart of Theory U [msg #1728].
- **Effect.** Working with the blind spot does more than train you out of automatism, which is ruinous in times of change. It helps you revise the whole paradigm of moving forward. Leadership goes beyond classical management and takes on the qualities of a "танца с реальностью" ("dance with reality") [msg #1728].
- **Card version.** Real change requires an inner shift. Systems often try to change symptoms: they rebuild processes, change roles, tighten control. Theory U points to a deeper level: for a new solution to come, changing the external plan is not enough; you must change the *source of action*. Sometimes this means letting go of the leader's old identity: the image of someone who always knows the answer, controls everything and must solve the problem immediately [img #1782].
- **Attention version.** "мы действуем так, как воспринимаем" ("we act the way we perceive"). Leadership of the future begins with a new quality of attention: the ability to see in a new way, hear more deeply and notice what used to fall out of the field of view [img #1780].

### Open mind, open heart, open will (presence-based leadership from the future)
Purpose: this is the author's first-person phenomenology of what Scharmer calls *leading from the future*. The presence skill gives a person access to the full spectrum of information about the world, so they notice "семена будущего, которые уже здесь" ("the seeds of the future that are already here"). They begin creating genuine innovations, acting with foresight and often against established norms [msg #1769]. The card version: in turning points, a leader does not just analyse past data but learns to notice the *emerging future*. This requires openness of mind, heart and will: going beyond stereotypes, sensing others and the situation more widely than one's own interests, and letting go of old ways of acting. "Лидер будущего — это человек, способный чувствовать ход вещей." ("The leader of the future is a person able to sense the course of things.") [img #1781].
1. **Open mind.** "Я не прибит гвоздями к своей картине мира." ("I am not nailed to my picture of the world.") You do not cling to ideas about yourself, your plans or your point of view to the point of becoming an unpleasant person. Instead you gain flexibility and a constant forward flow, absorbing new information "как губка" ("like a sponge") that reflects changes in the world and in yourself. Rather than imposing your ideas on the world, you let new data into them. You accept with curiosity even what triggers disagreement, fear or rejection. Your mental closedness no longer blocks your understanding of what has already died and what is being born [msg #1769].
2. **Open heart.** The author stresses this is not a metaphor but an ordinary human experience available to most people: openness not only to ideas but to feelings. You notice and allow the states, emotions and body sensations unfolding in you, and you also experience in yourself what others feel. "Только так между нами возникает настоящий контакт" ("Only this way does real contact arise between us"). You find yourself in a world of universal interconnectedness. It gives information and understanding that were completely missed before: a new layer of reality made not of concepts, data and ideas but of the living experiences of living people. The author says these sometimes move relationships, companies and countries more strongly than anything else. You stop crashing against the rocks of ignorance of this layer and find support and orientation in it. People begin to see each other differently, words and actions change, and genuine connection, support and partnership are born [msg #1769].
3. **Open will.** You do not only see with sharp insight and sense the world "буквально на уровне кожи" ("literally at skin level"). You do not only rest in contemplation, but actively and precisely bring in what should become your contribution. You can act unusually differently. You can see inaction as an important part of the process, yet take steps without delay as soon as a sense of the right direction appears. "Воля больше не связана страхом неудачи или осуждения, она открыта к эспериментам." [sic] ("The will is no longer bound by fear of failure or judgment; it is open to experiments.") [msg #1769].
4. **All together.** Open mind, heart and will create a completely different leadership reality. It applies not only in organizations but in relationships, emigration and parenting, wherever life needs our presence, involvement and agency (субъектность). Acting "из всего себя" ("from all of myself") rather than only from the mind, you stop relying on a narrow picture of external data, ideas and concepts, and you gain greater stability and adaptability [msg #1769].
5. **Results.** You see reality better, notice changes faster and let go of what is unneeded. You stop wasting precious time and catch opportunities not yet reflected in charts, strategies, plans or even your ideas about yourself. The author's examples: seeing a new product for the company in small talk at the water cooler; sensing precisely, at some moment, the end of a regime just by watching young people in the street; suddenly understanding clearly that you are no longer ready to be an entrepreneur or top manager [msg #1769].
6. **Felt quality.** Such leadership is not only productive but "свежо и благодатно" ("fresh and graceful"). Present in contact with life, we finally let go of the all-pervading anxiety and see what the next step is. "Там есть ясность, смелость и тихая счастливая наполненность." ("There is clarity, courage and quiet happy fullness there.") [msg #1769].
7. **How to start.** Beyond the group process, the author says you can start on your own: by studying Theory U, or by using any tools that lead to awareness and presence *in ordinary life*, not outside it [msg #1769].

### Ego-system to eco-system awareness
- **The leader as space-holder.** In Theory U the leader is not a lone hero but a person who knows how to create a space where others begin to see, think and act better. This is the move "от эго-мышления к эко-мышлению" ("from ego-thinking to eco-thinking"): from looking only from your own position to perceiving yourself as part of a larger system. Such a leader works not only with tasks and KPIs but with the quality of collective attention, trust, dialogue and shared meaning. "Лидер будущего работает не только с собой, но и с полем отношений" ("The leader of the future works not only with himself but with the field of relationships") [img #1783].
- **As lived experience.** After The Village (2026), the author describes a fact that Arawana Hayashi warned about and that contemplative traditions describe, and which still surprises him every time. When you fill any system with attention and presence, you naturally generate care, kindness and love in it. ### Social Presencing Theater (SPT)
**What it is.** SPT is "не спектакль и не терапия" ("not a performance and not therapy"). It is a special space where participants use simple movements and "living sculptures" to explore the *social body* (социальное тело): the invisible fabric of relationships, feelings and intentions through which communities, organizations and society as a whole manifest. It is used today in business, education and communities to catalyse needed change [msg #1515]. A later description, attributed to how SPT is described and to the Presencing Institute, calls it a body-based social methodology of change. It was created under the leadership of Arawana Hayashi in Boston to make visible the current reality of a social system and to explore the possibilities emerging in it. The Presencing Institute also writes that SPT helps a community see itself collectively [msg #1812].

**Origin.** Arawana Hayashi is a choreographer, meditator and co-founder of the Presencing Institute with Otto Scharmer. In 1974, touring as a dancer, she attended a lecture by Chögyam Trungpa at the newly opened Naropa University in Colorado. Trungpa's real presence struck her so much that she began practising meditation and soon joined the teaching staff. While studying the Japanese court dance *bugaku* and contemporary improvisation, she looked for a way to combine bodily wisdom, meditation and group work. SPT grew out of this [msg #1515].

**Principles and examples** [msg #1515]:
1. **Sculpture of the present and the future.** The group literally "builds" the current situation with their bodies, and then a possible future. This lets people *feel*, not just imagine, where the system wants to move.
2. **Practice of "not-knowing".** Short improvisations with no goal of "making it beautiful". The aim is to feel how the body reacts to the unknown and how the new is born.
3. **Presencing.** A special quality of attention in which we are aware of what is happening and at the same time listen to what is trying to emerge.

**How it differs from strategy sessions.** SPT offers no ready solutions and does not push the group in any particular direction. It creates conditions in which a solution becomes tangible through the *collective body*. Because of that, resistance to implementing it dissolves [msg #1515].

**Why it matters to companies and teams.** Companies are used to the language of strategies, numbers and plans, but a layer of reality slips past spreadsheets: invisible connections between people, collective emotions and patterns of "being stuck". SPT lets you touch this layer directly. Three benefits [msg #1515]:
1. **See hidden patterns.** Every organization has implicit dynamics such as tensions between departments, employees' fears and fatigue. SPT makes them visible before they spill into conflict or sabotage.
2. **Accelerate innovation.** When people learn to trust bodily knowing, unexpected insights appear. Teams begin acting from a sense of the future that is already being born, not from fear of repeating the past.
3. **Build a culture of trust.** Moving together, even simply, breaks down barriers. People feel they are one whole, which creates an atmosphere where ideas and decisions emerge naturally.

**Case (observed by the author).** At a corporate retreat where he watched SPT facilitators work, a team of 20 people started the session immersed in scepticism. Within an hour, after building a "sculpture of the future", they suddenly felt that the company held the hidden potential for a new product that no one had dared to discuss. After the retreat the idea became the basis of a strategic project. Lesson: SPT helps not just to manage a company but "чувствовать её" ("to feel it"). Hayashi's significance, in the author's words, is that she showed art and presence can become the heart of leadership [msg #1515].

**Mini-SPT as team closure (Resonance, 2026).** The Resonance team closed its integrating session with a short collective-movement practice from SPT. Each person could express their experience of the team as sculptures, that is, bodily shapes. By then they had already discussed and reflected on the project and thanked each other. The movement was added to feel this even more and to gain more joy, connection and unity, anchoring and unfolding the experience as shared movement. The author also uses SPT in Resonance and sometimes with the teams of business clients [msg #1812].

### The Village (Arawana Hayashi) and the facilitator's guidance
**What it is.** A practice by Arawana Hayashi of creating "a small village". It arose in Boston in the 1970s and today develops within SPT (described as a method of somatic awareness) and Theory U. The practice makes visible the social systems we live in: it helps you see and become aware of their influence on you, and find your own place in them [msg #1843]. It lets you recreate the social body in action [msg #1486]. The 2026 Moscow run was a three-hour practice with about 70 people in silent movement [msg #1844].

**Origin story (repetition).** In the 1970s in Boston, improvisation was focused on authenticity: "do whatever you feel." A friend asked the young dancer Hayashi a question that changed her life: if you want to teach improvisation, you must understand what elements *good* improvisation consists of. Not just any improvisation, but good. What distinguishes one from another if both are spontaneous? [msg #1842]. Her answer went against all the logic of novelty. She simplified the language of movement to the limit (walking, standing, turning, sitting) and repeated this form with her troupe, City Dance Theater, sometimes for several days or even weeks in a row, the same practice over and over. In parallel she studied at Naropa in Boulder, where the author and his partner Dasha later studied Mudra Space Awareness, a contemplative work with space and attention [msg #1842]. Understanding came through years of repetition, not by sampling methods. The quality of good improvisation has almost nothing to do with talent, technique, attractiveness or inventiveness. It depends entirely on presence in the body and attention to the whole. The answer to "what makes improvisation alive" could not be found by adding something new, only by cutting away novelty of form and repeating it again and again [msg #1842]. Thirty years later, in 2004, Hayashi met Peter Senge and Otto Scharmer, and The Village entered Theory U as a practice of direct experience of the social field. The practice itself has not changed since the seventies, "И в этом ее сила, а не слабость." ("And that is its strength, not its weakness.") [msg #1842].

**Facilitator guidance.** Before running it, the author imagined how Hayashi would instruct him. This is his imagined rendering, not a direct quote from her [msg #1843]:
1. **Don't make it "cool"; make it honest.** "Перформанс — не то место, откуда возникает живое." ("Performance is not the place the living arises from.") Instead: come as you really are, hold the form, trust the people, love whatever arises. "The Village сделает остальное — она умнее нас с тобой" ("The Village will do the rest — it is smarter than you and me"), and that is the whole joy of it [msg #1843].
2. **Speak from practice, not from knowledge.** You are not speaking as someone who knows something and will now pass it on. You speak from practice, from the hours you yourself have sat and walked. "Знание объясняет, практика приглашает." ("Knowledge explains, practice invites.") [msg #1843].
3. **Basic goodness underlies everything.** The one idea without which The Village does not work: "базовая доброта" ("basic goodness"). People and the moment itself are fundamentally good, before any improvement or self-work. The practice is already good before it begins. You do not create an experience for people; you remove what is superfluous so they notice what is already there: the space, each other, the simple dignity of walking and sitting. Good improvisation arises from presence in the body and attention to the whole, not from talent and inventiveness [msg #1843].
4. **Drop the ambition of depth.** You do not have to make it deep. "Глубина — не твоя работа, она приходит сама, когда никто ее не тащит." ("Depth is not your job; it comes by itself when no one drags it.") Your job is simplicity and precision [msg #1843].
5. **Keep lightness and humour.** There is a lot that is funny in The Village: people bump into each other, shuffle about, don't know where to put themselves. Don't protect the practice with seriousness. "То, что по-настоящему глубоко, не боится юмора." ("What is truly deep is not afraid of humour.") [msg #1843].
6. **It doesn't end when the session ends.** The Village continues in the "villages" people return to in the evening: families, teams, the city. Give people a living question to take home. It works longer and deeper than any answers [msg #1843].

### Novelty vs repetition in practice
The trigger was a question from several people before the 2026 Village: "а будет ли в этот раз что-то новое?" ("will there be something new this time?") [msg #1842].
- **Diagnosis.** In modern society and culture, experience has turned into content. We expect impressions to be fresh, programmes updated, the next season to come: watched, swiped, moved on. This feed logic quietly gets into inner work, where it does not belong: "did vipassana, now need a Zen retreat; tried this tantra, let's have a new one" [msg #1842].
- **What repetition does inside us.** When we do something for the first time, almost all our attention is eaten by the form itself: *what to do, where to go, how do I look, did I understand the instruction*. The psyche is busy orienting; that is how it is built, and the new is always processed first. When the form is familiar, attention is freed. It no longer has to be spent on *how* and can turn for the first time to *what* is happening, to what flew past the first time [msg #1842].
- **Rule.** "Новизна дает стимуляцию [и стресс], а повторение дает нам чувствительность." ("Novelty gives stimulation [and stress], while repetition gives us sensitivity.") The author calls these different modes of the nervous system and says the second is in acute deficit in modern culture [msg #1842].
- **Author's example.** He has meditated for more than 17 years with the same posture and the same breath, and in all those years never had two identical sittings. "Форма повторяется, а опыт никогда." ("The form repeats, the experience never.") [msg #1842].
- **Applied to The Village.** It cannot be repeated in principle. Only the container can be recreated; what is born inside it arises for the first time each time. Last year's village exists nowhere but memory. The next one does not exist yet and will arise as a short flash on the horizon of time, which can be carried away only as bodily experience and reflection. Seen this way, the author argues, many can find far more meaning than in the endless race for new experience and the next stimulus [msg #1842].

### Contemplative group journey in extreme conditions (Himalaya format)
This was the first deep mind "community group experience": a small group recruited openly and from programme alumni. Together with a mountain-expedition organiser, they went to the Himalayas for 12 days, combining trekking of 4–6 hours a day with slower camp stops and Theory U practices. The group work included a broad survey programme of meditation in different approaches, generative dialogue and group check-ins [msg #1619].
- **Difficulty.** In harsh conditions practices come hard: constant moving, loss of the usual food choice, cold, close contact with an unfamiliar group, and altitude up to about 4,000 m. Facilitators and group must constantly "сверять часы" ("sync watches") and work out how to distribute resources optimally [msg #1619].
- **Facilitator stance.** The author practised "радикальную открытость" ("radical openness"), which he promised to describe separately, so it is not explained here. He constantly monitored the group's state and agreed on the pace, not only on the trek but in the inner group process [msg #1619].
- **Intensity.** Even with relatively few practices, the experience was very intense and needed as much integration as a usual 10-day retreat. Because of the unique container (the conditions above, extraordinary nature, no connectivity), nearly every meditation and dialogue went much deeper than in the city [msg #1619].
- **Three things the author valued most** [msg #1619]:
  1. **Living through harsh conditions together.** You could always run a cosy retreat in Bali, but everyone does that. More importantly, in harsh conditions people open up and grow close remarkably. Masks and status fall away quickly when you talk with Tibetan children, cannot order a pour-over anywhere and spend days searching for hot water bit by bit. The author calls this useful for the modern city dweller as a way to re-evaluate things.
  2. **The strength of the community.** The group felt like two weeks with friends, without the usual difficult group dynamics. The author found such a group seemed impossible to assemble from more or less random people.
  3. **Highest-altitude Theory U practice.** In Muktinath, at almost 4,000 m, they held a *case clinic*, one of the key formats of Theory U. The author believes no one in the international community has done anything similar and plans to report it to the institute that develops the method.
- **Conclusion.** He plans to keep the format very limited, for example one truly strong event a year, as a way to build ties that are strong rather than wide. In two weeks he gained something like "plus 10 new friends". "Офлайн становится новым люксом, а офлайн с хорошими людьми, практиками и удивительными местами — драгоценностью." ("Offline is becoming the new luxury, and offline with good people, practices and amazing places, a treasure.") He also believes such formats are needed now and will grow in the coming years [msg #1619].

### Everyday deep-listening practice (listening to strangers outside your bubble)
Purpose: to train the levels of listening from Theory U, which the author often presents in the context of leadership as one of the most important skills of our time. His reasoning: "А если это навык, то его можно тренировать." (*And if it is a skill, then it can be trained.*) When he has resource for something new, he deliberately talks with people and practises deep empathic listening in random places [msg #1879]. The steps below are reconstructed from his barber story:

1. **Notice the default mode of the transaction.** In most service transactions we stay at the level of formal politeness: essentially closed to the person and perceiving them "как функцию" (*as a function*), as "Непонятный тип, который сейчас будет меня стричь" (*some unknown guy who is about to cut my hair*). The usual script is a couple of polite words and then silence under the buzz of the clipper [msg #1879].
2. **Decide to step out of your shell and take a risk.** "Но я решаюсь выйти из своей скорлупы закрытости." (*But I decide to step out of my shell of closedness.*) The author noticed that the barber looked unusual. He risked a question that is common in an American context but sounds rather odd in Russian: "какой у тебя бэкграунд?" (*what is your background?*). He had guessed the man was an immigrant. The barber understood easily and said he was from Tunisia, and that opened the door to a 30-minute conversation in which they looked at each other through the mirror [msg #1879].
3. **Ask all kinds of questions and drop your assumptions.** He asked many different questions, "полностью отбрасывая свои представления о том, что должен и не должен миру простой барбер" (*completely discarding my ideas of what a simple barber should and should not owe the world*). He listened without bias, openly. The questions went from personal ones (how many years here: eight; how life is in Russia and how Russians treat him: wonderfully, but there are cases) to politics and society. Because the barber was from Tunisia, the author asked why people from Morocco flee to Spain risking their lives, assuming the barber could understand this better than he could [msg #1879].
4. **Receive what comes, including views wider than the question.** The barber said that people without empathy, with cruelty and aggression, exist everywhere, including in his home country, and you cannot escape them wherever you move. On Morocco: life there is bad, the government does not care about people and builds no common well-being, and an ordinary person wants to live normally, with health and opportunities, and cannot be blamed for that. "Ты же не виноват, что родился в этой стране и с таким правителем" (*It's not your fault you were born in this country and with such a ruler*), he said, not addressing the author specifically [msg #1879].
5. **Answer briefly, hold silence, and offer something of your own.** The author answered "Не виноват" (*Not your fault*) and fell silent. Later he shared that for many years he has been moving around the world looking for his place, thinking (but not saying aloud) that he is not to blame either. The barber understood why. He admitted that he wants to move to Europe even though life here is not bad: he needs to earn money first, and has not left yet because he does not want to live illegally in Paris for several years [msg #1879].
6. **Stay with the pauses and non-verbal moments.** While the barber cut, the author watched him in the mirror and then looked out of the window at the lanes of Khamovniki. Noticing this, the barber seemed to change the subject and remarked on Moscow's beauty. The author agreed that the city is very beautiful, well kept and comfortable now. The barber stopped his scissors and added: "но красота — это не все" (*but beauty isn't everything*). The author just nodded [msg #1879].
7. **Notice the shift from facts to feelings, and close warmly.** Neither knew much about the other, but they were no longer "две функции, не машина для стрижки и не кошелек с деньгами" (*two functions, not a haircut machine and not a wallet of money*). They stopped being a set of facts for each other and became living people who had found something universally human in very different lives within twenty minutes: "общий знаменатель, который лежит скорее в чувствах, а не фактах" (*a common denominator that lies in feelings rather than facts*), the possibility to feel each other and even to trust. The author slapped the barber's palm and sincerely wished him luck. Leaving, he felt the barber's hopes and fears, and it felt not heavy but "тепло" (*warm*) [msg #1879].

Why it works and what it gives (author's claims): "Открытость требует ресурса, но его и добавляет." (*Openness requires resource, but it also adds it.*) When he listens like this, he says, he gets a better haircut, people work with him better in a team and value him more as a partner. "В мире где человек человеку человек преображается буквально все." (*In a world where man is a human to man, literally everything is transformed.*) He extends this to social problems. If we look at migration, or any other problem, and open up to people and listen to them, we risk seeing not crime, danger and dirt but living people who want to live normally, "И решения возникнут иного качества." (*And solutions of a different quality will arise.*) [msg #1879].

Conditions and pitfalls: the author does this "когда у меня самого есть ресурс на что-то новое" (*when I myself have resource for something new*). It is not a constant demand. Practice is most useful with people far from your own bubble. The main pitfall is the default: formal politeness that treats the other person as a function [msg #1879].

### Venture mindset — four of the nine principles, read through Theory U
Source: Rodin's review of *The Venture Mindset* (Russian title *Венчурное мышление*, cover subtitle "9 ПРИНЦИПОВ РОСТА БИЗНЕСА В ЛЮБЫХ УСЛОВИЯХ", *9 principles of business growth in any conditions*) by Ilya Strebulaev and Alex Dang [msg #1928, img #1928]. Definition in the post: the venture mindset is a mindset and style of action for a world where the future is fundamentally unpredictable. It does not try to guess the future. Instead it builds one's position so that uncertainty works for us [msg #1928]. The book has nine principles. The author gives "чтобы почувствовать вкус" (*to give a taste*) only four:

1. **Innovation outcomes follow a power law, not a normal distribution.** One breakthrough outweighs ten failures. So failure is the price of entry, and the real risk is not making the bet that could have changed everything. In a corporation the logic is usually the reverse: you are punished for a mistake but never for a missed opportunity [msg #1928].
2. **Consensus is the enemy of innovation.** If everyone in the room agrees, the idea is most likely already obvious. Bold decisions almost always look doubtful at the moment they are made [msg #1928].
3. **Bets are made in portions, not all in.** Traditional companies plan strategy five years ahead and launch a large budget cycle for it. Instead, moving towards the new is a series of steps, each of which opens a new piece of the map and raises the question: double down or exit? This is decided without regard to what has already been invested [msg #1928].
4. **Bet on people, not on the plan.** The plan will change anyway. What remains is the team's ability to learn and reassemble itself [msg #1928].

(!) INCOMPLETE: the source names nine principles but gives only these four. The other five are not given.

How the author relates it to his own experience: he read the book with "радости узнавания" (*the joy of recognition*). The logic of innovation and development, rather than of strengthening the status quo, had always been close to him and is now becoming more relevant. The book "словно легитимизировала мой собственный стиль жизни" (*as if legitimised my own lifestyle*) and many principles deep mind stands on, even though he is not a venture investor at all, "и в этом-то и фишка" (*and that's exactly the point*). When he left the corporation to build deep mind, he had nothing of what one usually relies on. New identity, market and product, no benchmarks or reference points: maximum uncertainty. He could not read anywhere how to act and spent years learning through experience how to create something new when you cannot plan linearly: you cannot bet on one product and have to try again and again. The book gave him a system that maps this experience clearly [msg #1928].

Link to Theory U: the author finds the logic strikingly consonant with Otto Scharmer's Theory U, which deep mind works with in its Theory U group process (Resonance). Scharmer at MIT started from the deep processes of innovator-leaders who can find new moves in the most difficult situations. Strebulaev and Dang at Stanford started from research on the venture industry. They arrived at largely one picture, "которую полезно осваивать и укреплять по обоим подходам" (*which is useful to master and strengthen through both approaches*) [msg #1928].

What the author found missing: "Динамичная, инновационная логика держится не на интеллекте, а на способности выдерживать незнание." (*Dynamic, innovative logic rests not on intellect but on the ability to tolerate not-knowing.*) That means living through unpleasant uncertainty and completely changing one's trajectory and one's ideas about oneself and the world. "Все это очень, если хотите, телесно!" (*All of this is very, if you like, bodily!*) He believes this ability used to set apart founders and rare innovators inside corporations, but is now becoming basic for any leader. The future is no longer just foggy: it seriously threatens to turn the familiar world upside down in the coming years. The environment no longer gives the stability on which classical management was built, so we will have to move closer to a mindset that is adaptive in these conditions [msg #1928].

### Co-sensing the emerging future vs foresight
At a deep mind meeting, people from literally all over the world went through a process of "совместного чувстования будущего" (*co-sensing of the future*) based on the principles of Theory U from MIT [msg #1946]. The author's distinctions:

- **What it is not.** It is "вовсе не технология форсайта или прогнозирования" (*not at all a technology of foresight or forecasting*). In his view these work largely only at the cognitive level and only on data from the past, which "на самом деле не очень корректно пролонгировать в будущее" (*in fact it is not very correct to extend into the future*) [msg #1946].

In it the structure of both personal and collective attention changes, and participants get access to a much larger volume of information than they are used to [msg #1946].
- **What it gives.** The shift in the structure of attention helps you see actions that are right for you and your context specifically. It works well in a group when special conditions are created in it, as at that meeting [msg #1946].

## Detailed insights

### Origin, status and reach of Theory U
Theory U was born in Silicon Valley and is now developed at MIT and in Scharmer's own organisation, the Presencing Institute, where Rodin studied in 2019 [msg #320]. Rodin describes it as an important modern methodology of organisational change and says deep mind uses it in almost all its projects, effectively with very different audiences, from school directors in Kazan to Qiwi's top management [msg #432]. Within a year and a half deep mind applied Theory U tools to processes in [msg #320]:
- the education system of Kazan;
- projects of Tiltech, an accelerator of "living companies" in Russia;
- Avito;
- PwC;
- deep mind's own retreat.
Later client mentions include the CEO-1 team of Qiwi and Yandex's HRD team [msg #1114]. Rodin's assessment: through this method people who practise nothing in particular get access to deep states, which makes it possible to work with a problem at the level of its cause and produces really big changes, and also allows Theory U to be applied to transformation of organisations and large social systems [msg #320]. Theory U "stands with one foot" in Silicon Valley, fits it perfectly, yet is not widely known there [msg #1129]. He sees Theory U as a skilful way of bringing awareness into systems [msg #1129].

Gurova (guest) adds: Theory U is heard of among professional coaches, in corporate and business circles, and among psychologists and educators. What hooked her was its philosophy that includes humanity's global problems. Scharmer often says many of the hundreds of innovator leaders he interviewed shared a sense of a dead end: "На уровне цивилизации мы постоянно и в разных сферах производим результаты, которых никто не хочет" (*At the level of civilisation we constantly, in different spheres, produce results that nobody wants*). Leaders at different levels want to take part in creating systemic or deep change but often do not know how [msg #465].

### Learning Theory U through MIT u.lab (Gurova's account)
u.lab: Leading From the Emerging Future is a free online course on edX that each year opens a cycle of learning the basics and practice of Theory U methods — practical tools for "awareness based system change". It includes live streams with Scharmer, much self-analysis and creativity, and a huge, very active worldwide community united by shared values, constantly creating something useful for the earth and people in both global and very local contexts [msg #465]. Gurova's trajectory shows how repeated cycles build on each other: in her first u.lab she was a fresh emigrant searching for a new identity; in the second she already had a group of like-minded people and several social projects; in the third she and a colleague supported Russian-speaking participants and created a community from which new individual projects grew; in the next she planned to bring her own business project at the intersection of psychology and design [msg #465]. Her stated hope: more conscious businesspeople, social activists, coaches, consultants and simply engaged people — leaders of their own lives — in the Russian-speaking world [msg #465].

### Case: Smart Course workshop — what a team with low hierarchy can do
Rodin ran an intro to Theory U for Smart Course, a company that creates educational programmes aimed at real change. He began by publicly stating his own non-resourceful state (poor sleep, slow thinking) and, given that, decided to follow the group: he offered them to choose between a bodily, a dialogue or a written practice, and they enthusiastically chose body work [msg #39]. His explanation of why this is rare: "Тело связано с травмой, сексуальностью и властью и обычно в компаниях наблюдается телесная скованность" (*The body is connected with trauma, sexuality and power, and usually in companies there is bodily stiffness*); people normally choose anything but moving together. "Если команде комфортно и легко вместе двигаться - это знак хорошего эмоционального контакта, прямой коммуникации, отсутствия страха власти и наказания." (*If a team is comfortable and at ease moving together, it is a sign of good emotional contact, direct communication and the absence of fear of power and punishment.*) He says this is a huge rarity. The workshop felt open and trusting, produced several ideas for joint projects, and everyone left energised [msg #39].

Conditions he identifies as making this possible at work [msg #39]:
1. A high inspiring goal — Smart Course's is "to create a world of mature people"; several team members sincerely said how much they love their work.
2. The whole team practises meditation; many do body practices (qi, aikido, dance).
3. The leader is neither authoritarian nor directive, yet is clearly and actively present in the process.
4. People are selected by values and resonance — how good they feel in the company's corporate environment.
The headline lesson: absence of hierarchy, a conscious leader and meaning in the work "can create a miracle" [msg #39].

### Leadership, challenges and the post-Soviet "overcoming" pattern
Theory U frameworks, which Rodin has worked with since 2019, taught him to look at leaders through the challenges they currently face; Theory U provides support for solving the hardest tasks and thus for confidently taking a personal or professional leadership position [msg #1013].
- **Definition of a challenge:** key life tasks that exceed us in complexity and require mobilising significant resources. Real challenges bring us to our knees before reality, demanding that we moderate our idea of our own omnipotence and strength. They are hard, but they are what allow moving forward rather than getting stuck. To grow it helps to distinguish main challenges from the background noise of routine tasks and to learn to solve them ever more effectively [msg #1013].
- **Theory U links leadership directly to challenges:** a leader manifests an intention — a wish to steer the world in a specific direction — which implies change inside and around oneself; attempts to realise it create daily difficulties or key challenges lasting years. So a leader's life is constant collision with challenges, and at a relatively high level of maturity and responsibility life will hardly resemble the journey "спокойно лежащего тоста с маслом" (*of a calmly lying buttered toast*) waiting to be eaten [msg #1013].
- **The "how": make patterns conscious.** Theory U raises awareness in a system in order to change it; it invites looking at established behaviour patterns — automatic programs, collective and personal habits related to the topic [msg #1013].
- **The main collective pattern:** in post-Soviet culture the main way to handle difficult situations is hardness and, honestly, cruelty applied to oneself and others to **overcome** challenges. It often yields results in the moment, but in Rodin's view the price is excessively high. Life becomes a series of obstacles; he has seen outstanding representatives of this paradigm literally living "by the sweat of their brow": overcoming themselves and others they lose sensitivity, hence vitality and the sense of contact with the world; they entrench a habit of violating themselves for results, multiply stress and easily fall into depression and burnout. Worst for corporate environments, reproducing the pattern multiplies a culture of violence around them — a culture that, on mythical and highly debatable grounds, reveres overcoming and violence as strength [msg #1013].
- **"Преодоление — сила слабых"** (*Overcoming is the strength of the weak*): of those not ready to truly change, grow and consider more consequences. By overcoming we try to press reality into our idea instead of taking responsibility for the diversity of consequences and results; the weakness becomes obvious once you stop reproducing it automatically and trace medium- and long-term outcomes [msg #1013]. Reflection questions offered:
  1. "Является ли умным то, требует постоянного насилия для продвижения вперед?" (*Is something smart if it requires constant violence to move forward?*) [msg #1013]
  2. "Получая насилием результат в моменте, какую цену мы заплатим за него завтра?" (*Getting a result through violence in the moment, what price will we pay for it tomorrow?*) [msg #1013]
  3. Is something that is constantly tense strong, or does it become rigid, lose creativity and break easily? [msg #1013]
- The overcoming pattern in leadership has already produced a corporate culture many people hate and physically ruins many lives. By bringing awareness to our automatisms we can change culture and create a work environment where the best sides of a person unfold and they need not constantly sweat and worry [msg #1013].

### Six observations from Theory U interviews with post-Soviet founders in New York and the Valley
Rodin interviewed founders from post-Soviet countries using the Theory U method, once three founders of one startup at once (Whizz, a bike-rental service for couriers across New York, with a $12M round). Observations [msg #1156]:
1. **The US market is hard but real** for those who actively worked and grew in the post-Soviet space over the last decade; there are many success cases, but all include good project experience before arriving in America [msg #1156].
2. **Economic advantage of Russian-speaking founders:** access to a market of remote employees with an understandable cultural code, in their native language, with salaries 2–3 times lower than American ones. It is an advantage at the start whose effectiveness declines over time [msg #1156].
3. **Few founders know how to service remote/hybrid work in terms of communication practices.** It seems new work formats should work by themselves; people do not want to spend resources on subtle things, but teams suffer from disintegration and broken contact [msg #1156].
4. **Founders must grow a lot in "ecological" (non-toxic) communication — clearly a needed skill.** America also has much workplace toxicity, but its communication norms rest on a more respectful position. Because of the culture, spread in Russian companies in recent years, of squeezing everything that moves and often not valuing employees, founders with that experience appear ruder and more abusive in the US; there they feel more sharply that it is a problem and must put extra effort into working on themselves [msg #1156].
5. **Almost everyone is skewed towards the external side of projects.** Attention to the inner and subtle — one's own state, communication with co-founders, informal ties in the team — becomes a priority only after the "survival" of the person or company, in which many get stuck for years for the sake of the US market. The imbalance is understandable but often hinders reaching that survival, because the distance is usually a marathon, not a sprint [msg #1156].
6. **Cultural integration weighs on people, and they lack skills, attention and understanding of what to do with it.** Starting to live outside the Russian-speaking bubble is hard, though the need is higher for founders than for other emigrants; this important question is often pushed to the periphery for a long time and procrastinated [msg #1156].
Rodin's overall impression: they inspire, going an incredibly hard path and literally pulling themselves and their teams into a completely new reality, which "always feels a bit like an intergalactic journey" [msg #1156].

### Presence, the AI world and who should learn these formats
Rodin's view: imbalance always holds potential; the potential of presence and sensitivity will be unlocked by leaders who are not afraid of misunderstanding and judgement and who bring presence and sensitivity into work processes that habitually ignore them. "The wind is favourable": unevenly, dividing the world into those lagging and those catching up, this potential will unfold. Success in the AI world increasingly depends on the ability to see a situation with fresh eyes and perceive it far more holistically than business used to. Hence he recommends that all scrum masters, team coaches, creators, entrepreneurs and leaders of any level learn such formats [msg #1114]. He also says he prefers to start new things himself, in close contact with people and reality — deep mind itself began five years earlier when Artem Temirov invited him to tell the Chernyi Cooperative team about decentralised management, while he was also experimenting with individual embodiment sessions and starting to work with teams; he keeps doing helping work "from the inside" so as not to lose contact with the essence of the product and the value created [msg #1114].

### Presence as healing of ruptures and "leading from the future"
Reflection after the 200-person co-sensing circle: there were warm faces, smiles and tears. Katya from Kyiv took the microphone and told the whole circle that there will be a long road to reconciliation, and it is unclear whether it is possible at all — yet she was there, with many others from Ukraine, and in that space people took steps not to be divided but to be near, united by presence and humanity even with tears in their eyes [msg #1178]. Rodin notes the simplicity of presence happens so powerfully that some can hardly find words for its transforming power, while others refuse to believe it and do not understand why the first are so inspired [msg #1178].

His theses [msg #1178]:
- However much work, money and friends we have, and whatever side we are on, in periods of great crisis and transformation we lose a lot; ruptures form in the living fabric of something whole, and healing them is the main thing to do as fast as possible.
- Books can inspire and point the direction, but the key ruptures — with nature, between people and with ourselves — must be healed by action in which we are fully present, listen deeply and respond authentically.
- In such actions arise the forces and answers to pass through unsolvable challenges and heal unhealable ruptures; acting from presence and openness we help ourselves truly engage with life, fearlessly take responsibility and stop turning away from reality however unsightly.
- This is the "лидерство из будущего" (*leadership from the future*) Scharmer teaches, and the "grand consciousness and awakening" to life that mystics teach.
- From presence in which anything may appear — both joy and frustration — answers arise that would not have arisen before.
- Everything in the books on spirituality, Buddhism, healing, psychotherapy and counselling behind him "requires living, not lying on a shelf" [msg #1178].
Deep mind's intended response: take responsibility for creating regular open spaces where this action is possible — open circles — and a non-corporate group of about one and a half months where in close contact people can find resources to move forward through the hardest transformations and get to know this way of living from openness and presence [msg #1178].

### Why Theory U is now the "human operating system for the AI era"
- **Tigran's story.** Tigran is a founder of a strong tech company whom the author met in Yerevan. After many years in Moscow he went to Armenia on intuition and met a woman, a former long-time collaborator of Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert at the MIT Media Lab, who soon invited him to the Media Lab. There he learned about Scharmer's Theory U, which kept "flirting" with him. In his words: "Она казалась очень важной, но немного оторванной от реальности." ("It seemed very important but a bit detached from reality.") Over the last few years it suddenly became clear: "Сегодня вижу ее не как замороченную философию, а как самый практичный мануал по современности." ("Today I see it not as convoluted philosophy but as the most practical manual for modernity.") [msg #1403].
- **The author's explanation.** The reason it suddenly makes sense is the nonlinearity, complexity and unpredictability of the AI era, in which habitual linear tools of thinking and acting look like "детским садом" ("kindergarten"). Theory U becomes a lens and practice that can create, ahead of time, the mindset an individual needs for the emerging tomorrow already visible today. That is leadership from the future rather than extrapolation of past data, which he says is the only thing that can help in such conditions. That is why he calls it leadership tool number one, whether you are running a huge company or just your own life [msg #1403].
- **What the group process aims at.** The author says Resonance tries to "load" into people a new operating system suited to a time when classical human project management will not be in demand, the leader's role will change radically, and nonlinear thinking and action are necessary. "Мы не просто обсуждает [sic] Теорию U — мы ее практикуем." ("We don't just discuss Theory U — we practise it.") [msg #1403].
- **COVID as proof case.** The 2020 retreat for Qiwi's CEO-1 team took place during COVID, amid total uncertainty. It was already clear that "ехать по рельсам стало проблематично" ("riding on rails had become problematic"), as was structuring reality through familiar data-based frameworks, because data describes the past. Just as the past held no COVID, it held no AI. The author says this only strengthens the demand for Theory U [msg #1728].

### Theory U as an evolutionary tool
Together with Jay Hao (Джей Хаос), a professor at a Melbourne technological university, the author views Theory U as an evolutionary tool. ### Embodied practices, hustle culture and psychological safety
- **Why outsiders devalue it.** Anticipating "Кто-то скажет: «Дураки»" ("Someone will say: 'Fools'"), the author argues that in today's hustle culture, and even more in "big dick culture", few can allow themselves such practices. Dismissive words surface quickly precisely because of these limits in people's minds: "Проще обесценить, чем признаться в своих собственных ограничениях." ("It's easier to devalue than to admit one's own limitations.") [msg #1812].
- **Mechanism.** Hustle cultures cannot allow themselves this freedom because they cannot handle the vulnerability these practices demand. They are built on violence and tension, which becomes a systemic factor that keeps a team out of psychological safety, the only state in which vulnerability is possible. From outside they may look impressive, but the author calls them terribly fragile at the base and not very effective [msg #1812].
- **What practising teams have.** The author believes such practices arise both from a very high level of team culture and from members' significant personal growth. Teams that can hold, and even enjoy, them already live with a different scale of trust and alignment, and with more joy and fulfilment from work. They can still push and be effective; Resonance itself needs high alignment, heavy administration and attention to detail. But they add whole new layers of reality to the workplace by including human nature in their attention and processes. At that level they can be in contact with vulnerable parts "не разваливаясь" ("without falling apart") [msg #1812].
- **Create, not just talk.** Such practices let a team not just talk about connection and trust or celebrate results, but actually create, live through and own them, and so keep growing stronger together. The author frames the closing movement as an interim result of individual and collective paths: people part after a finished project with a sense of great meaning and community [msg #1812].

### Presence as freedom from chronic lack
- **Observation.** After the three-hour Village, the last participant to speak said that although she was used to analysing and reflecting on her experience, she now felt she could skip it, and that this had special value. The author's reading: contemplative practices, including the Theory U approach, give a person the chance "просто быть со своим опытом" ("simply to be with one's experience") without needing to control it, shape it or extract anything from it [msg #1844].
- **Facilitator stance.** He no longer runs processes from an expert position or from above: "могу помочь формой, но без других она бесполезна" ("I can help with the form, but without the others it is useless") [msg #1844].
- **The habit of extracting life.** With 70 people in silent movement, the group created a chance to step out of the habit of extracting value from life. The author lists the forms this habit takes [msg #1844]:
  1. seeking benefit for one's social advancement in every contact;
  2. seeking sexual pleasure or acceptance in yet another neo-tantra;
  3. seeking a new insight about one's precious self at vipassana;
  4. seeking a place to offload uncomfortable emotions onto a psychotherapist.
  In this way even what was created to heal neurotic deficiency becomes a continuation of that neurosis: a deep sense of insufficiency. The author links this to what the Buddha named, which he says was mistranslated in early versions as "suffering" and is really a fundamental and often very quiet dissatisfaction [msg #1844].
- **What silence gives.** In often uncomfortable silence and slowness, the opposite of the stimulation that today's self-development formats are rich in, one can at some point become already whole "без необходимость [sic] добавлять сахар или соль в свой опыт" ("without needing to add sugar or salt to one's experience") [msg #1844].
- **Presence is always ours.** "Присутствие — всегда наше." ("Presence is always ours.") It is not an idea, insight or pleasure that was absent and suddenly appeared from an incredible master, substance or approach. It is the eternal, always-present ground and key foundation of existence, which we often forget while our attention is lost in endless fears and hopes [msg #1844].
- **Not drawing conclusions.** Asked what he saw in the group, how it differed from last year's and how today's Moscow and even Russia feel through it, the author found he did not want to draw conclusions at all. He offers two hypotheses. (a) The main unspoken state of the group was emotional rather than physical fatigue: from war, absurdity, abuse of power, the impossibility of influencing any of it, uncertainty, the "elephant in the room". (b) More simply, as we find a way to be more present, we lose the wish to return to the neurotic search for answers and the endless race for a better life. What remains is the wish "просто быть с радостью от того вклада, что уже сделан" ("simply to be with joy at the contribution already made") [msg #1844].
- **Small numbers still matter.** Seventy people in multi-million Moscow and Russia are like a drop in the sea, "И тем не менее, эта капля теперь есть в этом море и на него повлияет." ("And yet this drop is now in this sea and will influence it.") The author calls it a domino pushed: people gathered in July 2026 in a Moscow reached by drones, with queues for petrol and the need to carry cash, to share attention and presence with strangers [msg #1844].

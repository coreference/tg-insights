# Supporting teams through crisis

## What this covers

This part covers how the deep mind channel (a consultancy working on employees' mental state in organizations) approached team state during three crises: the 2020 COVID recession, the 2020 Belarus political crisis, and the start of the 2022 war. It includes deep mind's own claims and frameworks (crisis as a turning point, a six-element plan for conscious leaders, the PandaDoc case, the "нормально" problem) and a detailed summary of the Skolkovo report "Под давлением: лидерство в новых реалиях" (Under pressure: leadership in new realities), March 2022, which the channel shared. According to the source, the topic matters because political and economic crises can catastrophically reduce a company's productivity even without strikes, and because how a company treats its people in a crisis decides whether it rises or falls afterwards [msg #203, #280].

This part covers deep mind's writing (mostly by founder Max Rodin) from May 2022 to March 2024 about how crises — the war, mobilization in September 2022, mass relocation, the Belarus repressions of 2020, and the Crocus terrorist attack of March 2024 — affect people and teams in companies, and what leaders can do. It includes the at-risk groups, the findings of deep mind's qualitative research in Russian bigtech, the shift of responsibility for team state onto team leaders, adaptive coping after a terrorist attack, and deep mind's concrete support formats [msg #656, #724, #738, #904, #1018, #1019].

According to the source this matters because unresourced crises do not make people stronger but traumatize them, the trauma shapes all future decisions and results, stress leads to burnout, depression and illness, and tragedies reduce flexibility, clarity, creativity and effectiveness — so team results suffer directly [msg #656, #738, #1018].

Two later posts (August and October 2026) in which Max Rodin (deep mind) returns to working with teams and whole companies when people are especially anxious or emotionally strained. #1877 lays out the methodological base (research on psychological trauma), defines resilience as a systemic, resource-based property and sorts company crisis measures along two axes — external vs internal supports, built in advance vs in the acute phase. #1953, written after three strikes on Yandex data centres within a week, introduces the concept of continuous traumatic stress, describes how firefighting teams predictably degrade, and gives seven guidelines grounded in Hobfoll's five pillars and deep mind's practice [msg #1877, #1953].

Why it matters, per the source: companies usually lump four quite different conversations into one and therefore "часто делают не то и не тогда, когда нужно" (often do the wrong thing and not at the time it is needed) [msg #1877]; and under a mask of composure and heroism there is intensive burning of resources that, in a crisis, often nobody tracks [msg #1953].

Relation to the existing topic file: these posts extend the earlier Yandex frontline and PandaDoc cases (#1019, #387) with new detail, add a 2x2 supports map, a critique of burnout surveys, the concepts of continuous traumatic stress and Hobfoll's five pillars, and a second practical list that complements (not replaces) the earlier six-element plan (#280) and Regulate-Relate-Reason (#738). One figure differs slightly — see *Caveats* [msg #1877, #1953].

## Core ideas

- **A crisis is a turning point that sets an upward or downward spiral.** Companies either build real unity and loyalty by sharing difficulties with staff, or show that business and people go separately, after which the best employees leave (see *Crisis as a turning point*) [msg #203].
- **Political crises cut productivity sharply.** deep mind estimated a 30–50% productivity fall in Belarusian IT due to employee stress in 2020, and observed a 40–50% fall in PandaDoc's product team [msg #280, #387].
- **Standard wellbeing perks are not enough.** A subscription to an online-therapist matching service or a meditation app is insufficient even in normal times, let alone during a revolution; a dedicated plan is needed (see *Plan of a conscious leader*) [msg #280].
- **"Нормально" hides distress.** People outside combat zones often answer "fine", but this masks fatigue, anxiety, sleep problems and panic; suppressing emotions raises trauma/PTSD risk and leads to fear-driven decisions [msg #624].
- **Leaders under stress make poor decisions without noticing.** Most Russian leaders in early 2022 acted from freeze or fight/flight; stress shifts thinking from future goals to automatic habits and makes leaders over- or under-take risk [msg #637].
- **In crisis, humane leadership beats tough, directive leadership.** The Skolkovo report states that the pandemic debunked the stereotype of the tough, charismatic, directive crisis leader; recent research points to humanity, frequent supportive communication and explanation of direction [file #637].

- **Crisis grows people only when they have resources.** With enough mental and physical resources a crisis can make a person question their worldview and grow to a more complex level; without resources and support it leads to trauma instead. See *Two non-obvious at-risk employee groups* [msg #656].
- **Three persistent people-management challenges:** identity, communication/relationships, and people's state. They are not situational but reflect social processes that began before 2022 [msg #724, #738, #785].
- **State = trigger + self-regulation.** Companies focus only on external stressors; but state also depends on self-regulation skills, which can be trained at any moment regardless of circumstances [msg #724].
- **Leader state drives team state.** 90% of employees reduce engagement if the leader is stressed; science "clearly shows" the leader's state is a key factor of team state, so leaders need support first [img #784] [msg #1019].
- **Care for team state is shifting from the company onto team leaders** and becomes a career-defining skill [msg #904].
- **After collective tragedy, a team cannot simply work as before;** leaders should add emotion-focused coping (acknowledgement, open and tolerant communication) to the corporate habit of problem-focused coping [msg #1018].
- **Crises are an opportunity** — for transforming the work model and, in deep mind's experience, for teams to become more cohesive and trusting after being supported through a crisis [msg #724, #1019].

- **Resilience is physiological and systemic.** It is the stress-resistance of the nervous system with preserved flexibility, and in an organization it is a property of the system and its resources, not of an individual's character (see *Resilience as a systemic, resource-based property*) [msg #1877].
- **Resilience training without changing conditions is shifting responsibility.** Because resilience rests on resources, the lens for keeping performance in hard times is: which resources can we feed into the company [msg #1877].
- **Four different conversations, not one.** Supports are external or internal, and each is built either in advance or inside the acute phase (see *Two-axis map of crisis supports*) [msg #1877].
- **External administration of the crisis is not enough.** PandaDoc managed the crisis externally with great attention, yet the team's performance fell by more than half in the acute phase; without internal supports real resilience is unlikely [msg #1877].
- **Measure stress now, not burnout later.** Burnout is already the result of chronic stress; managing without a live state tool is "managing blind" [msg #1877].
- **Russian companies are entering continuous traumatic stress,** which has predictable psychological laws: composure first, then fatigue, disappointment, anger, burnout and a closing culture [msg #1953].
- **Someone concrete must own people's state,** the way an on-duty engineer owns an incident [msg #1953].
- **First action, body and connection; meanings later** (see *Seven guidelines*) [msg #1953].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Plan of a conscious leader or company in a political crisis (6 elements)
Purpose: the minimum set of elements that, in the author's tentative view ("Пока кажется" — "for now it seems"), a conscious leader's or company's plan should include in circumstances like the 2020 Belarus revolution. It grew out of deep mind's work with a Minsk company, where the author observed that conscious leaders in crisis begin to understand "можно и нужно искать специальные инструменты для помощи сотрудникам" (it is possible and necessary to look for special tools to help employees) [msg #280].

1. **Readiness to invest resources into stabilizing employees' inner state.** The company must be willing to spend money and time on this, not treat it as optional [msg #280].
2. **Very careful work on messages to employees and on all communication by founders or managers** — preferably together with consultants. What leaders say (and how) becomes a major factor in the team's state [msg #280].
3. **Regular diagnostics of team state.** Measuring how teams are doing on a recurring basis, not once [msg #280].
4. **Personal support for employees in the zone of heightened risk or stress.** Those most affected get individual help [msg #280].
5. **Group meetings with facilitators** to teach mindfulness and self-help skills and to hold safe discussions of what is happening [msg #280].
6. **Changes in business processes that legitimize reduced performance.** The organization formally accepts that people cannot work at full capacity, instead of pretending nothing changed [msg #280].

The author expected demand for such services only to grow in the current socio-political setup [msg #280].

### deep mind's crisis-support engagement with PandaDoc (Minsk, August–September 2020)
Context: PandaDoc, an American startup with a back office in Minsk, needed to stabilize staff during the Belarusian political crisis. During protests with much violence in the streets and in prisons, the product team's productivity fell by 40–50%. The company very actively helped employees and took part in politics, and turned to deep mind for professional support. The sequence of what deep mind did [msg #387]:

1. **Formed a working group led by an anti-crisis psychologist** [msg #387].
2. **Talked to every team member personally within one week** and analyzed the state of all participants [msg #387].
3. **Issued personalized recommendations** as a result of the diagnostics [msg #387].
4. **Gave psychological support at the team's most acute crisis points** [msg #387].
5. **Helped find resources inside the team** [msg #387].
6. **Supported the leaders themselves** [msg #387].
7. **Formed recommendations for recovery in the future** [msg #387].

deep mind framed the case as "как компаниям подготовиться к революции" (how companies can prepare for a revolution) and said it felt obliged to share the experience regardless of how events in Russia develop. It listed four further directions the story could be expanded into: (1) how exactly a company can prepare beforehand or help employees during political events; (2) what general conclusions can be drawn from living through such processes; (3) how a person can cope with violence and instability around them; (4) how to cooperate and determine one's place in events. (!) INCOMPLETE: these four directions are only named in this message, not developed [msg #387]. In February 2022 deep mind turned the PandaDoc experience into a guide for global startups with teams in Eastern Europe on how to keep a team working and support staff during political crises; global startups had again started contacting deep mind because their employees were under strong stress from uncertainty. (!) INCOMPLETE: the guide's content (an Inc. Russia article) is not included in the source [msg #576, #582].

### Two-step deepening of the answer "нормально"
Purpose: in group meetings, when most people answer "how are you" with "нормально" (fine), deep mind sometimes goes deeper with two steps [msg #624]:

1. **Ask people to rate their level of fatigue over the last weeks** [msg #624].
2. **Ask them to decode where exactly the current fatigue comes from** [msg #624].

Per the source, these two steps usually open "целый ящик пандоры" (a whole Pandora's box) of this "fine": behind it are fatigue from being unable to bear what is happening, anxiety about the future, sleep problems or panic attacks, and much else that is not ordinary or normal for these people [msg #624].

### Two harms of not being able to identify one's state
deep mind argues that for most people, being unable to identify their state, share it and act to change it "играет злую шутку" (plays a cruel trick) [msg #624]:

1. **Higher risk of trauma and PTSD.** People push experiences into "нормально", "держимся" (we're holding on), "окей", and suppress them into mind or body instead of living them through naturally. Suppressing or distancing from emotions also lowers overall sensitivity and level of consciousness, which creates much greater risk of psychological trauma or PTSD that would substantially change one's whole future life [msg #624].
2. **Worse quality of present actions.** Suppressed emotions break through more strongly and impulsively and make us act under them unconsciously — a cycle of self-fulfilling prophecies. If we are very afraid for the future but cannot handle that emotion skillfully, we decide and act out of fear: not from a vision of the desired future, our values and resources, but from fear of something unwanted. The author's metaphor: we race full speed into a wall, and instead of looking at the road and turning the wheel, we are fully focused on the wall itself [msg #624].

### Skolkovo study "Under pressure: leadership in new realities" — design
The report by the Center for Executive Coaching, Development and Career of the Moscow School of Management SKOLKOVO (March 2022) studied how Russian leaders met the crisis. The channel summarizes it as research on how Russian leaders behaved in the previous 2 months [msg #637, file #637].
- **Sample:** 74 leaders of large, small and medium businesses. Shares in the participant diagram: 51%, 29%, 15%, 4%, 1% across the categories owner of small/medium business, CEO or top manager of large business, CEO or top manager of small/medium business, top manager of a state corporation, NGO leaders. (!) UNCLEAR: the transcription does not show which percentage belongs to which category [file #637].
- **Position levels:** entrepreneurs, CEOs, vice presidents, board members, directors of divisions, enterprises and units [file #637].
- **Industries:** telecoms, IT, advertising, investment business, banks, development, real estate, oil and gas, gold mining, sea ports, FMCG, agriculture, manufacturing, transport, leasing, services, consulting [file #637].
- **Methods:** online survey (74 people), in-depth interviews (22 people), qualitative thematic and quantitative analysis. **Dates:** 11.03.2022–18.03.2022 [file #637].
- **Rationale for the sample:** people who, by their role, cannot sink into despondency for long or watch passively — entrepreneurs, CEOs and top managers actively running businesses, on whose decisions the results of the business and the wellbeing of hundreds or thousands of employees depend, who bear legal responsibility, and whose life meaning is often their projects, partnerships and legacy, now put in question [file #637].
- **Framing in the introduction (Ulanovsky):** the ancient Greeks, who gave us the word, considered a crisis a turning point; turning points are now so frequent that the word loses its original sense of rarity. The ability to overcome crisis stops being a narrow managerial skill and becomes necessary for almost everyone who manages people, if not for every person. For Russian leaders turbulence is a permanent part of their work; a Russian CEO answered his board's reproaches about plans not matching results: "Как мне добиться точности в прогнозах, когда" every year several "black swans" land in his pond. The current crisis, though just begun, is already no smaller in impact than the previous one [file #637].

### Skolkovo map of the leader's "headaches" (8 clusters)
The key challenges reflect (1) the specifics of industries and (2) the particular vulnerabilities of individual companies; nevertheless the report outlines these clusters (Table 1, March 2022) [file #637]:

1. **Foggy perspective (туманная перспектива):** uncertainty about tomorrow; conditions changing daily, situation worsening; impossible to build a strategy or influence the situation; risk of a big decline or explosive growth [file #637].
2. **Supply disruption:** equipment becoming more expensive; logistics; supply interruptions; shortage of spare parts; imported equipment and packaging; cost of raw materials and goods [file #637].
3. **Sales collapse (обрыв сбыта):** failure of sales channels; low sales; falling demand; clients' panic moods; customers leaving markets; risk of a non-payments crisis [file #637].
4. **Liquidity and payments:** international payments and transfers; high inflation and prices; exchange-rate differences and unstable ruble; cash-flow planning; falling household incomes [file #637].
5. **Team state:** people's psychological state; state of level-1 managers; salaries; how to retain the team and jobs; employee motivation; non-acceptance of global changes [file #637].
6. **IT restrictions:** restrictions on IT; hardware and software; instability of IT infrastructure [file #637].
7. **Resources and assets:** revising priorities and architecture; probability of bankruptcy; holding on to assets; threat of a domestic default; credit resources; external financing [file #637].
8. **Unclear requirements:** not understanding legal procedures, processes and consequences; new currency-regulation laws; sanctions; a ban on part of the work; requests from supervising state agencies [file #637].

The report then splits these into two groups. **Business challenges:** supply disruption; broken sales channels; problem assets and resource management; liquidity and payments; IT and software restrictions; unclear requirements. **Leadership challenges in the narrow sense:** daily changes; impossibility of forecasts; the team's psychological state; retaining people / forced layoffs; the leader's own mood [file #637].

### Three basic strategic reactions of leaders
From the first interviews the researchers formulated three basic behavior strategies, plus an "own answer" option [file #637]:

1. **Waiting (выжидающая):** "Взял(а) паузу, веду бизнес как раньше, не принимаю значимых решений" (took a pause, run the business as before, make no significant decisions) — chosen by 46%. These leaders paused to see what happens before deciding how to act [file #637].
2. **Active (активная):** made organizational changes, looked for new projects and resources — 43%. They actively reacted, seeking solutions to operational problems and new strategic opportunities [file #637].
3. **Radical (радикальная):** decided to exit management, relocate, close the business or its key lines — no answers. The researchers find this explainable: leaving a business and a top role usually takes time, deals and approvals [file #637].
- "Own answer" — 11%; these answers were folded into the list of management steps [file #637].
- Two limitations noted by the authors: no expat leaders in the sample who had to leave their posts in a hurry; and Russian leaders who decided to exit their business and role may not have taken part because the survey was of little relevance to them [file #637].

### Wheel of anti-crisis org design (4 directions, 13 categories)
The respondents described more than a hundred concrete steps, from revising marketing channels to reorienting production. The researchers grouped them into four directions, each with three to four categories, keeping respondents' original wording. They stress this is **not a list of recommendations** but actual steps taken; some complement each other, some are opposite (for example, honoring commitments to partners vs notifying them that work on old terms is impossible); each reflects a specific business situation. The wheel is not an exhaustive set of crisis decisions; it is meant for widening one's view of the range of possible actions, cross-checking and generating one's own ideas [file #637].

### Strategy-money-team triad
Across all categories, the most common and most mentioned actions were: (1) searching for and revising strategy; (2) decisions about money, prices and spending; (3) an honest conversation with the team. ### Leaders' attitudes in crisis — nine coping quotes
Why it matters, per the report: the leader's attitude to what is happening is "краеугольный камень" (cornerstone) of crisis leadership — hopelessness, optimism, acceptance or detachment shape how the leader acts, how much effort they put in after failures and what they transmit to others. Many leaders described a cyclical change of attitude, from anxiety, depression and catastrophizing to moderate hope, confidence and working mood, explained by the extreme volatility of news and forecasts (from catastrophic to moderately hopeful scenarios). To avoid overly rational, ready-made answers, researchers used an indirect approach: nine quotes metaphorically conveying types of attitude and action, built on R. Lazarus's classic coping strategies (active changing of the situation, positive reappraisal, planning, social support, self-control, avoidance, accepting responsibility, distancing). Respondents chose the quotes closest to their attitude [file #637]:

1. **Lao Tzu — 23% (most popular):** "Нужно твердо сохранять покой, и тогда все вещи" will change by themselves and we will only contemplate their return. Interpretation: when events in the country and world look beyond personal control, the idea of normalizing things by keeping one's own calm sounds hopeful. The authors' earlier study of successful CEOs in turbulent markets also noted their ability to keep a cool head despite pressure and failures [file #637].
2. **Mark Twain — 22%:** "Самый лучший способ подбодрить себя —" is to cheer up someone else. Respondents explained in interviews that by helping those who have it harder now, they bear their own difficulties more easily [file #637].
3. **Joseph Brodsky — 14%:** the moment you lay blame on someone or something, you undermine your own resolve to change anything. This describes accepting responsibility — what narrative practices call returning authorship of one's life and psychology calls returning the internal locus of control from external circumstances, other people and fate to oneself, one's choice and action [file #637].
4. **Dwight Eisenhower — 11%:** "Планы — ничто, планирование — все" (Plans are nothing, planning is everything) [file #637].
5. **Lewis Carroll — 8%:** you have to run as fast as you can just to stay in place [file #637].
6. **Eleanor Porter — 7%:** only having lost one's legs can one truly understand what happiness walking is [file #637].
7. **Morihei Ueshiba — 7%** (per the chart's adjacent figures): from time to time one must withdraw among high mountains and secluded valleys to restore one's connection with the source of life [file #637].
8. **Gay Langland — 5%:** sometimes the best thing to do is to do nothing [file #637].
9. **Franz Kafka — 4% (least chosen):** "Не нахожу себе места." — as if everything I owned had left me. The report finds it notable that top managers least often chose the quote expressing inability to cope with oneself and circumstances [file #637].

### Model of resilience practices (4 groups)
Leaders were asked how they cope with their own anxiety, depression and absorption in news and return to a working state. The answers were grouped into a model of resilience practices in four areas. The authors call the division conditional, since in human psychology "все связано со всем" (everything is connected to everything) [file #637].

**1. Mental practices (Table 6).** Rationale: our thoughts and ways of interpreting events directly affect what we feel and how we behave — the basis of cognitive-behavioral therapy; these practices belong to "hygiene of the mind" [file #637].
- **Reframing** — finding a new "frame" of perception, another interpretation of events that lets you act. Respondents: occupying the head with searching for new opportunities; seeing the situation as a unique growth opportunity; thinking "what can I do to restore the standard of living — create for substitution"; defining a vision of one's new life, place, role and tasks, rethinking life priorities; learning; looking for positive news and successful examples of working in crisis; reading history and economic reviews [file #637].
- **Accepting reality** — radical acceptance, known since the Stoics and key in modern dialectical behavior therapy: the strength of acceptance, patience and preserving dignity amid large events one cannot influence. Respondents: accepted as given that things will not be as before, there will be a new reality, losses are inevitable; looking to the future and seeking opportunities; recalling the experience of 1991 and 1998; awareness that the former world is gone; focus on preserving the company and profitability; "staying in Russia with the team, no hysteria"; "as an entrepreneur I am used to uncertainty and constantly changing conditions" [file #637].
- **Minimizing news** — uncontrolled news consumption has little to do with "being informed"; the report calls it a form of self-destructive behavior, auto-aggression when one cannot direct aggression outward to change something. Respondents: strictly limiting news reading to 2–3 times a day; not reading news more than 20 minutes a day; minimizing the news feed; mind hygiene; turning off all news channels and looking only at facts — what has already happened, not what will happen [file #637].
- **Anti-crisis plan** — one respondent formulated anti-crisis steps for his family and implemented them [file #637].

**2. Physical practices (Table 7).** Rationale: our state is our "base" determining how clearly we think in the morning, whether we see the "big picture" during the day and whether we have resources to help others; cognitive and emotional processes are always embodied; daily physical load, sleep and healthy food are healing in themselves [file #637].
- **Sport:** keeping physically and morally fit; actively doing sport; returned to sport and bought a massage course; lots of fitness; keeping or increasing training load; running; mandatory daily walks [file #637].
- **Keeping busy:** occupying the head with keeping work uninterrupted; working 24/7 without distraction; being as active as possible in all processes; keeping a daily routine; giving more time to work than to news [file #637].
- **Resting more:** temporarily moved to another country to calm down; short rest breaks — a trip out of town, a good bathhouse; taking vacation; resting more [file #637].
- **Sleep and nutrition:** sleeping more; trying to get enough sleep; special attention to food — more vitamins, vegetables and fruit, almost cutting out alcohol [file #637].

**3. Emotionally supportive practices (Table 8).** Each crisis has its own mood: in the pandemic its core was anxiety about health and a feeling of fragility; now it is rather a feeling of suspension and being "flooded" by news. Some leaders introduced company-paid individual and group psychological support sessions for employees; they themselves turn to psychology and spiritual practices [file #637].
- **Psychology:** "the hardest question today — I listen to lectures on psychoanalysis"; working with a psychologist; positive thinking; temporarily moving to another country to calm down [file #637].
- **Prayer:** praying more [file #637].
- **Meditation and yoga:** yoga and meditation every morning [file #637].
- **Hobbies:** doing a favorite hobby; music [file #637].

**4. Social practices (Table 9).** Rationale: modern psychology and philosophy say we construct identity and our world in communication and agreements with others; turning to people and groups that give new ideas, a sober view and support is one solution leaders use [file #637].
- **Choosing one's circle:** talking with people who have experience and can steadily bear crisis states; carefully choosing the circle, avoiding panic-mongers and haters; minimizing contact with "complainers"; talking more with positive people; talking with smart and calm people; staying in Russia with the team without hysteria [file #637].
- **Business mini-communities:** more meetings in energizing mini-communities; talking with acquaintances from other companies and industries for a fuller picture; keeping contact with counterparts at competitors to understand trends; a discussion in an e-club of one forum helped a lot; more meetings with partners; talking with other leaders [file #637].
- **Altruism and supporting others:** showing optimism and helping those in a critical situation ("against their background I see my affairs are in order"); talking with entrepreneurs who have it harder and trying to help; humanly supporting the team and instilling confidence in oneself as a leader who plans to care for people and keep jobs and salaries; running trainings and discussing problems, giving one's view; energizing people [file #637].
- **Close circle:** meetings with like-minded people; discussing possible solutions with friends and colleagues; time with loved ones and friends, going to the theater and visiting; more time with family; communicating with loved ones [file #637].

Most popular resilience practices overall, per the summary page: reframing the situation, doing sport, and carefully choosing one's circle [file #637].

### Two non-obvious at-risk employee groups
Purpose: to tell leaders which employees, beyond anyone lacking mental resources and supports, may be having an especially hard time during change (written May 2022) and deserve attention [msg #656].

1. **Young people under 25.** deep mind had spent a month teaching students of MIPT (МФТИ) to understand their states, gather mental resources and regulate difficult experiences; these are "bright heads" hunted by top international companies, and they feel "мягко говоря, не очень" (to put it mildly, not great). Why they are at risk: (a) the young have less experience of living through crises; (b) they feel what is happening more sharply because they sense the future better and feel the damage to natural, healthy development — they feel "что будущее забрали" (that the future has been taken away) and that the value of freedom, which really matters greatly, has been restricted right now. Combined with not-yet-developed self-awareness skills, the absence of psychotherapy and of an understanding of their emotions, the situation "может сильно травмировать это поколение" (may seriously traumatize this generation) [msg #656].
2. **Competitive, effective, achievement-oriented employees ("orange" in Spiral Dynamics).** People at this level find their support neither in satisfying power impulses nor in group approval, but in effectiveness and movement towards material goals. They lean on proven analytical frameworks and methods; by laying out life through product methodologies, doing research, they get "некоторую иллюзию контроля над реальностью" (a certain illusion of control over reality) — the belief that everything can be solved with the right approach and systematic work. When "движутся тектонические плиты" (tectonic plates are moving), these limited frameworks stop coping: life is more complex than a product approach, broader than effectiveness and bigger than any theory or belief. Habitual efforts may produce no result or even make things worse [msg #656].

Recommendation: if you have many such employees and the business depends on them, pay attention to them — talk with them more often, be more flexible this year, or give them an opportunity to develop self-regulation skills inside the company [msg #656].

### Three people-management challenges (deep mind bigtech research)
Context: in August 2022 deep mind ran qualitative research in Russian bigtech (several key Russian IT companies, finished early September) about current challenges in teams. Researchers studied very different leaders, "digging below the surface" for the most intense pains (rated 7–9 out of 10) that clearly affected team results. The pains generalized into three challenges [msg #724, #738, #785]:

1. **"Кто мы и где мы" (Who we are and where we are) — identity.** The habitual identity of individuals and whole teams eroded after February 24. Non-obvious insight: leaders and teams underestimate this question and have no tools for deep identity renewal. Six months on, people were only just coming out of the shock of war and starting to adapt more rationally. The changes are so fundamental they raise deeper questions than people were used to. Classic business-planning tools do not touch the layer of personal and team identity: they help "потушить пожар тревоги" (put out the fire of anxiety) and rebuild tactics, but do not let people truly regain ground under their feet. Adaptation requires not only (or not so much) business planning as work with the meta-picture, sensing the future, and personal and team values in a mode of continuous open dialogue. Teams that have developed facilitation as a way of interacting and are open to new "strange" group tools such as Theory U have an advantage [msg #724].
2. **How teams keep connectedness and distribute information flows — communication.** On top of familiar remote work came prolonged relocation and hybrid office modes that disrupt communication and community. Insight: information flows changed a lot and teams will have to reinvent communication again. With a hybrid office and truly global relocation, members of one team may be in very different time zones and circumstances, which lowers integration. It is unclear how to keep the former warm contact and informal, random connections. Communication in the new reality needs much more psycho-emotional resource and new tools not yet clear to teams [msg #724].
3. **How people feel and what they do about it — state.** Employees exhausted their adaptation resources and have long been in elevated stress, which affects work. Insight: see *Trigger plus self-regulation model* below [msg #724].

The October 2022 follow-up lists the same three in the order: psycho-emotional state of people; broken communication; broken personal and team identity [msg #738]. The December 2022 restatement: violation or loss of team identity; disintegration of relationships and communication; people's state. After repeated returns to leaders and presentations (including at EpicGrowth and other professional communities), deep mind saw team dynamics improve or worsen with circumstances, but the challenges did not disappear — "они не являются ситуативными" (they are not situational) and reflect objective social processes that began before 2022 and will continue in 2023 or beyond [msg #785].

**Window of opportunity.** Extraordinary external circumstances set organizational systems in motion again at the most fundamental levels. Attending to people-management challenges creates leverage for transforming the work model itself: bold leaders can bring current scientific knowledge about humans and modern group-interaction frameworks into work, creating unique or "iconic" ways of working that give an advantage in attracting and retaining talent and in a new round of productivity and innovation [msg #724].

### Trigger plus self-regulation model of employee state
A common bias in how companies view employee state (deep mind flags it as a question that strongly affects things): leaders and employees focus on the external circumstances that caused stress. That is their strength — they know how to reach goals and solve problems — but an excessively result-focused mindset becomes a limitation. State consists not only of the trigger (stressful circumstances) but also of self-regulation skills, the ways of reacting to stress. These skills can be worked on at any moment regardless of circumstances, which can substantially relieve one of the acute team pains. Not knowing this is a significant limitation for improving people's state "здесь и сейчас вне зависимости от обстановки на фронте и политической ситуации" (here and now regardless of the situation at the front and the political situation) [msg #724].

### Adaptive coping strategies after terrorist attacks
Context: written the day after the March 2024 terrorist attack. Terrorist attacks do not pass in a day; their consequences stretch over time and affect society's psychological, social and economic well-being. They evoke fear, anger, sadness and a sense of loss, and because of proximity and suddenness they hit the essential human need for safety and emotional stability — all of which should be recognized as natural [msg #1018].

Maladaptive strategies common in society (because nobody teaches people to live through such things): (a) denial and avoidance, which, according to research the author links, make recovery harder; (b) focusing on the negative — getting stuck in anger or fear without trying to process them constructively — which, per the linked research, raises the risk of PTSD and depression [msg #1018].

Adaptive strategies enabling effective coping:
1. **Active constructive action.** Everyone reacts as they can, but it is good when we can do something and see reactions around — people writing about feelings, expressing solidarity, sharing contacts of blood-collection centres, specialists reminding about witness trauma. The author admits these may not always be useful in substance ("нет нужды в таком количестве крови" — there is no need for that much blood; non-specialists often don't understand what they're talking about), but it is very healthy by nature: action keeps people from freezing in trauma and sends signals of care and connectedness that create the feeling of a normal society. Unlike denial and ignoring, such strategies are shown by the cited research to strengthen overall resilience [msg #1018].
2. **Seeking psychological support and social contact.** "Это время получать и давать поддержку" (This is the time to receive and give support), including therapeutic support. Ignoring support and advice is entirely legitimate, but given how intense and complex feelings can be, a psychotherapeutic conversation may be worthwhile — "особенно если нам кажется, что мы 'нормально'" (especially if it seems to us that we're "fine") or untouched [msg #1018].
3. **Working on psychological resilience skills.** Later on, systematic training of resilience skills is a good investment of time and money; research by Seligman, Csikszentmihalyi and other resilience psychologists, as cited, stresses teaching skills that promote psychological well-being and quick recovery after stressful events, especially for societies in crisis [msg #1018].

### Four formats of psychological support for teams in crisis
Context: after the March 2024 attack four leaders asked deep mind for supportive team workshops within a week. The author's view: this may be a good idea for leaders who, although they know more and more psychology, "не должны быть командными психологами" (should not be team psychologists) [msg #1019].

1. **One or two one-hour supportive meetings with a qualified facilitator** — the most frequent request. A specially trained person helps the team not to silence the topic (which would harm team dynamics) and also not to go deeper into it than is appropriate for working relationships; the session discusses things properly and regulates state "на уровне физиологии" (at the level of physiology). Why it works: it sends an important healing signal that both the events and the employees matter, and creates care at the workplace [msg #1019].
2. **Regular group sessions.** The first meeting is often expanded into more sustained support: group sessions with the team at least once a week, for example over the next month. This helps live through emotions optimally and build contact in the team [msg #1019].
3. **Support for the leaders themselves.** In Russia there is a pattern of creating all kinds of support for employees but forgetting managers, as if they should be resilient anyway — "Это, конечно, заблуждение" (This is, of course, a misconception). Since science clearly shows leader state is a key factor of team state, the author would first consider workshops supporting leaders [msg #1019].
4. **A resilience program (Less stress).** deep mind's resilience program, just used to help the Yandex.Education team fight fatigue. It not only supports the team in the moment but builds a clear foundation of resilience skills that do not disappear later [msg #1019].

### Resilience as a systemic, resource-based property (deep mind definition)
Purpose: the conceptual base deep mind uses at workshops, programs and consultations in business on supporting teams in anxious times. Methodologically it relies on the scientific base that emerged from research on psychological trauma [msg #1877].

1. **People go through traumatic events differently.** Some are traumatized less, some recover faster; this quality is called resilience ("резильентностью"), and it is "вовсе не эзотерично, а вполне физиологично" (not at all esoteric, but quite physiological) [msg #1877].
2. **What it is physiologically.** It is about how resistant the nervous system is to stress while still keeping flexibility and adaptability — as opposed to simply enduring hardship and "freezing up" ("*отмораживаться*"), which later presents an inflated bill ("выставляя повышенный счет после") [msg #1877].
3. **The slide definition shown at the talk.** The photo attached to the post shows Rodin speaking at Yandex's "Лига талантов" (League of Talents), module "Про себя" (About oneself), with a slide headed "how we understand stability — resilience". As legible in the image: the concept grew out of neurophysiology, from research on people who withstood heavy events and quickly got back into shape; today it is applied in psychology, business and athlete training; resilience is the nervous system's ability to keep balance under load, keep access to the neocortex and recover after stress; it is not rigidity but flexibility and recovery; and it is a skill [img #1877].
4. **It belongs to the system.** "Резильентность — свойство системы, а не характера отдельного сотрудника" (Resilience is a property of the system, not of an individual employee's character): it is entirely about the resources present in the system that help digest stress [msg #1877].
5. **Consequence for interventions.** "тренинг устойчивости без изменения условий — это просто перекладывание ответственности" (resilience training without changing conditions is simply shifting responsibility). Measures to preserve team performance should be viewed through the lens of which resources the company can be nourished with to increase its stability in the storm. "Мы не знаем, каким будет будущее, но уже в настоящем можем укрепить опоры, на которые эта буря придет" (We don't know what the future will be, but already in the present we can strengthen the supports this storm will hit) [msg #1877].

### Psychological dynamics of a firefighting team under continuous threat
Purpose: to recognize the "laws" of the psychology of continuous traumatic stress so the company works with the psychological mobilization instead of ignoring it [msg #1953].

1. **Name the reality: continuous traumatic stress.** A growing number of companies and people in Russia face what in Ukraine has long been known as continuous traumatic stress. The term was introduced by psychologist Gill Straker, who worked in South Africa under apartheid. "Обычная травма — про прошлое, а здесь угроза впереди и может случиться в любой из следующих дней" (Ordinary trauma is about the past, but here the threat is ahead and may happen on any of the coming days). Acknowledging this is important so as not to ignore the psychological mobilization behind it [msg #1953].
2. **Phase 1 — composure.** The heroes putting out fires are at first collected and focused [msg #1953].
3. **Phase 2 — after a few weeks.** Composure is replaced by fatigue, disappointment and anger. Many burn out, lose meaning and quit [msg #1953].
4. **Hidden cracks.** Under the routine "держимся, работаем" (we're holding on, we're working), thin places tear and contradictions surface [msg #1953].
5. **Closing of the culture.** Leaders, teams and culture move from an open position to a closed one: everything fixates on safety and development becomes difficult. This is normal when drones are flying in, and ruinous on a technology market that does not wait [msg #1953].
6. **Ownership.** Under the mask of composure, task focus and sometimes outright heroism there is inevitably intensive burning of resources that often nobody tracks in a crisis. Therefore a specific person must be responsible for people — just as the on-duty engineer is responsible for an incident [msg #1953].

### Hobfoll's five pillars of help after mass trauma
Stevan Hobfoll, generalizing research on help after mass traumas, identified five pillars: safety (безопасность), calming (успокоение), a sense of influence/efficacy (ощущение влияния), connection (связь) and hope (надежда). The post names them without further explanation; together with deep mind's practice with companies in crises they yield the seven guidelines below [msg #1953].

### Seven guidelines for supporting people in a company crisis
Purpose: simple orientation points from Hobfoll's pillars plus deep mind's crisis practice, applicable to Yandex and to any company that war "suddenly" reached [msg #1953].

1. **Tell the truth, including "we don't know yet".** "Неизвестность выматывает сильнее плохих новостей" (Uncertainty exhausts more than bad news) [msg #1953].
2. **Sleep and rotation of people on duty are the foundation.** Without them no support programs will work. Support programs also have their place, but with real experts in crisis help [msg #1953].
3. **Complete the stress cycle through the body every day:** movement, a long exhale [msg #1953].
4. **Ask each other "как ты сейчас?" (how are you right now?) and don't correct the answer** [msg #1953].
5. **Don't bring everyone to a common position.** It is enough that different views can be named aloud [msg #1953].
6. **Leader, remember:** the team reads your "держусь" (I'm holding on) as "чувствовать нельзя" (feeling is not allowed), and the culture gradually closes [msg #1953].
7. **Leave meanings for later.** "Сначала дело, тело и связь" (First the task, the body and connection) [msg #1953].

Rodin points to his Inc. article written after the Belarus protests, "Как поддерживать сотрудников, когда на улицах бьют и винтят людей" (How to support employees when people are being beaten and arrested in the streets), from the time deep mind worked on restoring the PandaDoc team, which was badly hit by the events [msg #1953].

## Detailed insights

### Crisis as a turning point
In the 2020 recession it may seem companies are just surviving and cutting staff, with no room for "happiness". deep mind's position is the opposite: this is "поворотный момент" for many companies that sets an upward or downward spiral. Companies can now create a truly united collective, share hardships, and increase commitment and loyalty — or visibly show their face, demonstrating that business and people go separately, after which the best employees will run as soon as they can. The author predicted that after the crisis many companies would take off thanks to enormous unity and trust in the team, which enable adequate and timely adaptation, while many others would stay in the old paradigm and start dying for good [msg #203].

### Belarus 2020: why a revolution hits work so hard
deep mind unexpectedly and very intensively started building what amounts to an "ambulance" competence for employees' mental state. Political crises can catastrophically affect company performance even without strikes; deep mind's estimate of a 30–50% productivity drop in Belarusian IT due to staff stress puts many organizations at risk of collapse. Mechanism described: during a revolution all usual supports collapse; people do not feel safe even at home — you can be detained in the street for no reason, ATMs may not give out money, planes may stop flying. Many experience a large inner conflict: on one side an acute need to participate and go to rallies, on the other fear and anxiety from real risks to physical and psychological safety. Living through this is hard, and helping is hard too; the author noted he had yet to make sense of everything learned from inside [msg #280].

The author also observed a shift in how leaders see the topic: two years earlier, when deep mind launched and spoke at conferences about the human state in the office, the topic made people laugh and drew skepticism — listeners thought caring for people in organizations would only become an agenda in the distant future. Then COVID, lockdown, and the economic and political crises came, and "the future suddenly arrived very sharply and painfully" [msg #280].

### Communicating with staff in 2022 — stance and messages
At the start of the war deep mind stated a stance: peace, political bargaining and annexation of territories are not in citizens' interests; the channel directs compassion and support to people in Eastern Europe. Its advice for individuals: in such times it is more important than ever not to give one's attention to media that inflate the information space; instead remember one's "субъектности" (agency) and right to participate in events — one can oppose actions that go against one's values and interests, and create peace, warmth and humanity wherever possible [msg #576].

For Russian companies with employees from Ukraine, deep mind recommended articulating very clearly to them that the company does not support the war simply because it is from Russia, and that people do not go out into the streets because the consolidated opposition was destroyed and spontaneous pickets lead only to prison terms and violence without results. The reason to say it: this is not always obvious to them. deep mind also asked readers to share its anti-crisis team plan with their bosses [msg #582].

### Why people say "нормально" and what the leader's task becomes
"Нормально" is a habitual word for describing oneself that describes "ровным счетом ничего" (precisely nothing). Nobody taught us at school to identify and describe the components of our state in detail — body sensations, feelings and emotions, general mood — and talking about it is not accepted in a culture largely built on trauma and denial of the importance of one's state. So "нормально" often protects us from facing the truth of contradictory and difficult experiences when it is unclear what to do with them and how to show them outwardly [msg #624].

When the world collapses, nothing is left but to start or continue getting to know one's inner experiences, which serve as the next level of supports. In the uncertainty of a BANI world, it would be great luck if the main leadership task became cleansing actions of negative emotions. Then we can take our eyes off the wall we are driving into and preserve what matters most for health and for the ability to interact with people — the "мякушку внутри" (soft core inside): sensitivity, empathy and the ability to experience emotions in their natural fullness. ### Leaders under stress (deep mind's reading of the Skolkovo study)
deep mind summarizes the study: leaders mostly acted from their own stress response — freeze or fight/flight: they either froze and stalled actions or launched frantic activity. Under strong stress an important shift happens: thinking switches from orientation to future goals to automatic, habitual forms of behavior. We cannot soberly assess our own effectiveness — "под картизолом [sic] кажется, что мы ведем себя правильно" (under cortisol it seems we are behaving correctly), but this is not so, because under strong stress we make bad decisions: taking on too much risk or acting overly conservatively. deep mind's references for these two effects are an MIT news item on stress and risky decisions and an NCBI/PMC article. The report includes recommendations on recovery and self-regulation practices; deep mind states its own team products reduce team stress by 30% within a month (a claim by the vendor, not independently evidenced in the source) [msg #637].

CONTRADICTION: deep mind's post emphasizes freeze vs frantic activity as stress reactions, while the report itself frames the 46% "pause" and 43% "active" choices as strategic reactions and presents pausing and "deciding not to decide" partly as deliberate management decisions [msg #637, file #637].

### Skolkovo findings in brief and the report's conclusion
- The most frequent word respondents used for their leadership challenges was "неопределенность" (uncertainty) [file #637].
- Leadership attitudes that stand out: keeping a "cool head", supporting others, and returning authorship of one's life when events are beyond personal control [file #637].
- Conclusion: the science of crisis management continues to be written; each crisis brings new topics. The pandemic finally debunked the stereotype that a crisis leader must be tough, charismatic and directive; almost all recent articles and research point to the opposite — showing humanity, frequent supportive communication with the team, and explaining where the organization is going and why. Russian organizations are where a new chapter is being written on continuing to work when key processes fail; it has a local, culturally specific context but contains much that is new for understanding organizational behavior and leadership. Crises are bigger than each of us and rarely painless, but, as respondents noted, they push us towards long-postponed changes — more diversification, better knowledge of one's vulnerabilities, more integration and adaptability. The authors wish readers to pass the turbulence as the Stoics taught: with dignity and resilience [file #637].

### How mobilization changed the landscape (October 2022)
- In the two weeks after mobilization teams had to react in emergency (= stressful) mode to keep as many employees as possible away from the military enlistment offices and prevent other losses: they set up support HQs, ran legal and psychological consultations, again relocated people urgently; team leads and top management met to reorient and adjust plans [msg #738].
- Companies fully tied to Russia feel worst: their leaders went through much anxiety about the consequences of the draft and escalation for themselves, their teams and the economy. Some triggers ended or paused, but an oppressive sense of uncertainty and "отсутствия света в конце туннеля" (no light at the end of the tunnel) remains [msg #738].
- Those that relocated employees fully (Georgia, Cyprus, other countries) and refocused on non-Russian markets — mostly tech companies already expanding internationally — feel better: removing the team from the country reduces the "truly enormous" stress of personal insecurity, but creates another heavy stress of temporary relocation or emigration [msg #738].
- For both groups and those in between, stress and its future consequences — burnout, depression, illness — become the priority challenge. Many leaders speak of it directly, but most leaders, team leads and HR managers do not yet fully see it as the main priority, because people usually don't understand what to actually do with stress and low morale in a team, and everyone tends to procrastinate such problems [msg #738].
- Most likely scenario per the author: after local relief comes a collision with a reality nobody has strength for — the war hasn't ended, relatives may be taken, people adapt to a new country or to loneliness in the old one; add recent years' history, sunless months and the usual year-end workload [msg #738].
- Communication: for many teams the question of new communication seemed to lose relevance — they either grew much closer (charter flights, joint apartment hunting, emergency strategy sessions) or fell out of contact so much that the task now is to restore rather than improve it [msg #738].
- Identity ("кто мы, как мы и куда идем" — who we are, how we are and where we're going) recedes in survival mode; until cortisol and adrenaline are somewhat processed and people basically adapt, this theme will ache less. Those who fully changed jurisdiction gain some clarity — "теперь они покоряют мир" (now they are conquering the world) [msg #738].
- These cases are generalizations; real circumstances differ. The challenges most likely only worsened. The author recommends close attention to working with people's psycho-emotional state, as befits a time of collective trauma, and perhaps finally doing this work not only with employees but also for top managers [msg #738].

### State management as a new company function
State is not an abstract term but the totality of bodily sensations, emotions and thoughts, constantly changing under many factors and directly affecting results in life and business. In managing people's state companies are where marketing was 20 years ago, when not all companies understood marketing as the key to growth; now a successful business without a marketing department is hard to imagine. State management is an entirely new function whose importance will only grow, especially for companies that want to thrive in a modern economy built on subtle skills — clarity and systemic thinking, creativity, the ability to hear each other [msg #784]. Supporting statistic on the card: "90% сотрудников снижают свою вовлечённость в работу, если лидер находится в стрессе" (90% of employees reduce their engagement in work if the leader is stressed) [img #784].

### The new task for leaders (September 2023)
From conversations with experts at Microsoft, Google and Apple during deep mind's research into practices of building a sustainable environment, the author concluded: people in the new reality stress, burn out and get depressed more — an objective trend companies must adapt to. But the duty of caring for the team falls "не только и не столько на саму компанию, сколько на лидеров команд" (not only and not so much on the company itself as on team leaders). Every team leader will have to grow a new focus, skills and processes — a natural evolution of the role. Any leader wanting a stellar career will need to understand their own and the team's state better, asking: What is happening to me? How does it affect the team? How do I help with stress and burnout in the team, and how do I bring people into a state of great performance? [msg #904]

Beyond managing tasks and goals, leaders must in their own way solve problems in the sphere of people's state. Some will resist, believing they worked fine before "без всей этой вашей психологии" (without all this psychology of yours); others will lead this evolution/revolution. The latter, in the author's opinion, will have far more successful careers, becoming more relevant to the new environment daily; active work on team state also yields user cases that can be presented at internal and external conferences, capitalizing one's weight in the company and supporting higher-level leaders. Example cited: Alexander Stogov, head of design at Avito, who presented such a case at a conference on new leadership [msg #904].

### Leading a team after a tragedy
Tragedies that foster PTSD (the author says the March 2024 attack was one) reduce flexibility, clarity, creativity and effectiveness, so a team "не может просто работать как раньше в понедельник" (cannot simply work as before on Monday). Given habitual avoidance tactics, it may look exactly like that — everyone buried in work — but inside things are likely hard and people think worse. To reduce the negative effect on productivity, leaders can bring "чуть больше качества человечности" (a bit more quality of humanity) into their range: acknowledge and pay attention to what happened and run more open and tolerant communication. This adds an emotion-focused coping strategy to the corporate world's usual imbalance towards problem-focused strategies [msg #1018].

Helping professionals: with growing demand, many start working for free. Everyone falls into their most habitual patterns in a crisis, so the author asks to care for them too: for a helper, a social crisis is not only a time of professional duty but a moment for extra care of their own state and resources [msg #1018].

### Yandex data-centre strikes as a case of infrastructure in war
- Context: three strikes on Yandex data centres in one week. Rodin says it is not surprising that infrastructure becomes a target in war — what is surprising is to be surprised by it in the fifth year of the war — but the situation should still be treated as a disaster. He states that what is happening is senseless and abnormal, everyone suffers from the war, and it must be ended as soon as possible [msg #1953].
- Why it is a disaster, in his view: huge resources that could go into development will go to restoration and firefighting; many talented people will spend months not on quality of life, innovation and new products but on simply keeping existing infrastructure running. "Заниматься спасением вместо развития чертовски грустно" (Doing rescue instead of development is damn sad), though far sadder is what has been happening all this time in Ukraine and the rhetoric officials use — calling their own analogous actions there two weeks earlier "принудительным цифровым детоксом" (forced digital detox) [msg #1953].
- The spring internet problems showed that technological infrastructure is a basic security layer not only for a company but for all of Moscow, so it really must be restored with all forces — but it is people who restore it [msg #1953].

### Stance: compassion instead of gloating
- Rodin acknowledges there are questions about today's Yandex and sees them becoming a basis for gloating. He considers it important in any conditions to keep humanity and not rejoice at grief — from technological losses to, even more so, human ones: "Злорадствование всегда стоит заменять в себе на сострадание" (Gloating should always be replaced in oneself with compassion) [msg #1953].
- Companies and people, especially those who stay in Russia, have to adapt to reality: they make decisions, make mistakes, perform heroic acts, and all of this has consequences. This should be discussed openly, but such a conversation is almost impossible today because of ideology, repressions and political interests [msg #1953].
- "Как и любой человек, компания в токсичной среде может подвергаться эрозии" (Like any person, a company in a toxic environment can undergo erosion). Still, by culture, people and approach to products Yandex remains for him the healthy, effective, normal Russia he would like to live in; he came to love its people through years of collaboration and friendship. Such places on the map should be viewed comprehensively, not from a position of moral superiority (personal opinion of the author) [msg #1953].

## Evidence and numbers

- **HBR (Gulati, Nohria, Wohlgezogen), "Who will succeed after the crisis", HBR Russia, July 2010:** of 4,700 public companies studied, the recessions of 1980, 1990 and 2000 ended badly — bankruptcy, takeover or sale — for 17%. About 9% of the same sample, within three years after the recession, not only recovered but outperformed competitors in sales and profit growth by at least 10%. The difference between winners and rivals was preparedness and adaptability [msg #203].
- **Bain report (cited in #203):** among companies that failed to grow even after the 2008 crisis, very few had contingency plans or well-thought-out alternative strategies, and "когда ситуация ухудшилась, они перешли в режим выживания, прибегли к резким сокращениям и действовали по обстановке" (when the situation worsened, they switched to survival mode, resorted to sharp cuts and acted ad hoc) [msg #203].
- **deep mind estimates:** 30–50% productivity drop in Belarusian IT from staff stress (2020); 40–50% drop in PandaDoc's product team during violent protests [msg #280, #387].

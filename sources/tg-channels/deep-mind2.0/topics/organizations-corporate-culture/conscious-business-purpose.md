# Conscious business and company purpose

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel author's case that business is entering a "new paradigm" — more effective, sustainable and humane — in which a company's purpose and values, not profit, are its foundation. It covers the Conscious Capitalism manifesto (Mackey and Sisodia), the author's own experiments with purpose at Deep Mind, Collins' research on core values, the critique of total measurement ("if it is not on the dashboard, it does not exist"), and the idea of steering a company by its inner truth instead of planning and control. According to the author, this matters because companies with a living purpose last longer, decide faster, attract people who share their values and cause less suffering — and because the alternative produces cynicism, burnout and harm to people and the planet [msg #4, #12, #183, #199, #479].

## Core ideas

- **Profit is not the main goal of a business.** The author repeatedly argues that a company should not set making money as its most important goal, especially if it wants extraordinary rather than average results; profit follows from serving a purpose (see *Collins' findings on core values*) [msg #10, #183].
- **People are a source, not a means.** Conscious business energizes people and directs that energy toward high goals; companies that unlock human creativity win in the long run (see *Conscious business manifesto*) [msg #4].
- **A living, manifested purpose makes a company sustainable, fast and integrated** (see *Three advantages*) [msg #12].
- **Having values matters more than which values.** The presence of a stable core value system creates integrity and durability; strategy and tactics change, core values and meaning stay [msg #183].
- **"Only what is measurable is manageable" is a myth.** Some of the most important elements of culture, such as sincerity, cannot be measured; fixation on metrics narrows perception and burns people out [msg #11, #479].
- **A company can be anchored in its inner truth** rather than driven by external circumstances, strategic planning and control (see *Machine vs living system*) [msg #199].
- **Humane business is both a moral and a competitive matter**: soft management reduces suffering, and people will flee toward less pain [msg #131, #245].
- **Business can be a practice** that transforms and nourishes the mind (see *Business as a practice*) [msg #1318].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Conscious business manifesto (Mackey and Sisodia)
Purpose: the author presents this as the manifesto he would choose for his own business — "лучше и не сформулируешь" (*you couldn't phrase it better*). He says a business of a new paradigm has appeared in the world, more effective, sustainable and above all more humane, and that he created Deep Mind Consulting to be part of that wave. The manifesto, quoted from John Mackey (founder of Whole Foods) and professor Raj Sisodia, has these components [msg #4]:
1. **People as source, not means.** "Бизнес должен рассматривать людей, как источник, а не как средство." (*Business should regard people as a source, not as a means.*) [msg #4]
2. **The coal vs Sun metaphor.** A means is like a piece of coal: you use it and that is all. A source is like the Sun — almost inexhaustible, constantly producing energy, light and warmth. There is no more powerful source of creative energy in the world than a strong person aimed at success [msg #4].
3. **What conscious business does.** It gives people strength and energy and encourages them to work as well as possible for the benefit of high goals; in this way business has a deeply positive influence on the world [msg #4].
4. **Opportunity is abundant.** There is no shortage of business opportunities: billions of people have needs that are not adequately met [msg #4].
5. **Long-term winners.** Companies that understand this and try to unleash the human creative impulse will win in the long run [msg #4].

### Three advantages of companies with a living purpose
Purpose: the author's argument for why a company's purpose matters, built on his own experience [msg #12].
- **Mechanism.** Having a goal focuses, channels energy in a specific direction and motivates; the author says this should work for any system — a person, a family, a company [msg #12].
- **Why material goals are not enough.** Purely material goals motivate poorly over a long distance. The author's father once told him that for many recent years all his work was reflected only in the change of the sum on his bank account — and that this depressed him greatly. If a result is not manifested and felt in the real world, no positive feedback loop forms, we do not feel flow in work, and motivation quickly fades [msg #12].
- The three advantages of companies with living, manifested purposes [msg #12]:
  1. **Sustainable (устойчивы)** — because employees are selected by values, are better motivated, and are more loyal.
  2. **Fast (быстры)** — because all decisions come from the purpose rather than from a heap of other opportunistic ("конъектурных" [sic]) factors, so decisions are made faster.
  3. **Integrated (интегрированы)** — because there is a clear direction in which everyone rows.
- **Effect on performance.** Leaders and employees inspired by a high purpose use their potential fully and are "ready to move mountains", which affects productivity and results [msg #12].

### Purpose questions used at Deep Mind
The author says Deep Mind thinks a lot about purpose and regularly makes "approaches" to it by asking itself two questions [msg #12]:
1. "зачем все это?" (*why all this?*)
2. "Что мы потеряем, если компании не будет?" (*What would we lose if the company did not exist?*)
His formulation of Deep Mind's purpose at the time: "Сделать бизнес более человечным." (*To make business more humane.*) [msg #12]

### Syncing a personal goal with company goals
A worked example from the author's career [msg #12]. Eight years before the post, working in digital, at an annual review with the CEO he proposed:
1. State your personal goal openly — his was "to learn how to do business".
2. Propose to synchronize it with the company's goals.
3. Ask for concrete means — "give me more managerial tasks" — and say your motivation will rise as a result.
Result: the following period was one of the most positive experiences in all his jobs; that CEO now heads a large company and they keep warm relations. Lesson as the author frames it: purpose focuses and motivates, for individuals as for companies [msg #12].

### Collins' findings on core values
While rereading "business classic" — Collins' research — the author collected these points in support of his claim that profit should not be the main goal [msg #183]:
1. **HP.** HP's founder called his main achievement the system of values and principles that influenced the management of companies worldwide. "The HP Way" is a carefully guarded value system that distinguishes the organization more than any of its products. It includes: contribution to technical progress; respect for each person; social responsibility; and "твердую веру в то, что прибыль не может являться конечной целью компании" (*firm belief that profit cannot be the ultimate goal of the company*) [msg #183].
2. **Merck.** George Merck, founder of the pharma company, said in 1950: "Мы стараемся помнить, что лекарства производятся для пациентов. Не для прибыли. Прибыль последует." (*We try to remember that medicines are made for patients. Not for profit. Profit will follow.*) He added that if they remember, it will not keep them waiting, and the better they remember, the larger it will be [msg #183].
3. **It was unpopular then.** Collins says the importance of this "additional dimension" is an obvious management standard today, but in the 1950s these people were disliked: other businessmen did not consider Packard one of them and were sure he could not run a large company. The author's remark: Collins wrote about the "obvious standard" in 2001 — implying it is still not obvious to many [msg #183].
4. **Conclusion confirmed by the research.** To become a great company you do not have to cherish customers (Sony does not), respect individuality (Disney does not) or compete on quality (Walmart does not). "Суть не в том, какая у вас система ценностей, а в том - есть ли она." (*The point is not what value system you have but whether you have one.*) Its presence creates integrity and durability; strategy and tactics change many times, but core values and meaning remain. Preserving core principles is the main trait of companies that have stood the test of time [msg #183].
Context: the author frames values and life principles as the organization's foundation, especially important "during the storm we are now in" (spring 2020). His earlier point was that an organization can be seen holistically — as living people creating value and influencing society — not as a set of functions for extracting profit [msg #183].

### Organization as machine vs organization as living system
Purpose: an alternative to the "illusion of control" and meaningless strategic planning, drawn from Deep Mind's own team process [msg #199].
- **Machine approach.** When everything around changes fast, anxiety about falling behind the market arises, along with the need to constantly check one's feelings against the field of external events. The author calls this the classic strategy of leaders who see the organization as a machine needing strategic analysis, planning and control; such machines start from external circumstances when defining goals [msg #199].
- **Living-system approach.** Rely on "свою внутреннюю правду и глубинные смыслы" (*your inner truth and deep meanings*). If you see the company as a living system with energy, a collective capacity to orient itself and a living purpose, the author claims no strategic planning and control are needed at all [msg #199].
- **Core principle:** "Если мы хорошо знаем кто мы и в чем наша фундаментальная ценность, то понимание что делать/ чего не делать приходит автоматически." (*If we know well who we are and what our fundamental value is, understanding of what to do / not do comes automatically.*) We do not run where the wind blows and do not try to predict the future, because such actions are just an illusion of control — a way to justify one's bustle and cope with fear [msg #199].
- **What the company strives for instead:** to hear and understand what it wants to become by its nature; to understand better what world it wants to build; and to succeed in embodying the desired version of itself [msg #199].
- **Decision criterion:** a sense of inner rightness in decisions, rather than others' opinions or external targets. The author's caveat: simple in words, but in practice it necessarily requires clarifying your deep purpose, values and principles [msg #199].
- **How Deep Mind applied it.** For a month the team went through a "team track": in an updated line-up they clarified who they are and what they do — their evolutionary purpose, values, products and target audience — and were close to replacing "consulting" in the name with "hub". Cost: it slowed client work in the field (fewer course launches, fewer presentations of new products sent to companies). Observed effect: instead of anxiety about not taking part in the race, "каждый глубокий разговор с командой о цели и ценностях меня сильно успокаивает" (*every deep conversation with the team about purpose and values calms me greatly*), switching him off from the meaningless competitive race and frantic attempts to understand what is going on around [msg #199].
- **Result claim:** even small team progress on these questions gives energy and motivates more than any numbers [msg #199].

### Business as a practice of conscious development
Purpose: the author's later (2025) view of what a business can be for its founder and people [msg #1318, img #1318].
- **Two faces of business.** Business grants many possibilities — from beautiful and useful products to a path of remarkable personal growth. On the other hand, it often becomes a "dirty sandbox" where people build castles on the saddest human qualities: bottomless striving for power and subordination, egocentrism, anger and greed. Outwardly such castles may inspire a sense of power and shine attractively, but their creators' minds are in a sad state — far from deep satisfaction, peace and wisdom, far from both their personal wellbeing and a worthwhile contribution to the common good [msg #1318].
- **The alternative.** If you add certain "filters" or a view of business, the company can be turned into a practice that transforms and nourishes the mind. Business can be a remarkable environment for growth — and for many "духовных избегателей" (*spiritual avoiders/bypassers*) more useful than ordinary spiritual practice [msg #1318].
- **The author's evidence from his own case.** He says he has not achieved much in conquering markets, but Deep Mind is a real project with real, big meaning that has passed the "valley of death" of 95% of startups [msg #1318].
- The talk's title card reads "Бизнес как практика осознанного развития" (*Business as a practice of conscious development*) [img #1318].

## Detailed insights

### Why people defend "cruel business" so fiercely
After posting that the main goal of business is not money, the author received a lot of abuse ("много гавна"). He read the violent reaction as a sign he was right: when the reaction to a view is very stormy, you are stepping on something sore — or on a foundation; people fiercely defend their habitual picture of the world so it does not wobble [msg #10]. He calls defending that "foundation" a dreary activity, because it is a defence of cynicism and harshness toward people — "отстаивание средневековья" (*defending the Middle Ages*) [msg #10]. His explanation of the mechanism: he does not believe people do it consciously; more likely they defend their own history — all the decisions bad for other people they made from beliefs about business's cruelty, such as "Мир такой, мне надо бороться. Бизнес не терпит слабость" (*The world is like this, I have to fight. Business doesn't tolerate weakness.*) Those are decisions to shout at others, fire them, play politics against them, lie to them. The more such decisions in one's history, the more guilt one would have to face if one admitted that business need not be cruel [msg #10]. In a later post he recalled that his feed wrote he was talking nonsense, understood nothing about business and asked about his work experience [msg #183].

### The measurement myth
- **The fallacy.** Conscious Capitalism — which the author calls one of those books he would like to split into quotes and put on billboards across the city — lists misconceptions about business. One he highlights: "Управляемо лишь то, что измеряемо. В культуре и людях слишком много неосязаемых величин. Значит в это и не надо инвестировать." (*Only what is measurable is manageable. There are too many intangibles in culture and people. So there is no need to invest in it.*) The book is about companies that benefit customers, employees and society [msg #11].
- **Mackey's position.** Measurement is very important, but one of the main mistakes of modern management is the belief that all important things can be measured and that what cannot be measured does not matter — an example of analytical intellect taken to absurdity. We should try to measure everything that can reasonably be measured, as it helps understand where results come from, but some of the most important elements of cultures, for example sincerity, cannot be measured at all [msg #11].
- **Fixation narrows perception.** The author paraphrases the Whole Foods founder: metrics are useful, but fixation on them can make us narrow-minded and lead us away from the broad picture or deeper perception. Humans have intuitive thinking that links very different realities and processes; it can give great insights and unexpectedly change all strategies, but it is not engaged when we are in active mode, tensely examining charts [msg #479].
- **The P&L cult.** "PNL — новый Бог в современных компаниях." (*P&L is the new God in modern companies.*) Everyone dances with a tambourine to appease it; to track every other process companies put up huge screens with numbers and charts — and if progress does not show there, it supposedly does not exist [msg #479]. The author is not against every action being meaningful and leading to consequences, but says the mainstream focus on total measurement of efficiency currently leads to a hellish life for managers and employees: they burn out and lose strategic vision and the **feeling** of current processes [msg #479].
- **Case 1 — the "mission control" company.** A company with a whole "flight control centre" had built a successful business by measuring practically every indicator. Its HR department bought Deep Mind's services because employees complained of stress, but the CEO did not, literally laughing that Deep Mind sold unmeasurable things. The author's lesson: they refused to substantially change their employees' reality because it was unclear how to measure it. His suggestion to simply talk to the employees about the results of the work was rejected completely; people get fixated on external "objective" data and cannot trust their subjective feelings at all [msg #11].
- **Case 2 — two founders of an international company.** They did not buy Deep Mind's stress-reduction products because they could not be measured objectively. Deep Mind does measure, but through surveys — how do you feel, how rested do you wake up. For the founders that was subjective and therefore unmeasurable, so they "simply refused to help their employees and solve an important problem" [msg #479].
- **Many things need no measuring.** The government of Scotland and its citizens know the new Theory U hubs are useful: they see a new process and feel their different participation in governing the country. Likewise employees in companies where Deep Mind runs mindfulness groups for stress reduction know they feel better; you do not need to measure them with rulers like an object — you can simply talk to them, "да, даже, если их 1000" (*yes, even if there are 1000 of them*) [msg #479].
- **Why "objective" metrics can mislead.** A person may feel morally better while their "objective" HRV improves only after several weeks — or not at all, because some hellish stress happens later. Does that mean mindfulness, therapy or embodiment do not work? The author says "we all know it works", and that this knowledge is far more accessible and objective than most of the "little measurements" (измеряшки, a dismissive diminutive) [msg #479].

### The changing image of business and capitalism
The author's view (January 2020): business is changing, step by step. The image of business, especially large corporations, is negative in people's eyes; he attributes global warming, plastic islands and stock market bubbles to the fixation on constantly increasing profit. Capitalism demands constant growth, but he argues it can be secured through reasonable use of resources and ethical attention to the entire value chain [msg #97]. "Old management" shakes its head: all its life it has focused on one goal — increasing shareholder returns — "like a sacred mantra", though nobody seems to have checked whether it works for the good. "New management" says thinking about business must change: companies are the most effective form of organizing people, so why not make them serve bigger goals and create joy for employees, customers, suppliers and the environment [msg #97]. He reads Zuckerberg's statement that billionaires should not exist at all as part of this trend: Facebook is already seen by many as a big corporate evil, and outside the trend of global change such statements would look very foolish. He recommends Conscious Capitalism by the Whole Foods founder as a good explanation of the new paradigm [msg #97].

### Fragmentary thinking as the root of profit-only views
The author cites the psychologist Luria, who described the problem of a simplified, fragmentary view of phenomena some 60 years earlier: people with mechanistic thinking narrow reality to fragments and assume that this is the true description of the situation — for example, that an organization is profit, structure and positions, or that life is determined by an economic base and a political superstructure. They cannot perceive themselves, let alone a company, holistically; hence the failure to see the importance of internal processes in a company or of a foundation of values that go beyond simple profit-making. The author says we saw where such simplified models lead in the examples of the USSR and fascism. His remedy: development and work with one's inner world helps one learn to see things closer to their real complexity and interconnectedness [msg #183].

### Soft management reduces suffering
Speaking at an embodiment conference about body and business, the author noted that soft and flexible management models are more effective and are the future (already the present in many companies), but that the discussion of their advantages — and of bringing bodily awareness into business — was fully focused on productivity: more creative, faster, people don't burn out. He added an aspect he considers very important: soft management also reduces suffering — less violence, less pain. This matters from a humanistic point of view, which he calls key, but it also leads directly to greater sustainability and competitiveness, because "Люди будут бежать туда, где меньше боли." (*People will run to where there is less pain.*) [msg #131]

### Humanity as the decade's competitive advantage (Adizes)
The author shares Ichak Adizes' explanation of why humanity in business is the main trend and competitive advantage of the coming decade. The restructuring will be hard because "feeling" is an inaccessible concept for the modern corporate world; to feel means a radical paradigm shift and a change of the organization's whole culture. Feeling will have to be learned — a difficult and far from quick development of new mind-sets and skill-sets [msg #245].

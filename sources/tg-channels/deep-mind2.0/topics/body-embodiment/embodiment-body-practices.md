# Embodiment and body practices

## What this covers

This file collects everything deep mind taught about the body as a resource for mental health, recovery from stress and trauma, personal development and organisational change: what embodiment is, how to organise exercise during burnout, which body practices are designed for stress and trauma, why posture is a mental-health habit, why the body belongs in corporate strategy, how hiking (or any movement) becomes an embodiment practice, and why embodiment is now described as a basic need. According to the source, the body is "a whole cosmos", unexplored for many, and ignoring it means losing "свой ценнейший ресурс" (*one's most valuable resource*) [msg #78].

## Core ideas

- **The body is suppressed from childhood and must be reclaimed.** Culture frames the body as sexual, sinful, tied to violence and power; its impulses must be suppressed or family and society will not accept us. We are told "не чувствуйте" (*don't feel*), then we push bodily sensations away ourselves, losing the natural connection with the body and with it a precious resource [msg #78].
- **Working with the body heals.** The author calls the embodiment approach modern, very effective and "научный факт" (*a scientific fact*): bringing movement, care and attention to the body, we recover and heal — treat psychological trauma, complete the stress cycle and develop emotional intelligence. deep mind gives much body content in its meditation courses and uses body practices to develop leaders and teams [msg #78].
- **Burnout is a nervous-system problem that movement can resolve — if the movement does not add stress.** See *Three rules for training with an exhausted nervous system* [msg #351].
- **Some body practices are purpose-built for stress and trauma** and can save a lot of recovery time compared with ordinary sport. See *Three practices for intense stress and trauma* [msg #826].
- **Posture is a mental-health habit.** An upright, comfortably open posture is presented as a small daily embodiment practice [msg #1005].
- **The body is where organisational change happens or gets blocked** — separating the body from change management is called a cognitive error of modern corporate culture [msg #1345].
- **Any movement can become embodiment practice** when done with non-violence and meaningful awareness, treating the body as subject, not object [msg #1473].
- **Embodiment is a basic need tied to wellbeing and identity**; living "in the head" makes identity fragile [msg #1733].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Three rules for training with an exhausted nervous system (burnout)
Purpose: sport was among the top recommendations deep mind gave to everyone burning out who came to them for help that year, but with a nuance: "**спорт не должен увеличивать суммарную стрессовую нагрузку.**" (*sport must not increase the total stress load*) [msg #351].

Background mechanism: "**Выгорание -- это крайняя форма хронического стресса.**" (*Burnout is the extreme form of chronic stress*). The nervous system "burns out" when for a long time we cannot exit the stress response and relax. Completing the stress response happens most easily through movement or attentive awareness — which is why sport and meditation are so often recommended for stress or burnout. But sport is itself a stressor for the organism, so under high stress and nervous-system load it must be done correctly [msg #351].

How to know the nervous system is depleted: it can be diagnosed by doctors, or — "кривовато" (*crookedly, i.e. roughly*) — on your own by HRV (heart-rate variability), newly appeared insomnia, or atypical difficulties controlling emotions [msg #351].

1. **Train in the morning.** Morning workouts balance the body's hormonal background. The author cites research: night sleep of people who train at 7 a.m. is 75% better than of those who train at 13:00 or 19:00. Why: physical load raises body temperature, so evening workouts can further damage sleep quality, the ability to recover and to cope with stress. If morning training is impossible, early evening can also work. Pitfall: super-early workouts at 3 or 5 a.m. can harm the heart and are better avoided [msg #351].
2. **Focus on short strength workouts instead of long cardio.** Short, intense sessions help with burnout, while long ones can quickly launch a negative loop. Mechanism given: during 30–60-minute cardio sessions we put the sympathetic nervous system into activity that prevents stress hormones from being cleared from the body; they accumulate and stop us from balancing the hormonal background. Short strength workouts with weights let the body produce more anabolic hormones, which improve sleep and wellbeing. Dose: such a workout should take no more than 30 minutes [msg #351].
3. **Train regularly.** Do at least two strength workouts per week, without skipping. For regularity, deep mind always recommends choosing activities you genuinely enjoy [msg #351].

### Three practices for intense stress and trauma (Yin yoga, slow flow yoga, TRE)
Purpose: unlike ordinary sport and many other practices, these three are "специально задизайнены для бережной интеграции стрессовых и травматичных событий" (*specially designed for the gentle integration of stressful and traumatic events*) and can save a lot of recovery time [msg #826].

The author's case (why and when to use them): he spent a week focusing on regulating stress and traumatic response because it matched his state and tasks. The next week held several hard decisions, meetings and workshops, where he wanted to be in a "расслабленно-включенном состоянии" (*relaxed-yet-engaged state*). The previous week had several trigger events touching past experiences of helplessness and anger; he was also a witness to incredible violence — knowing about missiles senselessly hitting residential buildings — and could not "place" it inside himself. His nervous system could not exit hyper-tonus on its own [msg #826].

Signs he used to recognise this state (a usable self-check): sleep got worse; he felt overloaded with information, with a specific unpleasant anxiety when many decisions have to be made and he cannot make them properly; concentrating became harder; the world around him seemed more negative [msg #826].

1. **Yin Yoga.** Some consider it "female yoga", but the author says it has nothing to do with gender ("Со мной всегда ходят накачанные красавчики" — *muscular handsome guys always come with me*). Key principle: the pose works without effort, through gravity; it helps you learn to fully surrender, let go and activate the parasympathetic part of the nervous system, which is badly needed after stress. This principle plus holding poses for a long time lets you, unlike other practices, relax deep, rarely used muscles — "по-настоящему расслабиться!" (*truly relax!*) [msg #826].
2. **Gentle flow yoga (Slow flow yoga).** A slow variation of hatha yoga that won't let you strain hard or sweat. Attention is directed inward, everything is done slowly and with eyes closed for greater concentration. Why: this recreates the connection between attention and bodily sensations — returning us into the body from which we "fly off" under stress. Also, slowing down without bodily freezing (as here) increases the subjective feeling of safety and switches the mode of the nervous system [msg #826].
3. **TRE — Tension, stress and trauma release.** A special sequence of exercises that lets you experience something like a natural, restorative tremor ("судороги" — *cramp/convulsion*) of the deep pelvic muscles. Chronic tension of these muscles is associated with trauma, and its gradual, delicate release with healing or completing stress cycles. It may sound scary, but in practice it is a slow, calm process: you see clearly how much unlived tension is in the body and learn to tolerate it. The author reports that after a session his gaze usually clears substantially and his body feels lighter. Caveat: some therapists criticise TRE for the risk of retraumatisation — being triggered after the practice and getting worse. Still, the author holds that TRE is more likely to lead to stable health than cathartic techniques such as dynamic meditations and ayahuasca; it is a simple, accessible way to work with deeply driven-in tension, especially if you respect the method's principle — "делать меньше и не пытаться интенсивно избавиться от эмоций" (*do less and do not try to intensively get rid of emotions*) [msg #826].

### Posture as a daily embodiment habit, extended into centering
Purpose: one of the "very effective habits for mental health" is "**вертикальная и комфортно расправленная поза тела**" (*an upright and comfortably open body posture*). Training such a body, together with the qualities it brings, can be a small daily embodiment practice for mental health [msg #1005].

Mechanism: when we are tense we usually contract — both along the vertical of the spine and at the level of muscles and fascia. This disrupts blood circulation, reduces natural flexibility, and creates tension and pain somewhere inside. All of this amplifies or fixes negative thoughts and a general sense of dissatisfaction and even suffering [msg #1005].

Components and evidence:
1. **Upright posture** can positively affect the sense of stability, confidence and mood, and reduce fear compared with a slumped posture while increasing speech rate — per a study the author cites (on slumped postures and stress responses, randomised) [msg #1005].
2. **Open (expanded) posture** can increase positive emotions, reduce fatigue and reduce self-focus in people with mild and moderate depression — per a second study the author cites [msg #1005].
3. **Cultural angle (author's opinion):** in the Russian-speaking context, where "терпение" (*endurance/patience*) is ambiguously reinforced even in religious culture, it seems especially useful to practise opening up and shedding masochistic patterns of self-violence at the level of the body [msg #1005].
4. **Centering:** if you add to a balanced posture the balancing of attention and work with an image — i.e. a small mindfulness practice — you get centering, which deep mind often teaches in teams. ### Why the body belongs in organisations: two facts and the reverse
Purpose: a critique of the "desomatisation" of modern corporate culture ("Body keeps the company"). Talking about the body in a strategy session still seems strange because business is supposedly about numbers, logic and strategy; the author argues from logic itself with two facts [msg #1345].

1. **In the future, which has already begun, no company survives without the ability to change seriously.** Change will never end — only more and faster. Any company must change a lot or very likely die, literally mastering the art of change. That art is often not about buying a new asset, selling an old one or producing something new; today it far more often depends on whether key teams can "*начать делать* что-то иначе" (*start doing something differently*). The author explicitly declines to seek proof in studies — "это консенсус" (*it's consensus*) [msg #1345].
2. **The body is the place where the new is embodied.** The concept of embodied cognition holds that thinking and behaviour are formed not only by the brain but by bodily experience. The ability to do something differently is not only linked to the body but is embodied in it — in how we breathe, move, speak and where we direct attention. Without access to the bodily level we stay in an illusory world of concepts that do not lead to sustainable change, because change happens in contact with reality. "Тело — первое и ключевое место возникновения нового." (*The body is the first and key place where the new arises*) [msg #1345].
3. **The reverse is also true: the body is the territory of obstacles to change.** Per Antonio Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis, emotional and bodily signals play a key role in decisions, especially under uncertainty. Deep patterns of reacting, choosing and avoiding the new live in the body: in the autonomic nervous system; in sensorimotor memory; in bodily reflexes to unsafety [msg #1345].

What decides whether the body helps or blocks change in people and organisations: whether we understand its role and state, and whether we have skills and a culture of working with that state. Theory U, which deep mind uses a lot in companies, describes the natural cycle of creating the new (i.e. change); it requires the body and bodily abilities — presence, deep listening, sensing what is happening inside and around. All of these require immersion in the body and the ability to stay with bodily sensations, which is hampered by a dysregulated nervous system and grows many times over in a regulated one. Dysregulation here means chronic stress and tension and bodily manifestations of psychological trauma; it impairs being in the body and feeling its signals, and therefore adapting to or creating the new. Hence organisations where people do not breathe, are tense and live in unsafety lose capacity for cooperation and innovation. The body is often treated as secondary at work although it plays a critical role in the survival and success of individuals and of organisations, which are moved by people; separating the body from organisational change or strategic management is "простая когнитивная ошибка современной корпкультуры" (*a simple cognitive error of modern corporate culture*) [msg #1345].

The accompanying slide "Тело - это место воплощения нового" (*The body is the place where the new is embodied*) states four steps of the argument [img #1345]:
1. Creation and change are not just inventing new behaviour.
2. They mean embodying it in gestures, voice, rhythm, actions.
3. A new state, a new identity, a new culture — all of it must be lived through bodily, otherwise it does not take root.
4. An organisation whose people are bodily tense, overloaded and not breathing cannot give birth to a culture of cooperation, empathy and innovation.

### Two principles of embodiment practice (author's own view)
Definition used: embodiment practice is an approach to development in which we look at the body as a subject, not an object — not using it functionally as in ordinary sport, not trying to squeeze a six-pack out of it or make it strong for Ironman courses. The body is "не где-то там, не такси для мозга. Тело, которое и есть я." (*not somewhere out there, not a taxi for the brain. The body that is me*). This both boosts development and changes the very paradigm of life — truly returning to the body and making it a friend. The author marks the two principles as "на мой вкус" (*to my taste*) [msg #1473].

1. **Non-violence.** The usual sport approach, in which the body is "overcome", can create much violence against the body. That deepens mind-body dissociation — the split created by a culture of violence from school onward — and blocks access to bodily intelligence and bodily wisdom. How: for conventional sport to become embodiment practice, decide, at least at the level of attention focus, to notice violence against yourself during the process and avoid it. Pitfall: violence is not always easy even to recognise at first [msg #1473].
2. **Meaningfulness and awareness.** For movement to become practice, understand its goal, how it affects you and why you need it in life — and notice these processes during and after. This is what the author calls inner facilitation: making sense of the activity, which adds an extra feedback loop to the system [msg #1473].

### Three levels of meaning of hiking as an embodiment practice
Context: written from a hike around Elbrus. The author notes a fast-growing outdoor trend in the Russian-speaking world that balances big-city life and its loaded pace with nature and movement. He says he is constantly learning non-violence in hiking but after years of attention it is "almost sorted"; the three levels show how he applies the practice to life beyond it [msg #1473].

1. **Develops overall wellbeing.** Hiking "hits inflammation from all fronts" — inflammation being considered today almost the main marker of health. It also develops self-care skills: attentiveness to the rhythm of load and rest right in the process, watching water, food and gear. In the office it is easy to get lost in thoughts and practise "анти-заботу о себе" (*anti-self-care*), for which you later suffer strategically; hiking re-builds healthy patterns through the body on a very visible example [msg #1473].
2. **Develops exiting automatism and finding breakthrough ideas** — or helps practise the U-process from Theory U. This strategy, which the author calls essential for our time, assumes that to solve complex problems you must pull yourself out of habitual mental "chewing gum". Each day of a hike first brings letting go of habitual rails, after which fresh solutions to important questions seem to come by themselves. The author stresses these are not uncontrollable insights but a process trainable in this practice [msg #1473].
3. **Develops attentiveness and clarity of mind.** Walking long but without violence and with care for the body, looking at nature, not consuming information: movement helps raise and ecologically live through emotions accumulated in the body; attention gradually calms on very natural objects — the movement itself, mountains, lakes — as opposed to thoughts often detached from reality. Together with being in vast spaces, this gradually makes the mind clearer, "абсолютно прекрасное чувство" (*an absolutely wonderful feeling*) [msg #1473].

Rule of application: there are many perspectives, but what matters is not a "correct" picture but individual tuning. Using this lens, any movement can be made into embodiment practice — radically changing the paradigm of the relationship with the body and adding harmoniously developing depth to movement [msg #1473].

### Embodiment as a basic need: having vs being a body, and the three forces pulling us out
Purpose: interpret a 2025 study in Frontiers in Psychology. The author's preface: to understand this you need no science — "танцуй, медитируй, исцеляй травму и возвращай безопасность в тело" (*dance, meditate, heal trauma and return safety to the body*); the body then gradually fills with presence, life and movement and we can inhabit it again. Science adds a language and lens for what many experience but do not name [msg #1733].

Findings as reported by the author:
- The ability to feel embodied — "*я здесь, в теле, живой*" (*I am here, in the body, alive*) — is declared a basic psychophysiological need directly linked to subjective wellbeing. Without embodiment, wholeness and fullness become a concept one can understand, discuss and even sell, but hardly experience; cut off from the body, we are cut off from our own wellbeing [msg #1733].
- Embodiment is described as a need connected with identity: a person who stays long "*в голове*" (*in the head*) loses stability of identity; it becomes more fragile and dependent on external evaluation and control, and the person suffers more [msg #1733].
- The study opens a new area, *embodiment motivation*: the motivation to feel oneself in the body, experience bodily vitality, its capacities and ways of expression — aiming to give the body an updated status, not as an object for biohacking but as the place where human motives, values and aspirations live [msg #1733].
- Embodiment is two things at once: *having a body* (object — can be evaluated/improved) + *being a body* (subject — I am a sensing body). Modern culture strongly skews us toward having over being. So besides a need for embodiment a reverse motive can arise — *disembodiment motivation*, the wish to reduce bodily self-experience when it is linked to discomfort [msg #1733].
- The article names 3 key domains of embodiment: own vitality + abilities; a healthy environment; bodily contact. Hence the author concludes the body can be returned less through sport than through bodily awareness, environment and loving contacts [msg #1733].

Why we don't live embodied — the three forces (author's own interpretation, "Мне кажется" — *it seems to me*):
1. **Pain, emotions and the nervous system.** When there is much pain, anxiety, fatigue and overload inside, bodily experience stops being pleasant, and a quite reasonable strategy arises: discard the body — escape into the head, the screen, control, achievements. Returning "home" is hard without integrating our history and teaching the nervous system to handle intense feelings [msg #1733].
2. **The cultural code of productivity.** We increasingly live in a culture valuing result over presence — not *being* but *doing, having, achieving*. In this logic the body is only an instrument to optimise, discipline, force and improve; it starts to feel external, like a project. Following Byung-Chul Han, in the modern society of tiredness a person becomes their own exploiter for external results, which alienates the body and creates distance from it [msg #1733].
3. **The illusion of control.** The key paradigm of rational industrial and post-industrial civilisation, used to conquering and controlling nature: a belief that if I "think right", "decide right", "control right", I can manage life. The body keeps breaking this paradigm, reminding a person daily that they are finite, vulnerable and context-dependent; in an era of uncertainty one wants to throw this part of reality out of attention and turn it down [msg #1733].

## Detailed insights

### What embodiment is and how it works
Embodiment is defined by deep mind as the integration of the body, bodily awareness and body practices into personal development or solving life tasks. Through the body it is easy to detect repeating patterns and work with them thanks to their visibility and inescapability: "если видишь как сжимаешься в ответ на стрессовый триггер, то уже не расскажешь себе сказки про свою эмоциональную непоколебимость" (*if you see yourself contracting in response to a stress trigger, you can no longer tell yourself fairy tales about your emotional unshakeability*) [msg #1005].

Embodiment unites psychology and physiology. Because we have been fundamentally and for a long time cut off from our bodies — though we are very bodily beings — body-related ideas are often seen as extravagant and negatively esoteric. The author concedes this is sometimes true, e.g. some osteopaths who, after feeling tension in the heels, start asking about your parents. He is still surprised at how effective embodiment tools are in deep mind's corporate programmes and how people still perceive them as something magical [msg #1005].

The author also notes in 2019 that the embodiment theme was rapidly gaining momentum worldwide and in Russia, and that he spoke at the first Russian embodiment conference, devoted to theories and practices of developing people and communities through the body [msg #78].

### Contrast between the body as object and as subject
Across posts the same distinction recurs: ordinary sport, biohacking and productivity culture treat the body as an object or project (to slim, pump up, make productive), whereas embodiment treats it as subject — the body that I am. This framing appears both in the hiking post (body "not a taxi for the brain") and in the Frontiers post (having vs being a body) [msg #1473, #1733].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The posture and sleep claims rest on single studies cited by the author; the posture effects are hedged ("может" — *may*) [msg #1005, #351].
- Self-diagnosing nervous-system exhaustion by HRV, insomnia or emotional control is called rough ("кривовато") by the source itself; doctors are the better route [msg #351].
- TRE: some therapists warn of retraumatisation; the author's counter-view (better than cathartic techniques) is his opinion [msg #826].
- The claim that the embodiment approach is "a scientific fact" and that the need for change is "consensus" are asserted without evidence in the posts [msg #78, #1345].

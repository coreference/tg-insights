# Ego, narcissism and authenticity

## What this covers

This topic gathers the author's essays on ego and narcissism. They cover the healthy and unhealthy forms of narcissism in a digital attention economy, the separate "I" as a perceptual illusion according to contemplative traditions and some neuroscience, and how to relate to narcissists with compassion. They also cover cynicism as a defence and the case for idealism as a mature choice, the "reverse cargo cult", real versus performative authenticity, losing and regaining authenticity by integrating feared parts, and the "inflation of competence boundaries" in entrepreneurial culture. The author argues this matters because, in a culture of narcissism, psychological health declines. He also argues that the idea of a separate self underlies personal suffering and humanity's global problems [msg #559, #964].

This part adds three later essays (September–October 2026) to the topic. They continue the existing file's line on performative authenticity [msg #1506], the culture of narcissism in an attention economy [msg #559] and authenticity traded for social acceptance [msg #948]. New here: (1) Aude Juillen's concept of **depersonalised sexuality**, which the author extends to the whole of modern life as "living by ideas of what is right instead of feeling"; (2) a critique of **visibility (проявленность)** as a narcissistic, market-driven construct, with **maturity as capacity** proposed instead; (3) **vulnerability as a status signal**, Goffman's backstage put on stage, the way capitalism absorbs its own critique, and the **dark room** as the response. The author says it matters because these patterns distance us from our own feelings, make experience into content and can cost us the very ability to live through experience (проживание) [msg #1910, #1954, #1958].

**Relation to the existing file.** #1958 extends the "Real vs performative authenticity" framework [msg #1506]. #1506 named imitating signs of sincerity and sharing vulnerability as a template. #1958 adds the sociology: *why* vulnerability became profitable (a class/status signal) and why no correct format can escape it. #1954 extends the Soviet "не высовывайся" theme from #559. #559 treated the post-Soviet heritage as what made self-promotion awkward for parents. #1954 adds a nuance: for people with that shame, speaking up can itself be a step of maturity. #1910 broadens #948's "trading authenticity for safety" into a cultural template mechanism. It also explicitly warns against the opposite extreme, narcissism [msg #1910, #1954, #1958].

## Core ideas

- **We have entered an era of narcissism.** In an attention economy, people who can manage others' attention around themselves gain money and status. Healthy narcissism is needed, but the culture pushes towards its unhealthy form [msg #559]. See *Five things that protect mental health*.
- **The facade of constant success is a defence against vulnerability.** People who show only a positive facade cannot make real contact, because admitting vulnerability is their "most terrible knot" [msg #939].
- **The separate self is an illusion of perception.** Contemplative traditions call this non-self. Siegel's interpersonal neurobiology and Seth's predictive theory of consciousness point the same way [msg #964].
- **Narcissists are people too.** Behind the facade is a sad child who got too little care. A non-demonising view is more useful than "run away" alone [msg #983].
- **Cynicism is a weakness, and idealism is maturity.** Cynicism is a defensive crust and a projection. Facing it directly gives freedom, gratitude and compassion [msg #1052]. Choosing ethics and goodness makes both your life and the world better [msg #1326].
- **The reverse cargo cult.** This is the nihilistic belief that everyone is secretly as cynical as oneself. It creates self-fulfilling prophecies [msg #1445].
- **Performative authenticity.** Being "real" becomes a tool for getting reactions. Real authenticity is the agreement between the inner process and its outer expression [msg #1506].
- **Lasting satisfaction comes from integration, not from acting out drivers.** Achievements powered by fear do not satisfy [msg #1556].
- **Success inflates the limits of competence.** "I can win, so I understand everything" is a bug, not a feature [msg #1759].

- **Depersonalised sexuality is a template for all of life.** Mass-culture images impose one uniform model, so we adapt, wear masks and rely on ideas instead of feeling. The author sees the same logic in relationships, career, parenting, money and power [msg #1910].
- **Visibility can only reveal what is already there.** Much of what is called проявленность is tension being discharged outward from an inner emptiness. It is a narcissistic construct heated by a capitalist environment [msg #1954].
- **Maturity is capacity, not exposure.** The question shifts from "will they see me" to "what do I bring into this space". The deciding factor is the source of visibility (hunger for confirmation vs surplus), not the order "inside first, then outside" [msg #1954].
- **Vulnerability has become a status signal.** Polished vulnerability needs a therapist, modern language and a safe environment, so it signals membership of a privileged circle. Social media has put Goffman's backstage on stage [msg #1958].
- **Capitalism eats its critique, so no format is "correct".** The only thing the market cannot digest is what is never exposed. Hence the dark room: a part of life that is lived, not narrated [msg #1958].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Five things that protect mental health in an attention economy / culture of narcissism
**Context.** The digital economy is built on attention. It favours people who have strong professional qualities and can also steer others' attention towards their persona, work and products. The author concludes "мы вступили в эру нарциссизма" (*we have entered the era of narcissism*), which he says can be seen even without clinical experience [msg #559].

His explanation is generational. For the parents' generation, showing oneself off was still awkward. They have one foot in the Soviet Union, with its traumas "*не высовывайся*" (*don't stick your head out*) and "*индивидуальность = эгоизм*" (*individuality = egoism*). Even without the trauma, Soviet culture was collective, which the author describes as a matter of those people's consciousness and values. Today, people who are not shy about selling themselves, and who build whole processes around it, can earn money and status in incredible ways [msg #559].

**Healthy vs unhealthy narcissism.** Healthy narcissism is needed for self-realisation. When a whole info-field is built around "Я Я Я Я" (*me me me me*) and self-promotion is culturally normal, we meet the unhealthy form increasingly often. The author lists its signs. It:
1. filters and evaluates people;
2. befriends only the "enlightened" or the successful;
3. does not feel emotions;
4. uses others;
5. ends up devaluing them.
Outwardly successful people stuck in unhealthy narcissism often have emptiness, pain or depression inside. These come from trying to avoid facing their own insecurity and lack of safety. The author says research shows narcissism leads to psychological poverty, not wealth (no specific study is named) [msg #559].

**The five protective factors.** As the attention economy and the culture of narcissism grow, the author says these will matter increasingly for mental health [msg #559]:
1. **Tools for developing a healthy ego.** His example is psychotherapy.
2. **Tools for softening and transcending the ego.** His examples are meditation and spiritual systems.
3. **Communities not based on material or work status.** In these, people are defined by human qualities and skills, not by professional or material status.
4. **Caring, human-centred corporate cultures.**
5. **Other places, tools, companies and societies that fill life with meaning.** He names the meaning of creating and serving.

### Three gifts of looking at cynicism directly
**Purpose.** This is for people who "choose to feel and sympathise" and who feel cynics looking down on them. The cynics treat openness and sensitivity as weakness. The text is written as an ironic "ode to cynicism", with the title "Спасибо всем, кто считает чувствование слабостью" (*Thank you to everyone who considers feeling a weakness*) [msg #1052].

**Mechanism.** The cynic's contempt is a projection. People throw onto us what they are afraid to admit in themselves. The author compares a projection to someone else's clothing that you did not choose: it can constrain and press. Projections that call normal things abnormal can cause irritation, bewilderment and even fear [msg #1052].

**Method.**
1. **Open even to this fear of weakness.** Stay true to the choice to feel, and feel the cynic's superiority, callousness, functionality and, in the end, cruelty.
2. **Notice the defensive crusts.** See the crusts that grow around people's fears and show up as self-confident cynicism. Also notice your own wish to defend yourself against them and close up.
3. **Look again and again, directly.** Look at the cynicism and at your own feelings about it, aware of the unpleasant "не зажмуривая глазки" (*without squeezing your eyes shut*).
This gives "невероятную мощь" (*incredible power*): "cилу светом своего осознавания помогать взрослеть частям, которые по-детски хотят сохраниться в тени" (*the strength, by the light of one's awareness, to help grow up the parts that childishly want to stay in the shadow*). Then nobody is left in there needing protection, and only space for love and compassion remains [msg #1052].

**The three results** for someone who has found this strength [msg #1052]:
1. **Freedom instead of hostility and irritation.** You stop being driven by fear. At the very least, you are freed from the depressing powerlessness of not being able to bear fear.
2. **Gratitude instead of anger at others' callousness.** You become grateful for the chance to be with fear rather than be shackled by it.
3. **More openness and natural compassion where frustration used to be.** You see how your own cynicism, and other people's, narrows understanding and takes away freedom, and you feel how sad the situation is.

**Lesson.** This way you do not get lost in a dishonest, unaware picture of the world. You use unpleasant projections to grow up further and perhaps help the space around you a little. The closing line reads: "Спасибо, черствость, жесткость и цинизм — вы стимулируете на подлинное бесстрашие, открытость и сочувствие" (*Thank you, callousness, harshness and cynicism — you stimulate genuine fearlessness, openness and compassion*) [msg #1052].

### Two arguments for idealism as maturity
**Trigger.** The author received a comment on a text with a clear moral position: "*Извини, что люди не такие, как тебе о них рассказали в сказках об общественном благе и морали*" (*Sorry that people aren't like what you were told in fairy tales about the public good and morality*). The point of the comment was that the world is cruel and not seeing it is stupid [msg #1326].

**Diagnosis of the comment.** The author calls it a classic attempt at self-assertion that is both devaluing and narcissistic.
- It is **devaluing** because its hidden message is "твои идеи наивны и глупы" (*your ideas are naive and stupid*).
- It is **narcissistic** because it rests on the position "я-то понимаю, как устроена реальность, не то что эти наивные глупцы" (*I understand how reality works, unlike these naive fools*).
He puts in the same category people who fiercely justify violence without seeing that ideas create reality. Attachment to the thought that violence is "natural" only increases its presence in the world. By repeating "так устроен мир" (*that's how the world works*), we proclaim and fix patterns that make shared life worse, and we become accomplices [msg #1326].

He says such comments do not hurt him personally. What they mark is a watershed. Some people have already chosen cynicism. Others are still thinking, doubting or have never considered their position. For the second group he explains why his "idealism", meaning writing that one should be ethical and choose the side of good, is "не наивность, а простая взрослость" (*not naivety but simple maturity*) [msg #1326]:

1. **Idealism makes my life better.** "Человек — это его выборы" (*A person is his choices*). His position did not come from rose-tinted glasses. He knows violence and cynicism first-hand from people for whom they are daily practice. For example, he worked closely for a year with a leadership team of the FSKN (the Russian drug-control service). Knowing how much cynicism and indifference there is, he deliberately chooses otherwise. He does this not because he believes in fairy tales, but because of the kind of person the choice makes him. Refusing to play along with cynicism keeps him open and empathetic, and these daily choices make him compassionate, strong and wise. Wisdom is not born from cynicism, because cynicism hides what is happening. Strength means bearing reality without hiding behind defences, not sheltering behind "realistic" justifications of violence. "Цинизм — это слабость" (*Cynicism is weakness*). Saying "the world is shit, so anything goes" is an immature escape from responsibility. It needs no courage, growth or struggle, and it leaves the person where they are, defending their stagnation in comments [msg #1326].
2. **Idealism makes the world around better.** He has no illusion that his actions will clearly improve the world. They are obviously not enough, and he is far from ideal. But once he understood that it matters to take responsibility, help others and change situations, he started doing it more in reality. He sees that in some places evil can be avoided and in others good can be added. This is not an abstraction, "как не поленится указать в комментариях очередной любитель пост-модерна" (*as yet another post-modernism fan will not fail to point out in the comments*). It is a real trace we leave in the world, and it differs from person to person. If cynics talked to people close to death, they would learn that this trace matters enormously. If the world really is shit, there are already enough people who have given up or simply use others: who kill, deceive, and buy and sell living beings. The question is whether we want to be among them, defending our irresponsibility with cynical ideas [msg #1326].

**Key question and conclusion.** The human norm is not carved in stone. Travelling the world is enough to see that people have created it and still create it. So the key question is: "выбираю ли я картину мира, которая ведёт к переменам? Или картину мира, которая только закрепляет страдания?" (*do I choose a picture of the world that leads to change, or one that only entrenches suffering?*). To live well and die peacefully, the author suggests choosing the first. This is not because the world is kind, but because "мир становится таким, каким мы его делаем. Как и наша собственная жизнь" (*the world becomes what we make it. As does our own life*) [msg #1326].

### Cargo cult vs reverse cargo cult
**Classic cargo cult (the story).** During World War II, Americans built military bases on small Pacific islands and supplied them by airdrops. This had a powerful effect on local cultures that had barely touched civilisation. The islanders saw the planes as gods bringing gifts, part of which went to them. After the war the planes suddenly stopped coming. To bring the miracle back, the islanders magically reproduced American actions. They built flagpoles, raised flags and climbed watchtowers, hoping the "iron birds" would return. They believed that an exact repetition of the ritual would bring everything back. When the planes did not come, they concluded for decades that the ritual was being done wrong and must be done more exactly. This is the classic cargo cult: "подмена причин и следствий" (*substitution of causes and effects*) [msg #1445].

**Its modern idealistic form.** The author says the same thing happens in modern entrepreneurial communities and in esoteric circles, and notes that he deliberately puts them in one sentence. The belief is that if you copy the behaviour of successful founders or rich people, you will get the same results. This is only outer imitation without understanding the depth of the underlying reality [msg #1445].

**Reverse cargo cult (the nihilistic opposite).** The ordinary cargo cult is an idealistic pattern. It has a nihilistic opposite that comes from the same mythic worldview and is widespread today. In the ordinary cargo cult, a person believes "*подражание успешным ведёт к успеху*" (*imitating the successful leads to success*). In the reverse cargo cult, the person believes "*все вокруг такие же циничные/поверхностные и т.д., как он, просто скрывают это*" (*everyone around is just as cynical/shallow as he is, they only hide it*). In this view, anyone who seems better is playing to the audience [msg #1445].

**Origin.** It comes from disappointment and pain. Someone hurt by human cruelty tries to protect themselves and starts to believe kindness does not exist at all. This produces generalising interpretations, all five of which the author lists [msg #1445]:
1. If a partnership looks kind and honest, there must be a hidden catch, because his own relationships had nothing like it.
2. If someone talks about values, they want to cheat you.
3. All businesspeople deceive.
4. Corruption is everywhere and everything is bought.
5. If a woman behaves tenderly, she must want something.
It is an extreme, nihilistic and also mythic view. It rests on an irrational belief that nothing genuine exists, and that anything better than how the believer usually behaves is fake, deception and a mask [msg #1445].

**Why it persists.** A worldview is not just a view of the world. It is a chain of past decisions. Revising it forces you to revise your whole life and face many wrong choices, so people hold on to the familiar picture with all their strength, even when it destroys them and their relationships [msg #1445].

**Self-fulfilling prophecy.** The most tragic part is that the belief makes itself come true. A person who does not believe in kindness tests it again and again in relationships until the other person gets tired and breaks down. Then he says "Ну вот, я так и знал" (*Well, there you go, I knew it*) and his picture of the world grows stronger. The author cites the Russian psychotherapist Ilya Latypov ("One on One with Life"): such projections onto the outer world lock us in a vicious circle, because the worldview creates behaviour that only reinforces the worldview [msg #1445].

**Way out.**
1. See the mechanism.
2. Become aware of what holds it up: pain, fear, loneliness and total disappointment in the world.
3. Do not exit into an equally mythic positive thinking. Move instead into a more adult, integral position "которая реабилитирует уязвимость, слабость и чувствительность" (*that rehabilitates vulnerability, weakness and sensitivity*).
Living in a simplified black-and-white world, where the ash of one's own inner fire seems normal, is easier than living in a multicoloured, pulsing, changing and endlessly complex one. "Проще, но не приятнее" (*Easier, but not more pleasant*) [msg #1445].

### Real vs performative authenticity
**Background.** In *Saving the Modern Soul*, the Israeli-French sociologist Eva Illouz analyses how modern culture turned sincerity, emotionality and authenticity into obligations. On social media this leads to many examples of the *effort to be* real instead of *simply being* real. Even a slight taste of playing at realness ruins it, because trying to be someone for others breaks the contact with oneself, and that contact is the source of authenticity [msg #1506].

The author sometimes reads a candid post and has a vague sense that its vulnerability is not an invitation to dialogue but subtle, unethical communication. In such posts, openness is used to get specific reactions: shock, admiration for courage, trust, or shares [msg #1506].

**Definition.** He proposes the term **перформативная аутентичность** (*performative authenticity*): "когда «быть настоящим» становится жестом для других, проявлением самозацикленности или способом усилить социальный капитал" (*when "being real" becomes a gesture for others, a display of self-absorption or a way to increase social capital*). He believes it often comes from *self-deception*. The person sincerely thinks they are real, but their actions flow from unconscious wishes to get something from others rather than from presence in their own life. Using Baudrillard's idea, he calls social media a space of "*симулякров искренности*" (*simulacra of sincerity*). A self-deceived person can pass the deception on, creating layers of substitution and "universes" cut off from reality [msg #1506].

**Criteria.** The author admits the line is always debatable because it concerns something subjective and socially and culturally defined. Relying on classical psychology and some modern ideas, he draws these mental boundaries [msg #1506].

Real authenticity:
1. The person acts from their own values and experiences, not to fit outside expectations.
2. They are aware of what they are doing and do not try to make an impression or prove their "realness".
3. The outer expression of the inner state is not necessarily beautiful, convenient or socially approved. It can be conflictual, vulnerable, even "unpresentable".

Performative authenticity:
1. Authenticity becomes a tool through which the person achieves something.
2. They imitate the signs of sincerity: telling "the truth", showing weaknesses, using the language of vulnerability. They do this within cultural trends and rules where such "vulnerability" is safe and profitable.
3. It often looks like a carefully edited "natural shot" on Instagram or an "honest" post with the right narrative.
4. Sometimes it reproduces a ready-made template of "how to be real". For example, today this often means sharing vulnerability.

**The decisive difference.** Outwardly the content can look the same. The difference is where it comes from and for whom it is made: "*из и ради контакта с собой*" (*from and for contact with oneself*) or "*из игры и ради образа*" (*from a game and for an image*) of being brave, vulnerable or special in others' eyes. This holds even when we have not managed to admit it honestly to ourselves [msg #1506].

**Author's self-application.** He understands the drivers that make people turn themselves inside out for the right responses, and allows that he may have such impulses too. Following them gives no deep satisfaction, because the exchange rests on deception, even if not malicious. We turn our inner world into a kind of product in order to get something, and the goals stay unspoken, both to the audience and often to ourselves. At such moments he reminds himself "я способен на большее" (*I am capable of more*). For example, he can share his genuine process without trying to shock anyone or make them think of him in a certain way [msg #1506].

### Driver model of losing and finding authenticity
**How authenticity is lost.** Because we lack basic attention to our inner processes, we do not see the point we usually act from: which impulses, emotions and ideas. We are unaware, or only dimly aware, of the key **drivers** of our life. These are so sticky that they seem to make us act under their magnetism [msg #1556].

The author's three examples:
1. Someone accumulates material wealth because they lack safety inside.
2. We may do anything to gather people around us, be adored and be put in a position of authority, because we fear being left without recognition.
3. Someone constantly seeks new sex because a feeling of abandonment lies underneath.
There is nothing wrong with earning money, making love or being a bright leader. But these bring no deep satisfaction when they result not from free choice but from being unable to resist unconscious drivers: "Это не мы подлинные и свободные в своей аутентичности — это наши страхи управляют жизнью" (*It is not we, genuine and free in our authenticity — it is our fears that run our life*) [msg #1556].

**Greatness can still come from this.** The model can produce something loud, great and astonishing. The author's examples:
- **Robin Williams** created remarkable examples of kindness that a whole generation grew up on. He did it while trying to make his stern, gloomy father laugh, or rather to get rid of the feeling that something was wrong in the family.
- **Jim Carrey** had a childhood so full of poverty and hardship that humour became a way to cope.
The author suggests that humanity may now be at a point where achievements usually come from this model: unconscious acting-out of the main drivers and automatic replay of scripts we cannot resist [msg #1556].

**Why it does not satisfy.** We feel very uncomfortable when we do not follow impulses that take us away from deep fears. Yet fulfilling them almost never brings deep satisfaction. Desired sex is followed by lostness and a new desire. The first million is followed by anxiety about losing it, emptiness, and a goal of earning ten. Robin Williams ended his life with depression and suicide. Jim Carrey has spent years trying to catch the wave of his past success again. "У них было все для счастья, но счастья не случилось" (*They had everything for happiness, but happiness did not happen*). Lasting satisfaction is impossible when we act out impulses that try to rid us of our own parts, our own fears: "В войне с сами собой не может быть победы" (*In a war with oneself there can be no victory*) [msg #1556].

**How authenticity is found: integration.** Lasting satisfaction is possible only by gaining wholeness, that is, by *integrating* the parts we try to run from. We bring them out of the unconscious, from the shadow, into awareness. That builds them into the personality instead of pushing them outside its borders. We become people who have these fears and deficits and can treat them in an inviting, caring way [msg #1556].

What *integrate* means, step by step:
1. Do not shy away from your parts, turn away from them or suppress them.
2. Treat them with attention and sympathy.
3. See the needs behind them.
4. Do not necessarily follow those needs impulsively or automatically.

**Outlook.** For the part of humanity that invests time, attention and money in integrating its ever-frightening parts, the author says new "times of integration" are probably coming. In these times life is created not from a constant attempt to get rid of something, but from a free wish to give something to the world: "Так мы возвращаемся к подлинности" (*This is how we return to genuineness*) [msg #1556].

### Depersonalised sexuality model and Juillen's four ways to reverse it
**Source and definition.** The clinical sexologist Aude Juillen introduces the concept in her book «Желать» (*To Desire*). The author says he bought the book that day and that, to his taste, the concept reflects key patterns of modern society and goes far beyond sex. Depersonalised sexuality (обезличенная сексуальность) is a widespread model in which mass-culture images have huge influence on a person and in effect deny our individual features. Regardless of who we are, what we love and desire, and our age and experience, the sexuality this model encourages is stereotyped and unified. "Она одинакова для всех — отсюда ее свойство обезличивать человека" (*It is the same for everyone — hence its property of depersonalising a person*) [msg #1910].

**Mechanism (per Juillen, as retold by the author).**
1. *Forced adaptation.* Under these images we have to show serious adaptability and give up what makes us unique. We force ourselves to do or not do certain things, think and behave differently from how we want, and constantly try on masks.
2. *Fitting the template.* We remake, adjust and squeeze ourselves into the template. Sexual life becomes cut off from the personality. It presses on us and forces constant self-control rather than bringing joy and pleasure.
3. *Adopting practices because they seem right.* We take up practices not because they suit us but because they seem correct. We reshape our appearance and change our ideas of sexuality to match the ideas dominant in society.
4. *The spectacle.* In the end we seem to live through a showy performance made of a set of images, ideas, poses, gestures and words. These only distance us further from our own feelings. Sexuality slips away increasingly, until at some point we stop feeling it at all and rely only on ideas instead of the more real layer of life [msg #1910].

**Author's generalisation.** He finds this picture very sad and says it too easily maps like a tracing onto other areas: romantic and friendly relationships, career, parenting, and relations with money and power. His example: he goes to the gym almost every day and sees many people who invest so many resources in their bodies "но как будто все дальше от него находятся" (*but seem to be ever further from it*). In his view, modern people in any sphere would rather rely on ideas of what is right for them than feel it. This helps them shed responsibility but in the end leads to sad consequences [msg #1910].

**Juillen's four ways to reverse the trend.** She proposes consciously turning the trend around, relying on critical thinking and consent to one's own uniqueness:
1. **Look critically** at everything we are shown about sexuality, especially what is presented as an ideal.
2. **Shield yourself** from the intrusive display of templates and other people's role models.
3. **Watch your own words and behaviour** so they do not spread the same depersonalising model further.
4. **Stop condemning yourself** for your life looking strange, imperfect, atypical or unsuccessful against generally accepted images, provided it suits you that way [msg #1910].

**Caveat and goal.** The author notes this applies to all of life, but everywhere it can be "одинаково легко в уме и сложно на практике" (*equally easy in the mind and hard in practice*). When a sphere of life fits the mythical average and understandable frames, everything is simple. "Но настоящий хардкор, а возможно, и настоящее ощущение жизни, начинается там, где мы в них совсем не укладываемся" (*But the real hardcore, and maybe the real sense of life, begins where we do not fit into them at all*). The road to this brave, at times very uncomfortable but honest living may be one of the most important ones to travel. On it we keep finding again "не правильную форму, а свою" (*not the right form, but our own*). The road must be travelled without falling into narcissism, which distances us from others just as endlessly. Travelled that way, the author says, it really brings us closer to stable well-being [msg #1910].

### Photographic developer metaphor of visibility

### Immature vs mature visibility
**Context.** In popular inner-work circles, he says, visibility is all anyone talks about: how to become more media-present, how to grow up and out. "Проявленность, как модная одежда — легко продается" (*Visibility, like fashionable clothes, sells easily*) [msg #1954].

**Immature visibility.**
- Much of it is a discharge of tension outward. The anxiety "я никто" (*I am nobody*) is discharged in posts, live streams and endless sharing of feelings.
- "Незрелая проявленность — вечный монолог про себя, которому нужна публика" (*Immature visibility is an eternal monologue about oneself that needs an audience*).
- Other people become a mirror and a source of constant confirmation, that is, a resource [msg #1954].

**Theoretical backing the author cites.** Christopher Lasch wrote "The Culture of Narcissism" back in 1979 about exactly this: a person without inner support lives by their reflection in others' eyes. Byung-Chul Han, whom the author has cited many times, adds in "The Burnout Society" that the modern achievement-subject exploits themselves, including through the need to be visible, and calls it freedom. In this frame "visibility" becomes "маркетингом, переодетым в духовность" (*marketing dressed up as spirituality*). Rich, multidimensional inner work turns into flat preparation of a product for market launch [msg #1954].

**Mature visibility (the author's preferred focus: maturity).**

### The separate self as illusion: contemplative and scientific views
This is the author's "scientific" follow-up to an earlier subjective note on the Ego [msg #964].

**Contemplative view.** A key idea in contemplative traditions is that we have an illusion of a really existing I/Ego, but it is only a limited perspective of perception. The author says these traditions should be integrated into scientific approaches as a knowledge base built up by humanity's most developed representatives. The traditions call the absence of a separate, indivisible self **non-self** (безсамостность), which he calls one of the hardest philosophical constructs to understand. Because it is so hard, it is unpopular in the mainstream. We see the world "из башни своего Я" (*from the tower of our I*). The I is inside and the world is outside, and perception in our society is dual, split into subject and objects, Self versus Others. We feel the I is somewhere in the head, behind the face. Culture says we are separate from others and that reality is exactly so and could not be otherwise [msg #964].

**Siegel's interpersonal neurobiology.** The author says science is beginning to show this view is mistaken. At a recent conference on human development in Stockholm, which the author attended, the neurobiologist Daniel Siegel pointed to a direct link between the idea of a separate self and mental disorders. Siegel founded interpersonal neurobiology. Taking a scientific approach, it holds that we are wrong to define the self as separate from others. In neurobiological reality, the mind is something that shows up and arises in a network of connections with other minds. So the idea of separateness dictated by modern culture is, in this account, a root delusion. It leads to constant problems and to living in a peculiar form of psychosis. The author calls it the key error of perception behind all of humanity's main global problems, felt as a fundamental deviation in modern society that needs resolving [msg #964].

Siegel's anecdote: when his child was born, his wife told him that if they wanted the child's life to be full of suffering, they should raise him with a strong idea of a separate self. Siegel's own words, as the author reports them: "Если мы так и останемся с этой идеей отдельного Я, то у человечества нет будущего" (*If we stay with this idea of a separate I, humanity has no future*) [msg #964].

**Practical proposal (Mwe).** Siegel relies on the scientific fact that language changes perception and action. He proposes no longer using the pronoun "I" and replacing it in speech with I/We, "Me/We=Mwe". This joins the individual I with the collective in language, as they are joined in reality [msg #964].

**Anil Seth's theory of consciousness.** The author says this theory plays the role for neuroscience that Dawkins' theory played for evolutionary biology. After many years of research, the neuroscientist Anil Seth proposed that we do not perceive the world objectively, as it really is. Instead we continuously construct it in predictions. According to the author, this confirms that dual perception is mistaken or illusory. Our dual I-perception is an evolutionary mechanism that helped separate organisms survive, but it has little to do with the more fundamental reality the traditions call "Как все есть на самом деле" (*How everything really is*). The mainstream still holds on to this perception, although both philosophy and science strongly challenge it, and we ourselves meet its significant limits and the problems it causes [msg #964].

**Conclusion.** These developments in parts of neuroscience show, the author says, how important it is to work with I/Ego perception in personal development. As neuroscience shows, that development cannot be separated from the collective results of human society [msg #964].

## Practical playbook

- **Choosing where to put your energy.** If you are among facade-driven people, do not expect contact. Help is possible only once someone is tired of their illusions and admits vulnerability. Until then, hold out a hand but "play with more grown-up people" [msg #939].
- **Rebuilding life around values.** The author's route was to choose genuineness, openness and authenticity as core values, tie work and profession to them, and build social circles where authenticity is welcome [msg #939].
- **Self-check before posting something candid.** Ask whether it comes from and for contact with yourself, or from a game and for an image. If you notice an impulse to shock or to shape what people think of you, remind yourself "я способен на большее" (*I am capable of more*) and share the genuine process instead [msg #1506].
- **Before reading a critique of your identity group,** take a position of curiosity and open-mindedness and ask the "defenders of your identity" to step aside [msg #1759].
- **Separate interest from competence.** Before speaking confidently outside your field, ask whether you have only "touched" it or have years of disciplined immersion comparable to your main field [msg #1759].

## Tools and resources mentioned

- **Wendy Behary, "Disarming the Narcissist: Surviving and Thriving with the Self-Absorbed"** (Russian edition "Обезоружить нарцисса. Как выжить и стать счастливым рядом с эгоцентриком"). This book by a New Jersey psychotherapist is popular among psychotherapists. The author's verdict:
  - It brings a rare voice of empathy and compassion to narcissism and gives several effective strategies for surviving, and even developing, relationships with overly self-absorbed partners, colleagues and friends.
  - The author is not personally drawn to the CBT approach it relies on, and the schema therapy is not explained very clearly.
  - Its great value is an integral lens. CBT is combined with a compassionate view, mindfulness and interpersonal neurobiology, because Behary studied and worked with Daniel Siegel, whom the author calls his favourite. It shows mindfulness fully integrated into a psychotherapy strategy, here for building healthy relationships where that is very hard without awareness.
  - The downside, shared by most such literature, is that it is hard to apply 100% on your own. A reader with average psychology knowledge would be better served by a workshop for practising Behary's schemas.
  - He recommends it to anyone in a relationship with a narcissist, or who grew up with parents who may have badly neglected the child's needs because of their own narcissism. [msg #983]
- **Anastasia Dolganova, "The World of the Narcissistic Victim: Relationships in the Context of Modern Neurosis"** ("Мир нарциссической жертвы"). Recommended in addition to Behary as a similarly non-demonising view that covers the victim's side a bit more. The verdict is "Ясная и понятная работа о нарциссической травме" (*A clear and understandable work on narcissistic trauma*) [msg #983].

- **Eva Illouz, "Saving the Modern Soul"**. Cited for the analysis of how modern culture turned sincerity, emotionality and authenticity into obligations [msg #1506].

- **James Hollis book (Russian edition).** Covers individuation and authentic life. The linked title is about finding meaning in the second half of life [msg #948].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- (!) UNCLEAR: #559 says "Исследования показывают" (*research shows*) that narcissism leads to psychological poverty, but names no study [msg #559].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: #1759 ends with "Завершение в комментарии" (*the ending is in the comments*). The conclusion of the Altman/OpenAI example is not in the source [msg #1759].
- The author's verdicts on Robin Williams and Jim Carrey are his own interpretations of their lives, not clinical findings. He hedges the general claim with "вероятно" (*probably*) [msg #1556].
- The author himself says the line between real and performative authenticity is "всегда дискуссионна" (*always debatable*), because authenticity is subjective and socially and culturally defined. He also admits he may have such impulses himself [msg #1506].
- The claims about Siegel and Seth are the author's retelling. He says Seth's theory "confirms" that dual perception is illusory, and calls separateness the cause of "all the main global problems". These are his strong readings, not results the source documents [msg #964].
- The author is open about his methodological preferences: he is not drawn to CBT, yet he recommends a CBT-based book [msg #983].
- Possible tension: #983 asks for love and compassion towards narcissists, while #939 recommends "playing with more grown-up people" for now. The author frames both as compassion plus realistic limits, so this is not resolved as a contradiction [msg #939, #983].
- Self-reference: the cynicism texts arise from comments on the author's own posts, and he describes such commenters as narcissistic and devaluing [msg #1326].

- The author's own hedges: the depersonalisation logic is "easy in the mind and hard in practice" [msg #1910]. Visibility is "во многом" (*largely*) a narcissistic construct, not entirely [msg #1954]. He says he does not believe in a correct format, so the dark room is offered as "может быть" (*maybe*) the most radical move, not a rule [msg #1958].
- (!) CONTRADICTION (acknowledged and resolved by the author): #1954 privileges maturity before visibility, yet admits that for people shaped by "не высовывайся" shame, speaking up is itself maturity. He resolves this by moving the criterion from order to source [msg #1954].
- Self-reflexive irony: the anti-visibility post itself went viral, and the author predicts his critique will become a new image ("the quiet expert") [msg #1958].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: the Boltanski and Chiapello research and the Patagonia sales increase ("кратно", *several-fold*) are cited without titles or exact figures [msg #1958]. The 60,000 Facebook views figure is the author's own report [msg #1958].
- Juillen's ideas are known here only as the author's retelling of a book he had just bought [msg #1910].
- The source contains typos "Узвимость" and "узявимость" for уязвимость (*vulnerability*); they are kept verbatim in the quotes [msg #1958].

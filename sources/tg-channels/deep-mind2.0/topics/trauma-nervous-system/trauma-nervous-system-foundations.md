# Trauma and the nervous system: foundations

## What this covers

This file collects what the deep mind channel teaches about psychological trauma and the nervous system: what trauma is, how it is stored in body and psyche, how it and chronic stress change the nervous system (hypertonus, hypotonus, window of tolerance, vagal tone, myelin), why a dysregulated person loses access to the neocortex, and what healing requires (trauma therapy, EMDR, long and disciplined work, deep intensive therapy). It also covers collective and transgenerational trauma, religious/spiritual trauma and the risks of deep spiritual practices for traumatised people. According to the author, trauma together with ethics is one of the two most important topics in self-development, and healing trauma adds more to quality of life than anything else [msg #124]; the state of the nervous system determines performance — decisions, dialogues and the breadth of perception [msg #475].

This part covers two newer posts (July 2026). #1861 announces a lecture by body therapist Nir Esterman. The post explains how an intergenerational ban on weakness turns into a ban on feeling, sends vulnerability into the shadow and puts a persona in its place. #1862 argues that no inner work removes trauma. Healing means integration. The post looks at the wish to get rid of trauma through three lenses (cultural, religious, psychological) and draws on Bruce Tift and Judith Herman. The author treats this as something that is "often missed" in the flood of ideas about therapy and trauma. He says getting it wrong makes therapy harder and draws people to dubious, cathartic methods [msg #1862].

**Relation to the existing knowledge file:**
- **Extends** the claim that trauma is often transgenerational (from the post on religious trauma, #1362). The new post describes one concrete mechanism: a survival rule of one generation (do not be weak) becomes the next generation's ban on feeling, and the forbidden part goes into the shadow [msg #1861].
- **Extends and sharpens** the existing ideas that "healing is possible but is real, hard work" and that "trauma cannot simply be let go" (#921, #1443, #759). The new post says trauma is never removed at all, only integrated, and treats this as "not a bug but a feature" [msg #1862].
- **Possible tension:** the existing file describes deep intensive therapy (#1443) with ~8-hour sessions and visible change in almost 100% of cases. #1862 describes PAT intensives where the author assists. In 9 of 10 cases these turn into deep trauma work. The post also warns against hoping for a magic cure "within one intensive." These do not contradict each other: #1443 talks about transformation, not removal. Still, readers should hold both together. (!) UNCLEAR: whether the method in #1443 is the same PAT method is not stated.
- **Adds** a new caution about cathartic methods. Until now the file warned only about deep practices (tantra, holotropic breathing, rebirthing) for traumatised people (#124). The new post calls cathartic methods obviously harmful and explains why they attract people [msg #1862].

## Core ideas

- **Trauma is the foundation of development work.** Without working through trauma there is no genuine maturity; trauma therapy is "the foundation of your development path" and should precede spiritual/transcendent practice [msg #124].
- **Trauma is very common.** The channel repeatedly cites that the majority of people are traumatised: 70–90% in Russia [msg #124], 6–7 out of 10 in Russia and the USA [msg #429], 7 out of 10 [msg #691]; in deep therapy "trauma is total" [msg #1443].
- **Trauma physically changes brain and body.** It is stored as neural routes and body tension, changes gene expression and creates neural "highways" reinforced by repetition, which is why it cannot be simply "let go" [msg #759, #921].
- **Trauma shows up as nervous system imbalance.** Psychological trauma manifests as a nervous system stuck in a costly stress response, easily triggered and slow to recover — linked to low vagal tone and insufficient myelin [msg #722].
- **Regulation precedes thinking.** A dysregulated person has no access to the smartest parts of the brain; communication, learning and good decisions require first returning to a regulated state [msg #704, #194, #169].
- **Every daily action is an investment in a nervous system modality.** Repeated stress, caffeine and scattered attention grow a nervous system used to living in anxiety; one can instead invest in the window of tolerance [msg #475].
- **Trauma is collective, not only individual.** Traumatised people and societies struggle to cooperate; organisational development can be seen as healing of systems [msg #438, #1362].
- **Healing is possible but is real, hard work.** Another life is available — people retrieve lost parts and regain freedom to choose reactions — but this takes courage, discipline and time [msg #921, #1443, #759].

- **Therapy will not rid you of trauma.** Removing trauma is not possible with any kind of inner work. At the end of trauma work the trauma is integrated into one's story, not expelled from it [msg #1862].
- **The wish to be rid of trauma comes from culture, religion and psychology.** It comes from a "repair" logic, from an exorcism myth, and from the logic of the trauma itself (splitting). See *Three lenses* [msg #1862].
- **Healing is bringing an exiled part home.** It is a marathon that needs preparation and constant practice. Part of it is run not by effort but by a gaze resting on flowers, i.e. by contemplative presence [msg #1862].
- **What is forbidden goes into the shadow.** A ban on weakness that once helped a generation survive is inherited as a ban on feeling. The forbidden part does not vanish but goes into the shadow, and a persona takes its place [msg #1861].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Trauma as unprocessed experience that blocks maturation
The author's foundational model of trauma, from a post titled "Про травму" (About trauma) [msg #124].
1. **Maturity is the source of lasting happiness.** Good managers are mature people: they understand their weaknesses, see other people's strengths, have a flexible communication style and can draw benefit from conflicts. The same applies to happy people — sustained satisfaction comes from maturity; happiness is gained not from outside but from healing inner conflicts [msg #124].
2. **Maturation as crisis resolution.** "Созревание - это поэтапное решение кризисов за счет выхода за их рамки." (Maturation is the stage-by-stage resolution of crises by going beyond their frame.) This is how new, more complex structures of the psyche are created [msg #124].
3. **Definition of trauma.** "Травма - это место истории человека, в котором он не обладал достаточным ресурсом для адекватной реакции на действия других людей или обстоятельства жизни." (Trauma is a place in a person's history where they did not have sufficient resources to respond adequately to other people's actions or life circumstances.) [msg #124]
4. **Mechanism of sealing.** We cannot cope with the pain and "seal off" (закупориваем) the experience. Then we keep it all our life in body and psyche, protecting the psyche from contact with it and protecting part of our personality from change [msg #124].
5. **Consequence: blocked growth.** Because of this we cannot grow in that area, i.e. cannot integrate the experience and go beyond its limits — which is exactly what maturation requires (step 2). Hence "Травма вместе с этикой - две самые важные темы в работе над собой." (Trauma together with ethics are the two most important topics in working on oneself.) [msg #124]
6. **Priority claim.** Based on his 10 years of conscious work with the psyche and modern psychotherapy, the author says healing from trauma adds the most to quality of life; trauma therapy is the basic task on the way to the norm and beyond it. He quotes the saying «Если хочешь трансцендировать, т.е. развить духовность, пройди сначала Фрейда» ("If you want to transcend, i.e. develop spirituality, go through Freud first") [msg #124].

### Three basic stress responses and the fight-response crash
Written at the start of the 2020 quarantine, after a conversation with an entrepreneur friend [msg #169].
- **Context:** people around seemed fine and even more active — working more, looking for new ideas and opportunities. Yet the most fundamental things had been pulled from under their feet: (1) personal freedom, (2) certainty about money and work, (3) safety of loved ones' health, (4) live closeness/intimacy. The typical reaction was "okay, nothing terrible, I'll sort it out, I just need to work more" [msg #169].
- **The fight response.** The author links this to one of the three basic stress responses — "бей" (fight). In it, when in danger, we become hyperactive; a hormone release lets us feel energy and strength on adrenaline and push hard. (!) INCOMPLETE: the post names "three basic stress responses" but only describes "fight"; the other two are not named in this message [msg #169].
- **Bad news 1 — the crash.** This energy runs out. When depends on the resources of the psyche, but for most people soon — in 1–2 weeks. Then comes "тлен" (ruin/decay): depression, aggression, exhaustion, burnout [msg #169].
- **Bad news 2 — automatic, not creative, behaviour.** A stress reaction activates automatic behaviour rather than conscious-creative behaviour — the amygdala rather than the neocortex. We seem very effective (100 tasks a day, thinking fine), but it is as if we had drunk a glass of wine: the genuinely wide picture is not available and the real ability to understand strategically where to run drops. Whether we will be pleased with the results of this frantic work later is "a very big question" [msg #169].
- **Author's conclusion:** there are simple, "not rocket science" ways to prevent this hormonal crash, keep clarity of mind and reach the end of quarantine in good shape; in his view this is the most important thing, because it will determine our place in the new economy. ### Three skills of self-management under pressure
From "Мозг под давлением" (The brain under pressure), drawing on neuroendocrinologist Robert Sapolsky [msg #194]. The pandemic, the author says, sets the task of reaching a new level in managing oneself:
1. **Know what states a human being can be in** [msg #194].
2. **Notice manifestations of automatic reactions** such as stress and strong emotions — for example scrolling, hostility to strangers, tunnel vision [msg #194].
3. **Keep rationality and empathy under prolonged pressure** [msg #194].
Why: the quoted Sapolsky material says the pandemic is a critical moment socially and neurobiologically — it forces many important decisions while creating a stressful environment, and that is exactly when it is easiest to err, «Потому что обычно под влиянием стресса мы принимаем худшие решения в жизни» ("Because under stress we usually make the worst decisions of our lives") [msg #194]. Sapolsky recommends staying vigilant; the author's opinion is that this is impossible without concrete skills: mindfulness, relaxation, and changing the stress response. The author also links this to his earlier writing on the importance of emotions for good decisions [msg #194].

### Nervous system modalities as investments (hypertonus / hypotonus / window of tolerance)
From "Нервная система за 10 минут" (The nervous system in 10 minutes) [msg #475].
- **Why it matters:** performance depends on the state of the nervous system — the decisions made, the dialogues held, the narrowness or breadth of perception [msg #475].
- **Investment principle:** "Все наши действия и обстоятельства – это инвестиции в определенную модальность нервной системы" (All our actions and circumstances are investments in a certain modality of the nervous system). If you walk a path often it becomes a road; so if we practise stress, caffeine and scattered attention every day, we grow a nervous system that is used to being and acting from anxiety [msg #475].
- Most active city dwellers today invest in one of two extremes:
  1. **Hypertonus (гипертонус).** We are stressed, anxious or afraid. Since most people experience emotions fairly unconsciously, this state can be recognised by physiological signs: breathing becomes faster or less full, heartbeat speeds up, sweating appears [msg #475].
  2. **Hypotonus (гипотонус).** We may experience depression, reduced activity, a feeling of numbness, lowered temperature, a wish to hide and freeze [msg #475].
  3. **Window of tolerance (окно толерантности) — the zone of optimal functioning.** We are in it when we balance between the two extremes. It is a nervous system state in which one can calmly live through life's circumstances without resorting to numbing and without panicking — a safe place where we do not need to activate defences (food, workaholism, sex, sport, jiggling a leg under the table) to calm down [msg #475].
- **Width varies.** Each person's window has a different width, depending on personal developmental history and daily circumstances. You sense leaving the window by how quickly you go into a hyper- or hypo-active state [msg #475]. Bessel van der Kolk is described as researching trauma, "которая уменьшает окно толерантности" (which narrows the window of tolerance) [msg #475].

### Window of tolerance audit
Purpose: widening the window of tolerance. Besides therapy and other development practices, we can influence the window's size by investing in everything that creates safety, comfort and calm for the nervous system [msg #475]. Minimum steps:
1. **Bring awareness to your states** and study your nervous system's responses [msg #475].
2. **Audit current "investments":** understand which places, people and practices bring you into the window of tolerance and which take you out of it [msg #475].
3. **Make corrections** — shift investment toward what keeps you in the window [msg #475].

### Low-vagal-tone vicious circle
From "Вагус — WTF?" [msg #722].
- **Vagus nerve:** the longest nerve in the body and the most significant for our state; it influences literally all of our well-being and behaviour [msg #722].
- **Healthy development:** optimal development requires a child to spend enough time next to an adult in a calm state who transmits stability and safety and helps the child regulate frustration. Then the child's nervous system forms a special structure — myelin — which helps maintain high vagal tone and gives resilience to stressors [msg #722].
- **Biological default:** we are biologically adapted to this kind of vagus functioning; body and psyche are made to spend most of the time in a calm, relaxed state, in which the vagus launches processes of rest, detoxification and recovery [msg #722].
- **What goes wrong:** if something goes wrong in development, not enough myelin forms, leading to low vagal tone. Low myelin is one of the most frequent consequences of emotional rejection in childhood or psychological trauma. The result is an unbalanced nervous system that is easily triggered and recovers slowly — "По сути психическая травма проявляется как дисбаланс нервной системы, которая застревает в затратном стрессовом отклике." (In essence, psychological trauma shows up as an imbalance of a nervous system stuck in a costly stress response.) Chronic stress in adulthood — unsafe living conditions and constant worry — can lead to a similar change in the vagus [msg #722].
- **The circle (5 links):**
  1. low myelin;
  2. easy activation of the stress response;
  3. difficulty recovering after stress;
  4. even more stress from the "rat race" to get back to a normal state;
  5. even more problems with health and life [msg #722].
- **Why self-help often fails:** people with chronic-stress symptoms spend much effort getting back to normal — supplements, detox, sport, trying to reduce inflammation — yet get no clear result, become frustrated and despair, because the problem may lie in the state of the nervous system itself [msg #722].
- **Way out:** adults too can grow myelin, as children do. One of the main ways is to enter safe, pleasant contacts with other people. Other ways to stimulate the vagus include singing/vocal practice and listening to binaural beats; the channel's Emotional balance programme collected vagus-stimulation practices that can be done independently at moments when the stress response is activated [msg #722].

### Regulation before the neocortex ("return to the cortex")
Why self-regulation is essential for communication and learning [msg #704].
- **Case:** at a conference Sasha Stogov, a design lead at Avito, presented a joint case with deep mind on communication in a team of designers. Employees were surprised by the link between toxicity in the culture and the skills of self-regulation and stress reduction [msg #704].
- **Author's thesis:** confirmed repeatedly in honest dialogues with leaders — we make bad decisions and communicate terribly when in anger, stress or scatteredness, i.e. physiologically when dysregulated (nervous system pushed out of balance into hyper- or hypotonus). For this reason deep mind, by the author's account the first in Russia, began three years earlier to teach team communication based on awareness of emotions and states and the development of self-regulation skills [msg #704].
- **Bruce Perry's explanation** (neuropsychologist, MD, PhD, from his book on trauma), as quoted in Russian translation:
  1. When the stress response system is activated, frustration, anger and fear can switch off parts of the neocortex. "Когда человек дизрегулирован, он элементарно не имеет доступа к наиболее умным частям своего мозга." (When a person is dysregulated, they simply have no access to the smartest parts of their brain.) [msg #704]
  2. Functioning depends directly on state: the longer we stay aroused, the more the primitive parts of the brain dominate [msg #704].
  3. Perry speaks of "возвращении в кортекс" (returning to the cortex) — returning to the place where rational communication is truly possible. A regulated person can connect with others in a way that allows rational communication; with a dysregulated person, nothing you say reaches the neocortex, and nothing can be retrieved from it [msg #704].
  4. For teachers: a regulated child can learn, a dysregulated child cannot. This applies to every human interaction — colleagues, family, children [msg #704].
  5. Regulation is needed to create safe contact, and contact is the most effective way to transfer information to the neocortex. Tutor, coach, therapist, mentor and boss all depend heavily on the ability to create access to the neocortex [msg #704].
- **The problem the author names:** in uncertainty and complexity we most need the neocortex and rational communication, yet that very uncertainty and complexity increasingly disrupts our state [msg #704].

### David Kessler's exercise: describing hard and joyful events factually
Offered by psychotherapist David Kessler to "lovers of reality transurfing" (people who believe one can simply switch perception) [msg #921].
1. Recall the 2 most traumatic events of your life and 1 joyful one, without giving them emotional value or importance [msg #921].
2. Ask: can I simply describe the events factually? [msg #921]
3. Then ask: can I find something positive to describe the hard events? [msg #921]
- **What it shows:** doing it, we see that memories literally, physically trigger emotions — we cannot simply jump out of our suffering. But transformation is also available there: gradually bringing new pathways into the past [msg #921].
- **Why:** the exercise is part of unloading the "psychological backpack" we carry through life. It helps us realise that every memory of the past is experienced in the present, and in the endless present there is a chance to re-evaluate our emotional ties to "the tyranny of the past" [msg #921].

### Two types of religious/spiritual trauma
From "Религиозная / духовная травма" [msg #1362]. The author says the topic rarely comes up in his field but should, because in the post-Soviet context it has a rich history.
- **RTS (religious trauma syndrome):** a study the author cites found about 27–33% of adults in the USA had experienced religious trauma and 10–15% currently suffer from its symptoms; the study stresses its prevalence and the need for clinical recognition. The author suspects figures are higher in the post-Soviet space, though this is not known for sure because public discussion and research there are blocked [msg #1362].
- **Two poles of the same pattern:** many carry an inherited fear of being punished, rejected or isolated for believing, which can cause automatic rejection of everything religious/spiritual. Others carry the same pattern at the opposite pole — a tendency to fall into dependent, often abusive relationships with something spiritual, seen today not only in formal religion but also in secular groups for meditation, tantra and the like [msg #1362].
1. **Type 1 — trauma linked to violence around religiosity.**
   - Trauma is not always about what happened to you personally; as neuro-research has shown, it is very often transgenerational — passed from generation to generation through body memory, family attitudes, and unrecognised fears built into the basic structure of one's relation to the world [msg #1362].
   - We descend from a culture in which religion was the framework of worldview, ethics and way of life, and that framework was violently destroyed: in the first 20 years of Soviet power about 40,000 churches were destroyed and tens of thousands of clergy were shot. The author calls this a collective psychological wound: if someone in the family prayed, it could cost their life; simply keeping icons was considered a sign of backwardness. This is barely discussed or reflected in society today, yet it settled in our bodies — as contraction and mistrust, or as mindless "falling in", where common sense and understanding of the importance of ethics and boundaries in spiritual processes are lost [msg #1362].
   - Much traumatising abuse also came from religious institutions and communities themselves: not only forced involvement in rituals and traditions but also the sad "statization of the sacred", when something meaningful is used merely for political manipulation [msg #1362].
2. **Type 2 — trauma from unethically transmitted or poorly integrated spiritual experiences.** Any genuine transpersonal experience into which one was led without a request and without respect for boundaries, and which one could not include into one's usual worldview, can become traumatising due to its intensity and scale. Later it manifests either as avoidance, devaluation or fear of everything spiritual, or as irrational falling into spiritual communities with a blind spot where ethics, power and the adequacy of leaders' and groups' behaviour in spiritual processes should be [msg #1362].

### Three conclusions from intensive deep therapy
The author, not a therapist, has for almost 7 months been co-facilitating 1–2 sessions a month in a new therapeutic method his partner learned in the USA (the method allows role-crossing, with nuances). Sessions last a usual 8 hours, and in almost 100% of cases obvious transformations happen. He says the conclusions will be nothing new to the psychotherapy community but, precisely because they are obvious, need clear and loud articulation [msg #1443].
1. **Most people live with core traumas that shape their way of being.** Most people's experience contains abnormal events that broke their integrity and blocked access to inner parts, qualities and processes. This is a huge spectrum of very different circumstances, often not obviously traumatic because we adapted to them all our lives. In intensive therapy the energy of the process almost always goes there — to moments where we lost aliveness and whose tension and pain persist; the author often senses cruelty and the inner weakness of the people who produced it. However tired we are of talk about trauma, calling it fashion, hype or infantilism is often a construction that protects from contact with one's vulnerability; reality does not change because of these defences — "в глубинной работе очевидно, что травма тотальна. И именно в ней теряется наша свобода." (in deep work it is obvious that trauma is total. And it is precisely in it that our freedom is lost.) [msg #1443]
2. **Most people live in a state of inner disconnection that cannot be called optimal.** A consequence of the first: even without crisis we often do not live in harmony or do not believe in it, which may follow from past traumas where we lost inner coherence. Trying to cope with the harshness of the world and our helplessness before it — when the split arises — we lose access to important parts. Without them we may become somewhat "angular" adults, boring to ourselves, too habitual, as if automatic. The author calls this habit of living in inner incoherence and automatism **disembodiment**: a state of losing contact with reality and the clarity of our feelings and sensations, when we "fly off into the head" and exist in a world of mental constructs — functioning, but without fullness, without a sense of a living flow of history in which authenticity born of one's own presence in life exists [msg #1443].
3. **A completely different life is available.** The main insight: not theoretically — the author sees people break through shame, fear and tears to parts buried in the past and extend a helping hand to them. With support, but by themselves, they return parts alienated for years or decades and regain integrity: life with the freedom to choose reactions rather than being captured by them; feeling pain and joy; handling life not as a problematic machine that forever needs control but sculpting it from plasticine, living its juiciness and changing its form in the dance of one's desires and the demands of the environment. Sadly, we adapt so long that we stop believing it is possible to be different, and desperately look for solutions outside, because going inside is the scariest thing. Hence the reminder: there is work to be done, it requires courage, and behind it is a different level of life — "Собранной, живой, чувствительной и настоящей." (Collected, alive, sensitive and real.) [msg #1443]

### Three lenses on the wish to get rid of trauma
**Purpose:** to see where the desire for a full, fast cleansing from trauma comes from. That desire makes therapy harder and draws people to dubious methods. The author suggests looking at "само это желание через три линзы, его обуславливающие" (this very wish through three lenses that condition it) [msg #1862].

1. **Cultural lens: the repair logic.** We are used to treating ourselves as a product that has a bug. On this view we must find the fault, delete it and return to factory settings. Repair logic works well with phones, but it quietly carries over to the psyche. This creates demand for a fast, magical cure, "желательно за один интенсив" (preferably within one intensive) [msg #1862].
2. **Religious lens: exorcism (Herman names this one herself).** Behind the fantasy of catharsis stands the mythic-magical metaphor of exorcism, an ancient idea of how reality works. Trauma is pictured as a demon that has got into us and can be driven out by a ritual. Many modern practices unknowingly repeat this drama of expulsion [msg #1862].
3. **Psychological lens: the urge to get rid of trauma repeats the logic of trauma itself.** The author calls this lens "самой глубокой" (the deepest). At the core of trauma lie rejection and a split: the moment when part of our experience became unbearable and was sent into exile. When we now try to tear that part out by the root, we act from the same logic of splitting, not from wholeness. We "Продолжаем ту же войну с частью себя, только под флагом исцеления" (continue the same war with a part of ourselves, only under the flag of healing) [msg #1862].

**Consequence of lens 3:** this is one reason cathartic methods are so popular "несмотря на их очевидную вредность" (despite their obvious harmfulness). They speak to the inner logic of splitting and to the learned sense that something is wrong with our parts. But they do not help integrate the trauma; they only exploit the fact that it is there [msg #1862].

### Therapeutic vs contemplative approach to healing (Bruce Tift)
- **Therapeutic approach.** In 9 of 10 PAT intensive cases the author has assisted in, deep trauma work unfolds. Situations, or whole series of them, come up, and the therapist helps the person regain agency (субъектность), strength and safety inside those situations. Put very simply, this is also how most therapy works: as labour we apply to a problem in our story. This labour often really does demand "пота, крови и слез" (sweat, blood and tears). The author says he has assisted in dozens of such cases and is "невероятно рад этим процессам" (incredibly glad about these processes) [msg #1862].
- **Contemplative approach.** Sometimes therapy looks quite different: a gaze that delights in living flowers, or a sense of hearing that suddenly notices the beauty of a melody. Bruce Tift separates the two approaches. In the contemplative one we stop fixing our story as a problem and simply stay with our experiences, whatever they are. In this quiet presence, which asks us to do nothing with reality, there may paradoxically be more healing than in active, directed work on problems [msg #1862].
- **Common ground.** Whatever the path, no inner work removes trauma; the end point is integration [msg #1862].

### Recovery as a marathon, not catharsis (Judith Herman)
- **The catharsis myth.** In her book *Trauma and Recovery* (Травма и исцеление), which the author calls classic and great, Judith Herman shows that the hope for catharsis is socially constructed and was long kept alive by early psychological theories. Catharsis here means being freed and cleansed of trauma by vividly reliving it [msg #1862].
- **How the myth shows up in clients.** Clients often feel it is enough to pour out their soul and dive into the most painful experiences, and that this will solve everything. Under this belief, therapy starts to look like "садомазохистической оргии, в которой пациент будет кричать, плакать, истекать кровью, умрет и возродится очищенным от последствий травмы" (a sadomasochistic orgy in which the patient will scream, cry, bleed, die and be reborn cleansed of the effects of trauma) [msg #1862].
- **The alternative image.** Healing means returning the exiled part home and building it into the story with acceptance and a right to speak. So Herman sets a different image against quick catharsis: recovery as a marathon that needs preparation and constant practice. The author adds a caveat: part of this marathon is run not by effort but by a gaze that falls on flowers, a gaze that can love everything perfect and imperfect already present in life [msg #1862].

### Shadow and persona formed by intergenerational trauma (Nir Esterman)
- **Example.** At a deep mind community meeting, Nir Esterman said: "В России есть такая история, что если ты слабый — ты умрешь. Черным по белому" (In Russia there is this story: if you are weak, you will die. In black and white.) [msg #1861]
- **Mechanism, in the author's words.** A ban on weakness that was a condition of survival for one generation is inherited by the next as a ban on feeling, for example on feeling vulnerability. What is forbidden does not disappear without a trace. It goes into the shadow, and in its place we show a persona: what we try to appear to be in order to be accepted [msg #1861].
- **Link to the "weak youth" myth.** The author ties this to his recent text on the myth of weak young people. There, so-called strength turns out to be not resilience but a persona with learned emotional anaemia, a way to survive in insane times [msg #1861].
- **What the lecture covers (outline only):** (1) what the shadow is and how it differs from the persona; (2) the difference between shadow and trauma; (3) how family and collective shadow forms around what was once a condition of the ancestors' survival; (4) why the qualities in others that trigger us most are exactly the ones we rejected in ourselves; (5) why one must not work with a client's shadow before working with one's own; (6) a shadow-work practice one can repeat on one's own [msg #1861, img #1861].

The author recommends listening to the lecture and doing the practice to understand how these processes affect us and shape the fate of different generations [msg #1861].

## Detailed insights

### Deep practices are dangerous with unhealed trauma
- Tantra, Kundalini and Dzogchen will not benefit you if you have unhealed trauma: "В этом случае вы не трансцендируете эго, вы его коллапсируете и отлетаете." (In this case you don't transcend the ego, you collapse it and fly off.) [msg #124]
- The author says most practitioners ignore this message although all scientific data support it. Moreover, it is precisely traumatised people who are most drawn to transcendent experiences: unconsciously they seek re-traumatisation, because part of their psychic energy is "stuck" and demands an outlet [msg #124].
- Rules from the same post: it is very bad if your psychotherapist or mindfulness teacher is not trauma-informed; avoid any body or psycho-specialist unfamiliar with trauma — yoga teacher, massage therapist, coach; and "!Бегите от тантры, недвойственных практик, холотропа, реберфинга и любых других техник, работающих с глубинными слоями психики, если у вас травма." (Run from tantra, non-dual practices, holotropic breathing, rebirthing and any other techniques working with deep layers of the psyche if you have trauma.) [msg #124]
- See also *Two types of religious/spiritual trauma*, type 2: spiritual experiences induced without request or respect for boundaries can themselves traumatise [msg #1362].

### Why trauma distorts time and presence
- The more traumatic a year, the faster it flies; the author notes that 2022 felt as if it had not happened, despite bringing a "whole wagon" of strong changes, with 24 February remaining a special day in history [msg #759].
- Survivors usually speak of the world being divided into "before" and "after", and the moment of horror stays fresh in memory down to the details. Such non-standard perception of time is characteristic of trauma: in moments of intense stress we "fly off" into fear, which, without integration of the difficult experience, stays with us as neural routes and bodily tension (зажимы). This is evolution, manifest in our physiology, trying never to allow the experience to repeat and not letting us forget it [msg #759].
- Fear prevents being in the present — feeling the body normally and living life more wholly and engaged — so life starts to slip through the fingers, and we get lost more and more in a looking-glass world of thoughts with little access to feelings and emotions [msg #759].

### A constructive worldview as support after trauma
The author's personal stance from a year-end reflection [msg #759]:
- Wherever we find ourselves, we can at least try to create a constructive support in the form of a constructive worldview. He likens it to a growth mindset, which "хоть не нашла научного подтверждения, работает на практике" (although it has not found scientific confirmation, works in practice) [msg #759].
- In a sense it does not matter how bad events were or how bad life was yesterday: the past cannot be changed, the present is what it is, the future has not arrived — but I can still influence myself in it. Not to control the world this time or create more safety for myself, but to do exactly what lies within a human's power and responsibility — become a little better while alive [msg #759].
- Concretely: grow something good in oneself every new day; learn to be a bit more attentive to oneself and others; and so be less and less captured by negative emotions (anger, envy, self-importance) or equally capturing patterns that always lead to trouble (the drive to extract benefit from everything, to gather as much as possible around oneself, clinging to one's own picture of the world) [msg #759].
- Every day is a chance to practise opening more to suffering in oneself and around, in order to truly know com-passion (со-страдание) — the kind that makes any anger impossible. "Рост — это постоянный процесс исцеления, очищение от прошлого и культивации чего-то, что вытаскивает нас за пределы Я." (Growth is a constant process of healing, cleansing from the past and cultivating something that pulls us beyond the self.) Nobody can take these possibilities away except oneself, and this gives hope [msg #759].

### Suffering is not simply a choice
- The meme "pain is inevitable, suffering is our own choice" is often used by people who began practices and got an insight about how they "themselves" create suffering, e.g. in the body. The author's view: although suffering may be a ghost of our own projection, memeifying spiritual truths devalues the hard work needed on the path of healing and growth [msg #921].
- By suffering we mostly mean not pain from an accident but pain from negative emotions and thoughts, which exists physically and is an echo or direct consequence of psychological trauma. Traumas are healed not by a simple switch of perception but through quite difficult work whose benefit comes through labour and discipline [msg #921].
- Neuroscience confirms the difficulty: trauma physically changes the brain and nervous system and changes gene expression. New neural pathways are created and later reinforced many times by repetition; habits of worrying or thinking negatively correlate with physical neural roads and even "autobahns" in the brain. So one has to work to find new paths and not fall onto the old ones at every trigger [msg #921].
- "Эмоциональная трансформация — это не трансферфинг реальности, а работа, и зачастую очень сложная и долгая." (Emotional transformation is not reality transurfing but work, and often very hard and long.) The author would like to say something like "just go to a psychosomatics session and it will pass", but it is not so [msg #921].
- Conclusion: with tools for processing grief and pain, eventually through courage and diligence, we can make suffering optional and thus confirm the meme — but the road is not at arm's length, so one should not tell suffering people "just stop". The author believes everyone too accustomed to their own suffering can, and maybe should, take some steps on that road [msg #921].

### Collective trauma and organisations
- Trend claim: companies of the future will have more and more psychology, and the author believes even more room will open for psychotherapy in companies [msg #438].
- Human science is turning to psychological trauma as the main root of mental health problems. Trauma creates a subjective feeling that the world and other people are hostile; it leads to destructive behaviour — conflicts, psychological acting-out and breakdowns. Traumatised people and societies find it very hard to switch to the rails of cooperation and partnership that modern organisations need; the author notes Russia's extremely high level of traumatisation, which makes it easy to inflate rhetoric of external enemies [msg #438].
- So trauma is not only individual but collective, with far-reaching consequences for society. Taking this and modern science into account, organisational problems can be viewed not only through business model and processes but through healing, recovery and further maturation of people — the view deep mind offers when working with companies [msg #438].
- Example: Meridian University in California launched a programme on psychotrauma and healing of systems, with modules previously unimaginable in business education: (1) leadership for transforming the whole system; (2) interpersonal neurobiology; (3) a body-based approach to trauma; (4) embodiment practices; (5) relationship development [msg #438]. The post also mentions the premiere of the film THE WISDOM OF TRAUMA with a conference as a key current event on psychotrauma [msg #438].
- In a lighter post on Prince Harry publicly undergoing trauma therapy (EMDR), the author presents it as an example of a big trend toward inner work and mental health: at that level it is no longer scary to admit not only stress but healing trauma, and he jokes that Russia's leadership should follow the example — perhaps aggression and paranoia in the country would ease [msg #429].

### EMDR as an evidence-based trauma method
EMDR is described as one of the few proven methods of working with trauma. The deep mind team cultivates trauma-therapy competencies for the safety and ethics of its programmes; at the time, 2 deep mind psychologists were members of the international EMDR association and helped people in this approach [msg #429].

## Evidence and numbers

- 70–90% of people in Russia are traumatised (author's claim, no source given) [msg #124].
- 6–7 out of 10 people in Russia and the USA have psychotrauma "по статистике" (according to statistics) [msg #429]; 7 out of 10 have psychological trauma [msg #691].
- Fight-response energy typically runs out in 1–2 weeks for most people (author's estimate) [msg #169].
- Religious trauma: 27–33% of US adults experienced it, 10–15% currently have symptoms (a study the author cites) [msg #1362].
- About 40,000 churches destroyed and tens of thousands of clergy shot in the first 20 years of Soviet power [msg #1362].
- Deep intensive therapy: ~8-hour sessions, visible transformations in almost 100% of cases (author's observation over ~7 months, 1–2 sessions/month) [msg #1443].

# Supplements and recovery protocols

## What this covers

How the channel author restores and protects mental clarity and physical state under demanding conditions: brain fog (after covid or simply from intense city life and work), frequent flights, and periods of high workload, stress and uncertainty. The topic spans an anti-inflammatory diet, healthy fats (MCT and ghee) taken as "bulletproof" coffee, a brain-support supplement shortlist, a seven-supplement stress list, a flight-recovery protocol, an ergonomic laptop setup, and a functional-medicine clinic's model of mental clarity. It matters, per the author, because a clouded mind instantly shows up in the quality of his work and his leadership of the company, so he switches on the "full arsenal" of recovery methods as soon as he notices it [msg #329]. Every supplement post carries the same caveat: the team has no medical or nutrition expertise, so consult a doctor and check your blood and contraindications [msg #329, #966, #1160].

## Core ideas

- **Brain fog is environmental, not only post-covid.** Many covid patients report a dull or clouded mind that does not pass right after the illness, but the same state is easy to get just by living and working actively in Moscow [msg #329]. The clinic speakers add the mechanism: city life and intense business keep cortisol chronically elevated (which physically affects the brain) and maintain chronic inflammation, so the brain "literally does not manage to fully clean itself" [msg #1079].
- **Clarity is multifactorial.** Both the author's seven-point protocol and the clinic's model treat clarity as the result of sleep, nutrition, movement, light/information hygiene, supplements and assessment together, not one fix [msg #329, #1079].
- **Inflammation is the common enemy.** Diet, supplements and movement are all framed as ways to lower overall inflammation, which "also negatively affects the ability to think clearly" [msg #329, #1079].
- **Mental clarity can be trained.** The author's addition to medicine: clarity is a natural property of consciousness, and meditation grows neural pathways supporting attention and memory — "the most direct way" [msg #1079, #329].
- **Introduce supplements one at a time.** Check contraindications, add one, observe several days, then add the next — this is how he caught a bad reaction to NAC [msg #1160].
- **Small "game changers" give large returns.** Simple, easy-to-adopt things like a laptop stand or a daily healthy-fat drink make a big contribution to one's state [msg #950, #966].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Seven-point protocol against brain fog
Purpose: what the author considers especially important to do when he notices fog in his head (written while he himself was ill with covid). He says he immediately switches on the full set for fast recovery [msg #329].

1. **Quality sleep.** During sleep the brain undergoes both physical cleansing and psychological integration of information. (The author refers to an earlier sleep guide of his; its content is not part of this source.) [msg #329]
2. **Anti-inflammatory nutrition with cognitive support.** Three moves:
   - *Remove* all allergens and inflammation-producing foods. First in line: all sugars and sweeteners, even natural ones; casein; gluten; non-organic red meat; any other foods containing toxins.
   - *Add* healthy fats. First tier: MCT (refined coconut oil) and organic ghee from grass-fed cows — the author drinks coffee with them every morning (see *Bulletproof coffee with MCT and ghee*). Second tier: unrefined organic olive, sesame, flaxseed and coconut oils.
   - *Increase* fresh, unprocessed, untreated organic foods: greens, vegetables, nuts, olives, fruits and berries.
   For a mitochondrial-support protocol he points to another channel's post and to Dave Asprey's Bulletproof diet (the linked protocol's content is not reproduced in the source). [msg #329]
3. **Supplements.** Supplements let you support brain neurofunction, mitochondrial work and lower general inflammation in a targeted way. His shortlist, which he usually takes through autumn and winter regardless of covid: (a) Fisetin; (b) True Focus (a NOW capsule product); (c) MethylFolate (Jarrow, 400 mcg capsules); (d) EPA/DHA Balance (Jarrow softgels; written "EPH/DHA" in the post); (e) High Absorption Curcumin (Doctor's Best, 500 mg). He describes all of them as substances supporting quality brain and nervous-system work, and adds that you should check your blood before and during taking any supplements and do it together with an adequate doctor. (!) INCOMPLETE: no doses, timing or per-item rationale are given. [msg #329]
4. **Information and digital detox.** Multitasking and mixed types of information "defragment" the brain and overload memory and attention systems. A day without the phone will certainly help; better still is permanently reducing screen time. [msg #329]
5. **Daily meditation.** It could be first on the list, since it is the direct way to work on clarity, but when physiology suffers badly, meditating is harder and people tend to drop it — hence physical basics come first. Choose whatever meditation works for you right now and that you can do regularly; concentration-building meditations take priority in this case. [msg #329]
6. **Daily movement.** Even while ill with covid, the author does exercises and moves whenever he can. Movement lowers inflammation, completes the stress cycle and supports brain function; he says so much evidence exists that movement cannot be ignored when discussing wellbeing or effectiveness. Minimum norm: 30 minutes of brisk activity per day. [msg #329]
7. **Light therapy.** Light is very important for mitochondrial health and for balancing circadian rhythms. With no sun, buy light-therapy lamps. For 2–3 hours before sleep, exclude screens and any blue or bright white light. [msg #329]

### Functional-medicine multifactor model of mental clarity
Source: a session at an integrative functional medicine clinic during NYC Tech Week, summarized by the author. He likes the integrative functional approach because the doctor does not view a disease as isolated from the rest of the body and environment but looks at the whole system, focusing on optimizing health, supporting function and the person's real daily life. The clinic therefore treats brain fog through a multifactor model ("as it should be"), and the author notes it matches the base recommendations in deep mind's own stress-management program. The goal is to "stop looking at life through a fogged-up glass" [msg #1079].

1. **Assessment of brain health and general markers.** Blood sugar, inflammation, nutrients, various profiles (lipid, hormonal, with the source itself ending the list with "и т.д.", i.e. the list is open-ended); presence of post-covid, chronic infections, cognitive dysfunctions. [msg #1079]
2. **Nutrition.** It must lower inflammation rather than raise it and supply enough nutrients. Big emphasis on reducing sugar and processed foods, and on supplements including pre- and probiotics. Fasting is recommended as a way to work with inflammation and detoxify the brain. [msg #1079]
3. **Sleep.** Called the most important foundation of health, which many high performers neglect without realizing they are giving up cognitive quality. Major brain detoxification happens in deep sleep, so do everything to sleep enough and well. [msg #1079]
4. **Exercise.** The best thing you can do for brain health and for balancing the hormonal system, which also strongly affects the brain; movement also lowers inflammation and helps clear cortisol from the body. [msg #1079]
5. **(Author's addition) Meditation.** What the doctors "literally forgot": mental clarity can be trained and is a natural property of consciousness. Through meditation we can grow neural pathways that support cognitive function — "the most direct way" to build attention and memory. He remarks, with irony, that this knowledge has apparently not yet seeped even into advanced medicine. [msg #1079]

### Flight recovery protocol
Purpose: reduce the stress load on the body before and after each flight. The author built it as someone who flies a lot (three flights in the previous week, which he calls normal for him). He admits parts are obvious, but stresses that the point is to actually *do* obvious practices [msg #867].

**Before and during the flight**
1. **Lower stress around travel day.** Plan and prepare departure and arrival, pack on time, cancel any other tasks and calls the day before. The fewer situations where he has to gut his luggage to get something out or arrives at the wrong airport, the better. [msg #867]
2. **Hydrate, sleep well and take supplements.** Drink "living" water that still contains minerals — always good, and especially before a dry environment: mineral water, good spring water, electrolytes. Coffee and alcohol can be replaced with matcha and hojicha, which he says help retain water in cells, countering dehydration. Sleep boosts immune capacity and avoids unnecessary stress in flight. The day before, adaptogens can be added, e.g. cordyceps or ashwagandha. Some studies, he says, show antioxidants such as vitamins C and E may help with the negative effects of long flights. [msg #867]
3. **Move and stretch.** Possibly the most important point: do not stay still on long flights. He has tested many times that standing up hourly, warming up and stretching changes how he feels "many times over", even on transatlantic flights — much less fatigue on arrival. Loose, comfortable clothing that doesn't press anywhere belongs here too. [msg #867]
4. **Practice concentration.** Airports scatter attention with many small actions and information. Calming attention for at least 10 minutes at any stage of the journey helps reintegrate the body's disintegrated needs and do yourself less harm during the flight. [msg #867]
5. **Don't eat airline food.** With rare exceptions it is bad. When possible he brings fresh plant food on board, especially berries rich in antioxidants and enzymes; this helps him feel fine. [msg #867]
6. **Reduce harmful information noise.** A cap, a hood and yellow glasses that shield skin and eyes from the harmful blue light abundant in airports and planes; earplugs against constant noise. [msg #867]

**After the flight**
1. **Ground yourself as fast as possible.** Do yoga on the ground without a mat, swim in the sea or a lake, or just stand on a natural floor. The author claims this is "proven" to help normalize the body's electrical charge, which changes during flight. [msg #867]
2. **Keep hydrating.** He drinks several liters of different good-quality water, takes electrolytes, applies a skin mask and moistens his eyes with Japanese vitamin eye drops. [msg #867]
3. **Fast or eat light fresh food.** Many top coaches who constantly fly recommend not eating on the first day after a flight, to help the body clear damaged cells through fasting. If that is very stressful for you, at least don't overeat, avoid heavy and carb-rich food, and add large portions of fresh vegetables and greens. [msg #867]
4. **Sleep enough and return to the natural chronorhythm.** He says this seems to be the one case where melatonin really can help sleep, by helping adjust the circadian rhythm to the new location. [msg #867]
5. **Get a massage.** As soon as possible he goes for a 2-hour massage to move lymph and carefully knead the whole body. [msg #867]

### Bulletproof coffee with MCT and ghee
Purpose: daily intake of "super healthy fats" that nourish body and brain — the author's "Game changer 2", which he says noticeably changes his life [msg #966].

- **Ingredients.** (1) Organic MCT oil — pure medium-chain triglycerides, described as the most rapidly absorbed fats available today; a source of saturated healthy fats said to harmonize internal systems and first of all raise endurance and give an instant energy boost. (2) Organic ghee from grass-fed cows — made by heating milk or butter above 100 °C, which evaporates moisture and harmful chemical pesticides; used for millennia in Ayurveda as a therapeutic remedy, believed to promote longevity, improve digestion and memory, and strengthen the nervous system. [msg #966]
- **Preparation and timing.** Blend black coffee with the oils in a shaker and drink it first thing in the morning before breakfast, then go meditate — the practice goes better. He has used decaf for more than a year (he wrote elsewhere about who that suits). The combination seems strange at first, then you get used to it and enjoy both taste and effect. Alternatives: add the oils to salads or drinks, or just take them by the spoon. [msg #966]
- **Dose.** Standard: 1 teaspoon of each oil per day; if there are no problems, gradually increase to a tablespoon. Ghee is not recommended above 1–2 tablespoons per day. [msg #966]
- **Contraindications.** MCT: the team found none except individual intolerance. Ghee: liver and biliary-tract diseases, the acute phase of ulcer and gastritis, pancreatitis, neoplasms, exacerbation of chronic diseases. [msg #966]
- **Effects the author reports on himself.** (1) His head clears quickly and strongly; (2) an even, "organic" surge of energy; (3) mood swings become softer; (4) less craving for junk food during the day — sweets and questionable snacks. [msg #966]

### Stress and high-load supplement list
Context: when deep mind prepared a program supporting teams and leaders through intense-load periods, they created several recommendation lists for different tasks together with a supplement specialist; this is part of the list aimed at supporting the body under stress — many tasks, much uncertainty, or overload from different processes. Each item gives mechanism (as stated) and the author's personal timing [msg #1160].

1. **Magnesium** — promotes relaxation by regulating neurotransmitters and helping lower cortisol; involved in more than 300 biochemical reactions, including regulation of muscle tension and nerve conduction. He personally likes two products — concentrated trace-mineral drops with other minerals and a magnesium powder (a Natural Vitality "Calm Sleep" product) — because they have well-absorbed formulas, whereas from others he often felt no effect. Timing: before sleep. [msg #1160]
2. **Reishi** — improves function of the adrenal glands that regulate stress, lowers cortisol, helps strengthen the immune system that often weakens under stress, supports healthy sleep, reduces fatigue and anxiety and improves general wellbeing, which he says is confirmed in studies of people with asthenia and chronic stress. Timing: during the day. [msg #1160]
3. **Lion's Mane** — its active compounds promote production of nerve growth factor (NGF), supporting nerve-cell health and neuron regeneration; may reduce cognitive stress and improve attention and memory; studies are said to show improved memory and attention and reduced depression and anxiety symptoms, making it useful for mental exhaustion in stressful periods. Timing: morning. [msg #1160]
4. **L-theanine** — increases alpha-wave activity, linked to relaxed wakefulness; helps regulate neurotransmitters such as serotonin and dopamine that affect mood and stress resilience; studies suggest it may also improve sleep quality. (!) INCOMPLETE: no timing given. [msg #1160]
5. **L-tryptophan** — raises brain serotonin, improving mood, lowering anxiety and improving sleep; also promotes melatonin production, helping regulate sleep that often suffers under stress. Timing: he takes it before sleep, though many take it during the day. [msg #1160]
6. **GABA** — reduces excessive neuronal activity and promotes relaxation; lowers arousal and helps with anxiety disorders without sedative effects, useful in periods of emotional overload. Timing: he takes small portions several times a day. [msg #1160]
7. **Ashwagandha** — lowers cortisol, reducing the body's stress response; has an anxiolytic (anxiety-reducing) effect supporting nervous-system health; clinical studies are said to show regular use can significantly reduce stress and anxiety symptoms and improve sleep quality and overall performance. Timing: during the day and before sleep. [msg #1160]

### One-supplement-at-a-time introduction scheme
The author's personal routine for starting anything new: (1) check contraindications (and consult a doctor, since the team lacks medical expertise); (2) add only one supplement at a time; (3) watch your reaction for several days; (4) if all is fine, add the second. Lesson from his own case: he once had an awful state from taking NAC, and this scheme is what allowed him to pinpoint the cause. Pair with the advice to check blood before and during supplementation [msg #1160, #329].

### Ergonomic laptop work setup
"Game changer 1": a laptop stand, which he finds obvious yet still unused by most people who work at computers; he constantly sees people in cafes and offices bending their heads down and creating unnecessary stress and fatigue in the body [msg #950].
- **Why.** The stand completely rid him of neck pain, gave more clarity in his head and improved his overall sense of comfort. He cites research that posture can strongly influence psychological state: an upright position (easy to hold with a stand, "impossible" without) creates confidence and good mood and makes it easier to cope with stressors and fatigue; the reverse also holds — hunching produces bodily discomfort that turns life grey and lets stress accumulate faster. His own addition: healthy posture aids mental clarity because blood moves naturally through the body and nourishes the brain. [msg #950]
- **Choosing a stand.** He tried three and settled on one that rotates around its axis, so you can easily change body position and adapt the laptop to it. Second criterion: it must fold easily and fit in a backpack so you always carry it. The accompanying photo shows his setup: a silver folding aluminium stand raising the laptop, a separate white wireless keyboard and a grey wireless mouse on a wooden desk [img #950].
- **Pitfall.** Some friends bought stands but could not use them because typing was uncomfortable. It is a whole setup: the stand needs an external keyboard, a mouse and a carrying bag. [msg #950]
- **Rule.** Don't postpone or skimp on a proper work setup — "it changes everything." Working standing is another option through which many people gained energy and health via correct posture. [msg #950]

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- **Disclaimers.** "В команде deep mind нет профессиональных экспертов по питанию" (*the deep mind team has no professional nutrition experts*) — consult specialists before applying [msg #966]; "у нас в команде нет медицинской экспертизы" (*our team has no medical expertise*) — consult a doctor and check contraindications [msg #1160]; check blood before and during supplementation with an adequate doctor [msg #329].
- **Personal experience, not trials.** Effects of bulletproof coffee, the laptop stand and flight practices are the author's self-reports. Mechanism claims for the stress supplements come from a list prepared with an unnamed supplement specialist; studies are referenced but not detailed [msg #966, #950, #867, #1160].
- **Strong claims stated as "proven".** Grounding is said to be "доказано" (*proven*) to normalize the body's electrical charge after flight; the source gives no evidence [msg #867]. Fasting after a flight to "clear damaged cells" is attributed to "many top coaches" [msg #867].
- **Melatonin.** The author is generally reserved: jet lag "seems to be the only case" where melatonin really helps sleep [msg #867].
- **Fasting** appears twice — after flights (coaches' advice) and in the clinic model for inflammation and brain detox — with the caveat that if it causes strong stress, at least avoid overeating [msg #867, #1079].
- **Meditation ordering.** In #329 meditation is placed fifth because physiology must be supported first; in #1079 it is called the most direct way to train clarity, omitted by doctors. Not a contradiction but a sequencing point [msg #329, #1079].
- (!) UNCLEAR: ghee described as rich in "monounsaturated omega-3 fatty acids" [msg #966].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: no doses for the #329 shortlist or the #1160 stress list; L-theanine has no stated timing [msg #329, #1160].

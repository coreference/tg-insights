# Crisis survival skills: STOP, TIPP and distraction

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's DBT (dialectical behaviour therapy) skills for getting through peak emotional states: how to recognise distress, the STOP skill for interrupting impulsive actions, body-based skills that rapidly brake emotional arousal (cold water / dive reflex, ice, paired muscle relaxation, intense exercise, relaxing breathing), the ПЕРЕЖИТь acronym of distraction methods with the conditions under which avoiding emotions is legitimate, and a five-step protocol for helplessness. The author frames these as tools for situations when emotions are too strong to be lived through directly — many posts were written in response to frightening collective events ("вот как сейчас" — "like right now") [msg #15, #20, #107, #109]. Note: the topic title mentions TIPP, but the source never uses the term TIPP; the body skills (cold water, intense exercise, paced breathing, paired muscle relaxation) are presented under the heading of body-based regulation skills [msg #18].

## Core ideas

- **Distress is stress beyond capacity.** Ordinary stress is useful mobilisation; distress is excessive stress that makes us non-functional. It can be recognised by "putting a ruler" to emotions: above 75 on a 0–100 scale [msg #15].
- **DBT skills are simple algorithms.** DBT is described as a proven-effective therapy for borderline personality disorder with many emotion-regulation skills; a skill is "простые алгоритмы из нескольких действий" (simple algorithms of a few actions) that help stabilise if applied [msg #15].
- **STOP is the most basic first-line skill**: stop, step back, look around, act mindfully. Its hardest part is catching the moment to stop; it requires practice [msg #15, #42, #109, #134].
- **Acting through the body is fast and crude — on purpose.** Body methods work within seconds to minutes, brake very high arousal, and can halt harmful emotion-avoidance like overeating or substance abuse [msg #18].
- **Avoiding emotions is sometimes the best option.** Contrary to the therapy cliché, avoidance is "most often" ineffective but sometimes legitimate — when pain is unbearable and either blocks necessary action or comes from a situation outside your control. Distraction methods are for peak emotions only; used constantly they are likely to harm [msg #20, #107].
- **Helplessness leads to exhaustion, not rescue.** Powerlessness is a reaction to another emotion (grief, fear) that cannot be relieved; it pushes frantic searching for a way out while preventing the calm needed to solve problems [msg #104].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Distress ruler: recognising distress
- **Definitions.** Stress is a normal state of the body that helps us gather ourselves and act quickly and appropriately. Distress ("дистресс") is excessive stress that the psyche cannot cope with: it makes us non-functional, prevents us being effective in the moment and, naturally, feels very bad [msg #15].
- **The ruler.** Imagine emotional amplitude on a scale from 0 to 100, where 0 is complete calm and 100 the maximal strength of emotion. Distress is anything above 75 [msg #15].
- **When we are usually in distress** (three situations listed by the author) [msg #15]:
  1. When the situation around us is very stressful — "вот как сейчас" (like right now).
  2. When we feel a need to do something right now, to react immediately in any way at all.
  3. When it is a sudden emotional peak.
- **What to do:** the author says she will primarily offer DBT skills to get out of crisis, starting with STOP [msg #15].

### STOP skill (СТОП)
**Purpose / when to use.** The most basic skill DBT offers first; an acronym for four actions [msg #15]. It helps you stop when you want to do something that may make things worse later, and change your mind — i.e. choose something healthier instead [msg #109]. The author links it to Viktor Frankl's idea that between stimulus and response there is always time in which we choose how to react, and "Именно здесь лежит наша свобода" (it is precisely here that our freedom lies). Sometimes that time is so short that it is hard to notice it and make a conscious choice instead of what emotions push us toward; STOP is a simple algorithm that helps obtain this freedom [msg #42].

**What it is meant to interrupt** — impulsive acts you may regret later [msg #134]:
- drinking alcohol or using psychoactive substances;
- shouting and quarrels;
- sinking into Netflix at midnight when it is time to sleep;
- eating a fifth dessert;
- 3 hours of obsessive thoughts about the same thing.

The author also gives the context of soothing behaviours that only look helpful: doomscrolling lowers anxiety in the moment but maintains it overall; alcohol distracts in the moment but overall only destabilises one's state; by contrast long walks or favourite music genuinely help [msg #109].

**The four steps** (Russian acronym С-Т-О-П; the English card gives STOP / TAKE A STEP BACK / OBSERVE / PROCEED MINDFULLY) [msg #15, #42, #109, img #134]:
1. **С — Стоп (Stop).** Literally stop. If you are reading the Facebook news feed, want to do something impulsively or have already begun — just freeze. This is a hard choice in the moment because you are revved up, and running on (reading, shouting, anything) is easier than not running. The card adds: "Не реагируйте! Не двигайтесь. Просто замрите." (Don't react! Don't move. Just freeze.) and "Сохраняйте контроль над собой" (Keep control over yourself) [msg #15, #42, #109, img #134].
2. **Т — Только шаг назад (Just a step back).** Step back — physically, by leaving the room, or internally, for example by putting the laptop aside. Focus attention on your breathing. Tell yourself you are taking a break — literally for a couple of minutes. The card adds: step aside, take a deep breath, distance yourself from what is happening [msg #15, #42, #109, img #134].
3. **О — Осмотритесь (Look around / Observe).** In crisis (during strong emotions) the horizon of attention shrinks to a single point in space, so even simply looking around helps: look out the window, examine the objects around you. Think about what you are feeling. Try to describe, without judgement, only the facts — what is happening, what others are doing and saying. The card: observe what is happening around and inside you, notice other people and your own feelings and sensations, "Не делайте выводы!" (Don't draw conclusions!) [msg #15, #42, #109, img #134].
4. **П — Попытайтесь действовать осознанно (Try to act mindfully / Proceed mindfully).** Think about what would be useful for you at this moment. What would be effective? Which actions could improve or worsen the situation? Only after that return to action. The card phrases the questions as: "Что я хочу от ситуации? Какие у меня цели? Какой выбор сделает ситуацию лучше или хуже?" (What do I want from the situation? What are my goals? Which choice will make the situation better or worse?) [msg #15, #42, #109, img #134].

**Short version and practice.** The skill is very simple: stop, leave the situation for a while, think over what is happening and what would be useful to do next. The hardest trick is finding the moment to stop — it needs practice, and you should not give up after the first failure. The steps can be done at virtually any moment, sometimes physically, sometimes only in your head. To test that it is possible almost always, the author proposed a game: everyone plans to do STOP at a fixed time (16:15 that day) regardless of what is going on — stop, step back (mentally is fine), look around, and think what a good continuation would be [msg #134].

### Body-based regulation skills — why act through the body
Described as very simple, very direct ways to cope when emotions overwhelm, when you cannot get away from obsessive thoughts, when there is an urge to do something impulsive, or when you cannot calm down. Advantages listed [msg #18]:
- they work very fast, from a few seconds to a few minutes;
- they help brake very high emotional acceleration;
- they can stop "harmful" ways of avoiding emotions, such as overeating or substance abuse;
- they work in the simplest, crudest ("топорно") way.

### Cold water (dive reflex)
**Mechanism.** The "рефлекс ныряльщика" (dive reflex) is a physiological reaction: when a person is immersed in cold water, the heartbeat slows and the parasympathetic nervous system activates. The parasympathetic system is responsible for inhibiting reactions and greater emotional control, so emotional overload is instantly replaced by an inhibition so abrupt it is surprising. Holding the breath plus the effect of icy water on the vagus nerve convince the nervous system that you are under water. Like other mammals, we switch on this reflex; the heart slows almost instantly and emotional reactions brake "Очень Резко (!)" (Very Sharply!) [msg #18, #41].

**How to do it:**
1. Full immersion is not necessary. Fill a basin, bowl or sink with very cold (from the fridge / icy) water [msg #18, #41].
2. Hold your breath and immerse your face for 30 seconds [msg #18, #41].
3. Use the window that follows: the effect lasts 5–7 minutes. In that time switch your attention, change surroundings, find someone to talk to — in short, insure yourself against slipping back into crisis and regroup [msg #18, #41].

**Substitutes (in decreasing strength):** if there is no basin and fridge water, press something very cold to the face, such as a bag of ice or frozen food — and don't forget to hold your breath; if that is unavailable, wash your face with cold water — though the stunning effect of the basin most likely will not follow [msg #18, #41].

**Caution:** be careful if you have heart problems [msg #18].

### Ice in the hand
Squeeze a piece of ice (an ice cube from the freezer) in your hand and hold it until it melts in your palm. Holding ice hurts but is not harmful in any way, so it lets you fairly safely switch from emotional suffering to physical sensations; it is described as a good "лобовой" (head-on) way to reduce the intensity of an emotion [msg #41, #20, #107].

### Paired muscle relaxation (парная мышечная релаксация)
**Principle:** we relax best if we have first tensed our muscles [msg #18].
1. Choose a word or phrase that calms you — e.g., banally, "я успокаиваюсь" (I am calming down).
2. One muscle group at a time, tense the muscles as hard as you can on the inhale.
3. On the exhale relax them as fully as possible, saying the chosen phrase to yourself.
4. Go through the whole body this way.

**Practice dose:** repeat often, about three times a day — then the chosen phrase on its own, without the exercise, will start to help you relax a little [msg #18].

### Intense physical exercise
**Why:** emotions prepare the body for action, and physical exercise is the action into which they can be channelled. **Dose:** at least 20 minutes of intense physical load — anything from squats and running to walking up stairs. **Intensity rule:** raise the pulse so that you can still talk but already feel serious exertion [msg #18].

### Relaxing breathing (расслабляющее дыхание)
Breathing is the most accessible way to cope with emotions when everything else is hard to do. Many people say breathing does not help them relax, but on follow-up questioning it turns out they breathed for a minute, saw no effect and quit. Do relaxation breathing for **no less than 5 minutes** [msg #18].
- Slow your breathing to 5–6 cycles per minute — in general, inhale for 5 counts and exhale for 7. If that is uncomfortable, adapt it to yourself.
- The exhale influences whether you calm down more than the inhale does, so in any variant keep the exhale longer.
- Breathe with the belly, not shy about inflating it properly on the inhale.
[msg #18]

### When avoiding emotions is useful
The author argues that therapists say "avoiding emotions is bad" so often that it has become a cliché — and that it is not true. Avoidance is most often ineffective because it leads to even greater emotional suffering, but sometimes it is the best we can do. Two criteria [msg #20, #107]:
1. When the emotional pain is unbearable and may prevent you from acting effectively where that is necessary.
2. When the emotional pain is unbearable and the situation causing it is outside your control and cannot be resolved immediately.

The author applied this to the current events: in 2022 she wrote that what was happening "наполовину подпадает под критерий 2" (half falls under criterion 2); in the 2024 repost, "Трагедия, которая произошла сейчас, попадает под критерий 2" (the tragedy that has just happened falls under criterion 2). The caveat: if you feel able to cope with your emotions, it is better to let yourself feel and live through them; the techniques below are for situations when you feel you cannot [msg #20, #107].

### ПЕРЕЖИТь — the DBT distraction acronym
DBT uses the acronym ПЕРЕЖИТь ("to live through") to remember ways of distracting from emotional suffering. Seven components [msg #20, #107]:
1. **П — помогите кому-нибудь (help someone).** Helping someone else who is also suffering from what is happening is a good solution from every angle: (a) attention switches very effectively, since most of us are strongly tuned to respond to other people's pain; (b) when helping we feel like our better selves and generally better; (c) helping can give more meaning to our own emotions, because through them we empathise with another.
2. **Е — если сравнить (if you compare).** It is said that in times like ours people watch either comedies to distract themselves or horror films to realise that things are still not so bad as long as zombies have not crawled out of the ground; this method is the second one. You can rewatch *Alien* and be glad you are not on a spaceship with an alien monster, or recall frightening things you have already gone through and survived. "Звучит сомнительно, но опыт показывает, что это работает" (It sounds dubious, but experience shows it works).
3. **Р — используйте Различные эмоции (use Different emotions).** Something surely evokes strong emotions in you — favourite novels, computer games, night raves. To switch away from emotions that feel as if they could crush you, you need other strong emotional stimuli, something capable of fully absorbing you. Recall what grips you most and is hardest to tear yourself away from — and use exactly that.
4. **Е — есть другие действия (there are other activities).** Some people are very good at escaping into activity when they cannot cope with feelings; the author, for example, can scrub her flat to a shine in about three hours. Look for an activity you sink into easily, in two recommended categories: (1) something that brings pleasure, e.g. self-care routines if they do; (2) something in which you feel your mastery — nothing special is needed: some people feel they are awesome at washing dishes or walking the dog.
5. **Ж — постройте Железную стену (build an Iron wall).** Scarlett O'Hara's "Я подумаю об этом завтра" (I'll think about it tomorrow) is a way of blocking feelings she could not bear in the moment. You can do as she did, imagine a wall, mentally go off into "внутреннюю Монголию" (inner Mongolia), or simply tell yourself "стоп, сюда мы не думаем" (stop, we don't think in this direction).
6. **И — используйте ощущения (use sensations).** Oscar Wilde's line "лечите душу ощущениями, а ощущения пусть лечит душа" (cure the soul by means of the senses, and let the soul cure the senses). Intense sensations switch attention even away from strong emotions: hold an ice cube until it melts (see *Ice in the hand*), use tastes — sour, spicy, bitter — or listen to very intense music.
7. **Т — течение мыслей перенаправьте (redirect the flow of thoughts).** Deliberately switch from the thoughts that cause crisis emotions to anything else: count passers-by on the street, solve equations in your head, choose the ideal dog breed.

**Rule of use:** these are methods for living through peak emotions. Used constantly they will most likely do more harm than good; but in particular hard cases only they can help [msg #20, #107].

### Five steps out of helplessness (бессилие)
**Model of helplessness.** Powerlessness is more than an emotion — it is a complex feeling with many faces: in it we live through despair, helplessness, loss of hope, rage and panic that come in waves, joined by thoughts of the end, of escape, of searching for a way out, of denying what is happening. It is not a reaction to the situation but a reaction to another emotion that cannot be relieved, such as grief or fear. That is why one can get stuck in it for a long time: it pushes you to do something, anything, to get out, find a solution and save yourself. The trap: effective problem-solving first requires calming down and coming to your senses, but helplessness, not allowing this, pushes you further through a labyrinth with no exit. It affects thoughts, body, perception of reality and behaviour; it feels like high tension and worry and at the same time submission and freezing, like loneliness and despair; thoughts alternate between searching for a solution and feeling that none exists. The author repeats deliberately: "бессилие не ведет к решению и спасению, бессилие ведет к истощению" (helplessness does not lead to a solution and rescue, helplessness leads to exhaustion). Context: she notes there seem never to have been so many social-media posts about powerlessness and loss of hope as now [msg #104].

**Steps** [msg #104]:
1. **Assess what is happening to you.** Is there tension in the body, and if so where? What emotions are you feeling? What is happening to your thoughts? What do you want to do from this state? Would doing it help you or harm you?
2. **Calm your breathing.** Put your hands on your belly, take normal inhales and long slow exhales, and watch your hands rise and fall. Continue regardless of the result for at least a couple of minutes.
3. **Change your thoughts.** Thoughts of powerlessness, even when you really cannot change what you dislike, will not help and will make things worse. Look at the emotion of helplessness itself with compassion and kindness. Think about what is in your power and what you control. Think about people who support you and share your values. Promise yourself you will do what you can when you can, and that right now you need to accept the situation as a given in order to help yourself. Turn yourself toward hope.
4. **Change your posture.** Don't freeze in one position: walk, do chores, dance ("я серьезно" — "I'm serious").
5. **Change the focus of attention.** Switch to sensory information: smell, touch, listen, taste, look, notice sensations in the body, movement through space, body position.

**Expectation:** don't expect the state to change all at once within 5 minutes; repeat all of the above as needed every time the feeling of helplessness returns [msg #104].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- Cold water: be careful if you have heart problems; substitutes (ice pack, washing the face) likely won't give the full stunning effect; the effect lasts only 5–7 minutes and must be used to switch attention and secure support [msg #18, #41].
- Distraction / avoidance methods are only for peak emotions and when you feel unable to feel your emotions; used constantly they will likely harm more than help [msg #20, #107].
- The claim that the "comparison" method works rests on the author's statement that "experience shows" it does, while acknowledging it "sounds dubious" [msg #20, #107].
- Helplessness work is not a one-off: don't expect change within 5 minutes; repeat each time the feeling returns [msg #104].
- STOP takes practice; failing to catch the moment at first is expected [msg #134].
- (!) UNCLEAR: the topic title names TIPP, but the source never uses that acronym; the body skills are presented as separate techniques, not as a TIPP sequence [msg #18].
- #107 is a near-verbatim 2024 repost of #20 (differences: "стремного" → "страшного" in the comparison method, and the criterion-2 sentence updated from the 2022 events to "Трагедия, которая произошла сейчас"); #42 and #109 repeat the STOP steps from #15 with new introductions (Frankl; doomscrolling and alcohol), and #42/#109 say "Во время сильных эмоций" where #15 says "В кризисе" [msg #15, #20, #42, #107, #109].

# Antisocial personality disorder: signs, myths and therapy

## What this covers

The channel's post continues a series ("our journey through diagnoses connected with emotional dysregulation") with antisocial personality disorder (ASPD, Russian АРЛ), and links a full Telegraph longread by the author, Lena Foer, «Антисоциальное расстройство личности: что это на самом деле» ("Antisocial personality disorder: what it really is"). The longread covers how the diagnosis is made, how it looks in women, myths (serial killers, "people without emotions", "they don't suffer"), the difference between ASPD, psychopathy and sociopathy, and what therapy can do. The author frames the value of the topic as myth-busting: «Чем больше мифов вокруг диагноза - тем больше нового можно узнать, если на пару шагов отойти от поп-культуры и стереотипов» ("The more myths around a diagnosis, the more new things you can learn if you step a couple of steps away from pop culture and stereotypes") [msg #229, article #229].

## Core ideas

- ASPD is a stable behavioural pattern, like other personality disorders, diagnosed by persistent traits across several life areas plus conduct problems before age 15; full detail in *Diagnostic signs of ASPD* [article #229].
- In women ASPD often looks different (self-harm instead of violence toward others, no childhood history, high comorbidity with BPD and anxiety), and because assessment tools were built on male samples, women more often go undiagnosed [article #229].
- Most people with ASPD never commit serious crimes; they live ordinary if unstable lives, and their central problems are irresponsibility, impulsivity, conflict and pervasive emptiness and boredom — and they suffer too [article #229].
- ASPD, psychopathy and sociopathy are related but different concepts [article #229].
- ASPD is treatable, though hard to work with: DBT builds self-control, MBT aims at changing the inner state; the author's conclusion is that ASPD is «сложное расстройство, но не приговор» ("a difficult disorder, but not a life sentence") [article #229].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Diagnostic signs of ASPD
Purpose: the key signs by which ASPD is diagnosed, according to the longread [article #229].

Core signs (10):
1. Indifference to the rights of others and to the idea of right and wrong [article #229].
2. Lying to gain advantage [article #229].
3. Insensitivity to other people's feelings, or disrespect for them [article #229].
4. Using charm or wit to manipulate others [article #229].
5. A sense of one's own superiority [article #229].
6. Criminal behaviour [article #229].
7. Aggression and threats toward others [article #229].
8. Absence of guilt and remorse for one's actions [article #229].
9. High-risk behaviour [article #229].
10. Irresponsibility and problems keeping commitments — at work and in general [article #229].

Rules for applying the list: not all signs have to be present, but the traits must be stable over time and show up not in just one area of life but in several [article #229].

Additional required criterion — behaviour problems before age 15 (4 kinds named):
1. Physical violence toward people or animals [article #229].
2. Destruction of property / arson [article #229].
3. Lying for gain [article #229].
4. Theft [article #229].

Context the author adds: like other personality disorders, ASPD is a stable behavioural pattern. People with it rarely seek help — which, she suggests with a hedge (perhaps), is why psychologists rarely write about it beyond articles of the type «как спасти себя от психопата» ("how to save yourself from a psychopath"). As with borderline and some other personality disorders, the severity of symptoms decreases over time [article #229].

### Specifics of ASPD in women (7 differences)
Purpose: as with many disorders, ASPD in women may look different from men, and the author lists how [article #229].
1. **No childhood history may be present.** Women may lack the history of typical before-15 conduct problems while having all the other symptoms. The author notes these studies were done on prison samples [article #229].
2. **Self-harm instead of violence.** Impulsivity and aggression in women are often expressed as self-harm rather than physical violence against others [article #229].
3. **Different item profile on psychopathy questionnaires.** On psychopathy questionnaires (psychopathy is not the same as ASPD but close to it), women scored higher on items about financial irresponsibility, impulsivity, lack of remorse and promiscuity — the author adds an ironic aside «(что бы это ни значило)» ("whatever that means") — while men scored higher on irritability, aggression and high-risk behaviour [article #229].
4. **Less variety in criminal activity** among women [article #229].
5. **Look more adaptive and are harder to recognise.** Women with ASPD appear more adaptive and capable of strategy, and are harder to spot because they seem to learn better from consequences — even though everyone with ASPD has some problems ("есть проблемки") with learning from consequences [article #229].
6. **Comorbid BPD.** Again in the prison study, a third of women with ASPD also had borderline personality disorder [article #229].
7. **Anxiety disorders.** One of the most common complaints and problems of women with ASPD is anxiety disorders. Against the popular image of the person with ASPD as «как Ганнибал Лектор, человек без эмоций» ("like Hannibal Lecter, a person without emotions"), anxiety disorders are not only common — they were practically the only thing shared in the adolescent experience of most adult women with this disorder [article #229].

Consequence: standard assessment methods were developed on male samples, so women more often remain undiagnosed [article #229].

### ASPD vs psychopathy vs sociopathy
Purpose: answers whether "sociopath, psychopath, antisocial" are the same thing. The author's answer: no, they are different things, though adjacent [article #229].
1. **Antisocial personality disorder** — an official medical diagnosis in the DSM-5 and ICD-11 classifications [article #229].
2. **Psychopathy** — not a diagnosis but a research construct, assessed with a special scale (PCL-R). It includes emotional coldness, lack of empathy and manipulativeness. Not everyone with ASPD is a psychopath. There is also a position according to which "psychopathy" names the most severe segment of the spectrum of antisocial traits [article #229].
3. **Sociopathy** — an outdated term, sometimes used informally; practically not used in scientific literature [article #229].

### What works in therapy for ASPD
Starting point: it used to be believed that people with ASPD cannot be helped; the author states "now we know this is not so" — the disorder responds to therapy, although working with it really is difficult [article #229].
1. **Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT, ДБТ)** — helps develop skills of emotion regulation and impulse control. Limitation: inner problems such as emptiness and the absence of an inner reward "for the good" («отсутствие внутренней награды “за хорошее”») remain; the gain is that self-control helps the person end up in prison less often [article #229].
2. **Mentalization-based therapy (MBT, МВТ)** — teaches understanding of one's own mental states and those of other people; "in theory" helps develop empathy and improve social functioning. Unlike DBT, it helps change precisely the inner state — but it is more suited to "high-functioning" clients [article #229].

Practical barriers: therapy requires time, money and motivation, and with ASPD all of these can be difficult. But those who do reach specialists can significantly improve the quality of their lives and relationships [article #229].

## Detailed insights

### Myth: people with ASPD are serial killers
- Most people with ASPD never commit serious crimes. Fights, drugs, theft and other "everyday" crimes are more frequent among them simply because such crimes are more frequent in general [article #229].
- Many people with ASPD live ordinary lives, work (though they may change jobs often) and build relationships (even if unstable) [article #229].
- Their problems are most often related to irresponsibility, impulsivity, conflicts and a pervasive feeling of emptiness and boredom [article #229].

### Boredom and the high arousal threshold
Boredom is, per the author, one of the defining features of the disorder, because the arousal threshold in people with ASPD is very high. What pleases and amuses the average person makes no impression on them; to feel something they have to do something very risky, intense or beyond the norm. This is the mechanism the author offers for the risk-seeking behaviour [article #229].

### Myth: only others suffer
It is commonly believed that people with ASPD do not suffer from it — only others do. The author disagrees: everyone suffers, since living with a constant sense of emptiness and breakdowns of control is hard («жить с постоянным чувством пустоты и срывами контроля - ну такое» — "living with a constant feeling of emptiness and lapses of control — not great") [article #229].

### Conclusion of the longread
People with this diagnosis differ; symptoms and their severity can differ "ОЧЕНЬ" — greatly; problems show up in different ways; and they can be helped. Stigma and pop-culture myths only hinder those who really need support and are ready to work on themselves [article #229].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The findings on women (missing before-15 history, one third with comorbid BPD) come from studies on prison samples, as the author notes; how they generalise is not discussed [article #229].
- The author hedges MBT's effect on empathy as "in theory" («в теории») and says DBT leaves emptiness and lack of inner reward untouched [article #229].
- The explanation for why psychologists rarely write about ASPD is the author's guess («возможно» — "perhaps") [article #229].

# Autistic and ADHD burnout, boom-or-bust and pacing

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's material on autistic and ADHD burnout: what it is, what causes it and raises its risk, how it shows up, how long it lasts and how to recover; the boom-or-bust cycle of overworking on good days and crashing afterwards, and the traits that make autistic and ADHD people prone to it; pacing systems for spending energy evenly; a reminder that the 40-hour week is not a health benchmark; and a forwarded card series with the main results of a survey of 1027 Russian-speaking autistic and ADHD people about work resilience. Lena Foer frames autistic burnout as "одна из больших и важных тем" (one of the big and important topics) that a single post can only open [msg #23]; Katya Klimochkina presents pacing as the way to break a cycle that otherwise makes each recovery longer [msg #167, #169]. The survey shows burnout as the most widespread work problem in its sample [img #271].

## Core ideas

- **Autistic burnout is a distinct, long-lasting exhaustion state** produced by stressors such as heavy expectations, masking and an unsuitable environment; it includes sensory vulnerability and loss of skills, and can last weeks to years (see *Autistic burnout 101*) [msg #23].
- **Autistic and ADHD burnout arises when inner resources are depleted** after long periods of strong stress, overwork and overexcitation; everyday tasks feel more numerous, cognition worsens and emotional well-being suffers [msg #167].
- **Boom-or-bust is a self-worsening cycle**: overworking on good days leads to crashes, and each round takes longer to recover from, ending in exhaustion and burnout [msg #167].
- **Specific neurodivergent traits make people vulnerable to the cycle**: hyperfocus, interoception problems, people-pleasing and internalized ableism [msg #168].
- **Ups and downs cannot be avoided, but pacing can interrupt the cycle**: the interest-driven, all-or-nothing nervous system makes swings inevitable, yet pacing systems spread energy more evenly and reduce "power outages" [msg #169].
- **The 8-hour day / 5-day week is a historical compromise, not a health norm**; weekly output is not a universal measure of health, and this matters for autism [msg #35].
- **Employment does not end burnout**: in the survey, burnout is the most common problem, many quit to protect themselves, and returning to work requires both manageable conditions and sufficient income [img #271, #272, #274].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Autistic burnout 101
Lena Foer's overview, offered as "началом разговора на тему" (the start of a conversation on the topic) rather than a complete treatment [msg #23].

**Definition.** Autistic burnout is "состояние эмоционального, физического и психологического истощения" (a state of emotional, physical and psychological exhaustion) that results from exposure to stress factors, may last from weeks to years, and involves heightened vulnerability to sensory stimuli, loss of skills and loss of the ability to function at the previous level [msg #23].

**What causes it (5 causes):**
1. Trying to meet high, very draining expectations — for example keeping a full-time job or studying at university [msg #23].
2. Having to mask a lot — suppressing autistic traits and mimicking the people around you: giving up stimming, wearing uncomfortable office clothes, being required to take part in small talk [msg #23].
3. Living in an environment that strongly mismatches your needs — for example working a shift at a summer camp, or living with sound sensitivity in a neighbourhood full of clubs and bars [msg #23].
4. Having to interact with people more than is comfortable for you, over a prolonged period [msg #23].
5. Changed life circumstances — a transition to autonomy, a new need to handle many more tasks, and similar shifts [msg #23].

**What raises the likelihood of burning out (4 risk factors):**
1. Gaslighting and invalidation — when, in response to talk of tiredness, you hear something like "все так работают, и никто не жалуется" (everyone works like this and nobody complains) [msg #23].
2. An environment where you cannot set boundaries, decline part of the load or ask for help [msg #23].
3. Lack of support [msg #23].
4. Being unable to pause and step out of the stressful conditions [msg #23].

**Symptoms (6 signs):**
1. Constant tiredness [msg #23].
2. Sensory stimuli affect your state more strongly than before [msg #23].
3. Executive dysfunction — problems carrying out everyday and any other tasks, from brushing teeth to school homework [msg #23].
4. What used to come easily is now hard [msg #23].
5. Health problems appear [msg #23].
6. Many strong emotions that are hard to regulate — or, the opposite, emotional numbness [msg #23].

Often there is a sense that the state affects every area of life, alongside anxiety that it will never end [msg #23].

**Duration.** From weeks — some say days, others say at least a couple of months — up to years [msg #23].

**How to recover (5 strategies):**
1. Use support: ask for help where you need it, spend time with people who understand and accept you and with whom you do not need to mask [msg #23].
2. Meet your stimming needs and give yourself time for special interests [msg #23].
3. Reduce load: less work and fewer other tasks, and less sensory load through a suitable sensory diet ("подходящей сенсорной диеты") [msg #23].
4. Mask less and allow yourself to do things, communicate and live in ways that feel comfortable — for example replacing in-person meetings with messenger chat, dressing for comfort first, and investing less "отчаянного желания соответствовать" (desperate desire to fit in) into small talk [msg #23].
5. Protect yourself: build boundaries, do not agree to everything you are asked, give yourself breaks and take holidays [msg #23].

**Re-entry and self-knowledge.** When you are ready, return to everyday activities gradually; do not rush yourself and approach recovery with self-compassion. Studying yourself is another important part of the process: if you know what burnout looks like for you, what to expect from it and what helps you recover, it is easier to help yourself [msg #23].

### The boom-or-bust cycle
Katya Klimochkina describes the cycle of «подъема» (rise) and «спада» (fall) as arising when, on "good days", we overwork and consequently become overtired, which leads to a lack of energy and motivation. It might seem you just need to recover, but after overworking it is not so easy to get back up [msg #167].

1. **Boom phase («фаза бума»).** On good days, when we are "full of energy" and enjoy doing things, we try to catch up on what we missed. We can overdo it, ignoring body signals and failing to notice that we have gone beyond our limits. During this phase we may experience heightened focus, creativity and productivity, be in flow and concentrated on one task or project, which raises output and achievement; it is often accompanied by absorption, enthusiasm and motivation. It happens especially often when we find a new special interest or an inspiring new project [msg #167].
2. **Bust phase (фаза спада).** After long periods of intense concentration and effort, we eventually enter the bust: fatigue, sensory overload, mental exhaustion and executive-functioning difficulties, leading to lower productivity, lower motivation and a general sense of being crushed [msg #167].
3. **Triggers and variability.** Many things can trigger the cycle — external demands, stressors, changes in routine, sensory overload; "буквально все" (literally anything). The cycle differs between people in length and intensity [msg #167].

**What is wrong with boom-or-bust (3 harms):**
1. When we constantly exceed our body's limits, recovery takes longer than usual and emotional burnout intensifies [msg #167].
2. Mood swings like a roller coaster: on good days we feel optimistic, positive and accomplished; on bust days depression, disappointment and melancholy roll in [msg #167].
3. Each pass through the cycle takes longer to return to baseline, so we eventually end up exhausted and emotionally burnt out [msg #167].

### Four vulnerability factors for boom-or-bust
Klimochkina lists traits that make autistic and ADHD people prone to the cycle [msg #168]:
1. **Hyperfocus + hyperfixation.** In hyperfocus time passes us by; being in flow is so pleasant that we forget to drink, eat or go to bed. One night of hyperfocus can disrupt the sleep schedule for a week [msg #168].
2. **Interoception problems.** Hidden difficulties perceiving body signals mean we may miss early signs of stress or fatigue and stop obeying our body's signals. This makes us more vulnerable to overstrain, because we may not realize we are tired or stressed until we are on the edge of catastrophic exhaustion [msg #168].
3. **People-pleasing («Пипл плизерство»).** A person may prefer overexertion to the risk of disappointing someone. It relates to masking but goes beyond it: "Если пообещал - доделаю" (if I promised, I'll finish), even at the cost of one's own comfort or sleep — hence overwork. The author's example: asking for second helpings ("очень вкусно, можно добавки?" — "very tasty, may I have more?") of food so salty it makes your jaw cramp, then suffering heartburn [msg #168].
4. **Ableism («Эйблизм»).** We often hold ourselves to neurotypical standards "recognized by society", then shame ourselves for failing to meet unrealistic expectations; this makes us strain, often followed by emotional burnout. Ableism (from English "able") is defined as a type of discrimination in which people without health problems are treated as the "norm" and as superior to people with health problems or particularities. This creates stigma that forces neurodivergent people to mask and adapt to society — a very energy-costly activity that leads to burnout. The author greets everyone not ready to fit the "society standard" of working 5/2 with an 8-hour day while also keeping the home clean, running and learning languages [msg #168].

### Pacing systems (systems of energy distribution)
**Starting point.** Given the autistic and ADHD nervous system, which is "управляется" интересом (driven by interest), prone to all-or-nothing reactions and to hyperfocus, ups and downs cannot be avoided; Klimochkina says to accept this fact without despairing. There is no panacea, but there are ways to get off the roller coaster: resisting ableism, setting effective boundaries with others, and prioritizing both tasks and recovery. By introducing pacing strategies we intervene in the cycle and break it, ensuring a more even distribution of energy during activity, which reduces the chance of "перебоев энергоснабжения" (power outages) [msg #169].

**What they are.** Pacing systems are often used by people with chronic pain, chronic fatigue or other chronic somatic illnesses — when not every day is sunny and lets you be productive. When your plans for the day are governed by your condition, the risk of overdoing it on a "good day" is high, so pacing strategies are a "must have". A pacing system is essentially any system that helps you stably monitor and manage energy and spend your strength in a balanced way [msg #169].

**Why they matter.** They help "выровнять наши энергетические затраты, чтобы мы не застревали в цикле выгорания" (even out our energy expenditure so we don't get stuck in the burnout cycle). The goal is a more sustainable life in which you feel more confident in your capacities and have more freedom of action in daily activities, rather than feeling out of control; you get predictability and a plan, which the author calls important [msg #169].

The three systems follow.

### Energy accounting («Бухгалтерия энергии»)
1. **Metaphor.** Treat energy like the income-and-expenses tracking in a banking app — the circle that shows your salary arriving and your farewell to it [msg #169].
2. **Assign costs and gains.** Each activity gets an energy value (withdrawal or deposit), and you balance income and spending against your balance on the "счету сил" (energy account) [msg #169].
3. **Worked example.** Start with 100 energy dollars. You woke up not well rested but have to get up (−15$). Vacuuming the floor (estimated −20$). Repotting plants (−10$ for the dirt, +5$ for the aesthetics). Sitting in traffic (−30$). Meeting friends in a noisy bar (−30$ and +15$). Total: 15$ left — "ну такое" (so-so); the plans for the day probably need reworking [msg #169].
4. **Battery variant.** If the financial metaphor does not suit you, a phone battery works the same way: you wake up at 31% charge — consider whether you will listen to music (−5%) or watch YouTube (−20%), and factor in when you will find a socket or power bank (+%) [msg #169].

Lesson of the example: an activity can both cost and give energy (repotting, a bar with friends), and summing a planned day in advance reveals when it will leave too little in reserve [msg #169].

### Spoon theory («Теория ложек»)
Similar to energy accounting but different: you start the day with a limited number of "spoons" representing your amount of energy — "Сколько есть - столько есть" (you have what you have). Activities deplete these spoons, so you must take into account how many spoons you have per day and distribute them sensibly among tasks [msg #169]. The difference from energy accounting, as presented, is that spoon theory counts only a fixed daily supply being spent, while energy accounting also books restorative "income" [msg #169].

### Traffic-light system («Светофор»)
Classify activities into three colours [msg #169]:
- **Red light** — tasks requiring large energy expenditure.
- **Yellow light** — activities of medium or neutral intensity.
- **Green light** — restorative, recharging activities.

How to use it [msg #169]:
1. a) Identify your red-light activities and place them in the right slots in your routine.
2. b) Alternate yellow- and green-light activities.
3. c) Carefully watch how many red-light activities you do during a day or week.

## Detailed insights

### The 40-hour week is not a norm
Lena Foer's "friendly reminder": an eight-hour working day and five-day week is NOT [msg #35]:
1. the optimal regime for a human;
2. average human productivity;
3. a norm to strive for;
4. a healthy way of living;
5. something that must suit you if nothing is wrong with you.

Forty hours a week is just what workers and capitalists managed to agree on at some distant point in history. There are people who work 12 hours a day and are fine; there are people who work 2 hours a day and are also fine and healthy — "не надо тыкать в них палочкой" (no need to poke them with a stick). People are built differently, and weekly output is not a universal measure of health, even if someone thinks so. Foer adds that although it might seem unrelated to autism, autism is very much relevant here [msg #35]. This links to the ableism factor above, where the 5/2, 8-hour standard is named as an unrealistic societal expectation [msg #168].

### Survey: work resilience of autistic and ADHD people (Lena Movshina × Neurodivergent Colleagues)
A forwarded card series presents the main results of a study titled "Work resilience of autistic and ADHD people" (Трудовая устойчивость аутичных и СДВГ-людей, per the cover card), by Lena Movshina with the project Нейроотличные коллеги (Neurodivergent Colleagues) [img #268].

**About the study.** Online survey of 1027 Russian-speaking autistic and ADHD people, all with at least one experience of paid work; answers collected May–July 2025. The sample has notably more women — 80.9%; ages are more evenly spread, from 18 to 59. Most participants came from neurodivergent communities, and the autistic respondents are people with low support needs. The authors caution that the results cannot be transferred to the whole neurodivergent community, but are still significant for representing part of the experience [img #269].

**Unemployment.** 25.9% of respondents were without work — a high share, the authors stress, given that the sample includes only people with paid-work experience and low support needs. Among respondents without completed higher education the unemployment share is twice as high: 35.9% versus 17.5% among university graduates. The authors' interpretation: people with different education levels had access to different fields of work and probably different opportunities to adapt the work environment to their needs [img #270].

**Burnout is the most widespread problem.** Symptoms of neurodivergent burnout were reported by: 74.7% of unemployed respondents (at their last job); 69.1% of working respondents (at their previous job); 48.9% of working respondents (at their current job). The share of burnt-out respondents falls toward the current job, but the problem does not disappear with employment: "человек может продолжать работать, но платить за это хроническим истощением" (a person can keep working but pay for it with chronic exhaustion) [img #271].

**Returning requires not just any job.** Among non-working respondents: 75% want to influence the volume of tasks; 72.5% consider stress reduction important; 70.4% need flexible working hours; 69% say pay sufficient to support themselves matters; 48.9% want to be able to talk about their autism and ADHD without a negative reaction. Conclusion: "Гибкость не заменяет финансовую устойчивость" (flexibility does not replace financial stability) — to return to work and not burn out again, people simultaneously need manageable conditions and sufficient income [img #272].

**Over 80% of working respondents changed occupation.** On their current job, 13.5% do the same as at their previous one; 44.4% changed occupation completely; 38.4% changed it partially. For neurodivergent people a non-linear career may be not "inconsistency" but a way to find work that better matches their needs and capacities. But a non-linear career carries specific financial risks, the need to change career positioning, and hiring prejudice [img #273].

**Quitting on one's own is not just a job change.** Quit voluntarily: 60.2% of unemployed respondents (from their last job) and 68.6% of working respondents (from their previous job). The authors call this an important signal for teams and managers: behind such a decision may lie months of overload, sensory and social exhaustion, conflicts, unclear tasks and inability to change conditions. "Часто увольнение становится последним доступным способом защитить себя" (often quitting becomes the last available way to protect oneself) [img #274].

**Industries.** The sample represents 160+ professions: 23.7% education, healthcare and helping people; 22% IT (development, tech support, product management); 14.1% digital (design, marketing, media); 13.4% trade, service, services; 8.3% physical labour, logistics, agriculture. The authors stress this is not a list of industries "suitable" for neurodivergent people: within one field, schedule, workload, communication, autonomy and availability of adaptations can differ greatly [img #275].

**Full report.** The cards show only a small part of the results. Per the final card, the full report has eight sections on employment, industries, burnout, quitting, money and returning to work; the navigation page pictured lists them as: why the study is needed and how it was run; who took part; working and unemployed — basic comparison; which industries autistic/ADHD people work in; difficulties at work; reasons and features of quitting; money, financial stability and support; returning to work and adaptation at the current job. Each section opens with a short summary and a reading-time estimate (whole report estimated at 55–85 minutes), and readers may build an individual route — read in parts, combine summaries with full texts, or use a browser text-to-speech. A continuation is announced for winter 2027 covering values and their influence on work, the influence of the "good employee" norm and self-perception as an employee, monetizing special interests as a career strategy, and general conclusions and recommendations for neurodivergent people and employers [img #277, #268]. ## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- Lena Foer presents her burnout overview as only the start of a conversation on a large topic, not a complete account [msg #23].
- Duration estimates vary by who you ask: days, at least a couple of months, or weeks to years [msg #23].
- Klimochkina is explicit that there is no panacea and that ups and downs cannot be fully avoided; pacing reduces, not eliminates, the swings — though in the same post she also says pacing strategies "ломаем его полностью" (break it completely). (!) CONTRADICTION: "cannot avoid ups and downs" vs "break the cycle completely" within msg #169 [msg #169].
- Survey limits stated by its authors: 80.9% women, mostly recruited from neurodivergent communities, autistic respondents with low support needs; results cannot be generalized to the whole neurodivergent community [img #269]. The education–unemployment link is interpreted with a hedge ("вероятно", probably) [img #270]. The industry breakdown is explicitly not a list of "suitable" fields [img #275].
- The forwarded cards are a summary only; the full report and its winter-2027 continuation were not part of the source, so their findings are not available here [img #277].

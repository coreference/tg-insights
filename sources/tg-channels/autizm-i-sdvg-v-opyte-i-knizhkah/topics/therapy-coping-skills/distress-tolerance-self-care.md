# Distress tolerance and self-care skills for neurodivergent people

## What this covers

This topic gathers two kinds of material. The first is crisis-survival skills from dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT), written by the channel author Lena Foer after a terrorist attack (the Crocus City Hall attack in March 2024): when avoiding emotions is legitimate, the ПЕРЕЖИТЬ acronym of distraction methods, the STOP skill for stopping yourself before a harmful "comforting" behaviour, and a list of ways to take care of yourself when you feel bad [msg #66, #67, #68]. The second is a card set by the CBT therapist Nastasya Krysko, written for the channel, on how neurodivergent people can take care of themselves: self-acceptance, a support team, professional help, a structured environment, time and task management, technology, and kindness to the body [msg #110, #120].

Why it matters, according to the source: some emotional pain is so intense that it blocks effective action, and some ways we calm ourselves (doomscrolling, alcohol) only look helpful while actually making things worse; and for neurodivergent people self-support strategies improve adaptation, quality of life and functioning in different areas of life [msg #66, #68, #110].

## Core ideas

- **Avoiding emotions is not always bad.** Therapists repeat that emotion avoidance is bad so often it became a cliché, and the author says that this is not true: avoidance is usually ineffective because it leads to more suffering, but sometimes it is the best thing we can do. The distraction methods are for surviving peak emotions, not for everyday use. Details in *Two criteria for when avoiding emotions is useful* and *ПЕРЕЖИТЬ* [msg #66].
- **Some coping only looks helpful.** Long walks and favourite music help; doomscrolling and alcohol relieve you in the moment but maintain or worsen your state overall. The STOP skill is a way to catch yourself and choose something healthier [msg #68].
- **Self-care when you are in pain starts with the basics** (sleep, food, water, walks, rest, hygiene) and with keeping your usual routines, because they ground you and create stability; do not trust the inner voice saying it is "not the time" for kindness to yourself [msg #67].
- **Self-care for neurodivergent people is universal in concept but individual in practice.** Krysko's position: the concept of self-care is common to everyone, but how we build it into life differs, so every tip should be adapted; self-care is a process of exploring yourself and finding what fits you [msg #110, #120].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Two criteria for when avoiding emotions is useful
Lena Foer gives two situations in which avoiding (distracting from) your emotions is useful [msg #66]:
1. **When the emotional pain is unbearable and could stop you from acting effectively where action is necessary.** Here distraction protects your ability to function [msg #66].
2. **When the emotional pain is unbearable and the situation causing it is outside your control and cannot be resolved immediately.** The author states that the tragedy that had just happened (the terrorist attack) falls under criterion 2 [msg #66].

Caveat from the author: "Если вы чувствуете, что способны справляться со своими эмоциями, лучше позволить себе их чувствовать и переживать" (If you feel able to cope with your emotions, it is better to let yourself feel and experience them). The techniques that follow are for situations when you feel you cannot do that [msg #66].

### ПЕРЕЖИТЬ — DBT distraction skills for emotional distress
Purpose: a mnemonic from DBT ("аббревиатура ПЕРЕЖИТь", the acronym "ПЕРЕЖИТЬ", literally "to live through") to remember ways of distracting yourself from emotional suffering when emotions feel crushing. Use it only for peak emotions in the situations defined above [msg #66].

1. **П — "помогите кому-нибудь" (help someone).** Help someone else who is also suffering from what is happening. The author calls it a good decision from every angle and gives three reasons: (a) the focus of attention switches very effectively, because most of us are strongly tuned to react to other people's pain; (b) when we help, we feel like our better selves and generally better; (c) helping can give more meaning to your own emotions, because through them you empathise with another person [msg #66].
2. **Е — "если сравнить" (if you compare).** Comparison with something worse. The author notes the saying that in times like these people watch either comedies to distract themselves or horror films to realise things are still not that bad as long as zombies have not crawled out of the ground; this method is the second one. Examples: rewatch *Alien* and be glad you are not on a spaceship with an alien monster; recall the frightening things you have already gone through and survived. "Звучит сомнительно, но опыт показывает, что это работает" (It sounds doubtful, but experience shows it works) [msg #66].
3. **Р — "используйте Различные эмоции" (use different emotions).** To switch away from emotions that seem able to crush you, you need other strong emotional stimuli — something able to absorb you completely. Ask yourself what evokes strong emotions in you: favourite novels, computer games, night raves, anything else. Remember what grips you most and is hardest to tear yourself away from, and use exactly that [msg #66].
4. **Е — "есть другие действия" (there are other activities).** Some people are very good at escaping into activity when they cannot cope with feelings; the author says that in such cases she can, for example, clean her flat until it shines in about three hours. Look for an activity that is easy for you to sink into. The recommendation is to search in two categories: (1) something that gives pleasure — for example self-care/grooming procedures, if they happen to be pleasurable for you; (2) something in which you feel your mastery. It does not have to be special: some people feel they are awesome at washing dishes or walking the dog [msg #66].
5. **Ж — "постройте Железную стену" (build an iron wall).** Blocking feelings you cannot bear right now. Scarlett O'Hara's line "Я подумаю об этом завтра" (I'll think about it tomorrow) is described as such a blocking method. You can do as she did, imagine a wall, go in your imagination to "внутреннюю Монголию" (Inner Mongolia), or simply tell yourself "стоп, сюда мы не думаем" (stop, we don't think in this direction) [msg #66].
6. **И — "используйте ощущения" (use sensations).** Intense physical sensations pull attention away even from strong emotions. The author cites Oscar Wilde's phrase "лечите душу ощущениями, а ощущения пусть лечит душа" (cure the soul by means of the senses, and the senses by means of the soul). A good head-on way to reduce the intensity of an emotion: take an ice cube from the fridge, hold it in your hand until it melts. Other options: tastes — sour, spicy, bitter; very intense music [msg #66].
7. **Т — "течение мыслей перенаправьте" (redirect the flow of thoughts).** Deliberately switch from the thoughts that cause crisis emotions to anything else: count passers-by on the street, solve equations in your head, choose the ideal dog breed — the source ends this list with "и так далее" (and so on) [msg #66].

Warning repeated by the author: these are methods for surviving peak emotions. "Если использовать их постоянно, они скорее всего навредят, чем принесут пользу" (If you use them constantly, they will most likely do more harm than good). But in certain hard cases only they can help [msg #66].

### СТОП — the DBT STOP skill
Purpose: a skill that helps you stop when you want to do something that may make you feel worse later, change your mind and choose something healthier instead. Context from the author: after terrible events we soothe ourselves in different ways; some help (long walks, favourite music), but some only look helpful — doomscrolling lowers anxiety in the moment but maintains it overall; alcohol distracts in the moment but in general only destabilises your state [msg #68].

1. **С — Стоп (Stop).** Literally stop and freeze — whether you are reading a Facebook news feed, about to do something impulsive, or have already started. The author warns this is a hard choice in the moment, because you are wound up, and running on (reading, shouting, anything) is easier than not running [msg #68].
2. **Т — Только шаг назад (Just a step back).** Step back physically, by leaving the room, or internally, for example by putting the laptop aside. Focus your attention on your breathing. Tell yourself you are taking a break — literally for a couple of minutes [msg #68].
3. **О — Осмотритесь (Look around).** During strong emotions the horizon of attention shrinks to a single point in space, so it can help just to look around, look out of the window, examine the objects around you. Think about what you are feeling. Try to describe only the facts, without judgement: what is happening, what others are doing and saying [msg #68].
4. **П — Попытайтесь действовать осознанно (Try to act mindfully).** Think about what would be useful for you at this moment. What would be effective? Which actions could improve or worsen the situation? Only after that return to action [msg #68].

### Self-care when you feel bad — 5 practices
Written by Lena Foer for people struggling after the attack [msg #67]:
1. **Start with basic needs.** Get enough sleep, eat at least three times a day, drink enough, take yourself for walks, rest, keep up hygiene. Simply attending to these things already grounds you and gives you back the ground under your feet. If it is hard, keep a checklist and enjoy the ticks, or literally give yourself gold stars for each thing done [msg #67].
2. **Don't neglect your usual rituals and routines** — they create a space of stability in a world that is shaking. If you always did morning exercises, keep doing them; if you always walked the dog in the park in the evening, go now too, even if it is harder. Think about which rituals could support you and try to find at least two [msg #67].
3. **Ask yourself, every time you remember, "что будет хорошо для меня сейчас?" (what would be good for me right now?).** Each time honestly try to find an answer — something you can change in your behaviour in the moment, even something very small. In behavioural science thoughts are considered part of behaviour, so "switching the focus of attention from a to b" also counts [msg #67].
4. **Return to the body.** Set a timer for a random interval; when it rings, quickly scan your body with attention and think how to make yourself more comfortable in the moment: does anything hurt? are you hungry? thirsty? do you want to change posture? relax your shoulders? [msg #67]
5. **Look at what makes you happy.** Listen to music, smell, touch and eat what makes you happy (ignore the eating point if you have an eating disorder). Try to come up with at least one idea for each of the five senses, and add these ideas to your calendar straight away [msg #67].

Closing rule: do not trust the voice that says now is not the time for this. "Нет времени, когда быть добрыми к себе было бы важнее" (There is no time when being kind to yourself would matter more) [msg #67].

### Self-care and neurodivergence — card set by Nastasya Krysko
CBT therapist Nastasya Krysko wrote the cards for the channel in two parts (part one on 2024-06-20, part two on 2024-06-26) [msg #110, #120].

**Introductory principles.**
- For all people, including neurodivergent people, it is important to find self-support strategies that can improve adaptation, raise quality of life and improve functioning in various areas [img #110].
- Each point can and should be adapted to yourself: the concept of self-care is common to all ("нотная" in the OCR, probably "общая"; see Caveats), but how we build it into our lives will differ [img #110].
- It is important to notice your needs and give time to restoring your strength. "Забота о себе это процесс, это исследование себя и поиск того, что подходит именно вам" (Self-care is a process — exploring yourself and searching for what suits you specifically). Tips and advice are reference points; our task is to lean on them and adapt them for ourselves [img #120].

**Block 1 — Self-acceptance and self-understanding.**
- Card 01: awareness of and emphasis on your personal strengths, together with a wise understanding of your weaknesses, helps reduce stress and supports a kind attitude toward yourself [img #111].
- Card 02 (as labelled in the set): taking part in support groups or communities lets you exchange experience, get emotional support or learn tips that might suit you [img #112]. (!) UNCLEAR: this card carries the same text as the support-team card 02 [img #122]; it may be a layout duplicate in the original set.

**Block 2 — Support team.**
- Card 01: social support from people who understand and accept you — possibly also people who understand neurodivergence — improves your psycho-emotional state and helps develop communication skills [img #121].
- Card 02: participating in support groups or communities lets you share experience, receive emotional support or pick up advice that may suit you [img #122].

**Block 3 — Professional help.**
- Card 01: consultations with psychologists or coaches who specialise in neurodivergence help develop effective strategies of self-management and adaptation [img #123].
- Card 02: medical and therapeutic help may be necessary to treat co-occurring conditions and improve overall health [img #124].

**Block 4 — Creating a structured environment.**
- Card 01: organising your workspace and creating a clear daily schedule minimise distracting factors and improve cognitive functioning [img #115].
- Card 02: stable, predictable routine actions and habits reduce anxiety and improve general wellbeing — for example morning self-care practices, evening rituals before sleep, having breaks [img #116].

**Block 5 — Time and task management.**
- Card 01: behavioural strategies such as making task lists or using the Pomodoro technique help structure activity and improve time management. Krysko acknowledges this skill can be difficult and will need to be learned, "но именно он будет помогать в будущем чувствовать себя лучше" (but it is exactly this skill that will help you feel better in the future) [img #113].
- Card 02: splitting large tasks into smaller stages ("baby steps") improves concentration and reduces the feeling of being overloaded. Mechanism: anxiety and overload in thoughts about what we have to do maintain procrastination and low mood, which lowers motivation to do anything — and it is precisely starting to do something that helps get out of this trap [img #114].

**Block 6 — Technology and self-care.**
- Card 01: apps for organising, planning and self-care can help us not forget — and not "give up on" ("не забывать и не забивать") — small but important actions. There is no single app that suits everyone; choose one you like for its design, simplicity of use and accessibility [img #125].
- Card 02: special devices — noise-cancelling headphones, room lighting/backlighting, smart-home-type programs — help reduce exposure to distracting factors; again, this is all individual [img #126].

**Block 7 — Kindness to your body.**
Sleep, food and some physical activity matter for all of us; they are a kind of base of self-care. Physical activity (any kind that suits you), sufficient and varied nutrition and regular rest — meaning breaks in every day, days off, and holidays — are the foundation of feeling well. Krysko notes it sounds simple but of course it can be hard to keep up [img #127].

## Tools and resources mentioned

- **Ice cube** — held in the hand until it melts, a direct way to lower emotional intensity through sensation (ПЕРЕЖИТЬ, "И") [msg #66].
- **Pomodoro technique** — recommended by Krysko as a behavioural time-management strategy alongside task lists [img #113].
- **Organisation, planning and self-care apps** — no universal app; pick by design, ease of use and accessibility [img #125].
- **Noise-cancelling headphones, room lighting, smart-home programs** — to reduce distractions, chosen individually [img #126].
- **Nastasya Krysko's Telegram channel** (t.me/nastasya_kr) — a channel on self-kindness by the CBT therapist; Lena Foer calls it "свой (отличный!) канал" (her (excellent!) channel) [msg #110].
- **Checklist / gold stars** — for tracking basic needs when they are hard to keep up [msg #67].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The ПЕРЕЖИТЬ methods are explicitly for peak emotions and for the two avoidance criteria; used constantly they are likely to harm more than help, and if you can bear your emotions it is better to feel them [msg #66].
- The "eat what makes you happy" suggestion should be ignored by people with an eating disorder [msg #67].
- (!) UNCLEAR: card #112 is labelled as card 02 of the self-acceptance and self-understanding block, but its text is identical to the support-team card 02 [img #122]; the source does not explain whether this is intentional [img #112].
- (!) UNCLEAR: the OCR of the intro card reads "концепция заботы о себе в целом нотная для всех" — "нотная" is likely an OCR error (probably "общая", common); the meaning "the concept is common for everyone, the way it is built into life differs" follows from the sentence [img #110].
- Krysko repeatedly hedges that everything is individual (devices, apps) and that tips are reference points to adapt, not prescriptions [img #120, #125, #126].
- The "if you compare" method is self-described as sounding doubtful, supported by "experience" rather than cited evidence [msg #66].

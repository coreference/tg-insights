# Human-machine interfaces and AI in science

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's posts on technologies that connect human bodies and minds to machines, and on AI used as an instrument of science. It covers wearables that read the face (Dolami VR mask) and silent inner speech (MIT AlterEgo), an AI that temporarily moves part of the user's body (Human Operator), round-the-clock driverless robotaxis, machine learning that designs new proteins and discovers antibiotics, a University of Tokyo robotics approach built on Jean Piaget's developmental theory, and William Gibson's novel/series The Peripheral as fiction about neural interfaces. The source frames these as signs of a human-machine future: devices that become part of everyday life, help patients without voice or movement, speed up drug discovery, and could be used in rehabilitation [msg #118, #156, #172, #182, #192, #206, #566, #734].

## Core ideas

- **Generative AI can design matter, not only pictures.** An architecture similar to DALL-E 2 was adapted to predict protein amino-acid sequences that reach a given structure, opening the way to proteins that do not exist in nature and to low-carbon materials; see *Generative protein design* [msg #172].
- **AI compresses drug discovery.** A neural network screened 7000 compounds, flagged 240, and lab testing yielded the new selective antibiotic abaucin; see *AI-guided antibiotic discovery* [msg #182].
- **Interfaces are moving to the face, inner speech and the body itself.** A mask carries facial expressions into VR, AlterEgo reads the neural signals of internally articulated words, and Human Operator lets AI move part of a person's body to practise a skill [msg #156, #192, #734].
- **Autonomous vehicles crossed a regulatory threshold.** San Francisco allowed Waymo and Cruise robotaxis to run 24/7 with no driver inside, conditional on preventing incidents [msg #206].
- **Developmental psychology can inform robot design.** A Tokyo lab uses Piaget's assimilation, accommodation, genetic epistemology and structuralism to model how a robot's internal functions should cohere and adapt; see *Piaget-inspired robotics* [msg #566, #567, #568, #569].
- **Fiction anticipates the neural interface.** The Peripheral imagines a screenless, controller-less VR headset with a neural interface that connects two real worlds [msg #118].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Generative vs discriminative models (as applied to protein design)
Purpose: the channel explains why a DALL-E-type model could be used to create proteins. Two kinds of models are distinguished [msg #172]:
1. **Generative model** (генеративная/порождающая модель) — a model that can generate new objects from the data it has received. DALL-E 2 is the example: it creates realistic images from user prompts written in natural language [msg #172].
2. **Discriminative model** (дискриминативная) — a model that only tells different objects apart; the channel's example is distinguishing cats from dogs (вроде кошечек от собачек, *like kitties from doggies*) [msg #172].
The researchers kept the generative architecture but swapped the task: instead of producing images from text prompts, the adapted model predicts amino-acid sequences of proteins that would achieve specified structural targets [msg #172].

### Generative protein design (MIT, MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab, Tufts)
What it is: machine-learning algorithms, developed by researchers at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, the MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab and Tufts University, that generate proteins with a predefined structure [msg #172].
How it works, in the order the source describes:
1. Start from a model architecturally similar to the one used in systems like DALL-E 2 (a generative model) [msg #172].
2. Replace the input/output: the goal is no longer an image from a natural-language prompt but a structural goal for a protein [msg #172].
3. The model predicts amino-acid sequences that would reach those structural goals — including proteins that do not exist in nature [msg #172].
Why it matters (the channel's heading «Чем это классно?», *Why is this cool?*): it would allow materials with desired mechanical properties such as stiffness or elasticity to be created; such protein materials could potentially replace materials made from oil or ceramics while leaving a much smaller carbon footprint [msg #172].
Outside view: Philip LeDuc, professor of mechanical engineering at Carnegie Mellon University, who watched the work from outside, said: «Эта конкретная работа увлекательна, потому что она в основном исследует создание белков, которые не существуют, и то, какими будут их характеристики с точки зрения механики» (*This particular work is fascinating because it mostly explores the creation of proteins that do not exist and what their characteristics will be from the point of view of mechanics*) [msg #172].
Author's extension: HGUM writes that she is thinking about how these technologies could be applied to create genome-correction proteins in the area of transgenerational epigenetic inheritance (TGI), and ends with «Exciting!». This is the author's speculation, not a result of the study [msg #172].

### AI-guided antibiotic discovery (abaucin)
Problem: Acinetobacter baumannii is a pathogen described as untreatable, causing pneumonia, meningitis and other serious infections; the MIT News headline adds that it lurks in many hospital settings and is drug-resistant [msg #182, img #182].
Pipeline as reported:
1. **Screen** — a neural network analysed 7000 drug compounds for their potential to suppress the growth of A. baumannii [msg #182].
2. **Shortlist** — the algorithm identified 240 antibacterial molecules [msg #182].
3. **Test in the lab** — those 240 molecules were tested experimentally [msg #182].
4. **Result** — scientists obtained a new compound, abaucin (абауцин), which kills the target bacterium selectively (точечно, *pinpoint*) and does not affect other bacteria [msg #182].
5. **Outcome claimed** — the forwarded post says abaucin treats drug-resistant infections «у мышей и людей» (*in mice and humans*); the attached MIT headline only says the compound could combat drug-resistant infections. See Caveats [msg #182, img #182].
The forwarding channel's verdict: «Поиск антибиотиков ещё никогда не был таким быстрым» (*The search for antibiotics has never been this fast*), followed by «наблюдаем дальше» (*we keep watching*) [msg #182].

### Piaget-inspired robotics (Intelligent Systems and Informatics Lab, University of Tokyo)
Context: at her first scientific conference, organised in Serbia by the Jean Piaget Society for the Study of Knowledge and Development, the author's favourite talk came from the Intelligent Systems and Informatics Lab of the University of Tokyo. The speaker explained how, in robotics, they apply Piaget's ideas of assimilation and accommodation, genetic epistemology, and structuralism (the author notes in passing that Piaget later turned from structuralism toward functionalism). The author promised to explain all of this later in simple and detailed form; the post itself gives the slides as the overview [msg #566]. The four slides form a progression:

1. **Piaget as a dynamic, biological, mathematical Kant (slide 3).** The speaker's framing: «Piaget is a Dinamic, Biological, Mathematical Kant» [sic], rendered by the author in Russian as «Пиаже — это Кант от биологии, математики и динамических систем» (*Piaget is the Kant of biology, mathematics and dynamical systems*). The slide quotes two Piaget texts. From *Genetic Epistemology* (1971): in sensory-motor intelligence there is already a certain logic of inclusion, a logic of orderings and a logic of correspondence, which Piaget maintains are the foundations of logical-mathematical structures; they are not yet operations but are the beginnings of what will become operations, and they already contain the beginnings of two essential characteristics of operations — a form of conservation and a form of reversibility (highlighted on the slide). From *Structuralism* (1968): even at this stage the child's behaviour may be called intelligent, but this intelligence is entirely sensori-motor, does not involve representation, and is essentially tied to action and coordinations of action (highlighted); here one meets the advent of logic, though limited in two essential respects. The highlighted passages put the emphasis on logic and intelligence beginning in action and its coordination, before representation; (!) INCOMPLETE: how the lab turns this into a robot design is not spelled out [msg #566, img #566].
2. **Assimilation: schema as mathematical (slide 12).** The slide shows Piaget's book *Adaptation and Intelligence: Organic Selection and Phenocopy* and a passage stating that empirical abstraction never operates by itself: to derive information from an object, even information that can only come from that object, an assimilatory apparatus is indispensable. «This assimilatory apparatus is of a mathematical» nature: it involves relating one or more classes (or schemes of action at the sensory-motor level, which are already a kind of practical concept), correspondences, functions, identities, differences or equivalences. In short, a whole range of instruments is needed for the very reading of experience, independent of later interpretations; these recording instruments make empirical abstraction possible but are not themselves derived from it. The slide title equates Piaget's assimilatory schema with a mathematical structure; (!) INCOMPLETE: how it is implemented in the robot is not explained [msg #567, img #567].
3. **Structuralism: irreducible wholeness (slide 15).** The slide «Structuralism: Irreducible Wholeness» shows a two-level model from the paper *Mental causation in a physical world: A self-causation model of downward causation* by Yoshiyuki Ohmura and Yasuo Kuniyoshi. Components:
   - *External causes x* enter the system [img #568].
   - *Macro level: neural network modules* — two supervenient functions f1 and f2 [img #568].
   - *Axiom, macro-level equation* — f1·f2 = f2·f1 (applying the two functions in either order should give the same result) [img #568].
   - *Supervenient macro-level feedback error* — for all x, the squared difference |f1·f2(x) − f2·f1(x)|² [img #568].
   - *Micro level: neurons, synapses* — the macro modules supervene on physical neurons and synapses [img #568].
   - *Control* — the macro-level feedback error is sent down to the micro level, which adjusts synaptic weights to reduce the feedback error [img #568].
   As drawn, the error defined at the whole-system (macro) level is fed back to change the parts (synaptic weights) — the downward causation of the paper's title — and the whole box is labelled Observation at macro level; the slide title presents this as structuralism's irreducible wholeness [msg #568, img #568].
4. **Toward accommodation.** The final slide «Toward Accommodation» shows the same input X feeding two paths: f1 then f2, and f2 then f1; the outputs are compared through the error |f1·f2(x) − f2·f1(x)|². The dashed box around the two paths is labelled *Internal Behaviour*; a loop from the error back to X is labelled *External Behaviour*. A list on the left names three properties: 1) Shape, 2) Color, 3) Size?? — with the question marks on the slide itself. (!) ## Detailed insights

### Wearables that read the face and silent speech
- **Dolami smart VR mask.** Framed as yet another replacement for Zuckerberg's legless and faceless digital avatars. The Japanese startup Dolami developed a smart mask that reads the user's facial expressions and transmits them to a VR avatar, using sensors built into the inside of the mask. Reported specs: expression transfer accuracy above 95% and latency of only 200 milliseconds; planned to go on sale at the end of 2023 for 90 dollars. The forwarding channel's claim: in VR your facial expressions will now appear exactly on your digital avatar [msg #156].
- **MIT AlterEgo.** MIT researchers presented AlterEgo, a device for communicating by thought: it reads neural signals associated with the internal articulation of words and accurately recognises what the person wants to say. Patients with multiple sclerosis and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis can use it without voice or movement anywhere — from counting purchases in a shop to choosing TV channels. Verdict of the forwarding channel: «Совершенно футуристическая штука!» (*A completely futuristic thing!*) [msg #192].

### AI that moves the body: Human Operator and the HARD MODE hackathon
- **The hackathon.** MIT launched the HARD MODE hackathon, a marathon for creating smart objects — devices able to perceive the environment around them, learn, adapt and interact with people. Organisers were the Design Intelligence Lab of the architecture faculty and the AHA group of the MIT Media Lab (the author jokes about whether Morten Harket sits on the board). The task was to go beyond the usual understanding of AI: instead of yet another chatbot or app, build real devices that can be held, worn, installed in a space and made part of everyday life. 200 specialists — engineers, designers and researchers — had 48 hours to create tangible, functional prototypes offering a new view of how people communicate and think, learn and work, develop and play [msg #734].
- **The winner, Human Operator.** It lets a person hand control of part of their body to AI for a short time, and during that time mechanically practise a skill the person cannot yet perform for one reason or another. The author's reaction: «Жутко (и) интересно!» (*Creepy (and) interesting!*) [msg #734].
- **Caveat on the demo.** In the project video, everything after 0:28 shows only hypothetical goals the engineers would like to reach, not achieved results [msg #734].
- **Author's opinion on use.** «На мой взгляд, эта технология, безусловно, была бы полезна в реабилитации.» (*In my view, this technology would certainly be useful in rehabilitation.*) She closes by asking readers what they would do with such capabilities [msg #734].

### Robotaxis
Waymo and Cruise received permission to operate driverless robotaxis (no driver inside) around the clock, which the forwarding channel calls a turning point for the self-driving industry. The decision came after a six-hour hearing and applies so far only to San Francisco. Before, autonomous taxis ran only at limited times and in specific locations; now they may drive across the whole city 24/7. Condition: the companies must ensure incidents are not repeated, otherwise the authorities will cap the number of driverless cars in the city. The attached Washington Post headline (Trisha Thadani and Jeremy B. Merrill, 10 August 2023) states that California opened the floodgates for self-driving cars after a state regulatory board ruling and calls it a pivotal moment for the industry. The forwarding channel jokingly predicts more protests in which people put traffic cones on robotaxi hoods [msg #206, img #206].

### Observations from the Piaget conference
The author reports that the conference had many scientists from Italy, France and Japan and, unexpectedly for her, Turkey (almost a third of the works); the programme consisted of talks, posters and discussions, with each theme made up of many 15-minute talks. She found the charm of the Tokyo talk not only in the depth of the approach but in how sweetly and naively the speaker put the Kant comparison [msg #566].

### Fiction: The Peripheral
The channel recommends the series The Peripheral, based on William Gibson's novel. The quoted Wiki synopsis: Flynne Fisher lives in provincial America, works in a 3D-print shop and plays VR games. At her brother Burton's request she agrees to test a new virtual-reality headset with a neural interface, with no screen and no controllers. The device transports her to a futuristic London at the end of the 21st century. Gradually Flynne realises the events are happening not in a game or simulation but in reality, and she must find out who connected the two worlds and why. The post is tagged as transhumanism and something to watch [msg #118].

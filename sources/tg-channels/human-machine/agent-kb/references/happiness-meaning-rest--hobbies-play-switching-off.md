# Hobbies, play and switching off

## What this covers

This topic gathers the author's posts on hobbies and rest: why she argues against shelving hobbies during a crisis, what a hobby is and whether it must counterbalance demanding work, whether "just resting" can replace a hobby, and why real recovery sometimes requires moving from focused to diffused brain mode. It also includes a conversation with a stranger who paints every day as her way of expressing herself, a recommendation of a "declaration of reader's rights" for guilt-free reading, and the lessons the author drew from a writing marathon about writing faster, together, and in parts. According to the author, play and recovery are what return balance and lightness and let a person get what they want "with small efforts" — so the contribution of hobbies is, for her, "priceless" [msg #122].

## Core ideas

- **Do not shelve hobbies in a crisis.** Even when resources are scarce and priorities shift, the author says plainly: "Ноуп. Найн. «Не делай так»" (*Nope. Nein. "Don't do that"*). In a crisis the resource loop reverses, so replenishment becomes a precondition for solving problems, not a reward after it (see *Crisis inversion of the resource loop*) [msg #122].
- **In balanced times, a hobby and work should not differ in kind.** Both should be voluntary, done for oneself and with inspiration; if work fails these criteria, the author would question whether the person is doing the right thing at all. The main remaining difference is pay [msg #128].
- **A counterweight activity is not universally needed.** Some people feel fine reading and watching videos in their own favourite field, paid or not [msg #128].
- **Recovery by changing activity is not enough for everyone.** Sometimes what is needed is a full switch from focused to diffused brain mode, which frees space to reassemble information and thought patterns [msg #128].
- **Hobby as a form of expression.** A daily creative practice can be an alternative language for feelings: "кто-то любит говорить, а я — нет, я люблю рисовать" (*some people like to talk, but not me — I like to draw*) [msg #126].
- **Read without guilt.** Avid, chaotic, multi-book reading is not shameful; readers have more rights than a perfectionist "cover-to-cover" society assumes [msg #143].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Important-or-not rule for anything you keep postponing
Purpose: the author's peacetime way of preventing procrastination, which she says is rooted in one of her values (she refers to an earlier post of hers on that value). Because of it, in normal life nothing holds her back from doing what she needs and wants to do [msg #122].
1. **If it is important** — allocate resources to it, and take its "шоу-стопперы" (*show-stoppers*, the blockers) apart "до основания" (*down to the foundation*) and eliminate them [msg #122].
2. **If it is unimportant** — put it on low priority, where it gets done "как-нибудь где-нибудь при удачном стечении обстоятельств" (*somehow, somewhere, if circumstances happen to align*). She adds: "И мне с этим ок" (*And I'm OK with that*) [msg #122].
Her summary line: "Если важно — чего ждать, если неважно — чего обсуждать?" (*If it's important — why wait; if it's unimportant — why discuss it?*) [msg #122].
Caveat from the author: crises "могут стать большим исключением" (*can become a big exception*) to this logic, for three reasons: (1) everything familiar has changed; (2) resources are regularly spent on basic comfort and on building a "new stability", and what is left is scraped together to put out fires — she remarks it is good when it is at least this way round and not the reverse; (3) it is not always clear what to do as the next step, and sometimes even right now [msg #122].

### What keeps people from hobbies in a crisis (three blockers)
In crisis periods, according to the author, the following can hold a person back from a hobby [msg #122]:
1. Simply a lack of resources — strength, time, money (the source adds "и пр.", *and other*) [msg #122].
2. The hobby's low priority — "ПРОСТО ВСЕГДА" (*JUST ALWAYS*), i.e. it never rises above other things [msg #122].
3. Sometimes beliefs such as «некогда развлекаться» (*no time for fun*) and «сейчас не до жиру» (*no time for luxuries now*, literally "not the time for fat") [msg #122].
Her answer to "should I then give up the hobby, put it on the back shelf?" is no [msg #122].

### Crisis inversion of the resource loop
The author's model of why hobbies matter more, not less, in a crisis [msg #122]:
- In a crisis "меняется принцип работы наших ресурсов" (*the way our resources work changes*): acceptance, empathy and support become more needed than solving problems as fast as possible — "хотя бы потому, что этого решения может просто не быть" (*if only because there may simply be no solution*) [msg #122].
- **Before:** "я решаю проблемы ➡ мне радостно и спокойно" (*I solve problems → I feel joyful and calm*) [msg #122].
- **Now:** "я решаю проблемы ⬅ мне радостно и спокойно" (*I solve problems ← I feel joyful and calm*) — the arrow reverses: joy and calm are what make problem-solving possible [msg #122].
- Why it matters: this knowledge helps leave the paradigm in which a person is "постоянно вымученно занят в ожидании святого будущего" (*constantly, painfully busy while waiting for a holy future*). Instead comes the understanding that nourishment is needed, through which one can make life fuller, move toward goals at a more measured pace and realise oneself despite changed circumstances [msg #122].
- By letting ourselves recover, reassemble and play, we do what returns "равновесие, лёгкость" (*balance, lightness*) and the ability to **receive** what is desired, interesting and important with **small efforts** — hence she sees the contribution of hobbies as priceless [msg #122].
- Her own example: what she lacks for her "local happiness" right now is snow and snowboarding [msg #122].

### Four criteria of a hobby
Starting from the dictionary definition (see *Definitions*), the author lists what the word "hobby" usually evokes — an activity that is [msg #128]:
1. **Voluntary** (добровольное) [msg #128].
2. **Done from time to time**, with some periodicity [msg #128].
3. **For oneself and for pleasure** (для себя и в удовольствие) [msg #128].
4. **Done with inspiration** (с вдохновением) [msg #128].
She deliberately rejects other common parameters — lightness, lack of a goal, cheapness, "unseriousness" — because they would not survive the criticism of devoted hobbyists ("заядлых ХОББИтов", a pun on hobbits), for example collectors or model builders; so she keeps only the four above [msg #128].

### Two-question analysis of "do I need a hobby?"
Triggered by a reader question: "Work hard — play hard? Нужно ли человеку хобби как противовес хардкорной работе? Или можно «просто отдыхать», без хобби?" (*Does a person need a hobby as a counterweight to hardcore work? Or can one "just rest", without a hobby?*). She splits it into two parts [msg #128]:

**a) Hobby as a counterweight to work.**
- Definition: a counterweight is something that compensates for the "burdens" of another thing; it may be opposite in properties or just noticeably different, while having equally strong weight [msg #128].
- On differences: if work does not meet the same four criteria as a hobby, the author would ask a series of "Why?" questions and whether the person is doing the right thing at all. In a world where society is not living through local wars and catastrophes and is not preoccupied with survival and food, it is "НОРМАЛЬНО" (*NORMAL*) to have no tangible sacrifices on any of these criteria and to have self-realisation. So, in her opinion, in a period of balance hobby and work should not differ in this respect [msg #128].
- The likely real difference is pay: hobbies are usually not paid. Yet a *beloved* activity can drift from one category to the other, sometimes provoking the debate "hobby or freelance?" [msg #128].
- Does a person need simply some *other* activity to switch to? For some yes, for some no; some feel great reading and watching videos from their favourite field, with or without money. So the counterweight mechanism may in many cases be in low demand [msg #128].

**b) Can one "just rest" instead of a hobby?**
- There seem to be kinds of rest about "как_бы_ничегонеделание" (*sort-of-doing-nothing*), but on closer look there is activity there too — from watching ducks ("медитативное созерцание", *meditative contemplation*, "between us") to lying in shavasana. Technically, then, these would partly fall under the hobby criteria [msg #128].

**Grounding the question.** The author reframes the core of the question as: does recovering resources by changing activity suit everyone? Her answer: "безусловно, нет" (*certainly not*). It matters not only to change the picture so that the used-up type of resource can be replenished. At times it is just as important to move completely from the brain's focused mode to its diffused mode, so that space appears for reassembling the received information, the algorithms used and the thought patterns — and, through that, for releasing and redistributing resources so that one can engage more easily in the activities one needs [msg #128].

### Writing-marathon lessons (six observations)
Context: the author took part in a writing marathon (its theme words "писать", "публиковать" and "Агментек" — *write, publish, Agmentek*). She wanted to quit "писателя в стол" (*writing into the drawer*), "a coming-out from the closet of introversion". Her two goals were: (1) learn to write faster, shorter and in portions — without fear of incompleteness; (2) use only what is already in her head and notes, with minimal new sources, because she very much wanted to give life to her materials rather than keep accumulating [msg #132].
What she noticed [msg #132]:
1. **A topic from outside is a real topic.** It makes writing easier: it serves both as a lens and as a box into which knowledge from one's head is selected. She perceived it as a "запрос" (*request*), which makes it more valuable than a mere post plan [msg #132].
2. **Doing it together feels great.** When people nearby write on the same topic there is community and support, especially when they read, like and comment on each other; her group also happened to be pleasant [msg #132].
3. **"I don't just exist, I think."** For most debatable topics her knowledge turned out to be fairly systematised — with a direction of the idea, arguments, and a position or options for resolving contradictions on the way to it. So she really does have something to say, "и это не иллюзия" (*and it is not an illusion*) [msg #132].
4. **Writing longreads is not a sign of tediousness.** If a topic is important to her there can be a great deal of substantive text, and that is normal; her example is a long post she had been writing for two weeks on what a person and humanity need to feel happy [msg #132].
5. **Not writing longreads is also normal.** Only some texts she saw as important to release in one batch; the rest can — and it is even fun — be released in parts, highlighting different facets [msg #132].
6. **Warmth breaks the ice.** To start writing she simply needs company and people interested in reading what she wants to tell — not only like-minded people or colleagues. Support and warmth are wonderful [msg #132].
What she leaves with: the wish to gather and write together again, to respond to interesting questions, listen to others' reasoning and discuss touching topics; the "cherry on top" of the challenge is "лёгкость самовыражения и открытость к контакту" (*ease of self-expression and openness to contact*), extended to every reader, in any form — marathon, sprint or a cosy conversation [msg #132].

## Detailed insights

### Definitions: what the dictionary says a hobby is
The author opens the "work hard — play hard" post with a bit of pedantry: the Russian explanatory dictionary (Efremova's, taken from DIC.academic) gives "hobby" as an enthusiasm, a favourite occupation — and also a weakness, a passion and, jokingly in her reading of the entry, "пенис" (*penis*). She jokes that the dictionary may be at fault, or the source, or the author considers hobbies, literally, nonsense — then moves on without arguing with "учёными мужами и жёнами" (*learned men and women*) [msg #128].

### Daily painting as a form of expression
The author describes a young woman painting opposite her. Asked (with an apology for disturbing) whether she paints to study, for herself or something else, the woman answered «Это хобби» (*It's a hobby*) and «Я занимаюсь им каждый день» (*I do it every day*). She has drawn as long as she can remember and carries a notebook everywhere. Asked what it gives her, she said she likes everything: the colours, the movement of the brush, the sensation of touching the canvas. She paints when she is sad, cheerful, afraid or simply calm — "Это как ещё одна форма выражения" (*It's like another form of expression*) for someone who does not like to talk. During the conversation she moved on to a second painting — a person painted in cherry red; the author notes with surprise how, step by step, beige, blue and green highlights appear on the "red alien" and "humanness" gradually emerges. The lesson carried by the story: a hobby practised daily can serve as an emotional outlet and a non-verbal language, giving sensory pleasure in the process itself [msg #126].
The attached photo [img #126] shows a watercolour in a sketchbook signed "S.N." in the top corner: a half-face of a person with dark, cloud-like hair against warm swirling orange, yellow and red forms, with a second figure in the lower corner, and a watercolour palette beside it [img #126].

### Reading without guilt
The author addresses "greedy book lovers", "serial letter-lovers", binge readers who take in several books, sometimes in one day, and love crisp pages even when they are virtual: "Стыдно? Виновато? Не надо" (*Ashamed? Guilty? Don't be*). Readers have more rights and freedoms than the "пуританскому обществу перфекционистов" (*puritan society of perfectionists*) of cover-to-cover readers believes. The rights she names explicitly: the right to read anywhere, without delay and passionately, suddenly and in front of everyone, aloud — or to tell nobody at all about what happened. She frames the manifesto as "for us, officially, from now on", with the cry "Либертэ, друзья!" (*Liberté, friends!*) [msg #143].

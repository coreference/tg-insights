# Autistic burnout versus depression

## What this covers

This topic gathers what the channel Нейропряность taught about telling autistic burnout apart from depression. It covers the definition of autistic burnout, the symptoms the two conditions share, the features more typical of each, the different meaning of social withdrawal in each, and why the two need opposite approaches (more activity for depression vs deep rest and unmasking for burnout). The source treats this as practically important: according to neurodivergent lou, the two conditions "обычно требуют разных подходов" (usually require different approaches), and a wrong label can make the person worse; according to the #589 post, recognising the difference is "первый шаг к адекватной помощи" (the first step to adequate help) [msg #461, #589].

## Core ideas

- **Autistic burnout is a distinct condition, not a form of depression,** though it is often confused with it — partly because it is still under-researched, so even autistic people may mistake it for depression or think they just need to try harder [msg #460, #459].
- **The two need different, even opposite, approaches:** behavioral activation / increasing activity usually helps depression but can worsen burnout; deep rest helps burnout but can worsen depression [msg #193, #461, #465].
- **They overlap heavily** (exhaustion, need for isolation, appetite changes, concentration problems, emotional dysregulation, fatigue, executive function problems), and a person can have both at once [msg #193, #462].
- **Discriminating signs** of burnout: a clearer cause (e.g. an overloaded schedule), heightened sensory problems, motivation and pleasure preserved but no energy, loss of skills and of the ability to mask; exhaustion is the core feeling. Depression: cause often unclear, low mood as the primary driver [msg #193, #463, #464, #465].
- **Social withdrawal means different things:** maladaptive red flag in depression, adaptive energy-management strategy in autistic burnout [msg #589].
- **Misdiagnosis has a systemic cause:** neurodivergent people meet neurotypical expectations by masking and overcompensating; when they crack, the exhaustion is labelled "depression", which shifts the focus onto the person instead of the expectations that broke them [msg #790].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Definition of autistic burnout (Raymaker et al., 2021)
The #193 post, introducing Megan Anna Neff's article, gives this definition: autistic burnout is a syndrome conceptualised as the result of **chronic life stress** and a **mismatch between expectations and abilities**, **without adequate support**. It is characterised by three features: (1) pervasive, long-term exhaustion — typically lasting **3+ months** ("обычно 3+ месяца"); (2) loss of function; (3) reduced tolerance to stimuli. Original wording: "Аутичное выгорание - это синдром, концептуализированный как результат хронического жизненного стресса, несоответствия ожиданий и способностей, без адекватной поддержки" (Autistic burnout is a syndrome conceptualised as the result of chronic life stress, a mismatch of expectations and abilities, without adequate support) [msg #193].

The earlier channel article (#28) frames it similarly: a special form of burnout that primarily affects people with ASD ("РАС") **and ADHD** ("СДВГ"), described as "уникальное и глубоко истощающее состояние" (a unique and deeply exhausting state) that arises from **prolonged stress or an acute stressful experience**, especially one connected with the need to adapt to the surrounding neurotypical world. The article sets out to cover what autistic burnout is, its main symptoms, its triggers, whether it is a medical diagnosis, and how it relates to depression and to neurotypical burnout; the teaser itself does not give those answers. ### Depression vs. autistic burnout Venn diagram ("Понедельник неправильных диагнозов" — "Misdiagnosis Monday")
A chart by Neurodivergent Insights (Megan Anna Neff's project), translated by Нейропряность, posted twice (#193 and again with #589). Two circles — depression (left), autistic burnout (right) — with an overlap ("Пересечение"). The chart's own disclaimer: "Визуализация схожего опыта. Не для диагностических целей." (A visualisation of similar experiences. Not for diagnostic purposes.) Its bottom line: "У человека могут быть оба состояния одновременно." (A person can have both conditions at the same time.) [img #193, img #589]

**Depression only (left circle), 7 items:**
1. **Behavioral activation** — usually responds well to the behavioral activation method [img #193].
2. **Heightened sense of worthlessness** — an excessive feeling of one's own uselessness [img #193].
3. **Depressive thinking** — negative thoughts about oneself, the world and the future [img #193].
4. **Depressed mood** — persistent low mood lasting **at least two weeks** [img #193].
5. **Sleep disturbances** — insomnia or excessive sleepiness are possible [img #193].
6. **Anhedonia** — loss of the ability to enjoy previously pleasant activities [img #193].
7. **Suicidality** — thoughts and behaviour connected with the wish to end one's life [img #193].

**Overlap (both conditions), 8 items:**
1. Changes in eating and appetite.
2. Social withdrawal (its different nature in each condition is explained under *Adaptive vs maladaptive social withdrawal*).
3. Impact on interpersonal decisions.
4. Difficulty concentrating.
5. Tearfulness, heightened emotional sensitivity.
6. Executive function impairments.
7. A feeling of emptiness.
8. Fatigue. [img #193, img #589]

**Autistic burnout only (right circle), 6 items:**
1. **Energy expenditure and burnout** — "Чрезмерная активность и поведенческая" / "активация могут усугубить выгорание" (excessive activity and behavioral activation can worsen burnout). This is the mirror image of depression item 1 [img #193].
2. **Sensory sensitivity** — heightened susceptibility to stimuli ("Повышенная восприимчивость к раздражителям") [img #193].
3. **Benefit from rest and unmasking** — self-care, sensory offloading, dropping the mask and immersion in interests can relieve burnout [img #193].
4. **Loss of the ability to mask** — this often makes the person look "more autistic" during burnout [img #193].
5. **Skill loss** — reduced executive functioning, communication and everyday/household skills [img #193].
6. **Thoughts of non-existence** ("Мысли о небытие") — a drive to escape demands and sensory input, sometimes leading to dreams of not existing. On the chart this sits opposite depression's "suicidality", i.e. the burnout version is framed as wanting to be free of demands/stimuli rather than the wish to end one's life [img #193].

How to use it, per the chart: compare which side's features dominate, remembering both can co-occur and that the chart is a visual aid, not a diagnostic tool [img #193].

### Similarities / burnout-specific / depression-specific factors (neurodivergent lou carousel)
A seven-slide carousel by neurodivergent lou, forwarded from the channel Нейр∞тлично. Opening thesis: "Аутичное выгорание часто ошибочно принимают за депрессию..." (Autistic burnout is often mistaken for depression...) [msg #459, img #459].

**Why it matters (slides 2–3).** Autistic burnout is still insufficiently studied even though autistic people have discussed it for a long time; because of this it is sometimes confused with depression although they are different states. Even autistic people themselves may not recognise their own burnout and may, for example, believe they are depressed or that they simply «нужно больше постараться» (need to try harder) to feel better. Distinguishing them is important because they usually need different approaches: a person with depression is often advised to increase activity to lift mood and cope with negative thoughts, whereas in autistic burnout raising the activity level can, on the contrary, make the state worse [img #460, img #461].

**Similarities (slide 4), 6 items:**
1. Need for isolation.
2. Feeling of exhaustion.
3. Difficulty with everyday tasks.
4. Emotional dysregulation.
5. Difficulty concentrating.
6. Changes in appetite. [img #462]

**Factors that may be more specific to autistic burnout (slides 5–6), 6 items:**
1. **A clearer cause.** Example from the slide: you notice that lately you have had a packed schedule ("плотный график") [img #463].
2. **Heightened sensory problems** [img #463].
3. **Wanting to do things but lacking the energy** — motivation and pleasure may still be there, but no strength to act ("Может по-прежнему быть желание что-то" … "делать (может иметься мотивация и" … "удовольствие), но не быть сил для этого.") [img #463].
4. **Skill loss** — e.g. being unable to do things you used to do [img #463].
5. **Deep rest works better when combined** with activities linked to special interests or meeting sensory needs — deep rest can be more effective against burnout in that combination [img #464].
6. **Exhaustion is the foundational feeling** of burnout ("Основополагающим чувством" / "при выгорании является истощение.") [img #464].

**Factors that may be more specific to depression (slide 7), 3 items:**
1. **The cause is not always clear** [img #465].
2. **Mood as the primary driver.** Mood can change in both conditions, but in depression low mood may be the main cause, whereas in burnout the lack of mood is rather a result of the frustration and exhaustion the burnout produces [img #465].
3. **Deep rest can worsen depression;** increasing the activity level may be more effective instead [img #465].

Note the hedging throughout: every factor is framed as "могут быть более" … "специфичны" (may be more specific), not a definitive criterion [img #463, img #465].

### Adaptive vs maladaptive social withdrawal
The #589 post ("Аутичное выгорание vs. Депрессия: Понимание различий в социальном отстранении" — understanding the differences in social withdrawal) argues that the nature of social withdrawal can be the key to telling the conditions apart, because it shows up differently in each [msg #589].
1. **In depression — maladaptive withdrawal.** Withdrawal from society is usually treated as a "red flag", a sign the depressive state is deepening. The person isolates because of a lack of interest, pleasure and motivation, which can lead to loss of meaning and a worsening state. The post calls this "дезадаптивное отстранение" (maladaptive withdrawal), which genuinely requires professional intervention [msg #589].
2. **In autistic burnout — adaptive withdrawal.** Citing Mantzalas et al. (2022), the post says that for many autistic adults social withdrawal is not avoidance but "адаптивный механизм" (an adaptive mechanism) and a strategic way of managing limited energy, which autistic people spend much faster in social interactions and under sensory load [msg #589].
3. **The energy-cost mechanism.** Every social interaction and sensory experience "costs" a certain amount of energy, and for autistic people the price can be disproportionately high [msg #589].
4. **Masking as the stressor.** Research (per the post) shows that stress comes from efforts to mask autistic traits and conform to neurotypical norms; constant "камуфляж" (camouflaging) leads to autistic burnout, which may be accompanied by anxiety, depression and even suicidal thoughts [msg #589].
5. **Practical implication.** Understanding that withdrawal in burnout is not maladaptation but a strategic form of self-help can lead to more effective support, letting autistic people engage with the world on their own terms in a sustainable way that is good for their wellbeing. Recognising the difference is the first step to adequate help and individualised recovery strategies [msg #589].

### Five societal expectations placed on neurodivergent people (drcomptonsmith, translated by Rin Bonner)
Thesis: "Нейроотличным людям часто ошибочно ставят диагноз «депрессия»." (Neurodivergent people are often wrongly diagnosed with depression.) This happens because the expectations placed on them do not match how their brain works. Society assumes neurodivergent people can:
1. Manage attention and energy consistently.
2. Tolerate noise, interruptions and changes patiently.
3. Handle social demands without much effort.
4. Keep working under pressure.
5. Self-regulate without accommodations. [msg #790]

**The mechanism of misdiagnosis.** Neurodivergent people adapt through masking and overcompensation and so meet these expectations — and the fact that this costs them dearly is rarely taken into account. When it becomes unbearable and "cracks" appear, others see only fatigue, loss of motivation and withdrawal into oneself, and call it "depression". But for many neurodivergent people this is not a loss of interest in life; it is "истощение сил после многих лет, когда человеку приходилось преодолевать себя" (depletion of strength after many years of having to push past oneself). The consequence: "Когда выгорание лечат как депрессию, внимание сосредоточивается на человеке, а не на нейротипичных ожиданиях общества, которые его сломили." (When burnout is treated as depression, the focus falls on the person rather than on society's neurotypical expectations that broke them.) [msg #790]

## Detailed insights

### Recurring differential markers across the sources
Pulling the frameworks together, the same discriminators appear in several independent sources: (a) **response to activity vs rest** — activation helps depression, worsens burnout; rest/unmasking/interests help burnout, deep rest can worsen depression [img #193, img #461, img #464, img #465]; (b) **motivation/interest** — lost in depression (anhedonia, withdrawal from lack of interest), preserved but unpowered in burnout [img #193, img #463, msg #589, msg #790]; (c) **sensory sensitivity** heightened in burnout [img #193, img #463]; (d) **skill loss** in burnout [img #193, img #463]; (e) **cause** — clearer in burnout, often unclear in depression [img #463, img #465]; (f) **exhaustion** as the core of burnout vs **mood** as the core of depression [img #464, img #465, msg #790].

### Depression can accompany burnout
The sources do not present the conditions as mutually exclusive: the Venn chart states both can exist at once, and the #589 post notes that burnout caused by camouflaging may be accompanied by anxiety, depression and suicidal thoughts [img #193, msg #589].

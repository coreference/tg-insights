## Pacing and energy accounting

### What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's material on managing a limited supply of energy when you are neurodivergent and burnt out or living with chronic fatigue or pain: why burnout happens, the boom-bust cycle, pacing and several energy-accounting systems, a practical traffic-light calendar, a decision flowchart for "do it or rest", a printable daily health tracker, the Mankoski pain scale, and a short note on why exercise does not always help. According to the source, understanding the causes and becoming aware of the boom-bust cycle is "a very important step" in recovering from burnout, and pacing, self-care and seeking support help bring burnout under control and recover much faster [#52]. Energy accounting is presented as a strategy for managing limited energy resources that is especially relevant to people in autistic burnout, aimed at overcoming fatigue and improving quality of life through conscious management of energy [#30].

### Core ideas

- Energy in burnout is a limited resource that has to be accounted for. The channel frames energy accounting ("учёт энергии") as a way of consciously distributing scarce energy, adapted to the needs of autistic people, to overcome fatigue and recover from burnout [#30, img #30].
- Burnout in neurodivergent people has identifiable drivers, and the boom-bust cycle is the one you can control. Heightened sensitivity and constant strain from adapting are joined by the boom-bust pattern; living in that pattern makes recovery take longer and longer. Pacing is the counter-measure (see *Causes of neurodivergent burnout* and *Pacing*) [#52].
- Pacing means dosing load to keep activity stable. Lena Movshina's gloss: pacing comes from English "pace", it is about dosing load so neurodivergent people keep a more stable level of activity and do not get hooked on the boom-bust cycle [#132].
- Decide each activity deliberately, weighing symptoms and recovery time. The do-or-don't flowchart asks whether you can finish, whether it will worsen symptoms, and whether there is recovery time before the next thing; activity is not to be avoided entirely, but managed [#289, img #289].
- Tracking reveals patterns, triggers and your actual energy capacity. Daily tracking of pain, energy, symptoms, sleep and sensory triggers, paired with pacing, supports a personalised recovery strategy and better communication with doctors [#123, img #123].
- Rating pain is itself hard for autistic people. Pain scales such as Mankoski help, but interoception difficulties can make it especially hard to sense and judge pain intensity [#425].
- Exercise can make things worse. The channel shared a video on why one can feel worse after exercise (content not available) [#563].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Causes of neurodivergent burnout (three drivers)
From the article Nastya translated on pacing. Burnout is described as a serious problem in the lives of many neurodivergent people, often accompanied by chronic fatigue, exhaustion and chronic pain, which make the situation even harder. The article's answer to "Почему это происходит?" (Why does this happen?) lists three drivers [#52]:
1. Heightened sensitivity ("Повышенная чувствительность"). The world around can be noisy, bright and chaotic, and that drains resources. Sensory load itself is an energy cost [#52].
2. Overstrain ("Перенапряжение"). The constant struggle to adapt to a world that is not always friendly to our neurodivergence takes a lot of strength [#52].
3. The boom-bust cycle ("Цикл", "взлет-падение"). Periods of intense activity take a lot of energy and are followed by a sharp drop in energy. When this becomes a way of life, increasingly more time is needed for recovery — i.e. the cycle compounds, lengthening each recovery [#52].

The article states that understanding the causes and recognising the boom-bust cycle is a very important step on the path to recovery [#52].

#### Pacing (load distribution) against the boom-bust cycle
- What it is. Pacing ("пейсинг") is translated as a system of load distribution ("система распределения нагрузки"). Lena Movshina explains the word comes from English "pace" (temp) and describes it as the dosing of load that neurodivergent people need to keep a more stable level of activity and not get hooked on the boom-bust cycle [#52, #132].
- What it is combined with. The pacing article says that pacing, self-care practices and seeking support together will help you take burnout under control and recover much faster [#52].
- Ways of keeping the energy account named in the article. According to Lena Movshina's summary, the pacing article offers several methods of energy accounting: the spoon system ("система ложек"), the energy "checkbook" (energy "чековая книжка") and the traffic light ("светофор"). #### Traffic-light energy calendar (Lena Movshina's four-step setup)
Purpose: a visual way to keep energy accounting in an ordinary calendar so that each day is balanced. Lena Movshina chose it after reading the pacing article because she had spent recent weeks in a "kick-yourself–crash" cycle ("пинок себя-падение") and wanted to focus on helpful practices [#132].

Rules ("Правила такие"). Every activity gets a colour:
- Red — strongly takes energy ("мощно забирает энергию").
- Green — gives energy ("дает энергию").
- Yellow — "видимо все остальное" (apparently everything else).
The goal is that every day adds up to a traffic light (a mix of colours); a whole day should not be entirely red [#132].

How she implements it ("Как я буду делать"):
1. Calls are red by default. Her calendar already holds calls, and they are red by default, because "Активность с людьми точно забирает энергию" (Activity with people definitely takes energy) [#132].
2. Plan other activities around the calls, with their colour. She schedules the remaining activities around the calls and enters each into the calendar in its colour [#132].
3. Stop adding to a red-heavy day and block green time. If she sees that a day already has many red items, she no longer adds tasks to it and puts a green block for the rest of the day [#132].
4. Review the week at the end of it. At the end of the week she looks at what the week was actually like, because otherwise she forgets that she "имела право устать" (had the right to be tired) — the review validates fatigue rather than leaving it unexplained [#132].

Her example calendar (photo) shows entries such as walking the dog and walking with a friend (10–11:30 AM), eating (11:30 AM–12:15 PM), making a cover image (12–2 PM), sleeping with the dog (2–4 PM), working (4:30–6:30 PM) and writing a post (6:30 PM), alongside a joking parallel "energy tracking" for the visiting dog that mostly sleeps (12:15–7:15 PM) [#132, img #132]. She committed publicly to tracking energy for a fixed period and reporting how it goes, inviting readers to join, e.g. by subscribing to each other's calendars [#132].

#### Do or don't? Decision flowchart for activity and rest
"Делать или не делать? Схема принятия решений по распределению активности и отдыха" (To do or not to do? A decision scheme for distributing activity and rest), originally by Stickman Communications, translated by Fiu-fiu (Аутичные переводы), card design by Nastya (Нейропряность). It is used before an activity to decide whether to do it now, adapt it, or rest [#289, img #289]. Read from the card image:

1. Question 1 — Смогу ли я с этими симптомами закончить дело? (With these symptoms, will I be able to finish the task?)
   - Yes → go to Question 2.
   - No or not sure → go to the "make it more manageable" box (step 4) [#289].
2. Question 2 — Вызовет ли это увеличение боли, усталости и других симптомов? (Will it cause an increase in pain, fatigue and other symptoms?)
   - No → go to Question 3.
   - Yes or not sure → go to the "make it more manageable" box (step 4) [#289].
3. Question 3 — Будет ли у меня достаточно времени на восстановление между этим делом и следующим? (Will I have enough time to recover between this task and the next?)
   - Probably → "Делай!" (Do it!).
   - No → the stop outcome (step 5) [#289].
4. Box — Могу ли я сделать задачу более управляемой: (Can I make the task more manageable:) — four options to check:
   - break it into small steps?
   - use aids/assistive tools ("средства помощи")?
   - ask for help with parts of the task?
   - do I have a strategy for stopping the task if it turns out to be too hard?
   - Yes (it can be made manageable) → go to Question 3 (recovery time).
   - No → the stop outcome (step 5) [#289].
5. Stop outcome — "Лучше не делать." (Better not to do it.) It is acceptable to do it if it is something urgent or a special case, but this must not become routine [#289].

Footnotes on the card: "*Упрощенная версия. Этот процесс может отличаться от человека к человеку." (Simplified version. This process may differ from person to person.) and "Активность может усиливать симптомы, но цель - не избегать их, а научиться управлять ими. В поиске" … "оптимального подхода допустимы эксперименты, и со временем он может меняться." (Activity can intensify symptoms, but the goal is not to avoid them but to learn to manage them. Experiments are acceptable in searching for the optimal approach, and it may change over time.) [#289]. The card is credited to StickmanCommunications.co.uk, "last reviewed May 2024" [#289].

#### Neurodivergent health tracker (daily sheet)
A free printable (or tablet-fillable) one-page daily tracker Nastya made: "Трэкер состояния здоровья для нейроотличных людей с хроническими болями" (Health-condition tracker for neurodivergent people with chronic pain). She hopes it becomes a companion for better understanding oneself and one's triggers and improving quality of life, giving more clarity, confidence and control over one's condition [#123].

Why it is useful — four stated benefits [#123]:
1. Connection with the body and the present. Daily tracking can help build a deeper connection with your own body and with the present moment.
2. Identifying patterns and triggers. Regular use lets you notice recurring patterns in your condition and identify the triggers that affect your wellbeing and lead to deterioration; this can help you develop effective strategies for managing those triggers.
3. A burnout-recovery strategy. Paired with pacing strategies, the tracker helps develop a personalised strategy for recovering from neurodivergent burnout: you better understand the size of your resource and which actions and lifestyle changes support your recovery.
4. Documentation for doctors. Keeping detailed documentation of your condition allows more effective interaction with doctors: you can give them accurate information, which eases diagnosis and choice of optimal treatment.

Sections of the sheet (transcribed from the image) [#123]:
1. Date ("Дата").
2. Pain level ("Уровень боли") — circles 1 to 10.
3. Pain symptoms (Симптомы боли) — lines to describe pain, with a day/night column (День/ночь) and front and back body outlines ("Вид спереди", "Вид сзади") to mark location.
4. How do you feel today? ("Как вы себя сегодня чувствуете?") — six faces from sad to happy.
5. Mood, clarity of mind, interoception ("Настроение", "Ясность разума", "Интероцепция") — each rated 1–5.
6. Energy level ("Уровень энергии") — morning and evening ("Утром", "Вечером"), each on a five-step face/battery scale.
7. Medications and dose ("Медикаменты", "Доза") — table.
8. Stress level ("Уровень стресса") — none / low / medium / high / maximal ("Отсутствие", "Низкий", "Средний", "Высокий", "Максимальный").
9. Other symptoms (Другие симптомы) — tick boxes: anxiety, fatigue, apathy, insomnia, nausea, depression, irritability, anger, sore throat, diarrhoea, vomiting, bloating, exhaustion ("Измотанность"), swelling ("Отек"), goosebumps/tingling ("Мурашки"), temperature, plus blank boxes for your own.
10. Meltdown / shutdown (Мелтдаун / Шатдаун; OCR garbled, read from the image) — tick which, then columns for triggers, description and what helped ("Триггеры", "Описание", "Что помогло").
11. Sleep ("Сон") — duration, nightmares yes/no ("Ночные кошмары: Да/Нет"), quality 1–5.
12. Physical activity ("Физическая активность") — none / a little / a lot / max ("Никакой", "Немного", "Много", "Макс."), plus details.
13. Sensory triggers ("Сенсорные триггеры") — tick boxes: sounds, tastes, clothing, temperature, texture, movements, people, visual, light, smells, pain, environment, plus blank boxes.
14. Notes ("Заметки").

#### Mankoski pain scale (0–10)
A numeric scale from 0 to 10, where 0 is no pain and 10 is pain so severe the person loses consciousness. Like other pain scales, it is a tool people with chronic pain can use to track pain systematically; it helps doctors better understand the intensity of a patient's pain, so that pain is adequately assessed and relieved. The channel cites painscale.com as its source [#425]. The levels:
- 0 — No pain ("Отсутствие боли") [#425].
- 1 — Very minor annoyance — occasional weak twinges. No medication needed [#425].
- 2 — Minor annoyance — occasional strong twinges. No medication needed [#425].
- 3 — Annoying enough to be distracting. Mild painkillers (aspirin, ibuprofen) are effective [#425].
- 4 — Can be ignored if you are really involved in your work, but still distracting. Mild painkillers relieve pain for 3–4 hours [#425].
- 5 — Can't be ignored for more than 30 minutes. Mild painkillers reduce pain for 3–4 hours [#425].
- 6 — Can't be ignored for any length of time, but you can still go to work and take part in social life. Stronger painkillers (codeine, acetaminophen-hydrocodone) reduce pain for 3–4 hours [#425].
- 7 — Makes it hard to concentrate, interferes with sleep. You can still function with effort. Stronger painkillers are only partially effective; the strongest painkillers (extended-release oxycodone, morphine) relieve the pain [#425].
- 8 — Physical activity severely limited. You can read and talk with effort. Nausea and dizziness appear as factors of the pain. The strongest painkillers reduce pain for 3–4 hours [#425].
- 9 — Unable to speak. Uncontrollable crying or moaning — close to delirium. The strongest painkillers are only partially effective [#425].
- 10 — Unconsciousness. Pain causes fainting. The strongest painkillers are only partially effective [#425].

Caveat attached by the channel: "Вежливое напоминание: аутичным людям может быть особенно сложно чувствовать и определять интенсивность боли из-за проблем с интероцепцией." (A gentle reminder: autistic people may find it especially hard to feel and determine the intensity of pain because of interoception problems.) [#425]. The medication levels are the scale's descriptors, reproduced from the source; the channel gives no dosing advice [#425].

#### Managing fatigue from illness or chronic conditions (guide)
Nastya, together with Nastya (Fiu-fiu) of Аутичные переводы, translated and adapted the guide "Как управлять своим уровнем энергии" (How to manage your energy level). Points stated in the post [#69, img #69]:
- Audience and aim. It is meant for people experiencing fatigue due to illness or a chronic condition; its aim is to help people manage their energy as well as possible so they can cope with more tasks.
- Fatigue is individual. Everyone's feeling of fatigue is unique and can have different causes, which is why it is hard to explain.
- Definition. Fatigue includes a lack of physical and/or cognitive energy that affects usual and desired activities ("отсутствие физической и/или когнитивной энергии, которое влияет на обычные и желаемые действия").
- Don't rush back. You may feel you need to return to usual activities as quickly as possible and feel pressure, but allow yourself time and do not hurry.
- Provenance. The guide was created after consultations with occupational therapists ("эрготерапевтами") and people living with fatigue.

## Internalized capitalism, ableism and self-worth

### What this covers

This topic collects the channel's material on how values absorbed from a capitalist, ableist society — "you are worth what you produce", "be normal", "don't be a burden" — become internal, shape a neurodivergent person's self-esteem, push them into masking and eventually into adult burnout. It covers Lindsey Mackereth's life-stage model of internalized capitalism in late-identified autistic/ADHD/gifted people, two self-check lists (7 signs of internalized capitalism, 15 signs of internalized ableism), the critique of the "high-functioning" label, the inner critic in autistic burnout, and the alternative the channel proposes: worth independent of productivity, real support instead of forced positivity, and accommodations as something one deserves. According to the source, this matters because these attitudes "разрушать психическое здоровье и чувство собственной идентичности" (destroy mental health and the sense of one's own identity) [#615] and lead to burnout "из-за постоянных попыток соответствовать чужим ожиданиям" (because of constant attempts to meet other people's expectations) [#448].

### Core ideas

- Internalized capitalism is a hidden driver of masking and low self-esteem in late-identified neurodivergent people. Self-worth becomes built on the ability to hide one's traits and to be productive, so it is conditional and collapses into burnout in adulthood. Detailed in the *Life-stage model* below [#448, file #448].
- Internalized ableism is the same mechanism turned on disability: believing that only "normal" people are fully valuable, and treating one's own needs, symptoms and labels as shameful. Detailed in the *15 signs* [#615].
- "High-functioning" measures usefulness to others, not wellbeing — it rewards keeping the system running and not causing problems even at the cost of wearing oneself out [#687].
- Self-criticism worsens autistic burnout: the drive to meet social norms intensifies the inner critic, which deepens isolation and causes still more burnout [#23].
- Forced positivity does not build resilience; support does [#442].
- Your worth is not defined by productivity or by meeting others' expectations — you do not need to "contribute to society" to be valuable, and you deserve accommodations [#448, #602].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Internalized capitalism in hidden neurocomplexity — life-stage model (Lindsey Mackereth)
Source: a printable PDF by Lindsey Mackereth, translated by Нейропряность, titled "ИНТЕРНАЛИЗИРОВАННЫЙ КАПИТАЛИЗМ В СКРЫТОЙ НЕЙРОКОМПЛЕКСНОСТИ" with the subtitle "Влияние на выявление нейроотличий, маскинга и самооценку" (Internalized capitalism in hidden neurocomplexity: impact on identification of neurodivergence, masking and self-esteem). It traces how a neurodivergence that has not been identified develops through life stages — childhood, adolescence, early adulthood, middle adulthood — showing in parallel what happens to identification, masking and self-esteem. Use it to understand one's own history as a late-identified person and why burnout arrived when it did. (!) UNCLEAR: the PDF is a diagram and the transcription lost its layout, so the exact placement of some boxes on particular stages is reconstructed from reading order; the content of each box is complete [#448].

Overarching frame of the diagram. It depicts the "Прямое и косвенное влияние капиталистических ценностей на человека в капиталистическом или близком к нему обществе" (direct and indirect influence of capitalist values on a person in a capitalist or near-capitalist society) [#448].

Stage 1 — Childhood: neurodivergence not yet identified. The diagram lists why the neurodivergence stays unseen:
1. Less noticeable traits.
2. Denial of differences by the family of origin, because of lack of awareness and/or internalized ableism.
3. Early masking.
4. A general lack of awareness of how autism and/or ADHD present in groups other than white men — for example people assigned female at birth, BIPOC, transgender people, immigrants.
5. Giftedness: academic success or passing tests plus bias can cause autistic and/or ADHD traits to go unnoticed or be ignored.
[#448]

Self-esteem in childhood is probably unstable because of factors tied to neurocomplexity:
1. Lack of validation from peers, family, teachers.
2. A feeling of being different inside, but without conscious understanding of why.
3. Parents who also heavily mask their traits and are themselves unidentified neurodivergent people, with or without internalized ableism.
4. Little or no access to accommodations at home or at school.
5. Social bullying and/or exclusion by peers, siblings or family (explicit or hidden).
Self-esteem may fall because the person internalizes differences in how their brain works as personal flaws and/or innate inferiority. Unconscious and/or conscious masking of neurodivergent traits begins, in order to avoid inner and outer conflicts by imitating neurotypicality [#448].

Stage 2 — Adolescence: neurodivergence ignored and/or denied. Discomfort from masking and/or from an environment not adapted to neurocomplexity leads to a rise in secondary symptoms: anxiety, depression, substance use, eating disorders, financial difficulties from compulsive spending used as a coping strategy. Masking becomes entrenched and identity confusion arises [#448].

Stage 3 — Early adulthood: a period of relative "success". A period begins of relative "success" in some academic or work environment that matches capitalist standards. For the first time in their life the person may experience:
1. Praise from authority figures (teachers, bosses, parents).
2. A sense of achievement and/or respect.
3. A sense of belonging to a community and/or acceptance by peers.
4. Consolidation of the masked version of oneself.
Self-esteem begins to rise thanks to capitalist successes at work or study — but it is based on the learned ability to hide one's traits and meet society's expectations. The person continues to pursue "success" within the capitalist system and builds a lifestyle around a growing income and the identity of a "хорошего работника/члена общества" (good worker / member of society). Self-esteem and self-image form around masking as neurotypical and around contribution to society/capitalism, and become dependent on them. Work and social usefulness turn into driving forces and daily stressors that regularly force the person beyond the limits of their masking capacity and the natural features of their nervous system [#448].

How internalized capitalism shows up at this stage:
1. Excessive workload (overwork).
2. Guilt about rest and leisure.
3. A constant striving for more (can lead to consumerism or status-chasing).
4. Prioritizing personal success over the wellbeing of society.
[#448]

Stage 4 — Middle adulthood: late identification and burnout. ADHD/autism is identified later in life, through professional diagnosis or self-diagnosis. Adult burnout: "Организм и психика требуют паузы после многих лет несоответствия потребностям" (body and psyche demand a pause after many years of their needs not being met). It may show up as:
1. Chronic fatigue.
2. Reduced tolerance to stimuli.
3. Difficulty with everyday tasks that used to come easily.
4. Problems with communication and/or concentration.
5. Social isolation.
6. Chronic pain not explained by illness.
[#448]

Way out — understanding and release from internalized capitalism through:
1. Reclaiming the right to rest.
2. Exploring and rethinking personal values.
3. Developing a sense of community instead of competition.
4. Conscious consumption.
Then: explore and define your self-worth outside the frame of capitalism, taking your neurocomplexity into account; adapt your life to your sensory, social and cognitive needs/preferences; discover what really matters and what brings genuine joy. This may mean choosing a non-standard lifestyle that fits individual needs, which may be seen as "unsuccessful" by capitalist standards. The end point of the diagram is "Истинное соответствие собственной аутентичности и нейрокомплексности" (true alignment with one's own authenticity and neurocomplexity) [#448].

#### Six effects of internalized capitalism on neurodivergent people
From the channel's post introducing Mackereth's material. Definition first: internalized capitalism is the process by which people unconsciously absorb and accept capitalist values and ideology; in a capitalist society people are taught from childhood to value productivity, competition, material success and personal achievement, and these attitudes become deeply rooted in self-perception and daily behaviour — capitalist norms and expectations shape thinking, actions and personal goals. For late-identified neurodivergent people (autistic, ADHD or gifted) it "часто играет коварную роль в формировании стратегий маскировки и развитии самооценки" (often plays an insidious role in forming masking strategies and in the development of self-esteem) [#448]. Its effects:
1. It masks neurodivergence, forcing the person to adjust to "normality".
2. It lowers self-esteem because the person does not match imposed standards of success.
3. It leads to burnout through constant attempts to meet other people's expectations.
4. It hinders accepting and loving one's own traits.
5. It harms physical and mental health.
6. It makes developing an authentic personality harder.
[#448]

#### Six steps to counter internalized capitalism
The post's list of what is important to resist this influence [#448]:
1. Become aware that internalized capitalism is present in your thinking — recognition is the starting point.
2. Rethink your values and goals — check which of them are actually yours rather than imposed standards of success.
3. Accept your neuro-traits as part of a unique personality rather than as defects to be hidden.
4. Find a community of like-minded people for support (the PDF frames this as community instead of competition).
5. Work on raising self-esteem outside capitalist standards — worth not measured by productivity or achievement.
6. If needed, see a specialist to work through these issues.
The post closes: "Помните, что ваша ценность не определяется продуктивностью или соответствием чужим ожиданиям. Каждый человек уникален и достоин любви и уважения просто за то, что он есть" (Remember that your worth is not determined by productivity or by meeting other people's expectations. Every person is unique and worthy of love and respect simply for being who they are) [#448].

#### 7 signs of internalized capitalism (Method Creative)
A self-check card posted as an addition to the internalized-capitalism post, translated by the channel from Method Creative [#449]:
1. Your self-esteem is tied to your work.
2. You put work above your health (mental and physical).
3. You only allow yourself hobbies that contribute to self-improvement or bring money.
4. You put work above relationships.
5. You feel ashamed when you take sick leave.
6. You feel guilty when you experience joy.
7. Rest feels like laziness.
[#449, msg #449]

#### 15 signs of internalized ableism toward yourself (@soyouredisabled)
Purpose: a self-check, presented as "твой знак (и, возможно, даже знак для окружающих тебя) проверить как у тебя дела" (your sign — and perhaps a sign for those around you — to check how you are doing). The post's premise: "Знание - это уже половина дела" (knowing is already half the battle). Ableism is defined as discrimination and prejudice against people with disabilities, based on the idea that only healthy and "normal" people are valuable and complete; internalized ableism can destroy mental health and the sense of one's own identity [#615]. The signs:
1. Feeling "not disabled enough" — believing your experience doesn't count because it doesn't match a certain "look" or "idea" of disability.
2. Feeling discomfort or being triggered when others talk about their experience of disability.
3. Using negative expressions about yourself — not treating yourself kindly or as if you are worthy.
4. Comparing yourself with other people.
5. Feeling relief, pride or joy when people "forget" that you are disabled.
6. Hyper-independence.
7. Feeling shame or awkwardness about your disability.
8. Hiding your symptoms — because otherwise it seems you won't be taken seriously, or to fit in, or to avoid judgement.
9. Minimizing or hiding your needs for accommodations and accessibility — trying to be a "удобным инвалидом" (convenient disabled person) who causes no trouble.
10. Telling your medical history in too much detail every time you ask for support.
11. Downplaying your difficulties and suppressing your needs and emotions.
12. Feeling uncomfortable taking up space (attention, room).
13. Avoiding labels like "disabled" or "disability" because you fear what others will think.
14. Disconnecting from your body.
15. Feeling guilty for wanting joy and connection while being disabled.
[#615]

### Detailed insights

#### "High-functioning" as a measure of usefulness
Lindsey Mackereth, quoted by the channel: "«Высокофункциональность» — это не о том, как ты себя чувствуешь или насколько хорошо справляешься внутренне. Это о том, насколько ты полезен для других." ("High-functioning" is not about how you feel or how well you cope inside. It is about how useful you are to others.) It is about meeting external expectations, keeping the system working and not creating problems — "даже если для этого приходится идти на износ и превышать свои пределы" (even if that means wearing yourself out and exceeding your limits). This links the functioning label directly to the internalized-capitalism logic of worth-as-usefulness and to the early-adulthood "success" stage of her model [#687].

#### Self-criticism and the inner critic in autistic burnout
The channel's introduction to a translated article states that self-criticism can hit neurodivergent people especially hard, creating extra obstacles on the way to self-acceptance and to overcoming autistic burnout. The mechanism given: neurodivergence affects how we perceive ourselves and the world; the striving to meet social norms and expectations can intensify self-criticism, which in turn deepens the feeling of isolation and being misunderstood and leads to even more burnout — a self-reinforcing loop. The article itself (on telegra.ph) covers what influences self-criticism and strategies to "усмирить нашего внутреннего критика" (tame our inner critic). (!) INCOMPLETE: the factors and strategies are in the linked article, whose text was not provided in the source [#23, img #23].

The channel author then shared her own experience in a "Личный опыт" (personal experience) article: while translating the self-criticism article she realized she had to some extent managed to tame her own inner critic — "Теперь он хотя бы не токсичит…" (Now at least it isn't toxic…). She explicitly disclaims expertise ("Не скажу, что я супер мега ниндзя гуру" — I won't say I'm a super mega ninja guru) and invites readers to share their own experience. (!) INCOMPLETE: her actual account and methods are in the linked article "Что стало с моим внутренним критиком" (What became of my inner critic), not included in the source [#33, img #33].


#### Beyond toxic positivity (Adam Grant)
The channel translated a note by Adam Grant ("Beyond toxic positivity", 11 Feb 2024). Its three points: in hard times, imposed positive thinking does not make people stronger — it only denies the reality the person faces ("In hard times, urging people to be positive doesn't boost their resilience. It denies their reality."). People who are suffering need not only moral support but a reliable footing to withstand life's upheavals ("People in pain don't need good vibes only. They need a hand to stay steady through all the vibrations."). Strength does not come from forced smiles; it comes when you feel genuinely supported ("Strength doesn't come from forced smiles. It comes from feeling supported.") [#442, img #442].

#### Worth independent of contribution; what autistic people deserve
An affirmation translated from Tracy Rodriguez, addressed to autistic friends: you are valuable, important and worthy of support; "Вам не нужно «вносить вклад в общество», чтобы быть ценными" (You don't need to "contribute to society" to be valuable); you are valuable regardless of what you do or don't do; "we are in this together", and here there is support and safety. It names three things autistic people deserve:
1. Sensory accommodations.
2. Flexible ways of communicating.
3. An environment that supports your needs.
This is the direct counter-statement to the "contribution to society/capitalism" basis of self-worth described in Mackereth's model [#602].

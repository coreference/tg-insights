# Autistic identity, community and disability pride

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's material on autistic identity and self-acceptance: a first-person critique of the phrase "don't let autism define you", a newcomer's guide to the norms of the autistic community (self-diagnosis, functioning labels, the term "Asperger's", identity-first language, unmasking, diversity of autistic experience, "you are not broken"), the meaning of Disability Pride Month and its flag, and a "hexagon" of six core autistic needs. According to the sources, it matters because autistic people are often made to feel "not good enough" or "worse than others" in a neurotypical world, masking demonstrably harms mental health, and acceptance and pride are framed as a form of resistance and even a life-saving process [msg #338, #560, #562].

## Core ideas

- **Autism is inseparable from the person.** The forwarded authors (neurodivergent_lou, autisticvintage) say autism shapes how they perceive and experience the whole world; without it they would not be themselves. Hence the rejection of "don't let autism define you" and of "person with autism" (see *Why "don't let autism define you" is problematic* and norm 5 below) [msg #336, #559].
- **Pride and acceptance are acts of resistance**, not denial of real difficulties: autism brings both joys and real limitations, and being affected by it does not make anyone a failure or a bad person [msg #338, #339].
- **Each autistic person's self-understanding is legitimate**, including those who do feel a separation between "person and autism" or who are not yet at the point of pride [msg #340, #561].
- **The community has explicit norms** that a newcomer should know (7 norms, see framework) [msg #555].
- **Disability is a natural part of human diversity**; Disability Pride Month and its flag express this [msg #71].
- **Autistic wellbeing rests on six needs** (the 6C hexagon): sensory comfort, own pace, community, special interest, freedom, meaning [msg #772].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Things to know if you are new to the autistic community (7 norms)
A forwarded carousel by autisticvintage titled «Вещи, которые стоит знать, если вы новичок в аутичном сообществе» ("Things worth knowing if you are new to the autistic community") [msg #555]. It sets out seven community norms, each with its reasoning:

1. **We accept self-diagnosis** («Мы принимаем самодиагностику»). Reasoning: according to the author, the diagnostic criteria for autism were based on studying only white cisgender boys, so the way people are tested for autism reflects how autism feels for one demographic group only. Many professionals still hold a very stereotyped picture of what autism looks like, so anyone who is not a white cisgender boy — including women, non-binary people, people of colour, LGBT+ people — can find it very hard to get diagnosed. There are also financial barriers. Because of all these barriers, the community welcomes self-diagnosed people «с распростертыми объятиями» ("with open arms") [msg #556].
2. **We do not use functioning labels** («Мы не используем функциональные ярлыки»). The terms "high-functioning" and "low-functioning", and also autism levels 1, 2 and 3, are described as «просто нейротипичные конструкции, используемые для оправдания поддержки или отказа в поддержке аутичным людям» ("simply neurotypical constructs used to justify granting or denying support to autistic people"). Reasoning: autism is a spectrum, there is no "low" or "high"; one person can struggle in one area and excel in another; and needs often change daily depending on many external factors that affect the capacity to cope with the world. The whole idea is called mistaken because "high-functioning" does not mean superiority or that the person needs no help, and "low-functioning" does not mean the person is incapable or worse than others [msg #557].
3. **The term "Asperger's" ("Aspie") is largely rejected in the community.** The author gives "some of many weighty reasons": (a) Hans Asperger, after whom the syndrome was named, was a Nazi; (b) it is no longer a diagnosis under DSM-5 — what used to be classified as Asperger syndrome is now simply part of autism spectrum disorder (ASD, РАС); (c) it is itself a functioning label (see norm 2) [msg #558].
4. **Most of the community prefers "Autistic" over "Person with autism".** Despite what specialists teach, most autistic people prefer to identify as autistic rather than as "people with autism". If you are autistic and prefer "person with autism" for yourself, you have every right to use it and ask others to use it for you; when speaking about others, the default is "autistic", because that is what the majority prefers. For most, "person with autism" can be offensive, because «Наш аутизм является неотъемлемой частью того, кто мы есть, без него мы бы не были собой» ("Our autism is an inseparable part of who we are; without it we would not be ourselves") [msg #559]. 5. **Learning to unmask is a hard but necessary process.** Definitions: masking is hiding autistic traits to appear neurotypical; unmasking is learning to accept your autistic traits rather than suppress them. Why it matters: it is a process of self-acceptance, and moreover, the author states, it has been proven that masking raises stress levels, harms mental health, leads to autistic burnout and even raises suicide risk — so learning to unmask «может быть буквально спасающим жизнь процессом» ("can be a literally life-saving process") [msg #560].
6. **There is no right or wrong way to be autistic.** The author is proud to be autistic and comfortable with her diagnosis, but says it is fine if you do not feel that yet. Social media shows many people who have radically accepted their autism; she hopes every autistic person will one day feel the same, and it is okay if you are not at that point of your journey yet. Every autistic person's path differs, and so does their attitude to it: how they stim, how they cope with difficulties or achieve success, what housing suits them, whether they can work or attend school — all of this varies from person to person, and no autistic experience is more valid or more correct than another [msg #561].
7. **You are not broken.** Some people will try to convince you that being autistic means being broken, damaged or somehow worse than others; the author calls this "incredibly far from the truth". Being autistic means nothing was ever wrong with you: the reason you were different was never a malfunction or defect. «Ваш мозг не сломан, он просто устроен по-другому» ("Your brain is not broken, it is just built differently"); there is nothing to "fix", because your brain works as it was meant to [msg #562].

### Hexagon of autistic needs (6C)
Created by autisticResearch (forwarded from the channel «Об аутичных женщинах, по которым не скажешь», "About autistic women you wouldn't guess are autistic"), offered «Вместо мемов» ("instead of memes") as a «гексагон аутичных потребностей» ("hexagon of autistic needs") [msg #772]. The cover card places 6C аутичного человека (the 6 C's of an autistic person — all six needs begin with the Russian letter С) in the centre, with six sides: Сенсорный комфорт (sensory comfort), Свой темп (own pace), Сообщество (community), Специнтерес (special interest), Свобода (freedom), Смысл (meaning) [img #772]. Each need is unpacked on its own card:

1. **Сенсорный комфорт — Sensory comfort** [img #773]:
   - understanding and accepting your own (sensory) needs;
   - being able to control your space and stimuli — light, sound, smell, texture;
   - the right to take pauses.
2. **Свой темп — Own pace** [img #774]:
   - extra time to process and respond;
   - a minimum of sudden switches;
   - allowing yourself to be slower than others.
3. **Сообщество — Community** [img #775]:
   - a few people who are "your own" («своих») and places where you are accepted as you are;
   - the opportunity to take part in shared activities;
   - people you can turn to for help without shame or judgement.
4. **Специнтерес — Special interest** [img #776]:
   - deep immersion in a favourite activity as a source of energy and competence;
   - dedicated windows in the schedule for it;
   - the opportunity to discuss it with someone interested and share findings and results.
5. **Свобода — Freedom** [img #777]:
   - to be yourself without a mask: loud or quiet, slow or fast, sensitive and inattentive — «любым» ("any way at all");
   - to choose the format of communication, relationships, employment, environment, workload and tasks;
   - to stim when you want;
   - to ignore, refuse, say "no";
   - to contemplate — simply to be, without evaluations, results, achievements;
   - time alone: for rest, reflection, fantasies, inventing new ideas.
6. **Смысл — Meaning** [img #778]:
   - doing what aligns with your values;
   - accepting your own clear goals instead of «так принято» ("that's how it's done");
   - looking for meaning if you want to, even if people say you are "overcomplicating everything" («всё усложняешь»).

### Disability pride flag symbolism
From the channel author's (Настя) post on Disability Pride Month [msg #71]. Each coloured stripe has its own meaning:
1. **Red** — physical disability.
2. **Gold** — neurodivergence.
3. **White** — invisible disabilities and conditions that have not yet been diagnosed.
4. **Blue** — emotional and psychiatric disability, including mental illness, anxiety and depression.
5. **Green** — sensory disability, including deafness, blindness, lack of smell, lack of taste, auditory processing disorder and all other sensory impairments.

The **faded black background** symbolises mourning and anger for victims of ableist violence and abuse. The **diagonal band** cuts across the walls and barriers that separate disabled people from "normative" society, and also represents light and creativity breaking through the darkness [msg #71].

### Goals of Disability Pride Month
Disability Pride Month is an annual event, usually marked in July, aimed at [msg #71]:
1. raising awareness of the rights and problems of disabled people;
2. celebrating the contribution of disabled people to society;
3. promoting the idea that disability is a natural part of human diversity;
4. fighting stigma and discrimination;
5. encouraging inclusivity and accessibility in society.

The month is meant to help disabled people be proud of themselves and their identity and to foster wider acceptance and understanding of disability. Disability pride, per the post, consists in accepting and respecting each person's uniqueness and perceiving it as a natural part of human diversity [msg #71].

## Detailed insights

### Why "don't let autism define you" is problematic (neurodivergent_lou)
A forwarded carousel by neurodivergent_lou, introduced with: «Они говорят: «Не позволяй аутизму определять тебя», не понимая, что вся моя жизнь проходит через призму аутизма...» ("They say 'Don't let autism define you', not understanding that my whole life passes through the prism of autism...") [msg #334, img #334]. The author's argument, step by step:

- **The phrase presupposes that autism is bad.** It is said with the best intentions, but it seems to come from the idea that autism is something inherently bad or shameful. For the author, autism is more complex: in daily life it really does impose limitations, yet being autistic is something she celebrates («чествую») — «Без аутизма я бы не была собой» ("Without autism I wouldn't be myself") [msg #335].
- **Autism shapes all perception.** Her autism affects how she perceives and experiences the world; it defines her existence in it; she sees and interacts with the world through the prism of autism. It gives her incredible passions, the joy of stimming and intensity of emotions, but also brings sensory overload and communication difficulties. Sometimes this makes existing very hard, but she cannot separate any of it from who she is at her core [msg #336].
- **Double standard with other identities.** When she talks about other aspects of herself — being a friend, a sister — nobody tells her those things don't define her. She asks whether this is because those aspects are considered socially acceptable, and whether it is linked to society often associating autism with deficits [msg #337].
- **The phrase pressures toward masking.** Unintentionally, it sounds as if she should not use autism as an excuse (as others see it); it makes her feel she must suppress the traits that outwardly show her autism and not accept her autistic identity. «Но я устала подавлять и игнорировать эти аспекты себя» ("But I'm tired of suppressing and ignoring these aspects of myself") [msg #338].
- **Pride as resistance.** For her, accepting and being proud of her autism is «акт сопротивления в нейротипичном мире» ("an act of resistance in a neurotypical world"), where autistic people often feel "not good enough" or "worse than the rest" [msg #338].
- **Being affected is not failure.** The phrase also reads to her as a call not to let autism affect her ability to communicate, work or take care of herself. But autism really does affect all of these, and that does not make her a "failure" or a "bad person" [msg #339].
- **Caveat — other views are valid.** Many existing narratives assume a separation between "the person and their autism", and if an autistic person feels it that way, that is completely normal. Every autistic person will have their own perception of what it means to be autistic, and that perception can change over time [msg #340].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- All material except the Disability Pride Month post is forwarded content from other authors (neurodivergent_lou, autisticvintage, autisticResearch); the views are theirs, presented by the channel [msg #334, #555, #772].
- neurodivergent_lou explicitly allows that some autistic people do experience a person/autism separation and that this is normal; autisticvintage similarly allows people to use "person with autism" for themselves. The community norms are majority preferences, not universal rules [msg #340, #559].
- The claims that diagnostic criteria were based "only" on white cisgender boys and that masking is "proven" to raise suicide risk are autisticvintage's statements; no studies are cited in the source [msg #556, #560].

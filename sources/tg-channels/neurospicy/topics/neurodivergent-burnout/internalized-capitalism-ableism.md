# Internalized capitalism, ableism and self-worth

## What this covers

This topic collects the channel's material on how values absorbed from a capitalist, ableist society — "you are worth what you produce", "be normal", "don't be a burden" — become internal, shape a neurodivergent person's self-esteem, push them into masking and eventually into adult burnout. It covers Lindsey Mackereth's life-stage model of internalized capitalism in late-identified autistic/ADHD/gifted people, two self-check lists (7 signs of internalized capitalism, 15 signs of internalized ableism), the critique of the "high-functioning" label, the inner critic in autistic burnout, and the alternative the channel proposes: worth independent of productivity, real support instead of forced positivity, and accommodations as something one deserves. According to the source, this matters because these attitudes "разрушать психическое здоровье и чувство собственной идентичности" (destroy mental health and the sense of one's own identity) [msg #615] and lead to burnout "из-за постоянных попыток соответствовать чужим ожиданиям" (because of constant attempts to meet other people's expectations) [msg #448].

## Core ideas

- **Internalized capitalism is a hidden driver of masking and low self-esteem in late-identified neurodivergent people.** Self-worth becomes built on the ability to hide one's traits and to be productive, so it is conditional and collapses into burnout in adulthood. Detailed in the *Life-stage model* below [msg #448, file #448].
- **Internalized ableism is the same mechanism turned on disability**: believing that only "normal" people are fully valuable, and treating one's own needs, symptoms and labels as shameful. Detailed in the *15 signs* [msg #615].
- **"High-functioning" measures usefulness to others, not wellbeing** — it rewards keeping the system running and not causing problems even at the cost of wearing oneself out [msg #687].
- **Self-criticism worsens autistic burnout**: the drive to meet social norms intensifies the inner critic, which deepens isolation and causes still more burnout [msg #23].
- **Forced positivity does not build resilience; support does** [msg #442].
- **Your worth is not defined by productivity or by meeting others' expectations** — you do not need to "contribute to society" to be valuable, and you deserve accommodations [msg #448, #602].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Internalized capitalism in hidden neurocomplexity — life-stage model (Lindsey Mackereth)
Source: a printable PDF by Lindsey Mackereth, translated by Нейропряность, titled "ИНТЕРНАЛИЗИРОВАННЫЙ КАПИТАЛИЗМ В СКРЫТОЙ НЕЙРОКОМПЛЕКСНОСТИ" with the subtitle "Влияние на выявление нейроотличий, маскинга и самооценку" (Internalized capitalism in hidden neurocomplexity: impact on identification of neurodivergence, masking and self-esteem). It traces how a neurodivergence that has not been identified develops through life stages — childhood, adolescence, early adulthood, middle adulthood — showing in parallel what happens to identification, masking and self-esteem. Use it to understand one's own history as a late-identified person and why burnout arrived when it did. (!) UNCLEAR: the PDF is a diagram and the transcription lost its layout, so the exact placement of some boxes on particular stages is reconstructed from reading order; the content of each box is complete [file #448].

**Overarching frame of the diagram.** It depicts the "Прямое и косвенное влияние капиталистических ценностей на человека в капиталистическом или близком к нему обществе" (direct and indirect influence of capitalist values on a person in a capitalist or near-capitalist society) [file #448].

**Stage 1 — Childhood: neurodivergence not yet identified.** The diagram lists why the neurodivergence stays unseen:
1. Less noticeable traits.
2. Denial of differences by the family of origin, because of lack of awareness and/or internalized ableism.
3. Early masking.
4. A general lack of awareness of how autism and/or ADHD present in groups other than white men — for example people assigned female at birth, BIPOC, transgender people, immigrants.
5. Giftedness: academic success or passing tests plus bias can cause autistic and/or ADHD traits to go unnoticed or be ignored.
[file #448]

**Self-esteem in childhood is probably unstable** because of factors tied to neurocomplexity:
1. Lack of validation from peers, family, teachers.
2. A feeling of being different inside, but without conscious understanding of why.
3. Parents who also heavily mask their traits and are themselves unidentified neurodivergent people, with or without internalized ableism.
4. Little or no access to accommodations at home or at school.
5. Social bullying and/or exclusion by peers, siblings or family (explicit or hidden).
Self-esteem may fall because the person internalizes differences in how their brain works as personal flaws and/or innate inferiority. Unconscious and/or conscious masking of neurodivergent traits begins, in order to avoid inner and outer conflicts by imitating neurotypicality [file #448].

**Stage 2 — Adolescence: neurodivergence ignored and/or denied.** Discomfort from masking and/or from an environment not adapted to neurocomplexity leads to a rise in secondary symptoms: anxiety, depression, substance use, eating disorders, financial difficulties from compulsive spending used as a coping strategy. Masking becomes entrenched and identity confusion arises [file #448].

**Stage 3 — Early adulthood: a period of relative "success".** A period begins of relative "success" in some academic or work environment that matches capitalist standards. For the first time in their life the person may experience:
1. Praise from authority figures (teachers, bosses, parents).
2. A sense of achievement and/or respect.
3. A sense of belonging to a community and/or acceptance by peers.
4. Consolidation of the masked version of oneself.
Self-esteem begins to rise thanks to capitalist successes at work or study — but it is based on the learned ability to hide one's traits and meet society's expectations. The person continues to pursue "success" within the capitalist system and builds a lifestyle around a growing income and the identity of a "хорошего работника/члена общества" (good worker / member of society). Self-esteem and self-image form around masking as neurotypical and around contribution to society/capitalism, and become dependent on them. Work and social usefulness turn into driving forces and daily stressors that regularly force the person beyond the limits of their masking capacity and the natural features of their nervous system [file #448].

**How internalized capitalism shows up at this stage:**
1. Excessive workload (overwork).
2. Guilt about rest and leisure.
3. A constant striving for more (can lead to consumerism or status-chasing).
4. Prioritizing personal success over the wellbeing of society.
[file #448]

**Stage 4 — Middle adulthood: late identification and burnout.** ADHD/autism is identified later in life, through professional diagnosis or self-diagnosis. Adult burnout: "Организм и психика требуют паузы после многих лет несоответствия потребностям" (body and psyche demand a pause after many years of their needs not being met). It may show up as:
1. Chronic fatigue.
2. Reduced tolerance to stimuli.
3. Difficulty with everyday tasks that used to come easily.
4. Problems with communication and/or concentration.
5. Social isolation.
6. Chronic pain not explained by illness.
[file #448]

**Way out — understanding and release from internalized capitalism through:**
1. Reclaiming the right to rest.
2. Exploring and rethinking personal values.
3. Developing a sense of community instead of competition.
4. Conscious consumption.
Then: explore and define your self-worth outside the frame of capitalism, taking your neurocomplexity into account; adapt your life to your sensory, social and cognitive needs/preferences; discover what really matters and what brings genuine joy. This may mean choosing a non-standard lifestyle that fits individual needs, which may be seen as "unsuccessful" by capitalist standards. The end point of the diagram is "Истинное соответствие собственной аутентичности и нейрокомплексности" (true alignment with one's own authenticity and neurocomplexity) [file #448].

### Six effects of internalized capitalism on neurodivergent people
From the channel's post introducing Mackereth's material. Definition first: internalized capitalism is the process by which people unconsciously absorb and accept capitalist values and ideology; in a capitalist society people are taught from childhood to value productivity, competition, material success and personal achievement, and these attitudes become deeply rooted in self-perception and daily behaviour — capitalist norms and expectations shape thinking, actions and personal goals. For late-identified neurodivergent people (autistic, ADHD or gifted) it "часто играет коварную роль в формировании стратегий маскировки и развитии самооценки" (often plays an insidious role in forming masking strategies and in the development of self-esteem) [msg #448]. Its effects:
1. It masks neurodivergence, forcing the person to adjust to "normality".
2. It lowers self-esteem because the person does not match imposed standards of success.
3. It leads to burnout through constant attempts to meet other people's expectations.
4. It hinders accepting and loving one's own traits.
5. It harms physical and mental health.
6. It makes developing an authentic personality harder.
[msg #448]

### Six steps to counter internalized capitalism
The post's list of what is important to resist this influence [msg #448]:
1. **Become aware** that internalized capitalism is present in your thinking — recognition is the starting point.
2. **Rethink your values and goals** — check which of them are actually yours rather than imposed standards of success.
3. **Accept your neuro-traits as part of a unique personality** rather than as defects to be hidden.
4. **Find a community of like-minded people** for support (the PDF frames this as community instead of competition).
5. **Work on raising self-esteem outside capitalist standards** — worth not measured by productivity or achievement.
6. **If needed, see a specialist** to work through these issues.
The post closes: "Помните, что ваша ценность не определяется продуктивностью или соответствием чужим ожиданиям. Каждый человек уникален и достоин любви и уважения просто за то, что он есть" (Remember that your worth is not determined by productivity or by meeting other people's expectations. Every person is unique and worthy of love and respect simply for being who they are) [msg #448].

### 7 signs of internalized capitalism (Method Creative)
A self-check card posted as an addition to the internalized-capitalism post, translated by the channel from Method Creative [img #449]:
1. Your self-esteem is tied to your work.
2. You put work above your health (mental and physical).
3. You only allow yourself hobbies that contribute to self-improvement or bring money.
4. You put work above relationships.
5. You feel ashamed when you take sick leave.
6. You feel guilty when you experience joy.
7. Rest feels like laziness.
[img #449, msg #449]

### 15 signs of internalized ableism toward yourself (@soyouredisabled)
Purpose: a self-check, presented as "твой знак (и, возможно, даже знак для окружающих тебя) проверить как у тебя дела" (your sign — and perhaps a sign for those around you — to check how you are doing). The post's premise: "Знание - это уже половина дела" (knowing is already half the battle). Ableism is defined as discrimination and prejudice against people with disabilities, based on the idea that only healthy and "normal" people are valuable and complete; internalized ableism can destroy mental health and the sense of one's own identity [msg #615]. The signs:
1. **Feeling "not disabled enough"** — believing your experience doesn't count because it doesn't match a certain "look" or "idea" of disability.
2. **Feeling discomfort or being triggered when others talk about their experience of disability.**
3. **Using negative expressions about yourself** — not treating yourself kindly or as if you are worthy.
4. **Comparing yourself with other people.**
5. **Feeling relief, pride or joy when people "forget" that you are disabled.**
6. **Hyper-independence.**
7. **Feeling shame or awkwardness about your disability.**
8. **Hiding your symptoms** — because otherwise it seems you won't be taken seriously, or to fit in, or to avoid judgement.
9. **Minimizing or hiding your needs for accommodations and accessibility** — trying to be a "удобным инвалидом" (convenient disabled person) who causes no trouble.
10. **Telling your medical history in too much detail every time you ask for support.**
11. **Downplaying your difficulties and suppressing your needs and emotions.**
12. **Feeling uncomfortable taking up space (attention, room).**
13. **Avoiding labels like "disabled" or "disability" because you fear what others will think.**
14. **Disconnecting from your body.**
15. **Feeling guilty for wanting joy and connection while being disabled.**
[msg #615]

## Detailed insights

### "High-functioning" as a measure of usefulness
Lindsey Mackereth, quoted by the channel: "«Высокофункциональность» — это не о том, как ты себя чувствуешь или насколько хорошо справляешься внутренне. Это о том, насколько ты полезен для других." ("High-functioning" is not about how you feel or how well you cope inside. It is about how useful you are to others.) It is about meeting external expectations, keeping the system working and not creating problems — "даже если для этого приходится идти на износ и превышать свои пределы" (even if that means wearing yourself out and exceeding your limits). This links the functioning label directly to the internalized-capitalism logic of worth-as-usefulness and to the early-adulthood "success" stage of her model [msg #687].

### Self-criticism and the inner critic in autistic burnout
The channel's introduction to a translated article states that self-criticism can hit neurodivergent people especially hard, creating extra obstacles on the way to self-acceptance and to overcoming autistic burnout. The mechanism given: neurodivergence affects how we perceive ourselves and the world; the striving to meet social norms and expectations can intensify self-criticism, which in turn deepens the feeling of isolation and being misunderstood and leads to even more burnout — a self-reinforcing loop. The article itself (on telegra.ph) covers what influences self-criticism and strategies to "усмирить нашего внутреннего критика" (tame our inner critic). (!) ### Beyond toxic positivity (Adam Grant)
The channel translated a note by Adam Grant ("Beyond toxic positivity", 11 Feb 2024). Its three points: in hard times, imposed positive thinking does not make people stronger — it only denies the reality the person faces ("In hard times, urging people to be positive doesn't boost their resilience. It denies their reality."). People who are suffering need not only moral support but a reliable footing to withstand life's upheavals ("People in pain don't need good vibes only. They need a hand to stay steady through all the vibrations."). Strength does not come from forced smiles; it comes when you feel genuinely supported ("Strength doesn't come from forced smiles. It comes from feeling supported.") [msg #442, img #442].

### Worth independent of contribution; what autistic people deserve
An affirmation translated from Tracy Rodriguez, addressed to autistic friends: you are valuable, important and worthy of support; "Вам не нужно «вносить вклад в общество», чтобы быть ценными" (You don't need to "contribute to society" to be valuable); you are valuable regardless of what you do or don't do; "we are in this together", and here there is support and safety. It names three things autistic people deserve:
1. Sensory accommodations.
2. Flexible ways of communicating.
3. An environment that supports your needs.
This is the direct counter-statement to the "contribution to society/capitalism" basis of self-worth described in Mackereth's model [msg #602].

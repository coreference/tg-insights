# Pacing and energy accounting

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's material on managing a limited supply of energy when you are neurodivergent and burnt out or living with chronic fatigue or pain: why burnout happens, the boom-bust cycle, pacing and several energy-accounting systems, a practical traffic-light calendar, a decision flowchart for "do it or rest", a printable daily health tracker, the Mankoski pain scale, and a short note on why exercise does not always help. According to the source, understanding the causes and becoming aware of the boom-bust cycle is "a very important step" in recovering from burnout, and pacing, self-care and seeking support help bring burnout under control and recover much faster [msg #52]. Energy accounting is presented as a strategy for managing limited energy resources that is especially relevant to people in autistic burnout, aimed at overcoming fatigue and improving quality of life through conscious management of energy [msg #30].

## Core ideas

- **Energy in burnout is a limited resource that has to be accounted for.** The channel frames energy accounting ("учёт энергии") as a way of consciously distributing scarce energy, adapted to the needs of autistic people, to overcome fatigue and recover from burnout [msg #30, img #30].
- **Burnout in neurodivergent people has identifiable drivers, and the boom-bust cycle is the one you can control.** Heightened sensitivity and constant strain from adapting are joined by the boom-bust pattern; living in that pattern makes recovery take longer and longer. Pacing is the counter-measure (see *Causes of neurodivergent burnout* and *Pacing*) [msg #52].
- **Pacing means dosing load to keep activity stable.** Lena Movshina's gloss: pacing comes from English "pace", it is about dosing load so neurodivergent people keep a more stable level of activity and do not get hooked on the boom-bust cycle [msg #132].
- **Decide each activity deliberately, weighing symptoms and recovery time.** The do-or-don't flowchart asks whether you can finish, whether it will worsen symptoms, and whether there is recovery time before the next thing; activity is not to be avoided entirely, but managed [msg #289, img #289].
- **Tracking reveals patterns, triggers and your actual energy capacity.** Daily tracking of pain, energy, symptoms, sleep and sensory triggers, paired with pacing, supports a personalised recovery strategy and better communication with doctors [msg #123, img #123].
- **Rating pain is itself hard for autistic people.** Pain scales such as Mankoski help, but interoception difficulties can make it especially hard to sense and judge pain intensity [msg #425].
- **Exercise can make things worse.** The channel shared a video on why one can feel worse after exercise (content not available) [msg #563].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Causes of neurodivergent burnout (three drivers)
From the article Nastya translated on pacing. Burnout is described as a serious problem in the lives of many neurodivergent people, often accompanied by chronic fatigue, exhaustion and chronic pain, which make the situation even harder. The article's answer to "Почему это происходит?" (*Why does this happen?*) lists three drivers [msg #52]:
1. **Heightened sensitivity ("Повышенная чувствительность").** The world around can be noisy, bright and chaotic, and that drains resources. Sensory load itself is an energy cost [msg #52].
2. **Overstrain ("Перенапряжение").** The constant struggle to adapt to a world that is not always friendly to our neurodivergence takes a lot of strength [msg #52].
3. **The boom-bust cycle ("Цикл", "взлет-падение").** Periods of intense activity take a lot of energy and are followed by a sharp drop in energy. When this becomes a way of life, increasingly more time is needed for recovery — i.e. the cycle compounds, lengthening each recovery [msg #52].

The article states that understanding the causes and recognising the boom-bust cycle is a very important step on the path to recovery [msg #52].

### Pacing (load distribution) against the boom-bust cycle
- **What it is.** Pacing ("пейсинг") is translated as a system of load distribution ("система распределения нагрузки"). Lena Movshina explains the word comes from English "pace" (temp) and describes it as the dosing of load that neurodivergent people need to keep a more stable level of activity and not get hooked on the boom-bust cycle [msg #52, #132].
- **What it is combined with.** The pacing article says that pacing, self-care practices and seeking support together will help you take burnout under control and recover much faster [msg #52].
- **Ways of keeping the energy account named in the article.** According to Lena Movshina's summary, the pacing article offers several methods of energy accounting: the spoon system ("система ложек"), the energy "checkbook" (energy "чековая книжка") and the traffic light ("светофор"). ### Traffic-light energy calendar (Lena Movshina's four-step setup)
Purpose: a visual way to keep energy accounting in an ordinary calendar so that each day is balanced. Lena Movshina chose it after reading the pacing article because she had spent recent weeks in a "kick-yourself–crash" cycle ("пинок себя-падение") and wanted to focus on helpful practices [msg #132].

**Rules ("Правила такие").** Every activity gets a colour:
- **Red** — strongly takes energy ("мощно забирает энергию").
- **Green** — gives energy ("дает энергию").
- **Yellow** — "видимо все остальное" (*apparently everything else*).
The goal is that every day adds up to a traffic light (a mix of colours); a whole day should not be entirely red [msg #132].

**How she implements it ("Как я буду делать"):**
1. **Calls are red by default.** Her calendar already holds calls, and they are red by default, because "Активность с людьми точно забирает энергию" (*Activity with people definitely takes energy*) [msg #132].
2. **Plan other activities around the calls, with their colour.** She schedules the remaining activities around the calls and enters each into the calendar in its colour [msg #132].
3. **Stop adding to a red-heavy day and block green time.** If she sees that a day already has many red items, she no longer adds tasks to it and puts a green block for the rest of the day [msg #132].
4. **Review the week at the end of it.** At the end of the week she looks at what the week was actually like, because otherwise she forgets that she "имела право устать" (*had the right to be tired*) — the review validates fatigue rather than leaving it unexplained [msg #132].

Her example calendar (photo) shows entries such as walking the dog and walking with a friend (10–11:30 AM), eating (11:30 AM–12:15 PM), making a cover image (12–2 PM), sleeping with the dog (2–4 PM), working (4:30–6:30 PM) and writing a post (6:30 PM), alongside a joking parallel "energy tracking" for the visiting dog that mostly sleeps (12:15–7:15 PM) [msg #132, img #132]. She committed publicly to tracking energy for a fixed period and reporting how it goes, inviting readers to join, e.g. by subscribing to each other's calendars [msg #132].

### Do or don't? Decision flowchart for activity and rest
"Делать или не делать? Схема принятия решений по распределению активности и отдыха" (*To do or not to do? A decision scheme for distributing activity and rest*), originally by Stickman Communications, translated by Fiu-fiu (Аутичные переводы), card design by Nastya (Нейропряность). It is used before an activity to decide whether to do it now, adapt it, or rest [msg #289, img #289]. Read from the card image:

1. **Question 1 — Смогу ли я с этими симптомами закончить дело?** (*With these symptoms, will I be able to finish the task?*)
   - **Yes** → go to Question 2.
   - **No or not sure** → go to the "make it more manageable" box (step 4) [img #289].
2. **Question 2 — Вызовет ли это увеличение боли, усталости и других симптомов?** (*Will it cause an increase in pain, fatigue and other symptoms?*)
   - **No** → go to Question 3.
   - **Yes or not sure** → go to the "make it more manageable" box (step 4) [img #289].
3. **Question 3 — Будет ли у меня достаточно времени на восстановление между этим делом и следующим?** (*Will I have enough time to recover between this task and the next?*)
   - **Probably** → **"Делай!"** (*Do it!*).
   - **No** → the stop outcome (step 5) [img #289].
4. **Box — Могу ли я сделать задачу более управляемой:** (*Can I make the task more manageable:*) — four options to check:
   - break it into small steps?
   - use aids/assistive tools ("средства помощи")?
   - ask for help with parts of the task?
   - do I have a strategy for stopping the task if it turns out to be too hard?
   - **Yes** (it can be made manageable) → go to Question 3 (recovery time).
   - **No** → the stop outcome (step 5) [img #289].
5. **Stop outcome — "Лучше не делать."** (*Better not to do it.*) It is acceptable to do it if it is something urgent or a special case, but this must not become routine [img #289].

Footnotes on the card: "*Упрощенная версия. Этот процесс может отличаться от человека к человеку." (*Simplified version. This process may differ from person to person.*) and "**Активность может усиливать симптомы, но цель - не избегать их, а научиться управлять ими. В поиске" … "оптимального подхода допустимы эксперименты, и со временем он может меняться." (*Activity can intensify symptoms, but the goal is not to avoid them but to learn to manage them. Experiments are acceptable in searching for the optimal approach, and it may change over time.*) [img #289]. The card is credited to StickmanCommunications.co.uk, "last reviewed May 2024" [img #289].

### Neurodivergent health tracker (daily sheet)
A free printable (or tablet-fillable) one-page daily tracker Nastya made: "Трэкер состояния здоровья для нейроотличных людей с хроническими болями" (*Health-condition tracker for neurodivergent people with chronic pain*). She hopes it becomes a companion for better understanding oneself and one's triggers and improving quality of life, giving more clarity, confidence and control over one's condition [msg #123].

**Why it is useful — four stated benefits [msg #123]:**
1. **Connection with the body and the present.** Daily tracking can help build a deeper connection with your own body and with the present moment.
2. **Identifying patterns and triggers.** Regular use lets you notice recurring patterns in your condition and identify the triggers that affect your wellbeing and lead to deterioration; this can help you develop effective strategies for managing those triggers.
3. **A burnout-recovery strategy.** Paired with pacing strategies, the tracker helps develop a personalised strategy for recovering from neurodivergent burnout: you better understand the size of your resource and which actions and lifestyle changes support your recovery.
4. **Documentation for doctors.** Keeping detailed documentation of your condition allows more effective interaction with doctors: you can give them accurate information, which eases diagnosis and choice of optimal treatment.

**Sections of the sheet (transcribed from the image) [img #123]:**
1. **Date** ("Дата").
2. **Pain level** ("Уровень боли") — circles 1 to 10.
3. **Pain symptoms** (Симптомы боли) — lines to describe pain, with a day/night column (День/ночь) and front and back body outlines ("Вид спереди", "Вид сзади") to mark location.
4. **How do you feel today?** ("Как вы себя сегодня чувствуете?") — six faces from sad to happy.
5. **Mood, clarity of mind, interoception** ("Настроение", "Ясность разума", "Интероцепция") — each rated 1–5.
6. **Energy level** ("Уровень энергии") — morning and evening ("Утром", "Вечером"), each on a five-step face/battery scale.
7. **Medications and dose** ("Медикаменты", "Доза") — table.
8. **Stress level** ("Уровень стресса") — none / low / medium / high / maximal ("Отсутствие", "Низкий", "Средний", "Высокий", "Максимальный").
9. **Other symptoms** (Другие симптомы) — tick boxes: anxiety, fatigue, apathy, insomnia, nausea, depression, irritability, anger, sore throat, diarrhoea, vomiting, bloating, exhaustion ("Измотанность"), swelling ("Отек"), goosebumps/tingling ("Мурашки"), temperature, plus blank boxes for your own.
10. **Meltdown / shutdown** (Мелтдаун / Шатдаун; OCR garbled, read from the image) — tick which, then columns for triggers, description and what helped ("Триггеры", "Описание", "Что помогло").
11. **Sleep** ("Сон") — duration, nightmares yes/no ("Ночные кошмары: Да/Нет"), quality 1–5.
12. **Physical activity** ("Физическая активность") — none / a little / a lot / max ("Никакой", "Немного", "Много", "Макс."), plus details.
13. **Sensory triggers** ("Сенсорные триггеры") — tick boxes: sounds, tastes, clothing, temperature, texture, movements, people, visual, light, smells, pain, environment, plus blank boxes.
14. **Notes** ("Заметки").

### Mankoski pain scale (0–10)
A numeric scale from 0 to 10, where 0 is no pain and 10 is pain so severe the person loses consciousness. Like other pain scales, it is a tool people with chronic pain can use to track pain systematically; it helps doctors better understand the intensity of a patient's pain, so that pain is adequately assessed and relieved. The channel cites painscale.com as its source [msg #425]. The levels:
- **0 — No pain** ("Отсутствие боли") [msg #425].
- **1 — Very minor annoyance** — occasional weak twinges. No medication needed [msg #425].
- **2 — Minor annoyance** — occasional strong twinges. No medication needed [msg #425].
- **3 — Annoying enough to be distracting.** Mild painkillers (aspirin, ibuprofen) are effective [msg #425].
- **4 — Can be ignored if you are really involved in your work**, but still distracting. Mild painkillers relieve pain for 3–4 hours [msg #425].
- **5 — Can't be ignored for more than 30 minutes.** Mild painkillers reduce pain for 3–4 hours [msg #425].
- **6 — Can't be ignored for any length of time**, but you can still go to work and take part in social life. Stronger painkillers (codeine, acetaminophen-hydrocodone) reduce pain for 3–4 hours [msg #425].
- **7 — Makes it hard to concentrate, interferes with sleep.** You can still function with effort. Stronger painkillers are only partially effective; the strongest painkillers (extended-release oxycodone, morphine) relieve the pain [msg #425].
- **8 — Physical activity severely limited.** You can read and talk with effort. Nausea and dizziness appear as factors of the pain. The strongest painkillers reduce pain for 3–4 hours [msg #425].
- **9 — Unable to speak.** Uncontrollable crying or moaning — close to delirium. The strongest painkillers are only partially effective [msg #425].
- **10 — Unconsciousness.** Pain causes fainting. The strongest painkillers are only partially effective [msg #425].

Caveat attached by the channel: "Вежливое напоминание: аутичным людям может быть особенно сложно чувствовать и определять интенсивность боли из-за проблем с интероцепцией." (*A gentle reminder: autistic people may find it especially hard to feel and determine the intensity of pain because of interoception problems.*) [msg #425]. The medication levels are the scale's descriptors, reproduced from the source; the channel gives no dosing advice [msg #425].

### Managing fatigue from illness or chronic conditions (guide)
Nastya, together with Nastya (Fiu-fiu) of Аутичные переводы, translated and adapted the guide "Как управлять своим уровнем энергии" (*How to manage your energy level*). Points stated in the post [msg #69, img #69]:
- **Audience and aim.** It is meant for people experiencing fatigue due to illness or a chronic condition; its aim is to help people manage their energy as well as possible so they can cope with more tasks.
- **Fatigue is individual.** Everyone's feeling of fatigue is unique and can have different causes, which is why it is hard to explain.
- **Definition.** Fatigue includes a lack of physical and/or cognitive energy that affects usual and desired activities ("отсутствие физической и/или когнитивной энергии, которое влияет на обычные и желаемые действия").
- **Don't rush back.** You may feel you need to return to usual activities as quickly as possible and feel pressure, but allow yourself time and do not hurry.
- **Provenance.** The guide was created after consultations with occupational therapists ("эрготерапевтами") and people living with fatigue.
(!) INCOMPLETE: the guide's actual techniques are in the linked teletype article, not in the source.

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The flowchart is explicitly a simplified version; the process may differ from person to person, and experimentation is acceptable while the approach changes over time [img #289].
- Activity may intensify symptoms; the stated goal is to learn to manage symptoms, not avoid them entirely — the flowchart's stop outcome still allows urgent or special cases, as long as they do not become routine [img #289].
- Pain self-rating has a limit for autistic people because of interoception difficulties [msg #425].

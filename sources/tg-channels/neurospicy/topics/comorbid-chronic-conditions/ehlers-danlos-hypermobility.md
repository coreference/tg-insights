# Ehlers-Danlos syndrome and hypermobility

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's educational material on Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (EDS) and hypermobility spectrum disorder (HSD): definitions, the 13 EDS subtypes and their genes, the full official hEDS diagnostic checklist (in Russian and English), the diagnostic pathway, a pre-appointment symptom questionnaire, the many comorbidities (pain, dislocations, digestive problems, dysautonomia, venous outflow from the brain, mast cell activation, anxiety), a candidate blood biomarker, and a patient-community overview of the situation in Russia. According to the source it matters because the condition affects many body systems at once, is largely invisible to others, takes on average about 12 years to diagnose, and is poorly known among Russian doctors [msg #79, #134, #222, #603].

## Core ideas

- **EDS is a group of 13 inherited connective-tissue conditions**; hypermobile EDS is by far the most common (90% of cases per #603) and the only major type whose gene is unknown [msg #204, #219, #603].
- **HSD is diagnosed when hypermobility symptoms are not explained by another condition such as EDS** — i.e. when someone has hypermobility and related symptoms but does not meet hEDS or other subtype criteria [msg #207, #221].
- **Connective tissue is everywhere, so EDS/HSD is multisystem**: pain, dislocations, GI, autonomic, sleep, mental-health, allergy and dental problems cluster together, which makes diagnosis hard and calls for multidisciplinary care [msg #79, #208].
- **hEDS diagnosis is clinical**: all three criteria (generalised hypermobility by Beighton score; two of features A/B/C; exclusion of other conditions) must be met at once [msg #375, #376].
- **A blood biomarker may be coming**: a 52 kDa fibronectin fragment was found in every hEDS/HSD participant of one study, but it is not yet validated [msg #222].
- **hEDS and HSD may merge**: the source reports the international "Road to 2026" project plans to treat them as one disease differing only in severity [msg #603].
- **Much of it is invisible**: people see someone smiling and working, not the pain, dislocations and dozens of hidden symptoms; the conditions also fluctuate day to day [msg #134, #209].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### 13 subtypes of EDS
The channel's comic states EDS is "группа из 13 наследственных заболеваний, которые влияют на соединительную ткань" (*a group of 13 inherited diseases that affect connective tissue*) [img #204]. Post #219 defines EDS as a group of inherited connective-tissue diseases characterised by joint hypermobility, increased skin stretchiness, easy bruising, a tendency to form scars and other soft-tissue anomalies; severity ranges from minor joint instability to life-threatening complications. The 13 subtypes are classified by clinical presentation, genetic cause and inheritance pattern [msg #219]. The comic card lists all 13 types: arthrochalasia, brittle cornea syndrome, kyphoscoliotic, musculocontractural, spondylodysplastic, cardio-vascular, classical, classical-like, dermatosparaxis, hypermobile, myopathic, periodontal and vascular [img #205]. Post #219 details the main ones:

1. **Classical EDS (кСЭД / cEDS)** — symptoms: skin hyperelasticity, atrophic scars, joint hypermobility. Gene: mutations in COL5A1 or COL5A2 [msg #219].
2. **Hypermobile EDS (гСЭД / hEDS)** — symptoms: joint hypermobility, frequent dislocations/subluxations, chronic pain. Gene: "Конкретная генетическая причина в настоящее время неизвестна" (*the specific genetic cause is currently unknown*) [msg #219].
3. **Vascular EDS (сСЭД / vEDS)** — thin translucent skin, extensive bruising, marked fragility of arteries, intestine and uterus. Gene: COL3A1 [msg #219].
4. **Kyphoscoliotic EDS (ксСЭД)** — congenital muscle hypotonia, scoliosis, eye fragility. Gene: PLOD1 [msg #219].
5. **Arthrochalasia EDS (аСЭД)** — severe joint hypermobility, congenital hip dislocation, skin hyperelasticity. Genes: COL1A1 or COL1A2 [msg #219].
6. **Dermatosparaxis EDS (дСЭД)** — marked skin fragility, sagging skin, easy bruising. Gene: ADAMTS2 [msg #219].
7. **Cardio-vascular EDS (кв-СЭД)** — severe progressive heart-valve disorders. Gene: COL1A2 [msg #219].
8. **Classical-like EDS (кп-СЭД)** — skin hyperelasticity, joint hypermobility and easy bruising but without atrophic scars. Gene: TNXB [msg #219].
9. **Periodontal EDS (пСЭД)**, 10. **brittle cornea syndrome (СХР)**, 11. **spondylodysplastic EDS (сд-СЭД)**, 12. **musculocontractural EDS (мк-СЭД)**, 13. **myopathic EDS (мСЭД)** — named only; the post says each has unique manifestations and genetic bases and promises to cover them later. (!) INCOMPLETE: symptoms and genes of these five are not given in the source [msg #219].

Although the types differ, the comic lists the most frequent shared signs: joint hypermobility and instability, skin hyperextensibility, tissue fragility (drawn as an arm in a sling and bandages), extreme fatigue, and chronic pain [img #206].

### Four forms of hypermobility spectrum disorder
HSD covers a range of conditions linked to joint hypermobility and is considered when a patient has hypermobility plus accompanying symptoms but does not meet the criteria for hEDS or another EDS subtype. It includes four forms [msg #221]:
1. Generalised joint hypermobility — Г-РСГ (G-HSD).
2. Peripheral hypermobility — П-РСГ (P-HSD).
3. Localised hypermobility — Л-РСГ (L-HSD).
4. Historical hypermobility — И-РСГ (H-HSD).
(!) INCOMPLETE: the source does not define how the four forms differ [msg #221].

### Diagnostic pathway for EDS and HSD
Post #221 describes diagnosis as a combination of clinical assessment, genetic testing and differential diagnosis, in four key stages [msg #221]:
1. **Clinical assessment.** (a) History: a detailed history focused on joint dislocations/subluxations, skin problems and family history. (b) Physical exam: assessing joint hypermobility with the Beighton score, skin elasticity and other characteristic signs [msg #221].
2. **Genetic testing.** Identifying specific mutations linked to the different EDS subtypes by sequencing the relevant genes, for example COL5A1, COL5A2, COL3A1 (i.e. the classical and vascular genes — hEDS has no known gene, see *13 subtypes*) [msg #221].
3. **Imaging.** MRI or ultrasound to assess joint and soft-tissue abnormalities and potential vascular problems [msg #221].
4. **Differential diagnosis.** Excluding conditions with similar symptoms, such as Marfan syndrome, osteogenesis imperfecta and other connective-tissue diseases [msg #221].

### 2017 diagnostic criteria for hypermobile EDS (checklist)
The channel shared the official checklist in English (v9, "for doctors across all disciplines to be able to diagnose EDS") and a Russian adaptation by the NGO «Не просто усталость», which states "Этот диагностический протокол предназначен для диагностики гСЭД врачами любых специальностей" (*this diagnostic protocol is intended for diagnosing hEDS by doctors of any specialty*) [file #375, #376]. The form has fields for patient name, date of birth, evaluating doctor and date of visit. Core rule: "The clinical diagnosis of hypermobile EDS needs the simultaneous presence of all criteria, 1 and 2 and 3." [file #376] — in Russian "необходимо одновременное наличие всех критериев: 1, 2 и 3" [file #375].

**Criterion 1 — Generalised joint hypermobility.** Beighton score out of 9; one of the following must be selected [file #375, #376]:
- ≥6 for pre-pubertal children and adolescents;
- ≥5 for pubertal men and women up to age 50;
- ≥4 for men and women over 50.

If the Beighton score is one point below the age- and sex-specific cut-off, two or more of these five questions must also be answered "yes" to meet the criterion [file #375, #376]:
1. "Can you now (or could you ever) place your hands flat on the floor without bending your knees?"
2. "Can you now (or could you ever) bend your thumb to touch your forearm?"
3. "As a child, did you amuse your friends by contorting your body into strange shapes or could you do the splits?"
4. "As a child or teenager, did your shoulder or kneecap dislocate on more than one occasion?"
5. "Do you consider yourself “double jointed”?" (Russian: "Считаете ли вы себя сверхгибкими?" — *do you consider yourself super-flexible?*)

**Criterion 2 — two or more of features A, B or C must be present** [file #375, #376].

*Feature A* — five or more of these 12 must be present (total scored /12) [file #375, #376]:
1. Unusually soft or velvety skin.
2. Mild skin hyperextensibility.
3. Unexplained striae distensae or rubae (stretch marks) on the back, groins, thighs, breasts and/or abdomen in adolescents, men or pre-pubertal women, without a history of significant gain or loss of body fat or weight.
4. Bilateral piezogenic papules of the heel.
5. Recurrent or multiple abdominal hernia(s) (Russian version: umbilical, inguinal, femoral).
6. Atrophic scarring involving at least two sites, without the truly papyraceous ("cigarette-paper") and/or hemosideric scars seen in classical EDS.
7. Pelvic floor, rectal and/or uterine prolapse in children, men or nulliparous women without a history of morbid obesity or other known predisposing condition.
8. Dental crowding and a high or narrow palate.
9. Arachnodactyly, defined by one or more of: (i) positive wrist sign (Walker sign) on both sides, (ii) positive thumb sign (Steinberg sign) on both sides.
10. Arm span-to-height ratio ≥1.05.
11. Mitral valve prolapse, mild or greater, based on strict echocardiographic criteria.
12. Aortic root dilatation with Z-score >+2.

*Feature B* — positive family history: one or more first-degree relatives independently meeting the current hEDS criteria [file #375, #376].

*Feature C* — at least one of [file #375, #376]:
1. Musculoskeletal pain in two or more limbs, recurring daily for at least 3 months.
2. Chronic, widespread pain for ≥3 months.
3. Recurrent joint dislocations or frank joint instability, in the absence of trauma.

**Criterion 3 — all of the following prerequisites must be met** [file #375, #376]:
1. Absence of unusual skin fragility — if present, other types of EDS should be considered.
2. Exclusion of other heritable and acquired connective-tissue disorders, including autoimmune rheumatologic conditions. In a patient with an acquired connective-tissue disease (e.g. lupus, rheumatoid arthritis), an additional hEDS diagnosis requires meeting both Features A and B of Criterion 2, and Feature C (chronic pain and/or instability) cannot be counted in that situation.
3. Exclusion of alternative diagnoses that may also include joint hypermobility through hypotonia and/or connective-tissue laxity: neuromuscular disorders (e.g. Bethlem myopathy), other hereditary connective-tissue disorders (other EDS types, Loeys-Dietz syndrome, Marfan syndrome) and skeletal dysplasias (e.g. osteogenesis imperfecta) — "include, but are not limited to" these. Exclusion may be based on history, physical examination and/or molecular genetic testing as indicated.

## Detailed insights

### Comorbidities and their prevalence
Post #79 (by Настя) summarises the most common comorbidities of EDS/HSD with approximate prevalence and, where available, ethnic differences [msg #79]:
1. **Chronic pain** — persistent, often musculoskeletal; a hallmark of EDS and HSD, affecting about 90% [msg #79].
2. **Joint dislocations and subluxations** — common in hypermobile EDS and HSD, about 70–80% of patients [msg #79].
3. **Gastrointestinal disorders** — IBS, gastroparesis, reflux; about 70% of people with EDS report GI symptoms. IBS is more common in people of European descent, but GI problems are seen in all ethnic groups [msg #79].
4. **Dysautonomia** — autonomic nervous system dysfunction including POTS; affects 50–80% of people with hEDS. POTS is more common in people of European descent though diagnosed in all groups [msg #79].
5. **Fatigue** — chronic, often severe and disabling exhaustion in about 80% [msg #79].
6. **Sleep disorders** — insomnia, sleep apnea, unrefreshing sleep; about 70% [msg #79].
7. **Anxiety and depression** — anxiety about 70%, depression about 50% [msg #79].
8. **Osteoarthritis** — degenerative joint disease in about 50%, especially with joint hypermobility; rates are higher among people of European descent and some Asian populations [msg #79].
9. **Cardiovascular problems** — mitral valve prolapse, aortic root dilation, aneurysms; about 30% of people with vascular and classical EDS [msg #79].
10. **Dental and orthodontic problems** — high palate, crowded teeth, gum disease; a "significant share" (no figure) [msg #79].
11. **Neurological problems** — migraines, Chiari malformation, tethered cord syndrome; about 50% [msg #79].
12. **Allergies and MCAS** — about 50% report allergy or MCAS symptoms [msg #79].

The post's conclusion: these comorbidities can affect several body systems, which complicates diagnosis; reported prevalence varies with cultural, environmental and genetic factors; and a comprehensive multidisciplinary approach is needed to improve outcomes and quality of life [msg #79]. The comic lists the associated problems as headaches, GI problems, autonomic nervous system dysfunction, mast cell activation syndrome and anxiety disorders [img #208].

### Digestive system in hEDS
EDS is a group of hereditary conditions of connective tissue; the hypermobile type is the one most often linked to digestive problems. In hEDS the collagen-rich connective tissue becomes more "stretchy", which affects not only skin and joints but the whole digestive tract [msg #74]. Common symptoms: acid reflux; chronic indigestion; constipation or diarrhoea; abdominal pain and bloating; nausea and vomiting. Symptoms vary greatly between people and can involve both the upper and lower GI tract [msg #74]. The linked article (translated by the channel, "GI problems in hEDS and HSD") also explains how autonomic dysfunction such as POTS can contribute, and lists tests and treatment strategies doctors may offer. ### Dislocations and subluxations
Slides adapted from The Ehlers-Danlos Society, made because the author often got questions about what dislocations and subluxations are [msg #124]:
- **Dislocation (вывих)**: "кости в суставе полностью отделяются друг от друга" (*the bones in a joint fully separate from each other*) [img #125].
- **Subluxation (подвывих)**: the bones shift relative to each other more than they normally should, but without full separation [img #126].
- **Where**: can occur in various parts of the body; the most common sites include the temporomandibular joint, shoulder, thumb and kneecap [img #127].
- **Consequences**: both can be painful and damage tissue in and around the joint; a dislocation can be extremely painful and distressing and requires urgent medical care [img #128].
- **Treatment**: they are treatable, which lowers the risk of recurrence and reduces pain and injury. The most common and effective treatment is physical strengthening, supported by taping and supportive aids, under the guidance of a physiotherapist [img #129].

### Management of EDS/HSD
Symptom management aims to relieve symptoms and prevent complications, and may include: pain management, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and support in education and employment [img #210]. EDS and HSD can affect people of every age, race, gender and body type; understanding the symptoms, support and adaptations can be very helpful [img #211]. The conditions fluctuate and affect a person day to day — the comic shows a hypermobile character curling small dumbbells while watching others lift heavy barbells, thinking "Как бы я хотела тоже так уметь..." (*how I wish I could do that too...*) [img #209].

### Invisibility of hypermobility
A card titled "The complexity of hypermobility: what people see and don't see" (originally by @hypermobilityMD and @positivitypotsedsmcas) [img #134]. What people see: a person who smiles and may look happy; who seems fine; who gets things done; who works; who leaves the house. What they don't see:
- pain; dislocations; osteoporosis; subluxations; sprains; osteoarthritis;
- skin tears; scar formation; skin stretchiness; skin softness; poor healing; easy bruising;
- muscle spasms; Chiari malformation; severe fatigue; arthralgia; flat feet; fibromyalgia;
- constipation; diarrhoea; abdominal pain; bloating; reflux; nausea; vomiting; irritable bowel syndrome; motility disorder;
- organ rupture; prolapse; vascular compressions; vitamin deficiencies;
- mast cell activation syndrome; sleep disorders; chronic fatigue syndrome;
- anxiety; depression; temporomandibular disorders; postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome [img #134].

### Venous outflow from the brain in EDS
A recent study (reported by the channel) found that in people with connective-tissue disorders such as EDS, impaired venous outflow from the brain can present in a unique way: these patients have more severe symptoms and worse quality of life than "ordinary" patients with venous-outflow problems. Common symptoms include headaches, dizziness and drug allergies. The researchers stress that doctors need to be aware of these features to improve diagnosis and treatment [msg #135].

### Candidate blood biomarker for hEDS/HSD
A study funded by The Ehlers-Danlos Society and published in the American Journal of Medical Genetics found potential blood biomarkers for hEDS and HSD. This matters because diagnosis has been hard for lack of established lab tests and molecular markers [msg #222].
- Design: blood samples from 466 adults, including 94 with hEDS and 80 with HSD [msg #222].
- Finding 1: a specific 52 kDa fibronectin fragment was present in the blood of every person with hEDS and HSD, and notably absent in healthy controls, people with other EDS types and people with various forms of arthritis [msg #222].
- Finding 2: its consistent presence in both hEDS and HSD suggests a shared pathophysiology, which questions the distinction between the two conditions [msg #222].
- Finding 3: a collagen I fragment was also found in everyone with hEDS and HSD, but it was present in other conditions as well (so it is less specific) [msg #222].
- Meaning: could lead to the first blood test for hEDS/HSD, a more reliable tool for clinicians, and shorter time to diagnosis, currently about 12 years on average [msg #222].
- Next steps: the authors stress that other researchers must confirm the results in further hEDS, HSD and control groups before diagnostic use. The Society is funding confirmation work. No diagnostic lab offers the test yet; if validated it could serve diagnosis, treatment trials and insight into causes [msg #222].

### EDS and connective tissue dysplasia in Russia
A post forwarded from the Russian patient channel Невидимые зебры (*Invisible zebras*) [msg #603]:
- **Scale (expert estimates voiced at the all-Russian congress «Человек и лекарство»)**: about 30,000 people in Russia with EDS of varying severity, and up to 30 million with undifferentiated connective tissue dysplasia (НДСТ) — "почти каждый 5-й человек" (*almost every 5th person*) has signs of connective tissue dysplasia. Experts stressed that connective-tissue disorders remain severely underestimated in Russian healthcare, a significant public-health problem given high prevalence and severe, sometimes life-threatening cases [msg #603, img #603].
- **Prevalence facts**: 90% of all EDS cases are the hypermobile type; 10 EDS types are ultra-rare (about 1 case per million); manifestations of hEDS often overlap with НДСТ signs [msg #603].
- **Proposed merger**: international experts in "Road to 2026" propose revising the boundary; according to "new international data" hEDS and HSD (equated in the post with НДСТ) are planned to be treated as one disease differing only in severity, which "может радикально изменить подход к диагностике, лечению и социальной поддержке" (*could radically change the approach to diagnosis, treatment and social support*) [msg #603].
- **Awareness gap**: many Russian medical workers are still unfamiliar even with the official 2017 EDS classification [msg #603].
- **Why it matters — the problems listed** [msg #603]:
  1. Clinical guidelines for EDS and connective tissue dysplasia need to be created in Russia.
  2. Low awareness of the prevalence and its many nuances: risks, complications, chronic pain.
  3. Difficulty getting qualified care, including free specialised rehabilitation, orthoses and assistive devices under ОМС, and access to a vertical (upright) MRI.
  4. No protocols for managing pregnancies and surgeries in EDS patients.
  5. No free genetic testing under ОМС when EDS is suspected after seeing a geneticist.
  6. Difficulty obtaining disability status and social support, because there is no clear grading of connective-tissue involvement severity.
  7. No specialised connective tissue dysplasia centres across the country with qualified doctors and systematic diagnosis and treatment.
  8. Low awareness of vascular and neurological risks, risk of sudden death and pregnancy complications.
  9. Underestimation of severity: connective tissue is key to the whole body, and in EDS its disorders can be systemic and affect many organ systems individually.
- **Conclusion of the post**: diagnosis and care must become a systemic healthcare task; specialised centres, clinical guidelines and better awareness among doctors are needed [msg #603].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- (!) CONTRADICTION: #219 and #204 say EDS has 13 subtypes, while #603 says "10 типов" are ultra-rare; these are compatible only if hEDS plus two others are not ultra-rare, which the source does not explain [msg #219, #603].
- (!) UNCLEAR: #603 equates HSD with Russian НДСТ ("гипермобильные спектры нарушений / недифференцированная дисплазия соединительной ткани") and states the hEDS/HSD unity "уже доказывается" (*is already being proven*); this is the patient community's framing, and the biomarker study in #222 only "suggests" a shared pathophysiology, pending replication [msg #222, #603].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: the prevalence percentages in #79 are given without the underlying studies [msg #79].
- Translation discrepancies between the two hEDS checklists: the Russian version writes the arm span ratio as "= 1.05" where the English has "≥1.05"; it calls the pain/instability feature excluded in acquired CTD "Особенность В" while labelling it "Особенность С" elsewhere (English: Feature C); and in Criterion 1 the Russian says "до 50 лет" without "pubertal". The English original is the reference [file #375, #376].
- The biomarker test is not available in any diagnostic lab and requires confirmation in other cohorts [msg #222].
- The venous-outflow study is reported without sample size or authors [msg #135].
- The hypermobility questionnaire is described as an initial screening aid for doctors, not a diagnosis; only part 1 was posted [msg #245].

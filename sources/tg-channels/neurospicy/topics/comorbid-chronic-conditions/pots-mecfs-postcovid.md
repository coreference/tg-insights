# POTS, dysautonomia, ME/CFS and post-COVID

## What this covers

This topic collects what the channel Нейропряность (a neurodivergence channel) and the posts it forwarded taught about chronic conditions that often accompany neurodivergence: POTS and dysautonomia, small fiber neuropathy (SFN), ME/CFS and post-COVID syndrome with post-exertional malaise, and a personal fatigue-signs scale from an author living with functional neurological disorder (FND). It also covers heat intolerance — a warning about antidepressants in hot weather and a detailed cooling playbook — and crowdsourced data on low-dose naltrexone. The sources stress that these conditions are frequently misdiagnosed, poorly known among doctors (especially in Russia), psychologized, and that the common advice "just exercise" can be harmful [msg #620, #287, #309, #834].

## Core ideas

- **POTS is a form of dysautonomia with a very broad symptom picture.** Because it shows up across many body systems, it is easily misdiagnosed; a careful evaluation helps early diagnosis and appropriate treatment (full checklist in *POTS possible-symptoms checklist*) [msg #620, #113, #114].
- **Post-exertional malaise is a pathology, not deconditioning.** A Dutch study forwarded by the channel found mitochondrial dysfunction, muscle damage, immune cells in muscle and microclots after exertion in post-COVID patients; people with post-COVID should avoid intense physical load [msg #287].
- **ME/CFS is a severe, disabling disease, not ordinary tiredness.** Its core is fatigue not relieved by rest, PEM, and cognitive/sleep/autonomic problems and pain; mechanisms are poorly understood and no treatment has been developed [msg #834].
- **Small fiber neuropathy can explain pain, autonomic symptoms, exercise and upright intolerance, fatigue and cognitive problems,** and is found in about half of post-COVID patients with pain in one study; it may be autoimmune or genetic (hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis) [msg #309, #385].
- **Fatigue escalates through recognisable personal levels.** Tracking your own signs at medium, high and full-exhaustion levels lets you see where you are [msg #203].
- **Heat is a specific risk.** SSRIs/SNRIs raise the risk of overheating and dehydration, and combining cooling tools works far better than any single one [msg #682, #683].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### POTS possible-symptoms checklist by body system (10 groups)
Purpose: to recognise that a scattered set of complaints may belong to POTS. The channel published it first as a series of image cards ("СПОТ часть 1", cards titled «ВОЗМОЖНЫЕ СИМПТОМЫ СПОТ», "possible symptoms of POTS") and later as a full text post [msg #113, #620].

Definitions given first:
- **Dysautonomia** is a state in which the autonomic (vegetative) nervous system, responsible for the body's automatic processes, works incorrectly, which can lead to a wide range of symptoms. POTS is one variety of autonomic dysautonomia [msg #620, #113].
- **POTS** is a state in which your heart beats faster than usual when you move from sitting or lying to standing. It is a type of orthostatic intolerance that presents with diverse symptoms, which can lead to misdiagnosis; a thorough evaluation can help early diagnosis and appropriate treatment [msg #620, #114].

The ten symptom groups (all "possible" symptoms — not everyone has all):
1. **Psychiatric:** anxiety related to the symptoms, hyperexcitability (гипервозбудимость), depression [msg #620].
2. **Cardiovascular:** increased heart rate, palpitations, chest pain or discomfort, high or low blood pressure [msg #620].
3. **Gastrointestinal (ЖКТ):** nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, bloating, abdominal cramps [msg #620].
4. **Genitourinary:** frequent urination, difficult urination [msg #620, #118].
5. **Extremities:** increased sweating in the limbs, cold and clammy feet and hands [msg #620, #117].
6. **Reduced cerebral blood flow:** brain fog, worsening memory, headache, pre-fainting (presyncope), blurred vision, dizziness, fainting, poor sleep quality [msg #620, #116].
7. **General:** fatigue, weakness, exercise intolerance, muscle pain, tremor [msg #620, #115].
8. **Lungs:** rapid breathing, shortness of breath [msg #620].
9. **Skin:** poor thermoregulation, increased or decreased sweating [msg #620].
10. **Legs and feet:** tremor, weakness, oedema, heaviness in the legs, colour change, cold, leg pain, restless legs syndrome, neuropathic pain [msg #620].

(!) INCOMPLETE: the 2024 card series is labelled "часть 1" (part 1) and only the cards for the general, cerebral-blood-flow, extremities and genitourinary groups are in this set; the 2025 text post gives the full list [msg #113, #620].

### Three-level fatigue signs scale (medium / high / full exhaustion)
Purpose: a personal early-warning list of how fatigue shows up, written by the author of a channel about functional neurological disorder (FND), inspired by a similar list from blogger Лена. It is offered as a model: readers are invited to compile their own signs [msg #203].

Levels: 🟡 medium fatigue; 🟣 high fatigue; 🔵 full exhaustion, defined as "several days in the mode of medium and high fatigue". Rule: as fatigue grows, items from the previous level carry over into the next one («пункты из предыдущего уровня» — "items from the previous level" — move on), so higher levels include the lower-level signs [msg #203].

**Medium (🟡):**
1. Remembering sad stories; philosophical questions start to worry her more [msg #203].
2. Harder to control tics and FND symptoms [msg #203].
3. The world feels too bright and too loud (sensory overload) [msg #203].
4. Much more stimming [msg #203].
5. Headache (often with physical fatigue) [msg #203].
6. Hard to sit still [msg #203].

**High (🟣):**
1. Walking in circles and barely being aware of it [msg #203].
2. Becoming very hot (feeling of heat) [msg #203].
3. Harder to control what she says to people — oversharing [msg #203].
4. Sad thoughts appear; she says she wants to die although in the moment she understands it is all untrue [msg #203].
5. Echolalia increases — speaking in templates and quotes, repeating after others [msg #203].
6. Not keeping word order and using wrong word endings [msg #203].
7. Hard to formulate thoughts; not understanding English / being able to speak only in a language her brain picked at random [msg #203].
8. Sometimes instead of sleepiness she cannot relax — a feeling of inner trembling [msg #203].
9. Cannot start tasks or stop doing them (in particular, scrolling the phone) [msg #203].
10. Cannot complete all items of her routine [msg #203].
11. Becomes irritable and tearful (with "chronic fatigue" she can cry over small things all day) [msg #203].
12. Hard to show empathy [msg #203].

**Full exhaustion (🔵):**
1. On waking she stares at the wall and does not grasp the urgency of tasks [msg #203].
2. Hard to communicate out loud [msg #203].
3. Food becomes tasteless [msg #203].
4. «Хочется ЛЕЖАТЬ» ("I want to LIE DOWN") [msg #203].
5. Body aches [msg #203].

### ME/CFS core symptom triad
From a forwarded awareness post titled «Иногда это не просто усталость!» ("Sometimes it's not just tiredness!"). Chronic fatigue is common in the modern rhythm of life, but it can hide one of the most disabling diseases — myalgic encephalomyelitis / chronic fatigue syndrome — and the similar post-COVID syndrome. ME/CFS is described as a severe disease with exhausting symptoms that limit physical and mental activity [msg #834]. Main symptoms:
1. Severe fatigue that does not pass after rest and limits normal functioning [msg #834].
2. Worsening after physical or mental effort that was previously tolerated normally — «постнагрузочное истощение» (post-exertional malaise) [msg #834].
3. Problems with concentration and memory, "brain fog", sleep disturbances, autonomic (vegetative) disturbances, pain [msg #834].

Patients face low awareness among doctors, psychologization of their illness and absence of social support measures. Despite research abroad, very little is known about the mechanisms of these syndromes and no treatment has been developed [msg #834].

### Small fiber neuropathy symptom map
Definition: SFN is a disease in which small-diameter nerve fibres responsible for sensation and/or the body's autonomic functions are damaged [msg #309].

Two distribution types (shown on the infographic as two figures) [msg #309, img #309]:
- **Length-dependent type:** usually begins with unpleasant sensations in the feet and palms that may spread up the body.
- **Non-length-dependent type:** in about a quarter of patients the small fibres are affected in different parts of the body from the start.

Symptoms on the infographic [img #309]:
1. Pain syndrome.
2. Sensory disturbances.
3. Dry eyes and dry mouth.
4. Excessive or insufficient sweating.
5. Constipation, diarrhoea.
6. Gastroparesis (slowed stomach emptying).
7. Dizziness and/or rapid heartbeat when moving the body to upright.

The text adds the pain qualities patients describe — burning, stabbing, cutting, shooting pains — and, because small fibres "conduct" many autonomic functions, symptoms such as orthostatic intolerance, sweating disturbance, irritable bowel syndrome and gastroparesis [msg #309].

Mechanism: when the small fibres that regulate blood vessels are damaged, blood supply to the muscles is impaired and blood return to the heart from the lower body decreases. According to the post, this may explain intolerance of exertion and of the upright position, as well as fatigue and cognitive impairment in a significant share of patients [msg #309].

Course: in some patients the disease progresses to a severe pain syndrome, severe hypotension and gastroparesis, which can lead to deep disability and severe physical suffering; few doctors in Russia know about SFN [msg #309].

### Criteria for genetic testing for hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis
SFN is not caused only by autoimmunity and other factors; it can also stem from genetic pathologies such as hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis. In this disease a genetic mutation makes the protein transthyretin unstable, so it accumulates in tissues in an abnormal form; this can damage nerve fibres including small ones, so patients may have pain, sensory disturbances and dysautonomia [msg #385].

The infographic gives the grounds for genetic testing as: **(A or B) + at least one of the C conditions** [img #385]:
- **A.** Progressive polyneuropathy affecting long nerve fibres; **or**
- **B.** Autonomic dysfunction: pain syndrome; orthostatic hypotension; orthostatic tachycardia; chronic diarrhoea and/or chronic constipation; erectile dysfunction.
- **plus one or more of C:** family history of hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis; unexplained weight loss of 5 kg or more; heart rhythm disturbances; vitreous opacity (clouding of the eye's vitreous body); impaired kidney function.

The post states that in Russia genetic testing for this disease is done free of charge when it is suspected, regardless of where the person lives, with the samples sent by post; people with SFN symptoms were invited to apply through a form (the application link itself is logistics and is dropped here) [msg #385].

### Summer cooling kit and the six combination rules
Purpose: practical heat management from the channel author (posted with tags #жара #терморегуляция, "heat", "thermoregulation"). Her key principle: «Эффективнее всего будет комбинирование этих аксессуаров.» ("Combining these accessories will be the most effective.") [msg #683]

**Accessories (11) and what each does:**
1. **Cooling towel** — soak in water and wring out; keeps the body cool, especially if there is wind [msg #683].
2. **Cooling blanket** — made of a special fabric that feels cool on contact [msg #683].
3. **Water spray bottle** — easy to carry in a backpack; do not use in direct sunlight [msg #683].
4. **Neck fan** — especially good in stuffy rooms and buses [msg #683].
5. **Under-clothing fan** — good when there is no wind outside [msg #683].
6. **Floor fan + basin of water** — extra cooling at home [msg #683].
7. **Cooling masks** — long-lasting local cooling effect [msg #683].
8. **Cooling patches** — can be stuck on the body [msg #683].
9. **Thermos bottle for water/ice** — take it on the road for access to cold water or ice [msg #683].
10. **Umbrella** (preferably not black and not transparent) — a barrier against direct sun [msg #683].
11. **Hand fan** — an alternative to the neck fan [msg #683].

Additional measures: a cold shower; electrolytes [msg #683].

**Why combinations beat single items — six rules:**
1. **Wet towel + neck fan.** A neck fan alone makes little difference in heat. Wet the cooling towel and "hang" it on your head and the neck fan's effect becomes much more noticeable at once. Spraying water on the face does the same — the neck fan then cools noticeably more [msg #683].
2. **Where to spray.** The spray bottle can be used on the face/neck, arms/legs and even on the hair. Hers produces a fine mist, so it does not feel unpleasant; she carries it in her backpack [msg #683].
3. **Best outdoor combo:** under-clothing fan (the kind that clips onto a belt and circulates air under clothes) + cooling towel on shoulders/head + neck fan. If there is no direct sun, add the water spray for a stronger effect [msg #683].
4. **No air conditioning at home:** if an ordinary fan is not enough, put a basin of cold water in front of it. For more cooling add ice or reusable cooling packs; buy several, keep them in the freezer and swap them through the day for continuous effect [msg #683].
5. **Frozen cooling masks:** keep several in the freezer (in a container, to avoid an unpleasant smell). Apply to the forehead/crown, the back of the head, or even the chest. The author's reasoning: these places are associated with the vagus nerve, which helps the nervous system switch to rest mode, and by cooling this nerve "we support its flexibility" (author's claim) [msg #683].
6. **Electrolyte balance.** In heat we lose a lot of fluid through breathing and sweating, which can cause migraines, weakness and presyncope, so rehydration is essential. Plain water may not be retained in cells, so electrolytes matter. Options: ready-made (preferably sugar-free), homemade, or Celtic salt as an electrolyte — a small pinch per litre of water, or a few small crystals under the tongue followed by plenty of water. The author adds the caveat that you should do your own research before using these tips, and reports: «Лично мне этот способ очень помог избавиться от мигреней и предобморочного состояния в жару.» ("Personally this method helped me a lot to get rid of migraines and presyncope in the heat.") [msg #683]

She closes that the principle is to experiment with different combinations to help your body cool down [msg #683].

## Detailed insights

### Post-exertional malaise in post-COVID: the Dutch muscle-biopsy study
The forwarded post (from the «Астра» charity, text by Анастасия and Нейропряность) opens with what post-COVID patients often hear when describing crushing fatigue even after light physical effort: «Займитесь спортом. Приведите себя в форму!» ("Do some sport. Get yourself in shape!"). This exhaustion after effort — post-exertional malaise — is described as a hallmark of post-COVID and similar complex diseases such as chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS). The idea that exercise helps has proved surprisingly persistent despite evidence that this is not physical deconditioning that patients can overcome by enduring the pain. The accompanying image is captioned (in the OCR) that sport harms in post-COVID [msg #287].

Key findings of the study by Dutch scientists published in Nature Communications (called "revolutionary" in the post), which examined the biological mechanisms of PEM in people with post-COVID:
1. **Mitochondrial dysfunction:** muscle biopsies showed serious disturbances in the work of mitochondria, the cells' "power stations" [msg #287].
2. **Muscle damage:** post-COVID patients showed muscle-fibre atrophy and a significant number of dying cells [msg #287].
3. **Impaired immune response:** abnormal presence of T-cells in muscle tissue [msg #287].
4. **Microclots:** increased formation of microclots in tissues after physical exertion [msg #287].

Conclusion stated in the post: the study confirms that PEM in post-COVID is «не просто результат «физической дезадаптации», а серьёзное патологическое состояние» ("not simply the result of 'physical deconditioning' but a serious pathological condition"). People with post-COVID should avoid intense physical exertion and consult doctors to develop individual rehabilitation programmes [msg #287].

### Small fiber neuropathy and post-COVID: autoimmune hypothesis
SFN is increasingly found in people with post-COVID syndrome. In a recently published study (journal *Pain*, 2024) SFN was detected in roughly half of the examined post-COVID patients who had pain. Harvard Medical School neurology professor Анна Окландер believes post-COVID SFN results from an autoimmune reaction; in support, several studies showed a positive effect of intravenous immunoglobulin or prednisolone. Therefore scientists and doctors worldwide are calling for larger placebo-controlled trials [msg #309].

### Heat and antidepressants
A warning post: if you take sertraline, citalopram, duloxetine, fluoxetine, mirtazapine or any other SNRI or SSRI, you are more prone to overheating and dehydration. Advice: be careful in the heat, stay in the shade, and drink enough water with electrolytes [msg #682].

### Low-dose naltrexone (LDN) for fibromyalgia / ME/CFS
Shared for those interested in how effective low doses of naltrexone are in fibromyalgia/ME/CFS, with information credited to the Facebook group Fibromyalgia Support. The attached chart is CureTogether's "104 Treatments for Fibromyalgia", plotting patient-rated effectiveness (vertical axis, about −0.4 to 1.2) against popularity (horizontal, 0 to 1.0) [msg #826, img #826].

What the chart shows (positions read approximately from the image):
- **"Surprisingly effective" quadrant** (high effectiveness, low popularity): low-dose naltrexone is the top-rated item on the whole chart (effectiveness just under 1.0 at popularity about 0.2); also here are hydrotherapy, T3, qigong, gluten-free diet, Xyrem and marijuana [img #826].
- **Popular and effective** (upper right): rest (highest among popular items, roughly 0.85), heating pad, sleep, hot shower/bath, meditation, reduce stress, massage, stretching, deep breathing, chiropractor, avoid alcohol, maintain hydration, Vicodin and yoga [img #826].
- **"Popular but less helpful" quadrant** (lower right): exercise (popularity about 0.8, effectiveness about 0.1), ibuprofen, acetaminophen, limit caffeine, vitamin D, Aleve, ice, glucosamine, gabapentin, melatonin [img #826].
- **At or below zero effectiveness** (rated as unhelpful or harmful on average): Lyrica, Cymbalta, Elavil, Wellbutrin XL, Effexor XR, Prozac, Savella, with SAM-E, Xanax, Provigil, 5-HTP and folic acid near the bottom of the positive range [img #826].

The post itself makes no claim beyond sharing the information; the chart is crowdsourced patient ratings for fibromyalgia, not ME/CFS specifically [msg #826].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The POTS list is of *possible* symptoms; it is not a diagnostic test, and the source itself stresses that the variety of symptoms leads to misdiagnosis and that a thorough evaluation is needed [msg #620].
- The cooling post's vagus-nerve explanation ("cooling supports its flexibility") and the claim that plain water may not be retained in cells are the author's own statements, not referenced; she herself says to do your own research before using the electrolyte tips, and her Celtic-salt result is a personal experience [msg #683].
- The SFN autoimmune hypothesis is one professor's view supported by several studies; the post itself says larger placebo-controlled trials are still needed [msg #309].
- The PEM study conclusion advises avoiding intense exertion and seeking individual rehabilitation programmes from doctors; it does not give a specific activity protocol [msg #287].
- ME/CFS: per the source, mechanisms are poorly understood and no treatment has been developed [msg #834].
- (!) UNCLEAR: the LDN chart is crowdsourced patient ratings (CureTogether) for fibromyalgia; it is applied by the post to fibromyalgia/ME/CFS together, and the linked articles' content is not reproduced in the source. Chart positions above are read approximately from the image [msg #826].

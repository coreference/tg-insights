# Accommodations, self-checks and daily supports

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's practical material on adapting everyday life for autistic and ADHD people: a forwarded essay arguing that each person needs their own accommodations and that weaknesses can be compensated through collaboration; a card of accommodation examples; a multi-card "autistic state self-check" covering energy, emotions, sensory, physical and other needs; a card series on why food and eating are hard for autistic people (including "safe food"); a card series on jobs that fit autistic traits; and the channel author's recommendation of a free AI support chatbot. According to the source, it matters because generic advice often fails neurodivergent people, who then blame and push themselves, worsening the situation [msg #266].

## Core ideas

- **Everyone is unique — including in weaknesses and needed accommodations.** Uniqueness shows not only in rare abilities but in vulnerabilities and in the accommodations a person needs; so methods that help others may not help you, and this is not a failure of effort [msg #266].
- **Collaboration can compensate deficits.** If something does not work for you at all, look for people with whom it will work; even two people with deficits in the same areas can compensate each other [msg #266].
- **Check in systematically.** When an autistic person feels unwell, a structured self-check of energy, emotions, sensory, physical and other needs helps locate what is missing and what to do about it [msg #468, #469, #470, #471, #472, #473, #474].
- **Eating difficulties in autism have concrete causes** — sensory hypersensitivity, safe foods that stop feeling safe, interoception problems, executive function problems, changing food properties, demand avoidance, social demands at the table, GI problems and coordination [msg #699, #700, #701, #702, #703, #704, #705, #706].
- **Autistic traits fit specific jobs.** The cards map ten occupations to the traits that make them suitable (routine, attention to detail, pattern-finding, limited social interaction, hyperfocus) [msg #579, #580, #581, #582, #583, #584, #585].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Compensating deficits through collaboration
Source: a forwarded essay from the channel Кораблик Леськи titled "Жить- сложно." ("Living is hard.") [msg #266].

1. **Recognise the concrete difficulties.** The author's worked example is running your own social-media channel: you may achieve some success and then hit "сплошную стену" ("a solid wall"), losing the ability to keep developing it without really understanding why, since "всё же вроде нормально было" ("everything seemed fine"). This is especially true if you are not a "нейротипиком в вакууме" ("neurotypical in a vacuum") who has no trouble with everyday activity, motivation, concentration and putting thoughts into words. The listed difficulties [msg #266]:
   - Executive dysfunction and insecurity may not even let you start writing.
   - "Мы можем раниться об негативный фидбек от читающих" ("We can get hurt by negative feedback from readers").
   - We may be unable to keep up regular activity — and regularity is the key to a channel's growth.
   - Even shaping a thought so that it is comfortable to read is far from a simple task.
   - We may simply be unable to stand some kind of activity at all — for example writing texts or choosing illustrations.
   The author adds that there are "many, MANY" other difficulties beyond these [msg #266].
2. **Understand why generic methods fail.** The internet has an abundance of methods for overcoming difficulties, including neurodivergence-related ones, but if you have tried some you probably noticed they work well in maybe a third of cases. The reason given: literally every person is unique — contrary to the "ты никто и звать тебя никак" ("you are nobody and your name is nothing") messages many heard while growing up [msg #266].
3. **Accept that uniqueness includes weaknesses.** The catch is that uniqueness is expressed not only in "я могу то, чего не может почти никто больше" ("I can do what almost nobody else can") but also in weaknesses, vulnerabilities and "необходимых нам аккомодациях и адаптациях" ("the accommodations and adaptations we need"). The author rewrites the phrase ironically: "I can do what almost nobody else can — including falling into a non-functional state from something so trivial, in others' opinion, that I myself won't understand what's going on" [msg #266].
4. **Avoid the self-blame trap.** The danger is that we start blaming ourselves or pushing ourselves, aggravating an already difficult situation, with the thought "ну ведь остальным помогает – я просто плохо стараюсь!" ("it helps everyone else — I'm just not trying hard enough!"). The main thing is to accept the simple fact that we are all different [msg #266].
5. **Find people with whom it works.** "Если у вас что-то вот совсем-совсем не получается либо вызывает ощутимые сложности – попробуйте найти тех, вместе с кем у вас это получится." ("If something really doesn't work for you at all or causes noticeable difficulty, try to find those with whom it will work.") Reasoning: we are social beings, and many of us may need some form of interaction with others to unfold our strengths [msg #266].
6. **Mutual compensation, even with shared deficits.** The author's key claim: even with deficits in the same areas, collaboration can compensate them and produce "две здоровые, сильные личности" ("two healthy, strong personalities"). The author announces future posts on how people can cooperate to mutual benefit when both sides are aware of their deficits and ready to help each other [msg #266].

### Autistic state self-check (Самопроверка аутичного состояния)
A card series by neurodivergent_lou, forwarded from Нейр∞тлично [msg #468]. Purpose: a set of questions to go through when you are not feeling right, each paired with concrete options. The sections in order:

**1. "Каков мой уровень энергии?" (What is my energy level?)** The card shows a row of batteries from almost empty (red) to full (green), with two branches [msg #469]:
- *Low battery* → ask: can you set aside time for rest or for an activity that will help restore your strength? The author's current favourite ways to recover: colouring, listening to music, walks in nature and watching series; and also simply lying in a dark room without any expectations — "тоже прекрасно" ("also wonderful") [msg #469].
- *High battery* → ask: how can you keep conserving your energy? Examples: take regular breaks, do not mask autistic traits when it is safe, and use your energy level to take care of yourself [msg #469].

**2. "Как я себя чувствую?" (How do I feel?)** Identify the emotion using an emotion wheel. The words on the wheel include: rejected, anxious, scared, mad, hurt, threatened, insecure, distant, guilty, anger, confused, despair, lonely, sad, surprise, startled, amazed, bored, disgust, excited, disappointed, joyful, awful, proud, avoidance, peaceful, optimistic [img #470]. Then ask what usually helps you when things are hard. If it is hard to come up with options, the author shares what she uses for anxiety [msg #470]:
- **Temperature** — hold an ice cube or rinse your face with water.
- **Intense exercise** — running up and down stairs, jumping and similar.
- **Paced breathing** — inhale for 4 seconds, hold the breath for 4 seconds, exhale for 4 seconds.
- **Progressive muscle relaxation** — tense each muscle group in turn for 5 seconds, then relax it.

**3. "Удовлетворяю ли я свои сенсорные потребности?" (Am I meeting my sensory needs?)** First determine the direction [msg #471]:
- *If you are overloaded:* sit in a dark, quiet room; wear loose, comfortable clothes; use sunglasses and noise-cancelling headphones; try different kinds of stimming.
- *If you are understimulated:* use a weighted blanket; jump; watch a visually stimulating video; light a scented candle; hug.

**4. "Как удовлетворяются мои физические потребности?" (How are my physical needs being met?)** — part one [msg #472]:
- *Am I getting enough fluids?* If not, try drinking something — not necessarily water, choose whatever is comfortable for you — or ask someone to help you with a drink.
- *Did I get enough sleep, or do I feel tired?* If short on sleep, set aside time for a short nap. Sometimes, when there are sleep problems, sticking to a regular schedule helps.
- *Am I getting enough food?* Can you get something to eat that is comfortable and safe? Or ask someone to help with food? Setting a regular alarm to remind yourself to eat may be useful.

**5. Physical needs — part two** [msg #473]:
- *Have I taken care of my physical health?* Have you taken the medications you need? Can you ask for help getting them? Do you feel ill or are you in pain? Can you meet those needs?
- *Would light exercise help?* The card stresses that exercise may not be accessible or helpful for everyone. But if it helps you, can you take a walk or even do a seated exercise using a YouTube video?
- *Have I taken care of my hygiene needs?* "Здесь нет никакого осуждения" ("There is no judgement here"), but for some people taking care of hygiene can improve mood. Did you brush your teeth? Comb your hair? Shower? Can you make these activities more comfortable from a sensory point of view?

**6. "Как обстоят дела с моими другими потребностями?" (How are my other needs?)** [msg #474]:
- *Have I spent enough time alone? Or do I need to meet people to recharge?* Can you schedule this into your timetable?
- *Can I change my physical space?* For example turn off bright light, draw the curtains, remove excess things, reduce noise in the room. Can you adjust the temperature?
- *Have I spent time on my special interests?* If it is hard to find time for them, there are ways to integrate special interests into daily routine — for example watching a video about your interest while brushing teeth or making tea, or listening to a podcast while commuting.

### Five + four reasons eating is hard for autistic people
A card series by neurodivergent_lou titled "Аутичны и голодны? Но никакая еда не кажется подходящей..." ("Autistic and hungry? But no food seems right...") [msg #699]. The reasons, in card order:

1. **Sensory hypersensitivity.** Autistic people are sometimes hypersensitive to sensory stimuli — textures, smells, colours — which can lead to aversion to certain foods. Eating food with an unsuitable texture, taste or smell can be "невероятно мучительным или болезненным" ("incredibly tormenting or painful") for autistic people — "это не просто дискомфорт" ("it is not just discomfort"). This differs from a neurotypical person simply disliking some food [msg #700].
2. **Safe food that stops feeling safe.** Some autistic people use the term "безопасная еда" ("safe food") or "та же самая еда" ("the same food") for food that brings comfort, causes no sensory problems and to which they are attached. But sometimes even safe food stops feeling safe — perhaps because we eat it so often that one day we get completely sick of it. Then we have to search for a new safe food; we can be very hungry while no food seems suitable — the situation the series is named after [msg #701].
3. **Interoception problems.** Interoception is the body's ability to provide information about its internal state; through it we feel body signals such as hunger. Autistic people often have problems with it, so it can be hard to tell when we are hungry and when full — which means we may eat less or more than others [msg #702].
4. **Executive function difficulties.** Executive functions are the set of skills that help us carry out everyday tasks and regulate and control our actions. Problems with them can make cooking difficult and complicate planning one's diet; it may also be hard to find motivation and energy for the task. As a result one has to rely on microwave food or easy-to-make dishes. Decision-making is also hard [msg #703].
5. **Changing textures and smells vs need for predictability.** Food textures and smells can change often, which is stressful for an autistic person who relies on predictability. Example: strawberries may have one taste on one day and a completely different taste and smell on another. Autistic people may value predictability because it helps them cope with a world that seems unpredictable [msg #704].
6. **The eating environment and demands.** For autistic people the eating environment can feel very overloaded and sometimes full of expectations. Autistic people — especially those with PDA (pathological demand avoidance, "ПИТ") — can struggle when eating feels like a "требование" ("demand"). This includes the "внутреннее требование" ("internal demand") — the feeling of hunger itself [msg #705].
7. **Social demands at the table.** There may be a need to make conversation at the table, hold eye contact or sit still. Autistic people sometimes find small talk hard, and sitting still is hard because one is in pain or needs stimulation for regulation [msg #706].
8. **Gastrointestinal problems.** Autistic people more often face GI problems, which can create difficulties around eating and pain [msg #706].
9. **Body coordination.** Coordination problems can make using a knife and fork or a spoon yet another obstacle to eating [msg #706].

### Job options for autistic people matched to traits
A card series by neurodivergent_lou, "Варианты работы для аутичных людей" ("Job options for autistic people") [msg #579]. Each card pairs two occupations with the traits/conditions that make them a fit:

1. **Graphic designer (Графический дизайнер):** creative work; having a defined set of instructions; the chance to do similar tasks every day with enough variety not to be boring [msg #580].
2. **Dog walker (Выгульщик собак):** ability to stick to a routine; getting sensory pleasure from nature; contact with animals; not too much social interaction [msg #580].
3. **Researcher (Исследователь):** attention to detail; using our lived experience to inform research; the possibility of deep immersion in topics of interest; pattern-finding; analytical work [msg #581].
4. **Administrative assistant (Ассистент администратора):** ability to do the same action again and again; logic and problem-solving; attention to detail; pattern-finding [msg #581].
5. **Support worker (Работник службы поддержки):** empathy; deep connection with one's values; keeping to routine; problem-solving; attention to detail [msg #582].
6. **Librarian (Библиотекарь):** a quiet place to work; creating and maintaining organisational systems; following routine; limited social interaction; predictability [msg #582].
7. **Book editor (Книжный редактор):** attention to detail; "Гиперграмотность" ("hyperliteracy"); methodicalness; precision; hyperfocus for long periods of time [msg #583].
8. **Software developer (Разработчик программного обеспечения):** logic; limited social interaction; doing the same or similar tasks again and again; attention to detail; finding regularities [msg #583].
9. **Teaching (Преподавание):** ability to give clear instructions; empathy; ability to advocate for others' interests; having structure and a set schedule [msg #584].
10. **Acting (Актерская деятельность):** ability to express emotions; creativity; and the observation that autistic people are sometimes forced to "play" neurotypicals — "Такое ощущение, будто мы и так постоянно играем роль в повседневной жизни" ("It feels as though we are constantly playing a role in everyday life anyway") [msg #584].
11. **Paralegal / lawyer's assistant (Помощник юриста):** ability to stick to templates; following clear instructions and procedures; attention to detail; ability to dive deep into topics; hyperfocus [msg #585].
12. **Social media management (Управление социальными сетями):** pattern-finding; creativity; attention to detail; limited social interaction; problem-solving [msg #585].

Recurring traits across the cards: attention to detail, pattern-finding, routine/repetition, limited social interaction, deep immersion/hyperfocus, problem-solving [msg #580, #581, #582, #583, #584, #585].

### Examples of accommodations
A card by neurodivergent_lou, "Некоторые примеры адаптаций" ("Some examples of accommodations"), lists five [msg #315]:
- **Virtual walks through new places on Google Earth** — exploring an unfamiliar place in advance, virtually (pictured with a street-level view).
- **Using a visual routine** — pictured as a phone app showing a day's routine steps.
- **Using noise-cancelling headphones and earplugs.**
- **Blocking time in your calendar labelled "без ожиданий" ("no expectations") in order to recover.**
- **Using an emotion wheel to identify emotions** — the same tool used in the *Autistic state self-check* step 2.
The card gives no further explanation of each item [img #315].

### AI support for neurodivergent people (Quixosis AI)
The channel author notes that many of "us" talk to ChatGPT and shares her find: an AI for neurodivergent people called **Quixosis AI**. She uses various versions of AI herself, and in terms of support this one seemed to her the most suitable for a neurodivergent person. It knows Russian (and apparently other languages), so you just start the conversation in whichever language is comfortable. Her first reaction: "почему ощущается столько человечность от ИИ, которую так редко можно встретить среди людей?" ("why does one feel so much humanity from an AI, which is so rarely found among people?"). She states it is not an advertisement and the tool is free [msg #316].
According to the author, Quixosis AI can [msg #316]:
- support neurodivergent people (autism, ADHD, and she suspects other kinds of neurodivergence);
- brainstorm diagnoses;
- suggest how to conduct a dialogue with a doctor and practise self-advocacy;
- act as a personal stress manager, suggesting what to do in emotional dysregulation and other states;
- select diets for people with autoimmune problems.
The attached screenshot shows the tone: the user writes that she is socially exhausted and wants to disconnect; the bot ("Neurodivergent Support") replies that rest and recovery are very important, invites her back whenever she is ready, and says she is taking big steps for her health — "я горжусь тобой за это" ("I'm proud of you for that") [img #316].

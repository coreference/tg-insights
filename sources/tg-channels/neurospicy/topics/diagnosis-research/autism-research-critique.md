# Reading autism and ADHD research critically

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's science explainers on the biology of autism — connectivity theories, infant salience-network findings, fMRI-derived autism subtypes and autism genetics — together with its critical breakdowns of specific studies and public claims: the Stanford Z944 mouse study, a study linking autism and vulnerable narcissism, the paracetamol-causes-autism claim, and the idea of Ozempic for ADHD. The recurring lesson according to the source is that autism is heterogeneous, that correlation is not mechanism, that sensational headlines overstate narrow findings, and that a single study's data cannot be generalized to all autistic people [msg #130, #317, #658, #718, #725, #755, #57].

## Core ideas

- **Autism brains differ in connectivity in both directions.** Research converged on autism involving both under-connected distant regions and over-connected neighbouring regions, but disagreed about where; infant studies were used to cut out confounds (see *Hypo- vs hyper-connectivity*) [msg #130].
- **In infants at elevated autism risk, attention to sensations and movement seems to "borrow" from social attention**: the salience network couples more to sensorimotor areas than to prefrontal social areas [msg #130].
- **Autism can be split into four connectivity-based subtypes**, each with its own neural pattern, genetic markers and behavioural profile (see *Four-step subtype pipeline*) [msg #317].
- **Autism genetics is complex and probabilistic**: de-novo and polygenic contributions, at least 185 associated genes that are not autism-specific, and incomplete penetrance — autism is "not a binary category" but an interaction of vulnerabilities, compensations and environment [msg #658].
- **Sensational "cure" or "comorbidity" headlines usually rest on narrow studies** (mice with one mutation; self-report questionnaires with unbalanced groups) whose limitations the channel lists item by item [msg #718, #725].
- **Paracetamol does not cause autism**; the author sees the claim as a correlation confound and as part of a pattern of blaming mothers, comparable to Wakefield's fabricated vaccine study [msg #755].
- **Ozempic for ADHD is still unproven**: there is not yet enough research to be confident of its success in correcting ADHD symptoms [msg #57].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Hypo-connectivity vs hyper-connectivity theories of autism
Purpose: the connectivity theories ("теории связности") propose that brain regions are connected and interact differently in people on the spectrum and outside it; the note says these theories currently play one of the central roles in understanding the biology of autism. The note was written by the author (Настя, forwarded from the channel "Human Gazing Upon Machine") as a quick summary answering readers' questions about a previous article [msg #130].

1. **Hypo-connectivity theory (теория пониженной связности).** In people with ASD, brain regions that are *far* from each other interact *less* and therefore less efficiently — reduced functional connectivity between distal regions. Given which regions sit at such distances in the brain, this may lead to problems in social communication and cognition [msg #130].
2. **Hyper-connectivity theory (теория гиперсвязности).** In people with ASD the number of connections between *neighbouring* (proximal) regions is *increased* compared with neurotypicals. This may lead to narrow specialization in information processing and hence to stronger focus on details at the expense of broader, context-aware understanding. The note says this theory fits the intense focus and restricted interests often observed in ASD [msg #130].

How the field moved (the "key findings" as narrated):
- There were many suggestions that the autistic brain is less connected than the neurotypical one in some places but better connected in other places; researchers agreed that both happen, but soon disagreed on *where* — results were influenced sometimes by participants' age, sometimes by experience, "то ещё миллион всего" (*sometimes by a million other things*). Under suspicion were the default mode network (DMN), the anterior cingulate and prefrontal cortex (PFC), the amygdala and the sensory cortices (SC). It was then decided that some factors should be excluded — for example by studying infants [msg #130].
- The latest data (2023–2024) found significant differences in brain structures of infants at risk of ASD, especially in the **salience network (SN)** — the network for detecting and filtering stimuli from the environment, which lets a person separate the main from the secondary in the information stream and direct attention accordingly. A footnote specifies the SN anatomy: it includes parts of the inner (medial) cortical surface — the anterior cingulate gyrus, the anterior insula and the pre-supplementary motor areas — plus nodes in the amygdala, hypothalamus, ventral striatum, thalamus and specific brainstem nuclei [msg #130].
- UCLA Health found that infants with the population-typical chance of developing ASD had stronger SN connections with prefrontal areas, which are critical for social attention and interaction, whereas infants at elevated risk had stronger SN connections with sensorimotor areas, where primary processing of movement and sensory information happens [msg #130].
- Conclusion drawn: "похоже, у потенциально нейроотличных людей внимание к ощущениям и движениям берёт в кредит у внимания к социальным аспектам" (*it seems that in potentially neurodivergent people, attention to sensations and movements borrows on credit from attention to social aspects*). ### Four-step pipeline used to derive autism connectivity subtypes (ABIDE fMRI study)
Purpose: to move from a broad ASD-vs-NT contrast to a fine, connectivity-based understanding of subtypes within the spectrum. The post ("Подтипы РАС: пересборка", *ASD subtypes: reassembly*, part 1, forwarded from "Human Gazing Upon Machine") defines its glossary — НТ = neurotypical, НО = neurodivergent, РАС/спектр = autism spectrum disorder (refers to ND people). Data: researchers examined 254 people with ASD and 295 NT from the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange, assembling a large dataset with resting-state functional MRI to analyze brain connectivity patterns. The first part of the series is in an earlier post not included here — (!) INCOMPLETE [msg #317].

1. **Compare NT and ASD.** Some connections were found to be broadly altered in ASD as a whole. However, the researchers acknowledged that these general patterns cannot explain the differences in symptoms and behaviour *within* the ASD group — i.e., a group average does not describe individuals [msg #317].
2. **Compare within the ASD group.** The data were split into two categories: (a) shared connectivity patterns — present more universally in people with ASD; (b) individual-specific connectivity patterns — unique patterns that differ between people within the ASD group [msg #317].
3. **Identify the key dimensions.** Within the individual-specific patterns, using mathematical methods such as orthogonal basis extraction, three main dimensions emerged — kinds of clusters describing how strongly particular regions are connected to each other: (i) a social-communication cluster; (ii) a repetitive-behaviour cluster; (iii) a speech and cognitive-flexibility cluster [msg #317].
4. **Group by unique connectivity profile and validate with genetics and behaviour.** Along the three dimensions each person in the ASD group was assigned according to how strongly the regions contributing to social communication (and the other dimensions) were connected. People with similar profiles were grouped by clustering algorithms into **4 subtypes**, each with a unique neural basis — patterns of how specific brain networks (such as the default mode network, the salience network; the source adds "и др.", i.e. further unnamed networks) are functionally connected. To check accuracy, the subtypes were cross-matched with behavioural assessments and genetic data: each subtype's connectivity profile had unique genetic markers and behavioural patterns, which both confirmed the models and added a biological basis to the classification [msg #317].

The post does not name or describe the four subtypes themselves — (!) INCOMPLETE [msg #317].

### Incomplete penetrance and modifying factors between mutation and phenotype
Purpose: to explain why the same genetic damage leads to autism in one person and not another (forwarded from the channel Идентификация с дуркой) [msg #658].
- **Definition of autism used:** a heterogeneous behavioural phenotype characterized by atypical social communication and unusually restricted or stereotyped interests. Its genetics is as complex as that of many other psychiatric disorders [msg #658].
- **Two kinds of genetic contribution:** (1) monogenic **de-novo** factors — mutations that arose for the first time in the person in a gene's structure and are absent in the parents; (2) polygenic factors — many variants across different genes, each contributing a small amount to the formation of behaviour in people with ASD [msg #658].
- **185 genes, but "associated", not specific:** recent Nature work identifies at least 185 genes whose variants are most often found in autistic people. The post stresses the wording "ассоцированность с РАС" (*association with ASD*) because there is as yet no convincing evidence that these genes are specific to the spectrum and do not appear in, for example, patients with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder (which often happens in other groups) [msg #658].
- **The 36% observation:** 36% of mutations that "cut off a significant part of the protein" in undiagnosed people (without an autism diagnosis) fall in the same coding regions of the gene as in autistic patients. At the protein level these mutations very likely cause similar functional consequences — protein truncation, loss of a key domain, inactivation. In other words, mutations in exactly the same "important" parts of a gene do not always lead to a clinical autism phenotype [msg #658].
- **Incomplete penetrance (неполная пенетрантность)** — a key principle of modern neuropsychogenetics: even if a mutation destroys protein function, it does not guarantee the carrier develops a behavioural disorder or an ASD diagnosis. Between the genetic defect and the phenotype lies a space filled with modifying factors, listed as: (1) sex; (2) polygenic predisposition; (3) epigenetics (the author notes this deserves a separate discussion); (4) environment; (5) developmental conditions; (6) level of social support; (7) stress load [msg #658].
- **Female Protective Effect:** women are known to have a higher threshold for clinical manifestation of ASD — they need a more severe mutation, or its combination with other vulnerabilities, for a pronounced autistic phenotype to develop [msg #658].
- **Behavioural compensation:** the same biochemical damage can be compensated. A mutation that disrupts synapse development may be partly offset by increased activity of alternative cellular signalling pathways, by neuroplasticity (restructuring of synapse architecture), or by an adaptive cognitive environment. This explains why two people with identical mutations in the same protein region can develop differently — one with clinical autism, the other without — even though their genes are damaged similarly at the molecular level [msg #658].
- **Conclusion:** "Аутизм — не бинарная категория, а результат взаимодействия уязвимостей, компенсаций и среды" (*Autism is not a binary category but the result of the interaction of vulnerabilities, compensations and environment*). Predicting it therefore requires not only knowing the mutations in clinical interpretation but understanding the multi-layered regulation of the behavioural phenotype [msg #658].

### Five critical limitations of the Z944 mouse study (checklist for reading "autism cure" headlines)
Context: a Stanford Medicine study published in Science Advances on the drug Z944 in autism triggered a wave of sensational headlines implying autism could now be fully cured. The channel's verdict in the title: "важная находка, но не "лечение одной дозой"" (*an important finding, but not a "one-dose cure"*). What the study actually did [msg #718]:
- It was conducted only on mice with the Cntnap2 mutation, which represents about 1% of ASD cases in humans.
- It found hyperactivity of the thalamic reticular nucleus (RT) and a link between it and autistic manifestations.
- Z944, a T-type calcium channel blocker, did increase social activity and reduce repetitive behaviour in the mice. Important: Z944 was originally developed as an epilepsy drug, not an autism drug.

The limitations, in the post's order [msg #718]:
1. **Heterogeneity of ASD.** ASD includes hundreds of different genetic variants. Results for one model (Cntnap2) cannot be extrapolated to the whole spectrum; what works for this variant may be ineffective or even harmful for other ASD variants.
2. **Causality.** It is unclear what comes first: RT dysfunction → autistic behaviour, or the reverse.
3. **Interspecies differences.** Mouse models do not reproduce the complexity of human social behaviour and neurodevelopment. Also, the study was conducted on males only.
4. **Comorbid conditions.** The study ignores the real complexity of autistic people. "Многие из нас" (*many of us*) have comorbidities (hEDS, MCAS; the source adds "и др.", without naming further ones) that could radically change the response to a drug. Example: with MCAS (САТК), mast cells may over-react to the drug, which could cause inflammation in the brain and worsen symptoms.
5. **Ethical aspects.** (a) Autistic children and people with high support needs cannot give informed consent, so "treatment" may be imposed on them; (b) there are no long-term safety data.

Bottom line: this is a typical situation in neuroscience — an interesting finding, but a huge gap between observed correlations, understanding of mechanisms and practical application. The study opens new questions rather than giving ready answers, let alone a "treatment". The post ends by asking readers what they think about the question of "treating" autism [msg #718].

### Five omissions of the autism / vulnerable narcissism study (checklist for reading self-report comorbidity studies)
Context: after the study "Manifestation of narcissistic vulnerability in adults with autism without intellectual disability" came out, the author repeatedly met the opinion that autism and vulnerable narcissism are comorbid ("ведь есть исследование!" — *after all, there's a study!*). Having read it, she did not come to that view and wrote a breakdown. She reminds readers she is not a specialist and that one should always treat online information critically and re-check sources and facts [msg #725].

What the study did [msg #725]:
- Aim: assess levels of narcissism (grandiose and vulnerable) in autistic adults without intellectual disability.
- Autism was established with international tests and scales: DSM-5, ADOS-2, AQ, RAADS-R, EQ.
- Narcissism was measured with PNI-52, a modern instrument for separating grandiose and vulnerable narcissism.
- Participants: 87 autistic adults and 1,487 people from the general population (controls).

Main results as reported [msg #725]:
- Autistic people scored significantly higher on the overall narcissism scale, and the difference was specifically in **vulnerable narcissism** (hypersensitivity to criticism, avoidance, shame, guilt), not the grandiose type (exploitation, self-aggrandizement).
- 28.7% of the autistic participants exceeded the threshold for pathological narcissism.
- No critical differences between men and women, and no influence of age or intelligence, were found.
- The correlation between autistic people's social difficulties and the severity of vulnerable narcissism was significant.

What was missed — the five omissions [msg #725]:
1. **No clinical assessment.** All participants answered on their own, without an interview with a psychologist or psychiatrist. Autistic people may have interpreted questions literally or wrongly, which strongly affects reliability; difficulties with self-assessment and reflection are a very common factor in autism.
2. **Questionnaires did not account for autistic traits.** Some autistic traits may be misread as narcissistic traits.
3. **Unaccounted comorbidities.** Autistic people often have C-PTSD/PTSD symptoms and rejection-sensitive dysphoria. Their features (hypersensitivity to criticism, avoidance, shame, guilt) are easily confused with vulnerable-narcissism traits on tests, because tests often do not specify the *motivation* behind a behaviour. Accurate differentiation needs an in-depth clinical conversation.
4. **Depression and anxiety not considered.** A serious gap, since internal states can "mask" or amplify some traits.
5. **Very different group sizes.** Only 87 autistic people vs 1,487 controls — strongly unbalanced samples; such disproportions do not reflect the real picture and make results hard to interpret.

What can be said for certain (author's conclusion): autistic adults without intellectual disability show a high correlation with hypersensitivity to criticism, avoidance, shame and guilt, but this cannot be directly attributed to vulnerable narcissism, because no in-depth clinical interview differentiating other possible diagnoses/co-factors was done. Hence the results are insufficient to claim that autism and vulnerable narcissism are comorbid, and they cannot be transferred to the whole autistic population given the unbalanced samples. A clearer picture of the comorbidity of autism and covert narcissism requires more precise data including a full diagnostic interview and a comprehensive mental-health examination. She adds that autistic people are not immune from having a personality disorder (НРЛ) or vulnerable narcissism; the point is only that the data of *this particular study* cannot be read as unambiguous truth about comorbidity. She invites feedback on the breakdown format and ideas for future breakdowns [msg #725].

## Detailed insights

### The paracetamol-autism claim
- Trigger: a statement by Trump that paracetamol is linked to autism. The author states that research shows there is actually no link [msg #755].
- **Her confound hypothesis:** autistic women more often experience pain/chronic pain and other health problems due to conditions such as hEDS (гСЭД / connective tissue disorder, ДСТ), POTS (СПОТ) and MCAS (САТК), and according to research these conditions worsen during pregnancy, which can lead to paracetamol use. From this she infers that autism is passed on genetically and paracetamol is merely a correlating factor, not the cause [msg #755].
- **More important point:** paracetamol is the only available painkiller that can be taken during pregnancy and that has no consequences for the child's development [msg #755].
- **Her social reading (opinion):** she feels women are once again being blamed for giving birth to autistic children, or that pregnancy is being made even more of a torture — a pregnant woman might be denied pain relief or face condemnation from a society that blindly believes "these fake claims". Autism, she says, is again being framed as a disease and a terrible "EPIDEMIC" — one that may be profitable — instead of money being invested in supporting autistic people. She is not surprised that people in power fabricate a narrative convenient to them, with women again blamed and restricted however possible [msg #755].
- **The Amish counter-example:** Trump cited the Amish as not using paracetamol and not having autism; in comments, Amish people themselves wrote that they indeed do not use paracetamol, but autism exists among them and their children [msg #755].
- **Historical parallel — Wakefield:** in the 1990s a scandalous study on vaccines and autism was fabricated. It had a sample of 12 children and no significance. Its author, Andrew Wakefield, had a direct relationship with a law firm preparing class-action suits against vaccine manufacturers and hospitals, alleging that vaccination causes autism in children — that was Wakefield's main gain (conflict of interest). Although this is findable online, some people still think vaccines are evil and cause autism. Now, she says, the innocent paracetamol will be blamed too [msg #755].

## Practical playbook

A reading checklist distilled strictly from the questions the channel asks of studies (each item is drawn from the breakdowns above) [msg #718, #725, #755, #317]:
- Who/what was studied? Mice vs humans, one genetic variant (e.g., ~1% of cases) vs the whole spectrum, males only [msg #718].
- Is causality established, or only correlation? Which comes first? [msg #718]
- Are comorbidities (hEDS, MCAS, C-PTSD, RSD, depression, anxiety) accounted for? [msg #718, #725]
- Was there a clinical interview, or only self-report questionnaires that may be read literally? [msg #725]
- Are the instruments adapted to autistic traits that may mimic the measured construct? [msg #725]
- Are the groups balanced and the sample large enough (cf. 87 vs 1,487; Wakefield's 12 children)? [msg #725, #755]
- Is there a conflict of interest (cf. Wakefield's law-firm ties)? [msg #755]
- Do group averages explain individual differences (cf. step 1 of the subtype study)? [msg #317]
- Are there long-term safety data and is informed consent possible for proposed "treatments"? [msg #718]

## Evidence and numbers

- ABIDE subtype study: 254 ASD vs 295 NT participants, resting-state fMRI, 3 dimensions, 4 subtypes [msg #317].
- At least 185 genes associated with autism (Nature); 36% of protein-truncating mutations in undiagnosed people fall in the same coding regions as in autistic patients [msg #658].
- Cntnap2 mutation ≈ 1% of human ASD cases; Z944 study in male mice only [msg #718].
- Narcissism study: 87 autistic adults vs 1,487 controls; 28.7% of autistic participants above the pathological narcissism threshold [msg #725].
- Wakefield vaccine study: 12 children, no significance [msg #755].

# Dictionary of change: paired concepts (Словарь изменений)

## What this covers

"Словарь изменений Кати Стрельниковой" ("Katya Strelnikova's dictionary of change") is a rubric of posts each built around one pair of concepts that people confuse. Every post opens with the same framing: the rubric is "О словах и понятиях, которые люди путают друг с другом. И дихотомии, которая чаще всего делает изменения невозможными." ("About words and concepts that people confuse with each other. And the dichotomy that most often makes change impossible.") [msg #673, #688, #707, #723, #738]

This topic covers five pairs: control vs choice, self-pity vs self-compassion, pleasant vs necessary, expectations vs faith, and refusal vs moderation. Each post gives definitions, explains how the "wrong" twin works, contrasts the two with paired example sentences, and ends with self-test questions. Per the author, the pairs matter because the wrong twin of each pair looks like the right one but traps people in cycles of tension, breakdown and guilt. The posts also refer back to an earlier first post of the series, "Насилие VS Усилие" ("Violence vs Effort"), which is not part of this topic's messages. (!) INCOMPLETE: the content of "Violence vs Effort" is not in the provided source. [msg #673, #688, #707, #723, #738]

## Core ideas

- **The wrong twin imitates the right one.** In each pair the counterproductive concept (control, pity, the pleasant want, expectations, refusal) looks like discipline, care or a goal, which is why it is so easy to confuse. Each pair is explained in full in *Frameworks* below. [msg #673, #688, #738]
- **Culture teaches the wrong twin from childhood.** Katya repeatedly blames the culture we grow up in, with its "pull yourself together", "patience and work grind everything" and "don't feel sorry for yourself", for making control and harshness seem like the adult way. [msg #673, #688]
- **The test is always consequences and direction, not momentary feeling.** Across the pairs the deciding question is where an action leads: does it make things really better later, does it lead where I want to be, does it answer "why do I need this". [msg #688, #707, #723, #738]
- **Coercion produces rebellion and relapse.** Control, bans and rigid expectations create tension and deficit, which end in rebellion, breakdown, guilt and a new round of harshness: a swing that does not stop until we step off it ourselves. [msg #673, #738]
- **Real adulthood means understanding, not enduring.** Choosing rather than controlling requires growing up in the sense of figuring out what is important and what we truly want, not learning to endure and suppress desires. [msg #673]

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Control vs Choice (Контроль VS Выбор)
**Background.** Katya says this pair was confused in her own head for a long time. We are born into a culture of control and hear from childhood: «Возьми себя в руки» ("Take yourself in hand"), «Соберись, тряпка» ("Pull yourself together, you rag"), «Мало ли чего ты хочешь» ("Who cares what you want"), «Терпенье и труд все перетрут» ("Patience and work grind through everything"). It feels as if adults know how to do this, since they tell us so. With this background, when something does not work, we draw the "logical" conclusion: not enough control, be harsher, restrict, oblige and monitor ourselves even more. [msg #673]

**Control — definition and mechanism.** "Контроль — попытка управлять собой через принуждение." ("Control is an attempt to manage oneself through coercion.") We decide how things should be and then force ourselves and life to match that picture. At the base of control is distrust of oneself, one's body and the process, as if saying to oneself: «если я тебя отпущу, ты все испортишь» ("if I let you go, you'll ruin everything"). Hence the need to hold, watch and never relax. Katya links this to a childhood illusion that somewhere there is an adult who holds everything in their hands and can do literally anything "if only they pull themselves together". But in her view life does not adjust, the body does not adjust, and we ourselves do not adjust. "И чем сильнее мы пытаемся продавить свое «как надо», тем меньше слышим, что происходит на самом деле" ("And the harder we try to push through our 'how it should be', the less we hear what is actually happening"). [msg #673]

**The control–rebellion–breakdown cycle.** Katya describes the loop step by step:
1. Constant struggle with oneself and life creates tension. [msg #673]
2. Tension leads to inner rebellion, for example when all you want is to lie down, or to eat only sweets for lunch out of spite ("назло кондуктору"). [msg #673]
3. Rebellion leads to a breakdown (срыв). [msg #673]
4. Then everything starts again, because the conclusion drawn is that even more control is needed to make it work next time. [msg #673]

**Choice — definition and mechanism.** "Выбор — способность действовать из понимания, а не из принуждения. А также понимание последствий выбора" ("Choice is the ability to act from understanding rather than from coercion. And also understanding the consequences of the choice"), for example that something will have to be given up. We do not force ourselves; we consciously decide because we see the connection between the action and what matters to us. At the base of choice are trust and contact with oneself: «я знаю, чего хочу, замечаю, что со мной происходит, и двигаюсь, опираясь на это» ("I know what I want, I notice what is happening to me, and I move relying on that"). When we choose, the struggle disappears: there is no need to force ourselves because we are doing what we decided. Flexibility appears: if something does not work, we do not blame ourselves for lacking willpower but investigate what needs to change for it to work. [msg #673]

**Paired examples.**
- Control: «Нельзя есть после шести» ("You must not eat after six"). Choice: «Я замечаю, что если ем поздно, плохо сплю, поэтому выбираю ужинать раньше» ("I notice that if I eat late I sleep badly, so I choose to have dinner earlier"). [msg #673]
- Control: «Надо ходить в зал три раза в неделю» ("I have to go to the gym three times a week"). Choice: «Движение дает мне энергию, поэтому я ищу, какое движение мне подходит» ("Movement gives me energy, so I look for the kind of movement that suits me"). [msg #673]

**How to tell control from choice — 3 self-tests.**
1. **The "skip once" test.** Ask yourself what will happen if I do not do "…" once, or recall such a situation from the recent past. If guilt washes over you and "everything is lost" ("все пропало") — it is control. If you calmly think "ну ок, завтра продолжу" ("okay, I'll continue tomorrow") — it is choice. [msg #673]
2. **The body-feeling test.** Control feels like tension, "I have to pull myself together". Choice feels like calm and clarity, even before something difficult. Katya asks: are you joyful and calm? Or do you think with disgust about what you will have to do, while everything inside contracts? [msg #673]
3. **The "why do I need this" test.** Ask «зачем мне это» ("why do I need this") and listen to the answer. «Потому что надо», «так правильно», «стыдно иначе» ("because I must", "that's the right way", "it would be shameful otherwise") — control. «Потому что мне от этого лучше», «я так хочу» ("because it makes me feel better", "I want it this way") — choice. [msg #673]

**Growing up.** "Чтобы выбирать, а не контролировать, нужно по-настоящему повзрослеть." ("To choose rather than control, you need to truly grow up.") Not in the sense of learning to endure, force yourself and push your desires aside, but the opposite: figuring out what is important, what suits you, what you really want and why. Katya says she often sees content claiming that being an adult is boring and hard, and she disagrees. At the start of work she tells every client «Мы будем делать только то, что вы захотите» ("We will only do what you want"); clients do not believe it and are later very surprised. When we understand what is important to us and why, there is no need to control ourselves: it is enough to do what we want and not do what we do not want, although this is not always pleasant and sometimes not easy. [msg #673]

### Self-compassion vs Pity (Жалость VS сострадание)
**Background.** Katya says self-sympathy and self-care are complicated in "our culture": «То пятилетка за два года, то Иисус терпел и нам велел» ("Either the five-year plan in two years, or 'Jesus suffered and told us to'"), «Не надо себя жалеть» ("Don't feel sorry for yourself"), «Соберись» ("Pull yourself together"). This is "буквально вода, в которой мы плаваем с детства" ("literally the water we swim in since childhood"). For many people (for her too, for many years) the very idea of self-care sounds suspicious. The real problem, she says, is a mess in our heads: most often we confuse self-care and self-compassion with pity and indulgence. In recent years she increasingly sees both compassion and pity in herself, and does not shy away from calling the latter «токсичная жалость» ("toxic pity"), because it is what pushes her towards decisions that do not lead to a better state and flourishing, but rather to greater suffering. [msg #688]

**Self-compassion — definition.** "Самосострадание — видеть, что тебе сейчас сложно и плохо, и выбирать позаботиться о себе так, чтобы стало лучше." ("Self-compassion is seeing that you are having a hard time right now and choosing to take care of yourself so that it becomes better.") Really better, not for a second. Examples Katya gives: get up and do morning exercise because you know you will feel more energetic afterwards; go to bed at ten because you need strength tomorrow; make a salad instead of eating a bun because in an hour you would be hungry and angry again. And sometimes self-compassion is allowing yourself not to do something, accepting the inevitable, giving up the "convulsions" (отказаться от конвульсий). What makes it compassion is that it is chosen from the place of «мне это сейчас правда нужно» ("I really need this now"), not «я же такая бедная-несчастная, надо себя пожалеть» ("I'm so poor and miserable, I should feel sorry for myself"). [msg #688]

**Pity — definition.** "Жалость — выбирать то, что кажется облегчением в моменте, но на деле уводит еще дальше от желаемого состояния и целей." ("Pity is choosing what seems like relief in the moment but actually leads you even further from the desired state and goals.") Its typical voice: «Ты так устала, полежи с сериалом» ("You're so tired, lie down with a series") — never mind that you won't fall asleep afterwards; «Ой, ты так не выспалась, не делай зарядку» ("Oh, you didn't get enough sleep, skip the exercise") — never mind that it is precisely the exercise that gives energy; «Ты уже такая голодная, съешь булку» ("You're so hungry already, eat a bun") — never mind that in an hour you'll be even hungrier. In the moment it feels like care; in fact it is indulgence that has to be paid for. [msg #688]

**The caring-mother test.** Katya asks the reader to think: would a caring mother endlessly feed her daughter ice cream and let her skip school at the first "I don't want to"? Perhaps you do that with your own children? [msg #688]

**Why they are confused.** Both start from the feeling «мне тяжело» ("this is hard for me"). "Жалости можно дать Оскар за то, как талантливо она мимикрирует под заботу." ("Pity could be given an Oscar for how talentedly it mimics care.") [msg #688]

**4 differences Katya identifies.**
1. **Aftermath.** After an act of self-compassion things really get better. After pity it always gets worse — in an hour, a day, a week. [msg #688]
2. **The question asked.** Self-compassion asks «Что мне сейчас действительно нужно?» ("What do I really need now?"). Pity asks «Что меня быстро успокоит?» ("What will calm me down quickly?"). [msg #688]
3. **Movement.** Self-compassion moves you forward, sometimes through discomfort, but always in the right direction. Pity gives temporary relief but leaves you in the same place with your problems. [msg #688]
4. **Inner figure.** Self-compassion is the caring adult inside you. Pity is the inner child who rebels and demands a candy. [msg #688]

**Consequences prompt.** If it is still hard to tell them apart, Katya's tip is to always remember consequences and ask: "То, что сейчас хочу сделать, приведет меня туда, где я хочу быть? Или это временное облегчение, которое сделает хуже?" ("Will what I want to do now lead me where I want to be? Or is it temporary relief that will make things worse?") [msg #688]

### Pleasant vs Necessary (Приятное VS Необходимое)
**Origin.** At her practicum "В этот раз получится!" ("This time it will work!") the group discussed what "I want" means, and Katya noticed this links directly to the previous post on pity and compassion, so she continued the theme. [msg #707]

**Premise.** "Мы, люди, вообще-то незамысловато устроены: хотим, чтобы было хорошо, и не хотим, чтобы было плохо." ("We humans are actually simply built: we want things to be good and don't want them to be bad.") The only problem is that we constantly confuse sensual pleasure in the moment with the real "good" — the state in which it is truly great to live. [msg #707]

**Two kinds of "want".**
1. **The pleasant want — about pleasure.** Momentary, sensual: eat a cake, cancel a workout, get stuck in the phone, treat yourself here and now, switch quickly, feel sorry for yourself. [msg #707]
2. **The necessary want — about depth.** Not a momentary whim but a sincere, urgent desire that leads to long-term happiness and flourishing. [msg #707]

**Personal example: massage.** Katya goes for massage and it is often unpleasant for her. A client of hers said «массаж для меня — не отдых» ("massage is not rest for me"), and Katya agrees. She plays padel, which tightens her right arm and shoulder; she runs and walks a lot, which tightens her calves and thighs. When the masseuse works these places she wants to slide off the table or at least whimper. But she has no doubt she needs massage, because without it she would not feel free and great in her body and could not keep enjoying sport. It is the necessary thing that "I want" even when it is unpleasant. [msg #707]

**Necessary does not equal unpleasant.** "необходимое не равно неприятное" ("necessary does not equal unpleasant"). This is not about "the more you suffer, the more useful it is". Massage is her example of the necessary that is sometimes unpleasant, but the necessary is very often pleasant: getting enough sleep, eating tasty and filling food, walking in good weather, hugging someone close — all are "кайф" (a delight). Pleasantness is simply not the main criterion. The necessary is defined not by whether it is pleasant but by where it leads us; sometimes the path is unpleasant, sometimes very pleasant, and both are normal. [msg #707]

**Spinoza: freedom as recognized necessity.** Katya cites her favourite quote of Benedict Spinoza: "Свобода — это осознанная необходимость." ("Freedom is recognized necessity.") We are used to thinking freedom means doing "what we want" — and it does, but wanting has different meanings: «Хочу тортик» ("I want a cake") and «Хочу быть здоровой» ("I want to be healthy") are different kinds of wanting. In her reading, Spinoza says that the slave of (pleasant) desires is not free: he jerks after every "want" like a marionette pulled by strings. "По-настоящему свободен тот, кто понимает свою истинную природу и свои настоящие потребности — и осознанно выбирает то, что ему действительно нужно." ("Truly free is the one who understands his true nature and real needs and consciously chooses what he really needs.") Not under the stick, not through violence against oneself, but from deep understanding that this leads to the life he wants and the state in which he flourishes. "Осознать необходимость — значит перестать быть заложником приятного." ("To recognize necessity is to stop being a hostage to the pleasant.") Real freedom, and real pleasure too, live in that conscious choice, because "нет ничего приятнее, чем жить жизнью, которая тебе по-настоящему подходит" ("there is nothing more pleasant than living a life that truly suits you"). [msg #707]

**How to tell them apart — 3 signs.**
1. **Volume and persistence.** The pleasant shouts loudly, stamps its little feet and demands immediately. The necessary speaks quietly but does not go away. [msg #707]
2. **Aftertaste.** The pleasant gives quick relief and an aftertaste of «ну зачем» ("why did I do that"). The necessary sometimes requires effort but leaves behind «это было правильное решение» ("that was the right decision"). [msg #707]
3. **Time frame.** The pleasant is about now. The necessary is about us as a whole. [msg #707]

**The pleasant is not the enemy.** Cake, a TV series and a day on the sofa can also be necessary. The point is not always to choose the "useful", but to see what exactly we are choosing, why, and where it leads us. [msg #707]

### Faith vs Expectations (Вера VS Ожидания)
**Why we swap faith for expectations.** Katya constantly sees, in her personal experience and her practice, people trying to replace faith with expectations. Honestly, believing is scary: faith has no guarantees, deadlines or numbers, only a direction and a readiness to keep walking without knowing how much further. Expectations give an illusion of certainty and control. «Минус пять килограммов» ("Minus five kilograms") sounds clearer than «я просто буду сбалансировано есть, больше двигаться и высыпаться, а тело само найдет свой вес» ("I'll simply eat in a balanced way, move more and sleep enough, and the body will find its own weight"). [msg #723]

**Related sayings of hers.** She often says that before "just do it" you need "just believe it". A phrase clients hear from her with enviable regularity: "Disappointed? That's what you get when you have expectations". She has also previously written about the "похмелье от ожиданий" ("expectation hangover"). [msg #723]

**Her tongue-in-cheek formula.** "Формула успеха: Верить, что дойдешь, и при этом не представлять, куда именно и когда." ("Success formula: believe that you'll get there, while not imagining exactly where and when.") [msg #723]

**Faith — definition.** "Вера — внутреннее знание, что я двигаюсь в важном для меня направлении и дойду, даже если пока не понимаю, как именно и когда." ("Faith is the inner knowing that I am moving in a direction important to me and will get there, even if I don't yet understand exactly how and when.") Regardless of what obstacles come. It is about direction, not a point on the map; about what is in my power: my actions, my persistence, my readiness to try again. [msg #723]

**Expectations — definition.** "Ожидания — конкретная картинка будущего." ("Expectations are a concrete picture of the future.") With numbers, deadlines and form. They are almost always about things that only partly depend on us: how the body will respond, what people will say, how circumstances will turn out. When reality diverges from the picture, expectations say «У меня не получается / Со мной что-то не так / Это не мое» ("It's not working for me / Something is wrong with me / This isn't for me") — insert your own version. [msg #723]

**4 differences, with paired examples.**
1. **Direction vs a dated result.** Faith is about direction and trusting oneself; expectations are about a concrete result by a concrete deadline. Expectation: «Минус пять килограмм за месяц» ("Minus five kilos in a month"). Faith: «Я выстраиваю питание и движение, которые мне подходят. Тело придет к своему весу» ("I'm building eating and movement that suit me. The body will come to its weight"). [msg #723]
2. **What I do vs what should happen.** Faith relies on what I do; expectations rely on what should happen. With the same "minus five kilos in a month" example, the number on the scale depends not only on me: on the body itself, the current lifestyle, the environment and similar factors. The faith version is the same as above. [msg #723]
3. **After a failure.** Faith looks for a new path; expectations look for someone to blame. Expectation: «Больше не ем сладкое» ("I don't eat sweets anymore") — then she eats cake at a friend's birthday and immediately: «Я слабовольная» ("I'm weak-willed"). Faith: «Я выстрою здоровые отношения со сладким» ("I'll build a healthy relationship with sweets"). Overate candies? Instead of criticism and verdicts, questions: «Что меня к этому привело? Как избежать этого в будущем?» ("What led me to this? How can I avoid it in future?"). [msg #723]
4. **Energy.** Faith gives energy to continue. Expectations take it away: first on tense waiting, then on disappointment. Expectation: «В следующем месяце буду заниматься три раза в неделю» ("Next month I'll train three times a week") — missed two days, and that's it, «опять не смогла» ("failed again"). Faith: «Я стану человеком, который занимается спортом регулярно. Сколько это займет, столько и займет» ("I'll become a person who exercises regularly. However long it takes, it takes") — missed two days, went on the third. [msg #723]

**"Believe not in success, believe in reality".** Katya was reading Pema Chödrön's book «Как жить и как умирать» ("How to Live and How to Die" in Russian translation) and found her teacher's saying, which in Katya's opinion explains the whole confusion: «Верь не в успех. Верь в реальность» ("Believe not in success. Believe in reality"). Belief in "success" (a very concrete picture of the future) is exactly expectations: confidence that everything will work as we want and on our terms. But success cannot be relied on: sometimes things go as we want, sometimes not. Reality always happens, and it can be counted on. If we stop perceiving every refusal and failure as a personal vendetta against us, a surprising freedom appears: to be equally open to the desired and the undesired. Katya says this seems to be the deepest form of faith. Her summary line: "Не «у меня все получится», а «что бы ни случилось, я с этим справлюсь и пойду дальше»." ("Not 'everything will work out for me', but 'whatever happens, I'll cope with it and move on'.") [msg #723]

**Self-test question.** "Я понимаю, куда иду, и верю, что дойду? Или ожидаю, что будет именно так и именно к этому сроку?" ("Do I understand where I'm going and believe I'll get there? Or do I expect it to be exactly like this and exactly by this deadline?") [msg #723]

### Refusal vs Moderation (Отказ VS Воздержанность)
**Origin.** A thought from the newsletter @justakindreminder inspired the post: moderation (воздержанность), they write, is not asceticism at all but the skill of managing one's desires and passions. [msg #738]

**Katya's position and the refusal swing.** "Я против аскез, марафонов и отказов от чего-то" ("I'm against asceticism, marathons and giving things up"). She says this played a bad joke on her many times ("привет 20 лет рпп" — "hello, 20 years of eating disorder"), and both from her own experience and years of health-coaching practice she has observed in full colour how they end. Her examples: 30 days without sugar, and on day 31 a whole box of candies at once; two weeks without alcohol, then "I deserved it" and a hangover for the whole weekend; a social-media detox after which you scroll until 3 a.m. Then the familiar path: rollback, guilt, "I have no willpower", and a new marathon from Monday. "Качели, которые не заканчиваются, пока мы сами с них не слезем." ("A swing that doesn't stop until we ourselves get off it.") [msg #738]

**Refusal — definition and mechanism.** Refusal is an external promise of "never again". It is held by willpower and fear («если разрешу себе хоть чуть-чуть, все пропало» — "if I allow myself even a little, all is lost"). Instead of figuring out our relationship with something, we simply remove it from life. "А потерпеть, увы, ничего не проясняет." ("And enduring, alas, clarifies nothing.") Instead it launches deficit thinking: the forbidden becomes the most desired, and we are back on the "holding on / breaking down" swing. [msg #738]

**Moderation — definition.** Moderation is the skill of managing one's desires: noticing them, weighing them, understanding their origin and choosing the measure — how much I really need for it to be enough. It is held not by prohibition but by understanding: "я знаю, зачем мне это, сколько мне в радость и где начинается «во вред»" ("I know why I need this, how much of it is a joy to me, and where 'harmful' begins"). [msg #738]

**Why they are confused.** Both look like "I'm restraining myself", but they work differently. [msg #738]

**5 differences, with paired examples.**
1. **"Never" vs "how much I need".** Refusal: «Больше не ем сладкое» ("I don't eat sweets anymore") — then a friend's birthday, and in go half a cake, those cookies over there and a box of Raffaello ("so as not to get up twice"). Moderation: «Я не отказываюсь от сладкого. Но выбираю десерт, который люблю и который правда хочу. И ем его с удовольствием, а не с чувством вины» ("I don't give up sweets. But I choose a dessert I love and really want. And I eat it with pleasure, not guilt"). [msg #738]
2. **Control vs choice.** Refusal rests on control; moderation on choice (linking to the first pair above). Refusal: «Удаляю инстаграм навсегда» ("I'm deleting Instagram forever") — and then can't tear away for 4 hours. Moderation: «Я захожу в инстаграм, когда мне что-то нужно, а не когда мне скучно» ("I go on Instagram when I need something, not when I'm bored"). [msg #738]
3. **Deficit vs relief of tension.** Refusal creates deficit; moderation relieves tension. Refusal: «Никаких сериалов по вечерам» ("No TV series in the evenings") — then a binge till morning despite an important meeting. Moderation: «Посмотрю одну серию и пойду спать, потому что завтра хочу проснуться бодрой» ("I'll watch one episode and go to bed because tomorrow I want to wake up fresh"). [msg #738]
4. **Fear vs understanding.** Refusal grows from fear; moderation from understanding. Refusal: «Не держу дома орехи, а то съем всю пачку» ("I don't keep nuts at home, or I'll eat the whole pack"). Moderation: «Орехи стоят на полке. Я знаю, что горсти мне хватает. А если однажды съем больше, ничего страшного не случится» ("The nuts are on the shelf. I know a handful is enough for me. And if one day I eat more, nothing terrible will happen"). [msg #738]
5. **Avoiding vs answering "why do I need this".** Refusal avoids the question; moderation answers it. Refusal: «Больше никакого вина после работы» ("No more wine after work") — and then… (left to the reader's imagination with a grin emoji). Moderation: «Что я пытаюсь снять этим бокалом? Усталость? Тревогу? Может, мне нужен не бокал, а выходной?...» ("What am I trying to relieve with this glass? Fatigue? Anxiety? Maybe I need not a glass but a day off?"). [msg #738]

**The exception: truly addictive substances.** "Смуты добавляет, что есть исключение." ("Adding to the confusion, there is an exception.") Things that really cause addiction — alcohol, nicotine, substances and anything that messes with our dopamine — and she explicitly says sugar does not count ("нет, сахар не в счет"). For people prone to addiction, measure simply does not work there: one glass for them is not "one glass" but a ticket back into bondage. Katya says she unfortunately knows what she is talking about ("на чем я только ни сидела" — "what haven't I been hooked on"): she has not drunk for more than seven years and has not smoked for more than six. But even this is not refusal for her in the sense of "forbidden, I endure". She worked out why she needed it, what it costs her, and why she should not indulge in "чуть-чутем" ("just a little bit"), and chose to live without it. [msg #738]

**Self-test questions before giving something up.** "Какие у меня отношения с тем, от чего я хочу отказаться? Что я на самом деле ищу в этом? И как еще я могу это получить?" ("What is my relationship with the thing I want to give up? What am I really looking for in it? And how else can I get it?") [msg #738]

## Detailed insights

### How the pairs connect
- Refusal vs moderation explicitly reuses control vs choice: difference no. 2 is "refusal rests on control, moderation on choice", and the "why do I need this" question appears as a test in both (control test 3; moderation difference 5). [msg #673, #738]
- Pleasant vs necessary grew out of pity vs compassion: Katya noticed the "what does 'want' mean" discussion "напрямую перекликается" ("directly echoes") the pity/compassion post. In both, the pleasant/pitying option is momentary relief that leaves things worse, while the necessary/compassionate option may involve discomfort but leads where you want. "Feel sorry for yourself" is even listed as one of the pleasant wants. [msg #688, #707]
- Faith vs expectations reuses sweets and training examples close to those of refusal vs moderation ("I don't eat sweets anymore" + friend's birthday cake; "three times a week" training), showing the same rigid vow failing under the expectations lens (self-blame "I'm weak-willed") and under the refusal lens (binge). [msg #723, #738]
- Expectations, like control, are described as giving an "illusion" of certainty and control, and both lead to self-blame when reality does not comply. [msg #673, #723]

### The cultural and personal background
Katya frames the wrong twins as learned: control from the "culture of control" we are born into, harshness towards oneself from Soviet and religious messages ("five-year plan in two years", "Jesus suffered"), where self-care sounds suspicious. She presents herself as having been inside these confusions: control vs choice "долго путалась у меня самой" ("was confused for a long time in my own head"); she sees "toxic pity" in herself; she lived 20 years with an eating disorder and past addictions. Her lessons are offered from that lived experience plus her coaching practice. [msg #673, #688, #738]

## Practical playbook

All self-test questions from the rubric in one place (each explained in its framework above):
- Control vs choice: "What happens if I skip this once?"; "Does this feel like tension or calm clarity?"; "Why do I need this?" [msg #673]
- Pity vs self-compassion: "What do I really need now?" vs "What will calm me quickly?"; "Will this lead me where I want to be, or is it temporary relief that will make it worse?" [msg #688]
- Pleasant vs necessary: is it shouting and demanding now, or quiet but persistent; will it leave "why did I" or "that was the right decision"; is it about now or about me as a whole? [msg #707]
- Faith vs expectations: "Do I know where I'm going and believe I'll get there, or do I expect exactly this by exactly this date?" After a slip, ask "What led me to this? How to avoid it in future?" instead of blaming. [msg #723]
- Refusal vs moderation: "What is my relationship with this? What am I really looking for in it? How else can I get it?" [msg #738]

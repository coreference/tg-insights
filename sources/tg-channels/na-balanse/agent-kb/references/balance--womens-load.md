# Women's fatigue and social expectations

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's posts on why women tire more and get depressed more often than men, and on the social expectations behind this: the superwoman ideal, internalized misogyny and everyday sexism, beauty burnout, the "third shift", the cycle, care work and guilt, Seligman's analysis of women's depression, and Kate Manne's "Individuals vs Donors" model applied to the question of war. The author, health coach Katya Strelnikova, says she works mainly with women and within an anti-patriarchal approach; in four years of work she sees "the same thing" over and over and concludes it is "not chance but a system" [msg #240, #452]. Her stated aim is to help women see that exhaustion and depression are often logical consequences of social patterns — which, being learned, can be changed [msg #452, #517].

## Core ideas

- **Women have it harder, so women should support each other.** The author starts from acknowledging inequality and frames supporting women "both with deeds and with money" as "our big mission" [msg #240].
- **Women tire more because of a system, not personal weakness.** The superwoman ideal says "you can do everything" and silently adds "and you must"; the result is fatigue from too many roles, constant vigilance, beauty standards, living against the cycle, caring for others and guilt (see *Six reasons women tire more than men*) [msg #452].
- **Beauty burnout is a real state** of fatigue, anxiety and overload from pressure to meet beauty standards, with six recognisable symptoms and three coping moves [msg #301].
- **Women get depressed roughly 1.5–2 times as often as men**, and simple explanations (hormones, genes, money, complaining more) do not account for it; Seligman points to learned helplessness, rumination and the cult of thinness — all learned and therefore changeable [msg #517].
- **The current system splits people into Individuals and Donors**, and women are mostly Donors; the author argues Donor values (care, empathy, support) could ground a world with no room for war, and that change starts with oneself [msg #562].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Anti-patriarchal approach (acknowledge inequality first)
The author describes her working stance: she "very much loves men" but works mostly with women and within an anti-patriarchal approach whose first step is to **acknowledge the current inequality and all the difficulties connected with it that women have to overcome** [msg #240]. She shared it in response to an "angry comment" claiming "это всё придумали" ("it's all made up"), pointing to fresh statistics from a Russian (not Western) study as evidence — she stresses that research about Russia specifically is rare [msg #240].

Her practical version of support for women, as she lists it [msg #240]:
1. She chooses to **pay women generously**.
2. When shopping, she **checks who owns the business**.
3. She **praises** women.
4. She **gives compliments**.
5. She **teaches**.
6. She **supports**.

Reasoning: her behaviour "takes nothing away from men or from me", but she always has "a little more attention and time" for women — to replenish the energy women have to spend on "невидимый бесплатный труд и движение против течения" (invisible unpaid labour and moving against the current) [msg #240].

### Six reasons women tire more than men
Source: the sixth post in the author's series "I'm 40 and I'm fed up, or why are we all so tired?" [msg #452]. She opens with a disclaimer: she is against speculation and "competitions in suffering", but after four years of working with women she keeps seeing the same thing and thinks it is a system [msg #452].

Background — the superwoman ideal: over the last hundred years women went from "housewife" to "superwoman", gaining freedom, education, the right to earn, choose and build a career. Now a woman must be successful, kind, groomed, caring and "preferably not tired". "Можно все. Но никто не сказал, что это все совместить не получится." (Everything is allowed. But nobody said that it's impossible to combine it all.) In the author's account, the superwoman image appeared in the mid-20th century together with active advertising: after the war the world faced a new, independent woman and tried to return her to the kitchen, and marketing quickly found a way to bring women back under control — "продав нам идеал" (by selling us an ideal). Women were told "You can do everything!", with the unwritten addition "and you are obliged to do everything" — be a CEO and mother of three, cook like a restaurant, volunteer at a shelter, be in great shape and grateful for everything, all with ease and a smile. "Но давайте уже признаемся: это утомительно." (But let's admit it already: it's exhausting.) [msg #452]

The six reasons:
1. **Too many roles.** A woman is a professional, a partner, a mother, and a manager of logistics, finances, relationships and "the weather in the house". Even when there is a partner, the emotional and organisational management of the family is "almost always on us". Each task costs not only time but also attention and energy. The author adds her own opinion that motherhood is a full-time job [msg #452].
2. **Constant switched-on-ness (включенность).** The author states that the female nervous system is built to pick up signals — crying, emotions, intonations, micro-movements. What makes women empathetic also makes them vulnerable to sensory overload. Being with children is not rest but full engagement; even when someone else is looking after the child, "ты все равно «боковым зрением» на стреме" (you are still on alert "with peripheral vision") [msg #452].
3. **Beauty standards and chronic self-improvement.** Women now pursue "male" attributes of success and have also set up an internal race: to be mindful, groomed, resilient, beautiful — but not anxious, not angry and preferably radiant. She quotes Naomi Wolf's *The Beauty Myth*: the quality called beauty supposedly exists objectively and universally; women must want to have it, and men must want women who embody it. This is the **third shift**: women work, they care for others, and then they also "work on themselves" — body, skin, grooming, nutrition, sport, inner balance. "Женщины выгорают от внутренней конкуренции: между собой и собой идеальной." (Women burn out from internal competition: between themselves and their ideal selves.) [msg #452]
4. **Living against the cycle.** The author holds that female energy is tied to the menstrual cycle: there are weeks of activity and weeks of decline, productive days and days when you need to ease off. But women live "as if every day were ovulation" — resourced, in shape, in a good mood, "ready for achievements" — and when they aren't, they keep forcing, pressing and overriding their nature. The cycle becomes a nuisance "хотя должен быть компасом" (although it should be a compass). The price: fatigue, hormonal disruptions and guilt about one's own physiology [msg #452].
5. **Constant care — not for oneself.** Caring for loved ones is "in our DNA", which the author calls wonderful but energy-consuming; given this predisposition, women easily give themselves away completely. She cites a heroine of her "На балансе" interviews: "Пока в кувшине нет воды, сложно ее разлить" (While there's no water in the jug, it's hard to pour it). Many women pour from an empty jug for years — sometimes not from great love but from the habit of being needed [msg #452].
6. **Guilt.** Guilt for anger, irritation, wanting to be alone, not rejoicing in every day, not being "in resource". "Мы не просто устаем, мы еще и виним себя за усталость. И устаем еще больше." (We don't just get tired, we also blame ourselves for being tired. And get even more tired.) — a self-reinforcing loop [msg #452].

The post ends by asking readers whether they agree and what they would add [msg #452].

### Beauty burnout - 6 symptoms
A post forwarded from the channel "Ко-Лаб" (shared by the author) says the success of the film *The Substance* recalled the term **beauty burnout**, which arrived a few years ago together with social-media trends that are often inapplicable to real life. Definition: a state in which constant pressure to meet beauty standards causes fatigue, anxiety and a sense of overload; women spend time, money and energy maintaining their appearance but experience stress instead of pleasure [msg #301].

Symptoms [msg #301]:
1. **Content fatigue** — the constant flow of beauty information causes overload.
2. **Financial burden** — spending on procedures and products becomes onerous.
3. **Trend fatigue** — new trends seem endless and exhausting.
4. **Social isolation** — getting ready for events becomes so complicated that it is easier to decline.
5. **Appearance anxiety** — panic before going to work or a meeting if one's look isn't "perfect".
6. **Hyper-awareness** — constant attention to one's own appearance causes discomfort.

### Beauty burnout - 3 coping steps
The same post gives three ways to cope [msg #301]:
1. **Revisit priorities** — determine what really matters to you and what is imposed by society.
2. **Simplify the routine** — drop superfluous procedures and products.
3. **Focus on health** — instead of chasing an ideal, take care of your physical and mental condition.

The post closes by asking readers whether they have felt this and how they coped [msg #301].

### Seligman's three factors behind women's higher depression rates
Source: the author read Martin Seligman's *What You Can Change and What You Can't* over the holidays (she notes he is "that same" Seligman of learned optimism and character strengths). The text is over twenty years old, yet some parts sound "frighteningly relevant" to her [msg #517].

The claim: women suffer from depression more often than men — "not slightly", but stably and systematically. Seligman, using late-20th-century data, writes that depression in women occurs about twice as often as in men. This is not explained by women seeing therapists more often, by them "complaining more", or by differences in income or education; even when men and women with the same living conditions are compared, the gap remains [msg #517].

Simple explanations Seligman rules out, as summarised by the author [msg #517]:
- **Not hormones** — they have an effect, but not enough to explain such a gap.
- **Not genes** — the genetic contribution to depression exists in both sexes.
- **Not work or money** — rich women are still more depressed than rich men.

The three factors that remain [msg #517]:
1. **Learned helplessness.** From childhood girls are more often taught to be accommodating, careful and dependent. Their efforts are more often ignored, their anger isn't encouraged, their initiative isn't always noticed. Growing up, a woman meets a world where her role is constantly devalued — in the family, at work and in public space. And helplessness, according to Seligman, is a direct path to depression.
2. **Rumination.** When something goes wrong, women more often start to *think* — to chew it over, analyse, look for the cause in themselves. Men more often *act* or at least distract themselves; sometimes destructively (alcohol, working to the limit, aggression), but they still get out of looping thought.
3. **The cult of thinness.** In cultures where thinness is the ideal, women have more depression and more eating disorders; conversely, where this ideal is absent, there is no gender skew in depression.

Seligman's key point, per the author: these factors are "not carved in stone or set in concrete". They are not "female nature" but learnable, socially supported patterns — which means they can be changed [msg #517].

### Individuals vs Donors (Kate Manne, via the Nagoski sisters)
The author found this concept in the Nagoski sisters' book *Burnout* (read in January); it belongs to philosopher Kate Manne and, she says, explains much about the current system [msg #562]:
- **Individuals (Индивидуумы)** — those who have the right to realize their human essence: achieve, conquer, take up space, go toward a goal. Their values: competition, achievement, power, autonomy.
- **Donors (Доноры)** — those obliged to provide this right to others: give themselves, support, care, serve — and to do so sincerely, wholeheartedly, expecting nothing in return. Their values: care, empathy, support, self-giving.

"Угадайте, люди какого пола Доноры по большей части?" (Guess which sex the Donors mostly are?) [msg #562]

The author's interpretation: this goes deeper than men vs women — it is a system where some hold power and others submit, some realize themselves and others make that possible. She calls the system "male" not to build a new division but because men built it over millennia, and women were allowed to play in it "only recently, to put it mildly"; to advance in the game you have to play by its rules [msg #562].

**The Donor syndrome.** The authors (the Nagoskis) call it a virus that you catch with your first breath. Its most insidious symptom is the conviction that it isn't a disease but the norm — common sense, "the way the world works". The author's suggestion: you can at least start asking questions [msg #562].

**Cultural archetype example.** Joseph Campbell, author of *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*, when asked about the heroine's journey, reportedly answered that women don't need to go anywhere — the woman is already where she needs to be, she is the destination the man must reach. The author laughs at it (🤡) but argues it is not just one scholar's sexism but an archetype sewn into culture — fairy tales, language, upbringing. Boys are taught to conquer and achieve; girls to be a convenient "destination". A girl who wants her own journey, goals and voice has one option: join the Individuals' race, adopt their values, play by others' rules and prove she is "no worse" [msg #562].

**Author's own case.** She did exactly that for roughly the first ten years of her conscious life — honestly trying to achieve, compete and conform — "Пока не поняла, что выигрываю в игре, в которую не хочу играть" (Until I understood that I was winning a game I don't want to play) [msg #562].

### Steps toward a world built on Donor values
Context: under her Instagram post about war (seen by almost 50,000 people) the author ran a poll: if women ruled the world, would interaction between people be more empathetic, ethical and peaceful? She expected men to come and explain why she was wrong, but it was women who answered "no": "Власть портит всех" (Power corrupts everyone). She grants they are "not wrong", but clarifies she does not mean the same game with a player of the other sex at the top ("те же яйца, вид сбоку" — the same thing, viewed from the side). She means a world built on Donor values, which "could become the basis of another world — one in which war might simply have no place" [msg #562].

Her hedges: nobody knows for sure; but a world where all children — not boys, not girls, but all — are taught compassion as systematically as mathematics is a different world, in which the question probably wouldn't arise. She admits her reasoning is philosophical and perhaps realizable only after humanity's self-destruction or with "very strict face control" for a Mars colony 😅 [msg #562].

What to stop [msg #562]:
1. **Stop militarizing children** — remove the cult of strength as the only language of respect.
2. **Stop setting people against each other in the media.**
3. **Stop provoking states of greed and scarcity.**
4. **Stop sowing panic.**

What to do instead [msg #562]:
1. **Teach empathy.**
2. **Promote mindfulness** — not as a trendy wellness trend but as the skill of noticing yourself, noticing another, and stopping before causing harm.
3. **Develop compassion** as "the most difficult and rare form of courage".
4. **Above all, start with yourself** ("начать с самой себя").

The post's image card carries a Jiddu Krishnamurti quote that supports the last step: nothing more is needed — look at yourself, observe yourself, explore yourself, because in the state we are in we will create a monstrous world; you can fly to the Moon, Venus, Mars, but you will always carry yourself there; change yourself first, and to change, look at yourself, go deep into yourself — observe, listen, learn; it is not a teaching or a message, you can do it yourself if you want [img #562].

### Why the author keeps returning to this
In the depression post the author says Seligman's findings strongly resonate with what she sees in her work "and in herself": often women's depressive episodes are a logical consequence of living in constant tension, self-control, doubt and the attempt to be "enough" (достаточной). For her the most alarming question is not why women suffer from depression more often, but why, 20+ years later, we still see the same statistics [msg #517].

## Evidence and numbers

- Seligman (late-20th-century data): depression is about **2 times** more common in women than in men; the gap persists when living conditions are equal and is not explained by therapy-seeking, complaining, income or education [msg #517].
- WHO reports 2024–2025, as cited by the author: in adulthood depression is diagnosed in women about **1.5 times** more often than in men; in adolescence the gap is even larger — girls start to "overtake" boys in depressive states precisely from puberty. The author's conclusion: this is not an artefact of the time or a feature of the nineties but still the norm [msg #517].

- Numbers differ by source: Seligman's late-20th-century data give about 2×, the WHO 2024–2025 figure the author cites is about 1.5× in adults. The author treats both as showing a persistent gap rather than as a contradiction [msg #517].

Women in her poll objected that "power corrupts everyone"; she accepts they are "not wrong" but says she means a different system, not a sex swap at the top [msg #562].
- She stresses calling the system "male" is not meant to create a new division, and that the Individual/Donor split is "deeper than men and women" [msg #562].

# Gadgets and trackers

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's views on wellness technology: a structured review of the Lumen metabolism tracker, a wish-list of wellness gadgets, Katya Strelnikova's personal experience of the "nocebo effect" from wearing an Apple Watch, her rules for when trackers are justified, a forwarded 2x2 scheme matching tracker use to a person's interoception and anxiety, and a list of nine free apps she uses for self-observation [msg #21, #26, #100, #101, #587, #661]. According to the source it matters because trackers can both help (building a routine, learning body signals) and harm (anxiety, neurosis, a false sense of control, losing contact with the body), and because the wellness market sells devices that cannot replace basic habits [msg #26, #101, #597, #652].

Part 2 adds two posts. Both apply the channel's tracker skepticism to new devices: the "biological age" metric in Whoop and Oura, and body-composition readings from home smart scales (Xiaomi) [msg #670, #739]. The existing file already covers the nocebo effect, the 5 situations when trackers are needed, the Six-month rule, the interoception x anxiety scheme and the "trackers as a tool, not an oracle" post. These two posts extend that material in three ways:
- They give a specific mechanism for why the numbers are unreliable. Biological age is calculated from indirect proxies, and body fat is extrapolated from the legs only.
- They show again how such numbers feed anxiety.
- They show again Katya's stance of not letting gadgets decide whether she is "normal".

Why it matters, according to the source: these "diagnoses" are delivered "таким уверенным голосом, с графиками и цветными шкалами" (in such a confident voice, with graphs and coloured scales). For anxious people they become "новая ось, вокруг которой закручивается тревога и недостаточность" (a new axis around which anxiety and a sense of inadequacy spin) [msg #670, #739].

## Core ideas

- **No device "hacks" metabolism.** Gadgets can fine-tune a routine for advanced users, but sport, walking, good sleep and healthy food remain the actual work (see *Lumen gadget evaluation*) [msg #26].
- **Trackers can cause a nocebo effect.** Negative readings make a healthy person feel unwell: "see stress in the app — feel stress from what I saw — I'm stressed" (see *The Apple Watch nocebo story*) [msg #100].
- **Long tracking without a purpose leads to neurosis.** More than six months of tracking without a current change to measure is, per Katya, a direct road to neurosis (see *Six-month rule*) [msg #101].
- **A tracker is only a tool with a specific purpose.** It is needed in particular life phases (see *5 situations when trackers are needed*), and must not be given the authority of an oracle [msg #101, #652].
- **Who benefits depends on interoception and anxiety.** People who sense body signals poorly and are calm get the most benefit; anxious people risk dependence or self-doubt (see *Interoception x anxiety scheme*) [msg #661].
- **The most effective tools are free.** Katya's stance on biohacking and fashionable gadgets: the basics (sleep, nutrition, movement, recovery) and free apps are enough [msg #587, #597].

- **Biological age in Whoop and Oura is marketing wrapped in science.** The number comes from indirect indicators, not from clinical methods. See *Biological age in Whoop and Oura* [msg #670].
- **For anxious people a biological-age number causes neurosis, not motivation.** Only people who are fine with anxiety and with contact with their body can use it as a normal stimulus to change something [msg #670].
- **Scales know for certain only your weight; everything else is a guess.** Fat, muscle, bone and water come from a closed manufacturer formula applied to a measurement of the legs. See *How smart scales estimate body composition* [msg #739].
- **Smart-scale fat readings are systematically fragile.** The formula is built for an "average" woman, and fat distribution, age, time of day, coffee, training, cycle phase and foot condition all shift the reading. See *4 reasons a smart-scale reading goes wrong* [msg #739].
- **Do not let a gadget decide whether you are normal.** Katya's conclusion from both posts: "Я больше не разрешаю гаджетам решать, нормальная ли я." (I no longer allow gadgets to decide whether I am normal.) [msg #739].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Lumen gadget evaluation (Marina Sidorich's review)
Purpose: a review done at subscribers' request (they asked for it in comments under the wish-list post) of Lumen, a popular wellness gadget whose marketing promises to "hack" metabolism, increase fat burning, help lose weight and raise energy. Marina notes the marketing team did its job — after viewing the website you already want to order it [msg #26]. The review is structured in seven parts:
1. **Essence of the device.** Everyone wants to know how to burn calories more efficiently, build muscle, sleep better and be energetic. The tracker's key task is to assess the body's individual ability to switch between two kinds of fuel — fats and carbohydrates. This is called metabolic flexibility ("метаболическая гибкость"). The review states that the more effective it is, the higher insulin sensitivity, the better the state of the mitochondria, and therefore overall health markers [msg #26].
2. **How it works.** Lumen determines whether you are burning fats or carbohydrates at the moment by measuring the CO2 level in your breath. Based on 2 weeks of data collection, it gives recommendations on what can be adjusted in nutrition and training to make metabolism more efficient. Previously such tests could only be done in a clinic; now there is a smart gadget [msg #26].
3. **Who it is for.** Marina's conclusion after studying the question: definitely for advanced healthy-lifestyle users who have already implemented general principles of nutrition and sleep and are ready to observe their metabolism in more detail. "Прибор вас «не похудеет»" (the device won't "make you lose weight"), but it can help find individual insights. It may be very useful for marathon runners and athletes, to replenish carbohydrate stores in time and recover faster [msg #26].
4. **Contraindications** (as listed on the manufacturer's site): diabetes; asthma; COPD (ХОБЛ); pregnancy; thyroid diseases; kidney disease; cancer [msg #26].
5. **Pros mentioned in online reviews:**
   - the ability to observe and analyse your routine — how nutrition, sleep and training affect your individual metabolism;
   - a treasure trove of information and advice on how to increase energy levels;
   - everyone mentions a great and extensive collection of recipes [msg #26].
6. **Cons:**
   - there are no independent clinical studies;
   - it can lead to disordered eating behaviour, fixation on food, or misinterpretation of results [msg #26].
7. **Personal verdict.** No device will "hack" metabolism or make us lose weight — only our conscious behaviour will. With or without Lumen you still need regular exercise, walks, good sleep and healthy food. But it is nice to have the option to fine-tune your routine and training to yourself even more precisely [msg #26].
Side note: the Lumen website has a free metabolism test; Marina took it, it "praised her mitochondria", and gave the main reminder that to burn carbohydrates efficiently you need good sleep — 7–9 hours of real sleep [msg #26].

### 5 situations when trackers are needed
Purpose: Katya's checklist for deciding whether wearing a tracker is justified, written as the follow-up to her nocebo post [msg #101]:
1. **When we are restructuring our routine** ("Когда перестраиваем режим") — e.g. in her own case, changing her daily schedule [msg #100, #101].
2. **When we are instilling a habit of movement** — tracking the amount of movement until it becomes habitual (she now knows she walks no less than 15,000 steps without checking) [msg #100, #101].
3. **When we are learning to recognise the body's signals of fatigue** [msg #101].
4. **Or the sensations in different cardio zones at the initial stage of cardio training** — she used it while learning to control cardio zones; now she knows the sensations [msg #100, #101].
5. **On the recommendation of a specialist or doctor** [msg #101].
Katya's own past reasons that fit these: surfing (tracking waves), changing her routine, learning to control cardio zones, monitoring movement. Once these were no longer relevant — she moved away from the waves, wakes up without an alarm, knows her step count and cardio-zone sensations, feels when she has slept enough and understands at body level when she needs to replenish energy — she concluded "Теперь можно не трекать, а жить." (Now you can stop tracking and just live.) [msg #100].

### Six-month rule
Katya's rule: "Трекинг показателей больше шести месяцев и без наличия текущих изменений, которые нужно замерять, — прямая дорога к неврозу." (Tracking metrics for more than six months, without any current changes that need measuring, is a direct road to neurosis.) — and therefore to a worsening of one's state [msg #101]. Two further mechanisms she gives:
- Measurements interfere with real contact with the body, however tempting it is to rationalise sensations through apps and the "knowledge" of what you feel [msg #101].
- They give a fictional sense of control — hence the despair when something goes "not according to plan". "(Wake up, Neo. У тела нет никаких планов)" (Wake up, Neo. The body has no plans.) [msg #101].

### Interoception x anxiety 2x2 scheme (forwarded from Daily Reminder)
Purpose: a self-described "(псевднонаучная)" [sic] (pseudo-scientific) scheme that grew out of several weeks of posts about trackers, to show for whom trackers help and for whom they harm [msg #661].
Axis 1 — **interoception**: people perceive body signals differently — hunger, fatigue, even heart rate. One interoception test asks you to compare an external rhythm with your heartbeat: how synchronous are they? The hypothesis: perhaps for people who sense their bodies poorly, trackers are an ideal support for interoception [msg #661].
Axis 2 — **neuroticism (anxiety)**: some people are simply more anxious than others, and sensor data can strongly reinforce that anxiety. Doctors, the post says, advise anxious people who complain about sleep to take off their sleep trackers, to remove an extra reason for worry [msg #661].
The four quadrants from the image (read from the image itself; the OCR text is scrambled) [img #661]:
1. **High anxiety + poorly sense body signals:** *Трекер подменяет ощущения; риск зависимости* (the tracker replaces sensations; risk of dependence).
2. **High anxiety + sense body signals well:** *Начинают сомневаться в собственных ощущениях* (they start doubting their own sensations).
3. **Low anxiety + poorly sense body signals:** *Получают максимум пользы* (they get the maximum benefit).
4. **Low anxiety + sense body signals well:** *Быстро понимают, что не узнают ничего нового* (they quickly realise they won't learn anything new).

### How smart (bioimpedance) scales estimate body composition
Purpose: this is Katya's explanation of why "Весы точно знают только наш вес. Обо всем остальном они гадают." (Scales know for certain only our weight. About everything else they are guessing.) The scales make three steps, and only the first is an actual measurement [msg #739].
1. **They send a current through the legs.** When we stand on the scales barefoot, they pass a weak current from one foot to the other. Muscles contain a lot of water and conduct current well, while fat conducts almost none. The harder it is for the current to pass, the more fat the scales suspect [msg #739].
2. **They see only the legs.** The current goes from one leg to the other through the pelvic area. The scales do not see the belly, chest or arms at all. Katya calls this the catch: "Прикол в том, что весы оценивают только ноги." (The catch is that the scales assess only the legs.) [msg #739].
3. **They extrapolate with a closed formula, and fat is found by subtraction.** The manufacturer's closed formula takes the leg data plus our height, weight, age and sex, and extrapolates how much muscle, bone and water we have. Fat is then simple subtraction: our weight minus what the formula calculated. Katya frames this step as a magic trick ("И дальше начинается магия, следите за руками" — and then the magic begins, watch the hands) [msg #739].
What follows from this mechanism: any error in the estimate of muscle, bone or water ends up in the fat figure automatically [msg #739].

### 4 reasons a smart-scale body-fat reading goes wrong
Katya's list "Где еще что-то может пойти не так" (where else something can go wrong) [msg #739]:
1. **The formula is built for an "average" woman.** If we have more muscle than average, especially in the upper body that the scales cannot see, the scales will undercount the muscle and record everything undercounted as fat [msg #739].
2. **Fat distribution differs between people.** If someone naturally stores fat mostly on the hips, scales that look only at the legs may overestimate fat for the whole body [msg #739].
3. **Age by itself adds fat in the formula.** Part of the percentage is drawn simply because "нам не 25" (we are not 25) [msg #739].
4. **Conditions at the moment of weighing.** Morning, coffee, a workout, the phase of the menstrual cycle, or dry or cold feet can each lead the scales to decide there is more fat [msg #739].
A related point: the "ideal weight" in the app is calculated from BMI. BMI knows nothing about muscles or bones, or about how we feel ("я — супер", I feel great) [msg #739].

### Two-scales consistency check (Katya's self-experiment)
Katya calls this "деталь, которая окончательно убеждает в газлайтинге" (the detail that finally proves the gaslighting) [msg #739].
- A year earlier, different scales in the USA showed her 5 kg less fat at the same body weight [msg #739].
- If she had really gained 5 kg of fat in a year, that would be about 5.5 litres of fat. In her words, that is five and a half cartons of milk spread around her body, and it would be impossible not to notice [msg #739].
- Her body is the same. Only the scales and their formula changed [msg #739].
- The lesson: when two devices disagree this much while the body visibly has not changed, the disagreement shows that the formula is the problem, not the body [msg #739].

## Detailed insights

### The Apple Watch nocebo story
Katya put on an Apple Watch a couple of years before the post, attracted by listening to podcasts without a phone and tracking waves (surfing). Gradually she became a person whose day starts with the Sleep++ app and continues with Welltory — "how else would I know whether I slept well and how I feel?" [msg #100].
One morning she saw a "bad" sleep score, then energy and health readings "at zero", and fell into despair and rage at her powerlessness: "Какого хрена я столько делаю, а получаю это?!" (What the hell, I do so much and get this?!). After calming down she realised she felt worse than before the measurements — and this was not the first time [msg #100]. Her formula: "Вижу стресс в приложении — испытываю стресс от увиденного — я в стрессе." (I see stress in the app — I feel stress from what I saw — I'm stressed.) [msg #100].
She found she was not alone: everyone has heard of the placebo effect (what we believe in helps), and with the global fitness-tracker market forecast to reach $139 billion by 2028, a conversation about the nocebo effect of gadgets has started. Definition (also on the glossary card): the nocebo effect is when a person feels bad not because they are unwell but because they believe this should make them feel bad; in this context, negative tracker readings cause feeling unwell despite a healthy state [msg #100, img #100].
Result: two weeks without the Apple Watch — "Полет нормальный." (Flight normal.) [msg #100].

### Two years later: trackers as a tool, not an oracle
Following a post by Marina, Katya reminded readers of the nocebo post and said she has for some time worn an Apple Watch again and glances at it, but without her former fanaticism [msg #652].
She observes a mass abandonment of trackers — it seems "soooooo last season", trend-setters have already given them up; the pendulum has swung the other way, and she predicts Whoop will soon stop being fashionable and we will increasingly hear of people giving up trackers. On one hand this is an example of how trends and marketing work; on the other, a tracker is just a tool. At certain moments in life it is certainly needed, useful, and can help return attention to oneself and one's state. The key is, when putting it on a wrist or finger, to understand what goal we are pursuing — and not to give it the powers of a Pythia (oracle) who knows better than us what we need [msg #652].
She is surprised how easily we hand over to someone outside the right to decide how we feel and what we need: first scales, then cycle calendars, then steps, then sleep, then HRV. Each new instrument promises we will finally learn the truth about ourselves — as if we did not already have a built-in, ancient, perfectly working sensor inside [msg #652].
Why is it so hard to trust our own body without an intermediary? A number on a screen seems more reliable than a sensation, although the sensation came first — the body knew about lack of movement, lack of sleep, fatigue, hunger and anxiety before anyone told us. Her conclusion: "Мое будущее не за развитием технологий, а за развитием чувствования и познания себя." (My future lies not in developing technology but in developing sensing and self-knowledge.) [msg #652].

### Skepticism toward biohacking and unnecessary technologies
Katya laughed out loud on a run listening to an episode of the podcast «Никакого правильно» about gadgets for women — "it would be funny if it weren't so sad", because someone still produces these gadgets and someone buys them. The topic of unnecessary technologies is broader; she often writes about wellness independence and gadgets [msg #597].
She cites Kayla Barnes-Lentz, who spent almost $300,000 on anti-ageing only to conclude that the most effective tools are free — "это, кстати, все, что нужно знать о моем мнении о биохакинге и модных гаджетах" (this, by the way, is all you need to know about my opinion of biohacking and fashionable gadgets) [msg #597].
She cites an RBC study on quality of life: the trend is toward everyday self-care practices, basic habits (sleep, nutrition, movement, recovery), preventive actions and skepticism toward fashionable technologies (respondents are skeptical of coaches too). She was especially pleased by the finding that people are tired of achievements and self-improvement [msg #597].

### Who a biological-age number harms (Katya's quote to Inc.)
Katya says that in her years of practice she has not met people for whom the biological-age story caused anything other than neurosis ("что-то, кроме невроза"). She allows for an exception. People who are fine with anxiety and have good contact with their body may find the figure a normal stimulus to change something. But the people who usually come to her have heightened anxiety. For them, the phrase "вам не 42, а 48" (you are not 42 but 48) is not motivation. It becomes "новая ось, вокруг которой закручивается тревога и недостаточность" (a new axis around which anxiety and a sense of inadequacy spin) [msg #670].

How this connects to part 1: it is the same split as the *Interoception x anxiety 2x2 scheme* in the existing file. Low anxiety plus good contact with the body means the number is harmless or useful, and high anxiety means the number does harm. Here Katya applies that split to a specific metric [msg #670].

Katya places the post inside her long-running conversation about wellness technologies and trends. The earlier parts, all summarised in the existing file, are the nocebo effect of trackers, wellness independence, gadgets she ended up not needing, and the pendulum swinging the other way as trend-setters began giving up Whoop. ### The Xiaomi scales story: from self-punishment to laughing out loud
Katya weighed herself on Xiaomi smart scales for the first time in a year. She again received two "(pseudo)discoveries" about herself [msg #739]:
1. **"I am obese."** In fact her weight is normal (71–73 kg) and her fat is within the norm. She knows this from the mirror ("ожирением там не пахнет", there is no hint of obesity there) and from a recent bioimpedance test on a professional device. That device showed 24.5% fat, while the scales showed 33%. It also noted that BMI does not reflect the real picture in her case, because she has more muscle than the norm [msg #739].
2. **"I need to lose 10.5 kg."** Yet the last time she weighed 61 kg, people cautiously asked whether she was all right [msg #739].

She calls the scales a "third voice" in a running argument about her body. She had recently written about why she will never be thin. She had also written that people consider her thin while she does not. The scales are the third voice, and in their view she is not just fat but obese [msg #739].

Katya describes the emotional effect of such numbers and how it has changed for her [msg #739]:
- In the past, these "diagnoses" would have thrown her off balance for weeks. Her mood would sour instantly. She might immediately go on a diet, and she would walk around for several days feeling something was wrong with her. The scales could plunge her into horror, self-hatred ("самоненависничество" [sic]) and self-torment.
- She explains why: the scales deliver their verdict "таким уверенным голосом, с графиками и цветными шкалами" (in such a confident voice, with graphs and coloured scales).
- Now she simply reacts with astonishment out loud.

This is the nocebo pattern from part 1, applied to scales instead of a sleep score. Katya's lesson is a stance she states directly: "Мое тело нельзя свести к формуле, написанной для женщины, которой не существует." (My body cannot be reduced to a formula written for a woman who does not exist.) [msg #739].

The post closes by asking readers to share it if scales have ever gaslit them and to send it to a friend who is constantly upset by "вердикта неодушевленного предмета" (the verdict of an inanimate object). It also asks readers what their relationship with scales is like [msg #739].

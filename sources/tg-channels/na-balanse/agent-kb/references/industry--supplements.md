# Supplements: benefits and harms

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's evidence reviews and cautionary tales about dietary supplements: Katya Strelnikova's 48-day NMN self-experiment with sleep-tracker screenshots, Marina Sidorich's reviews of vitamin D and collagen, a forwarded story of a stroke linked to high-dose omega-3, a chat answer debunking the fullerene C60 hype, and Katya's three-part series "Как навредить себе витаминами. И почему не надо слушать специалистов в интернетах" (How to harm yourself with vitamins, and why you should not listen to specialists on the internet). According to the source, it matters because supplements are active substances, not "just vitamins": they can overdose, interact, and be pushed by incompetent experts, by deepfakes of competent doctors, and by AI chatbots manipulated by manufacturers [msg #617, #635, #650].

Every supplement post carries the channel's disclaimer: "Посты в этом канале не являются рекомендацией к использованию добавок" (Posts in this channel are not a recommendation to use supplements) and a doctor must be consulted [msg #31, #34].

## Core ideas

- **Supplements are active substances that can harm.** Fat-soluble vitamins accumulate, some water-soluble ones and minerals cause damage at long high doses, and supplements interact with each other and with drugs (full list in *Vitamins and minerals that can harm*) [msg #617].
- **"Practically no side effects" claims can be wrong.** Katya's NMN experiment gave real energy but also insomnia, a rash and a depressive episode, which resolved after stopping [msg #31, #32, #33].
- **Fashionable supplements often lack human evidence.** NMN/NAD+, berberine, adaptogens and C60 are cited as hype with thin or animal-only data [msg #617, #637].
- **"Pills feel like medicine, supplements feel like self-care" is a dangerous illusion.** Anything that really changes the body can change it in the wrong direction too (omega-3 story) [msg #636].
- **Vitamin D is the one supplement the channel's co-author endorses** — at the minimal recommended dose or an individual dose after a doctor and a test — while warning of overdose [msg #34, #617].
- **Collagen's benefit is not proven by independent research,** and it will not work without a healthy lifestyle [msg #99].
- **Online advice is unreliable at three levels:** incompetent experts, deepfaked competent doctors, and AI chatbots that can be steered by hidden manufacturer instructions [msg #635, #650].
- **Lifestyle first, supplements last.** "Костыли и примочки систему не заменят" (crutches and gimmicks will not replace the system) [msg #650].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Diary timeline method for linking a supplement to side effects
Purpose: to find out whether scattered symptoms are caused by a new supplement, rather than dismissing them as coincidences. Katya did not connect her symptoms to each other or to NMN at first; the connection became obvious only when she looked at her diary and drew a timeline: "когда посмотрела в дневник и нарисовала таймлайн, все встало на места" (when I looked at my diary and drew a timeline, everything fell into place) [msg #31].
1. **Keep a diary while taking anything new** — she recorded the dates when sleep problems and the rash started [msg #31].
2. **Lay the events on a timeline.** Her timeline: 19 November — started NMN; 28 November — first noted sleep problems; 28 November — also first noted the rash on her legs; 5 January — stopped NMN; 9 January — for the first time in 1.5 months the Sleep++ app showed a sleep score of 100 [msg #31].
3. **Compare the symptoms with the known side-effect profile of the substance's class.** She stopped trusting the claims that the product "практически нет побочных эффектов" (has practically no side effects) and that it is not directly related to B vitamins although it is a B3 derivative, and checked the side effects of B vitamins: sleep disturbance, heightened excitability, skin lesions — exactly her three symptoms. "Круг замкнулся" (the circle closed) [msg #31].
4. **Stop the substance and check whether the symptoms go away**, using objective data where possible (sleep tracker, see *Evidence and numbers*) [msg #31, #32, #33].

### Safe self-testing rule
When to use: "Если уж очень хочется попробовать что-то самостоятельно" (if you really want to try something on your own) [msg #617].
1. **Only one new product at a time** — so that you can tell what affected you [msg #617].
2. **Observe yourself for at least a week** — how you sleep, how you feel, whether there are any unusual reactions [msg #617].
Caveat from the author: this helps understand what affected you, but some consequences, such as vessel calcification from vitamin D overdose, accumulate silently and without symptoms. "Поэтому лучший наблюдатель здесь все равно врач и анализы, а не самочувствие" (so the best observer here is still the doctor and lab tests, not how you feel) [msg #617].

### Decision path before taking supplements ("Как действовать")
Katya's three-question chain at the end of the series [msg #650]:
1. **Is something bothering me?** Examples: fatigue, hair loss, poor sleep, low energy [msg #650].
2. **Do I understand the causes?** She stresses that fatigue, hair loss, poor sleep and low energy are symptoms, not causes ("симптомы, а не причины") — a supplement aimed at a symptom skips finding the cause [msg #650].
3. **How long ago was I at the doctor and/or did I take lab tests?** The path ends in a real specialist and tests, not in self-prescription [msg #650].
Overarching rule: first sort out lifestyle — sleep, food, movement, stress. "Добавки — это именно «добавки». К тому, что уже работает." (Supplements are exactly "additions". To what already works.) [msg #650].

### Three takeaways of the "How to harm yourself with vitamins" series
Katya's own summary of all three parts [msg #650]:
1. **Vitamins can harm.** At best they turn into "дорогостоящую мочу" (expensive urine). Detail in *Vitamins and minerals that can harm* [msg #650, #617].
2. **Doctors who recommend supplements to everyone indiscriminately raise doubts about their competence.** Worse, competent doctors may not know they are promoting something — deepfakes can no longer be told apart [msg #650, #635].
3. **Consulting AI about health is possible, but with nuances.** Even if you phrase the request correctly and interpret the chatbot's advice correctly, you may get unregulated advertising of drugs and supplements (see *Indirect prompt injection*) [msg #650].
Conclusion: do not listen to people from the internet and chatbots; observe yourself, use your head, and involve real specialists (doctors) [msg #650].

### How a supplement hype is born (C60 pattern)
Katya calls fullerene C60 "классическая история, как рождается хайп вокруг добавки" (a classic story of how hype around a supplement is born) [msg #637]:
1. **One study, not in humans** — in rats, and only 6 rats [msg #637].
2. **An accidental result** — it showed a "doubling of life", although the scientists were not studying that at all [msg #637].
3. **An article comes out, biohackers pick it up, then marketers** [msg #637].
4. **Mass adoption** — "И вот уже мамы пьют масло с молекулой из космической химии" (and now moms are drinking oil with a molecule from space chemistry) [msg #637].
5. **What is missing:** no clinical trials in humans, no long-term safety data [msg #637].
Her verdict: the molecule itself is interesting, but far more research is needed before deciding to take it ("сейчас ничего пока непонятно" — right now nothing is clear) — unless you want to be that very test subject experimenting on yourself. Her general opinion on supplements: "не стоит в себя ничего пихать личшнего" [sic] (you shouldn't stuff anything extra into yourself) [msg #637].

### How indirect prompt injection makes AI recommend a product
Definition: a supplement manufacturer embeds invisible instructions in its website — text a human does not see but AI does [msg #650]. Mechanism, step by step:
1. You ask a chatbot a question about a supplement, and the bot goes to search the internet [msg #650].
2. It makes a search query, gets a list of sites, reads their content and forms an answer [msg #650].
3. If the manufacturer's site is in the results — which can be ensured through ordinary SEO promotion, i.e. literally bought — the AI reads the page together with the hidden instructions [msg #650].
4. The AI starts recommending that particular product; "одному богу известно, насколько это полезно именно вам" (God only knows how useful it is for you specifically) [msg #650].
Evidence: a 2025 JAMA Network Open study the author cites simulated hundreds of AI dialogues, some with hidden manufacturer instructions; in 94% of cases the AI changed its recommendations, and sometimes recommended harmful drugs (for example, recommending to pregnant women drugs that are prohibited in pregnancy). The scariest part, per Katya, is that this is neither regulated nor labelled in any way [msg #650].

## Detailed insights

### NMN: what it is and why people take it
NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide) belongs to the vitamin B3 group, but strictly it is a "derivative" of the vitamin rather than the vitamin itself — a point often made when side effects are discussed, as if it were "не одно и тоже" (not the same thing). The molecule is a direct precursor of NAD+, which plays an important role in energy production. From age 30 the amount of NAD+ in cells steadily declines, so taking NMN is meant to raise NAD+ levels again [msg #31].

Katya describes herself as "slightly crazy" about finding energy and a lover of self-experiments. Having already introduced basic energy practices, she noticed NMN because Andrew Huberman ("всеми любимый Хуберман", everyone's favourite Huberman) and many respected doctors regularly promote and take it. Her superficial internet research found nothing alarming — even that the supplement has practically no side effects — so she bought it [msg #31].

### Katya's NMN protocol
- Duration: 48 days, 19 November to 5 January [msg #31].
- Dose: followed the package recommendations ("usually quite safe"): first 10 days 2 × 0.5 g, then 1 × 0.5 g per day (she reduced it because of the side effects described below) [msg #31].
- Form and timing: powder under the tongue, after waking and a morning glass of water, then sitting down to meditate [msg #31].
- Product choice: there are many fakes and low-quality products; she chose one with good reviews, plus "дядя Андрей Хуберман" (uncle Andrew Huberman) said it was fine [msg #31].

### NMN effects: energy, yes
Short answer to "did NMN give energy": yes. She felt it immediately — the sensation was like the film shot of a dilating pupil; later she found Reddit comparisons with amphetamine. During meditation she felt her brain and body come alive. She adds that energetic people do not jump out of bed either — she is still sleepy at that point [msg #31].

Through the day she felt increased energy, more strength and higher productivity — though in hindsight she would rather call it "возбужденностью" (agitation/over-arousal). She seemed to need less time to sleep: she fell asleep later (or did not sleep at all) and still woke without an alarm as usual [msg #31].

### NMN side effects that "don't exist"
Three changes occurred while she was taking it, which she did not immediately link to each other or to NMN [msg #31]:
1. Sleep problems and general over-excitability of the nervous system.
2. A depressive episode.
3. Pimples / a rash on her legs that she had never had before (her husband affectionately called it "пузырями пошла", "broke out in bubbles").
They matched the B-vitamin side-effect list (sleep disturbance, increased excitability, skin lesions), and her timeline showed sleep problems and rash both starting nine days after she began; she stopped NMN. "Совпадение? Не думаю." (Coincidence? I don't think so.) [msg #31].

Will she take it again? She is not sure she needs it permanently at this stage of life; she thinks she may return to the idea after 60, since judging by reviews it is effective then — but she would first have to find her own dosage [msg #31]. Later, in the vitamins series, she reframes NMN as a fashionable supplement without an evidence base (see *Fashionable supplements*) and recalls that it "broke" her sleep [msg #617].

### Vitamin D: the case for (Marina Sidorich)
Vitamin D is the only supplement recommended in the official dietary guidelines of many countries. Nearly 1 billion people on Earth are deficient — by Marina's estimate, every 8th subscriber [msg #34].

What is known: paediatricians recommend it against rickets; it has long been known to help absorb calcium and phosphorus needed for bone formation. Its action is broader: anti-inflammatory, antioxidant and neuroprotective properties that support immunity, muscle function and brain activity. Its role in increasing insulin sensitivity and preventing cancer cell growth is being researched [msg #34].

Who is deficient: deficiency is very common among people in northern latitudes, but not only — SPF cosmetics and clothing also block vitamin D production ("block the penetration of vitamin D", as written), and some liver, kidney and intestinal diseases can be predictors of deficiency [msg #34].

Atypical symptoms of deficiency: fatigue, muscle weakness, pain or cramps, depression [msg #34].

Doses: the most common daily recommendations are 400 IU for children under 12 months, 600 IU for ages 1–70, 800 IU for people over 70. Not all experts agree; many organisations recommend up to 2000 IU for adults and 1000 IU for children. A doctor can recommend the dose based on individual tests [msg #34].

Overdose risk: there is a risk of "передознуться" (overdosing), so consult a doctor and ideally test your vitamin D level before taking it. Excess and high doses can lead to calcium stones [msg #34].

Marina's personal experience: although she spent a year under the sun of Spain and Thailand, her test showed a severe deficiency — so chronic that it had already affected her TSH (ТТГ) hormone production. At the time of writing she had been taking a supplement for 4 months, awaiting the next test; she clearly had more energy and better mood, but she notes everything works as a complex because she also eats well, sleeps well and does various sports [msg #34].

Verdict: "Конечно, стоит" (of course it is worth it) — at the minimal recommended dose or an individual dose after consulting your doctor [msg #34].

### Vitamin D: the harm side (Katya)
Katya calls vitamin D the most popular and advertised supplement; it is indeed often lacking (though, she notes, hers is fine). Overdose causes hypercalcemia: calcium is washed out of the bones and deposits in the kidneys and blood vessels. She personally knows a woman who was treated for kidney stones after high doses of vitamin D prescribed by a nutritionist. Vessel calcification from overdose accumulates silently, without symptoms, which is why tests and a doctor beat self-observation [msg #617].

### Collagen: magic or myth (Marina Sidorich)
Collagen is a vital component of skin, bones, muscles and tendons; without enough of it these lose elasticity and firmness. With age, collagen production naturally falls by about 1% per year on average, and with menopause a woman loses up to 30% of skin collagen within 5 years [msg #99].

Evidence: the benefit of taking collagen is not yet proven by independent research. Some randomised controlled trials showed improved skin elasticity, better mobility or less joint pain, but most if not all collagen studies are fully or partly funded by industries that benefit from positive results — so it is hard to say how effective supplemental collagen really is [msg #99].

When collagen supplements will not work: collagen is destroyed by excessive alcohol, smoking, lack of sleep, physical inactivity and nutrient deficiency (protein, vitamin C, copper, zinc). Under these conditions there will probably be no skin effect from supplements [msg #99].

Habits that protect natural collagen, besides a healthy balanced diet [msg #99]:
- wear sunscreen or limit time in direct sun;
- do not smoke and avoid second-hand smoke;
- work with stress — constantly high cortisol can reduce collagen production;
- move and exercise to stimulate circulation and strengthen tissues.
Marina's rule: "ни одна добавка без ЗОЖ не работает" (no supplement works without a healthy lifestyle) [msg #99].

### Vitamins and minerals that can harm (series part 1)
Katya opens by saying she is enraged by content like "best pharmacy products for summer", "vitamins every woman must take", "top supplements for 100 roubles that will change your life" — from nutritionists, and especially from doctors who took the Hippocratic oath — and argues it is worth scrolling past [msg #617]. Her list:
1. **Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)** accumulate in the body — roughly, you can overdose on them [msg #617].
2. **Vitamin D** — overdose causes hypercalcemia (see *Vitamin D: the harm side*) [msg #617].
3. **Vitamin A** accumulates in the liver and can damage it; especially dangerous during pregnancy [msg #617].
4. **Vitamin E** in large doses reduces blood clotting and can lead to bleeding, including haemorrhagic stroke [msg #617].
5. **Iron** — "perhaps the most underestimated supplement by risk". People like to take it "for energy" without ferritin tests; a separate story is deciding on our own to raise ferritin within the normal range because bloggers said it should be higher. Excess accumulates in tissues and can lead to liver cirrhosis, diabetes, cardiomyopathy [msg #617].
6. **Vitamin B6** — an unexpected guest: water-soluble, so supposedly excreted, but long-term high doses cause peripheral neuropathy: numbness, tingling, impaired coordination [msg #617].
7. **Zinc** — long-term high doses can cause copper deficiency, and hence anaemia, lowered immunity, neurological symptoms [msg #617].
She stresses: "И это прям далеко не все" (and this is far from everything) [msg #617].

### Fashionable supplements without an evidence base
- **NMN and NAD+** — one of the loudest anti-ageing and wellness trends. Impressive in mice; in humans only isolated, unfinished studies. Katya states the FDA banned selling NMN as a supplement in the US because it needs to be tested and certified [msg #617].
- **Berberine**, called "natural Ozempic" — per Katya, it is therapy, not prevention [msg #617].
- **Adaptogens, from ashwagandha to chaga** — studied very unevenly. She personally knows people who "broke themselves" with these supplements [msg #617].
- **Fullerene C60** — see *How a supplement hype is born*. Bryan Johnson, who spends millions on his longevity, does not take C60, which Katya finds telling: for all his obsession with optimisation he filters fairly strictly by evidence [msg #637].

### Interactions we don't think about
Katya: we often do not consider how supplements interact with each other, and it matters [msg #617]:
- **Omega-3 and vitamin E thin the blood** — combined with anticoagulants this is a risk of bleeding from a minor injury.
- **St John's wort (зверобой)**, the popular "natural antidepressant", reduces the effectiveness of oral contraceptives and some antidepressants. Her husband, while on antidepressants, accidentally drank tea with St John's wort and was badly frightened: he experienced an altered state of consciousness, a racing heart, "и вот это все" (and all that).
- **Magnesium** interferes with absorption of some antibiotics.
Lesson: when prescribing yourself vitamins from the internet, you can miss very important factors [msg #617].

### Omega-3 and the stroke story (forwarded post by Marina Demidova)
Marina Demidova had lunch with Olya Shemyatovskaya (leader of the neurologist and psychiatrist team at DocMed) and discussed, in an evidence-based way, supplements and "капельницы молодости" (youth IV drips). Olya told her horror stories "you won't read in articles"; the one that struck Marina most was a woman of about 30 who took high doses of omega-3 supplements and, as a result, had a stroke [msg #636].

Mechanism given: omega-3 usually lives in our heads as something useful (for heart, vessels, brain, skin). But high doses of omega-3 show an unpleasant signal in large studies — they may raise the risk of atrial fibrillation. Atrial fibrillation is not just "the heart beating strangely"; it is an arrhythmia in which a clot can form, and the clot can travel to the brain and cause an ischaemic stroke [msg #636].

The psychological lesson: people fear "pills" but calmly take a handful of supplements, because a pill feels like medicine while a supplement feels like self-care with only good effects. "Но если вещество реально что-то меняет в организме, оно может менять не только в ту сторону, в которую обещает реклама" (but if a substance really changes something in the body, it can change it not only in the direction the advertising promises) — nor only in the direction promised by a person in a white coat whose recommendations follow pharma promoters. Conclusion: "осторожнее с БАДами" (be more careful with supplements) [msg #636].

### Deepfake doctors (series part 2)
Katya's point: online doctors are a risk not only because they may be incompetent, but because competent doctors may not even know they are recommending something. She learned this from Olya Titova's lecture on AI and health in the Biosisters community: deepfake doctors are already impossible to tell from real ones, and real doctors — even excellent specialists — simply do not know their image is used in ads. She stresses it is not conspiracy theory: The Guardian wrote about it in December 2025 [msg #635].

Case: British paediatrician David Taylor-Robinson found himself in 14 TikTok videos advertising menopause supplements with turmeric and Himalayan shilajit. In one, his deepfake seriously described a non-existent menopause symptom called "thermometer leg" (when you get hot at night and need to stick a leg out from under the blanket). The professor has nothing to do with women's health. TikTok at first refused to remove the videos as not violating its rules. "Это новая индустрия" (this is a new industry), not an isolated case [msg #635].

What frightens her even more than deepfakes: total trust in AI for health questions and recommendations, where there is "полнейший Дикий Запад" (a complete Wild West) and no regulation at all [msg #635].

### AI as a health adviser (series part 3)
Increasingly, people go for advice not to a doctor or even social media but to Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and the like — Katya admits she does too: it is convenient, fast, free, no waiting for an appointment, no discomfort of talking to another person. In that month alone she twice heard from clients about being "deceived by AI" on health; one client got so offended she did not open it for three days (the question involved 3,500 euros) [msg #650].

Her assessment: the reliability of AI medical advice, especially in women's health, is "пока не очень" (not great yet), even given how fast models improve. And it is not only the AI: the human factor plays a huge role — how we phrase the question, how accurately we interpret the answer, and whether we apply it correctly to our situation. On top of that comes indirect prompt injection (see the framework above) [msg #650].

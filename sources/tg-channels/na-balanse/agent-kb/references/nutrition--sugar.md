# Sugar, sweets and glucose

## What this covers

This topic gathers what the channel taught about sugar: why the calorie count alone does not describe the effect of food on health and energy (Robert Lustig via the Huberman podcast, and an excerpt from Mark Hyman's book "Сахарная ловушка"), how excess glucose overloads the mitochondria and drains energy, why fructose is singled out as especially harmful, and how to fit sweets into life without strict bans, binge-restrict cycles or self-blame. It matters, according to the source, because sugar spikes measurably affect energy through the day, because excess glucose "легко приводит к упадку сил" (easily leads to a loss of strength), and because strict prohibitions raise the risk of breakdowns ("срывов") [msg #28, #30, #170].

## Core ideas

- **Calories are not equal.** The number of calories that go into the mouth is not what the body counts, absorbs at the same speed, or uses in the same way; some calories heal, others harm. Katya Strelnikova (after Lustig) and Mark Hyman both reject "a calorie is a calorie" as the basis of health and weight loss [msg #28, #29].
- **Fast sugar spikes glucose and insulin; insulin is the villain of metabolic health.** Lustig considers insulin one of the main drivers of worsening metabolic health, obesity and reduced lifespan; Hyman lists the downstream harms of high insulin [msg #28, #29].
- **Glucose is needed, fructose is not.** Glucose is vital for energy and hormones; fructose (in soda and shop sweets) is described as addictive, functionless and damaging. Fruit and berries remain recommended because their fibre limits fructose absorption [msg #28].
- **Too much glucose drains energy.** Mitochondria make energy from glucose, but overload produces free radicals, oxidative stress and mitochondrial death — the stoker buried in coal [msg #30].
- **Sweets belong in life, without bans.** Eat sweets after a real meal with protein and fibre, keep sugar to about 10% (5–10% added sugar), and build toward roughly 85% whole foods by adding foods and non-food joys rather than restricting [msg #30, #170].
- **Cravings have causes beyond willpower.** Lack of sleep, under-eating and a bland diet drive sweet overeating; comfort-eating in hard periods signals unmet basic needs and calls for kindness, not self-scolding [msg #170, #257].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Four reasons calories are not equal (Lustig, via Huberman)
Purpose: to dismantle the slogan «Калория — это просто калория!» (*"A calorie is just a calorie!"*). Katya Strelnikova listened to the December Huberman episode with Professor Robert Lustig on how sugar and processed food affect health — her summary: they "делают нездоровье из здоровья" (*turn health into un-health*) — and picked the parts relevant to the channel. She strongly recommends the whole episode to anyone interested in metabolic health and weight loss [msg #28].

Who uses the slogan and why: according to the author, it is used by marketers of food giants — to downplay the influence of food on health — and by specialists who promise to make you "lose 30 kg in a month" — to sweeten the pill for those who still believe a quality result is possible without changing lifestyle and views on food. In both cases the claim concerns only a crude count of calories at the entry to the mouth, and in that very narrow sense, the author concedes, "они, прости Господи, правы" (*they are, God forgive me, right*). But for health and energy level, calories are not the same [msg #28].

1. **Calories eaten are not equal to calories credited.** The calories that enter the mouth are not always the calories the body "credits". Almonds "officially" contain 160 calories, but because of their fibre only about 130 are absorbed. Protein is an even stronger case: the body spends more energy processing amino acids, so a fatty steak of 1,600 calories may look very high-calorie, but only about 750 are actually "stored" [msg #28].
2. **Calories are absorbed at different speeds.** Calories from fast-carbohydrate food (sweets, chips, soda and similar) are absorbed almost instantly, raising blood glucose and, after it, insulin. Dr. Lustig considers insulin one of the main factors of worsening metabolic health, rising obesity and shortened lifespan; the author adds that glucose spikes have been proven to affect energy levels through the day. Whole food — fibre, proteins, fats and complex carbohydrates — behaves differently and, as a rule, does not start this "инсулиновое колесо Сансары" (*insulin wheel of Samsara*) [msg #28].
3. **Calories can be beneficial or destructive.** Fibre forms a gel-like substance in the gut; this is the part of calories that is not absorbed and instead feeds beneficial gut microbiota. This lets the microbiome produce short-chain fatty acids such as acetate and butyrate, which have anti-inflammatory and protective effects against chronic metabolic diseases and conditions such as Alzheimer's disease. Proteins, fats and complex carbohydrates are necessary for the body to function; simple sugars are "пустые калории" (*empty calories*) that bring no benefit and only create energy imbalance and metabolic disorders [msg #28].
4. **We need glucose, not fructose.** Glucose is so important for producing energy and hormones that in extreme cases the body produces it even without carbohydrate intake. Fructose, often found in sodas and shop-bought sweets, is described as addictive and as having no function in the human body. Moreover, the post says, our bodies cannot digest fructose, so it destroys them — causing "дырявый кишечник" (*leaky gut*), fatty liver and interfering with normal mitochondrial function; mitochondria, as the channel explained earlier, are decisive for metabolic health and energy production [msg #28].

Fruit caveat (P.S. to the post): fruit also contains fructose, but fruit — and especially berries — can and should still be eaten, because they contain a lot of fibre, which hinders fructose absorption and feeds the microbiome [msg #28].

### 750 calories of soda vs 750 calories of broccoli (Mark Hyman)
Katya Strelnikova recalled where she had seen the same argument — Mark Hyman's book «Сахарная ловушка» (*"The Sugar Trap"*; full Russian subtitle: reclaim your health from sweets manufacturers and overcome unhealthy craving for junk food in just 10 days) — recommended reading it and shared an excerpt (book pp. 43–46). It is part of Hyman's "10-day detox diet program" introduction, which argues that you must first get rid of the thoughts and beliefs that doom weight-loss attempts: "Если вы будете продолжать мыслить и действовать по-прежнему, то и результат получите прежний" (*If you keep thinking and acting the same way, you will get the same result*). Hyman claims the vast majority of modern dietitians and doctors approach weight loss fundamentally wrongly — if their advice worked and were doable, we would all be slim and healthy — and the media add confusion. He then dismantles myths, of which the excerpt covers two [file #29].

**Myth one: all calories are equal.**
- *The sixth-grader test.* Show children 1,000 calories of broccoli and 1,000 calories of sweet soda and ask whether they affect the body the same way; they answer "No!" unanimously, because everyone intuitively understands the nutritional difference. Hyman quotes Mark Twain: "Проблема со здравым смыслом в том, что он есть далеко не у всех" (*The problem with common sense is that not everyone has it*). He supposes this is why doctors, nutrition specialists, government, food producers and media keep promoting the outdated, scientifically refuted idea, and calls the claim that you must burn more calories than you eat to lose weight fundamentally wrong [file #29].
- *Conservation of energy does not apply to a living body.* In a lab ("closed system"), burning broccoli and cola of equal energy value releases the same energy — Hyman does not dispute that. But a living, breathing, digesting organism is not an isolated system: food interacts with the body physiologically and each bite is transformed in its own way [file #29].

**Step 1 — trace 750 calories of soda.** A 1.5-litre bottle of regular Coca-Cola has 750 calories, all from sugar: 186 g, or 46 teaspoons — and many people really drink that much.
- The gut absorbs its glucose and fructose almost instantly because there is no fibre to slow it.
- Glucose spikes blood sugar → insulin jumps → a chain of hormonal reactions. High insulin increases belly fat stores, increases inflammation, raises triglycerides and lowers HDL, raises blood pressure, lowers testosterone in men and contributes to infertility in women.
- Insulin acts on the brain and increases appetite: it blocks leptin, the main appetite-controlling hormone; leptin resistance rises, so the "you are full" signal does not reach the brain, which keeps thinking you need food. The pleasure centre is activated and an internal reinforcement loop starts, making you want ever larger amounts of sugar — "Так формируется и закрепляется ваша зависимость" (*This is how your addiction forms and becomes fixed*).
- Fructose is worse: it goes straight to the liver and is turned into fat, which increases cellular insulin resistance and leads to chronically high insulin, so all the food you eat turns into dangerous belly fat. The liver becomes fat-infiltrated, inflammation rises; chronic systemic inflammation leads to more weight gain and "diabesity" (Russian диажирение, hyphenated across a line break in the excerpt: a combined term for diabetes plus obesity), and anything that promotes inflammation worsens insulin resistance.
- Fructose also sends no report to the brain about the calories just received and does not lower ghrelin (the hunger hormone), which normally falls after eating natural food.
- Soda lacks vitamins, fibre, minerals and phytochemicals that help process calories — "empty" calories that cause full-scale problems. The body does not perceive soda as food, so you eat far more calories during the day than needed.
- Sugar takes over the taste buds, so any food that is not distinctly sweet seems not tasty enough. Hyman's self-test: give up sugar entirely for a week, then taste a glass of blueberries — sweet; then taste the same blueberries after a big glass of sweet cola — they seem bland and uninteresting [file #29].

**Step 2 — trace 750 calories of broccoli.** These calories are also mostly (not all) carbohydrates, which is why Hyman explains carbohydrate types: carbohydrates are plant compounds of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, formally split into sugars and starch (which the body turns into sugar). The key difference is the effect on blood sugar: high-fibre, low-sugar carbs like broccoli's are absorbed gradually and cause no glucose or insulin spikes, whereas table sugar and bread are fast-absorbed and make glucose shoot up. Broccoli's slow carbs bring more good than harm.
- 750 calories = 21 cups of broccoli, containing 67 g of fibre. Broccoli is 23% protein, 9% fat and 68% carbohydrate, so 510 calories come from carbs; sugar in all 21 cups amounts to a mere one and a half teaspoons, and the remaining carbs have the low glycemic index typical of all non-starchy vegetables because they are digested very slowly [file #29].

**Step 3 — compare the outcomes.**
- You physically could not eat 21 cups at once — it would not fit in the stomach.
- If you did, fibre means the body absorbs only a small part of those calories, and very slowly: no glucose or insulin spikes, no fatty liver, no hormonal mess.
- The stomach stretches (unlike with soda — carbonation bloating does not count), signalling the brain that you are full.
- The pleasure centre is not engaged: no internal reinforcement, no addiction.
- The broccoli optimises metabolism, lowers cholesterol and inflammation and speeds up detoxification: its glucosinolates stimulate the liver's toxin removal; the flavonoid kaempferol is strongly anti-inflammatory; vitamin C and folates protect against cancer and cardiovascular disease; glucosinolates and sulforaphane change gene behaviour to help restore and maintain sex-hormone balance, lowering the risk of breast and other cancers [file #29].

**Conclusion.** The same number of calories in different foods can have radically different physiological effects: "Одни калории вызывают зависимость, другие – лечат" (*Some calories cause addiction, others heal*); some promote obesity, others speed up metabolism. Food is not only calories but information — with each bite you pass the body a set of encoded instructions that lead either to healing or to disease. Hyman's closing question: a large glass of Coca-Cola or a plate of boiled broccoli? [file #29].

### Stoker-and-coal metaphor of mitochondria overload (Jessie Inchauspé)
Purpose: to explain how sugar "takes away" energy. Marina Sidorich builds on the channel's earlier post on where energy comes from: energy is created by mitochondria, and one of the main ingredients is glucose [msg #30].

1. **Everything becomes glucose.** All carbohydrates are broken down to glucose in the small intestine. The most glucose is in ordinary white sugar, but there is also a lot in bread and flour products, white rice, pasta, potatoes, honey, dried fruit, fruit juice, sausages, shop-bought sauce, chips and other processed foods. The modern diet is built on large amounts of glucose, so even without eating many sweets ("но мы-то едим" — *but we do*), we still consume quite a lot of sugar [msg #30].
2. **The stoker.** In "Glucose Revolution" Jessie Inchauspé compares a mitochondrion to a locomotive stoker who is brought coal non-stop: «Кочегар не может сжечь уголь быстрее, поэтому вокруг него накапливаются кучи.» (*The stoker cannot burn coal any faster, so piles accumulate around him.*) Coal piles up to the ceiling, he can barely move or feed the fire, "Поезд останавливается, люди злятся, кочегар увольняется" (*The train stops, people get angry, the stoker quits*) [msg #30].
3. **What it means in the cell.** While raw material is moderate and sufficient, work runs smoothly. With excess glucose, free radicals accumulate in cells, leading to oxidative stress and mitochondrial death [msg #30].
4. **Conclusion.** "Глюкоза — жизненно-необходимая молекула, но ее избыток легко приводит к упадку сил" (*Glucose is a vital molecule, but its excess easily leads to a loss of strength*) [msg #30].

### Three rules for sweets that keep energy stable (channel)
Marina Sidorich's "what to do" list [msg #30]:
1. Do not deny yourself the pleasure of a favourite dessert — but not instead of a meal and not on an empty stomach.
2. Make sure the daily diet has enough vegetables, whole grains and legumes, quality protein sources and healthy fats, and give sugar only 10%.
3. Move, to burn the energy received from food.

### 85/15 balance by addition, not restriction (Katya Galasheva)
In an interview by Marina Sidorich, Katya Galasheva — nutritionist with a medical degree and coach — answers whether sweets can be part of a healthy lifestyle: "Именно этому я и учу!" (*That is exactly what I teach!*). The interview opens from the channel's premise that, from personal experience, "чем строже себе что-то запрещаешь, тем выше риск срывов" (*the stricter you forbid yourself something, the higher the risk of breakdowns*) [msg #170].
- **The proportion.** The balance between sweets and the rest of the diet is not 50/50 but roughly 85/15. The more whole foods (about 85%), the easier it is to reduce sweet cravings [msg #170].
- **How to get there: add, don't restrict.** Move toward this proportion gently and gradually. Add: protein foods, whole grains, fats, vegetables, fruits, greens and berries — and also comfortable physical activity, new and forgotten hobbies, and emotions outside food [msg #170].
- **"Safe doses".** It is considered that added sugar may make up to 5–10% of the diet. But Galasheva relies first on how the person feels inside rather than on a set portion or exact measurement, because any restrictions cause an even greater emotional need for sweets [msg #170].
- **Her main goal**, as a specialist with a "нетревожным подходом" (*non-anxious approach*): "научить осознанно подходить к выбору сладостей и понимать свои эмоции вокруг них" (*to teach a mindful approach to choosing sweets and understanding one's emotions around them*) [msg #170].

### Mindful check before eating a sweet (Katya Galasheva)
1. Ask whether you are actually hungry right now and need a proper main meal instead.
2. If you still want the chocolate bar — ask which emotion it is covering.
3. Ask why you are eating it at this moment.
4. General recommendation: eat the sweet after a main meal that contained protein and fibre; absorption is then slower and you will not be hungry again in 30 minutes [msg #170].

### Three hidden causes of overeating sweets (Katya Galasheva)
Causes that, in her words, few people think about [msg #170]:
1. **Lack of sleep.** Hormonal balance is disrupted and ghrelin (the hunger hormone) rises, which strengthens sweet cravings. Sweets give energy quickly but for a short time.
2. **Under-eating.** This can be too little food over the week or in the first half of the day — for example, skipping breakfast or a meagre lunch leads to overeating in the evening.
3. **A meagre and tasteless diet.** When food brings no pleasure, a person may compensate with sweets.

She adds there are many other causes and the problem should be looked at as a whole; many of her clients work in parallel with her (the nutrition specialist) and a psychologist, which she says makes the work many times more effective [msg #170].

### Self-kindness instead of self-blame when comfort-eating (Marina Sidorich)
Context: Marina Sidorich's personal story in the first months after her second daughter's birth — heavy physical wear from chronic sleep deprivation, no usual rhythm, no time for herself; a period when you want to find a comfort zone and stay in it. Her comfort turned out to be food: emotional snacks, fast carbs, everything tasty and sweet, plus increased appetite while breastfeeding. She caught herself thinking «Что ж я за коуч по питанию с очередной печенькой в зубах?» (*What kind of nutrition coach am I, with yet another cookie in my teeth?*) [msg #257].
1. **Recognise the mechanism.** It is hard to put eating in order and stop seeking comfort in food while other basic needs are unmet — "И это нормально" (*And that is normal*). With chronic sleep deprivation, constant stress and lack of movement, sweets and other comfort food become an easy way to restore joy and energy, if only for a moment [msg #257].
2. **Replace scolding with a question.** Instead of self-criticism ask: «Что я могу сделать для себя прямо сейчас, чтобы поддержать своё состояние?» (*What can I do for myself right now to support my state?*) [msg #257].
3. **Accept what you are not ready to change, care for yourself another way.** She is not ready to give up the cookie now, and that too is normal; instead she chose a beautiful yoga mat and downloaded an app for morning practice. For her this is not about perfection but about finding balance; eating will normalise once she starts recovering strength and filling up with energy [msg #257].
4. **Small doable steps.** In difficult periods small, manageable steps prove not only easier but much more effective than strict rules. "Главное — быть к себе чуть добрее" (*The main thing is to be a little kinder to yourself*) [msg #257].

## Detailed insights

### Should sweets be eaten at once or spread out? (Katya Galasheva)
She calls it a question of balance. For people used to strict restrictions, it can help to allow a small sweet after every meal; gradually they learn to choose when and how much they really need. Some clients prefer sweets in the evening, but with awareness comes the understanding that it is not always necessary. The key is finding an approach that works for each individual [msg #170].

### Myth two: bad heredity cannot be helped (Mark Hyman)
- **The myth.** It is easy to believe your physiology is a lottery — you inherited an obesity or diabetes gene, your parents and grandparents were overweight, so nothing can be done [file #29].
- **Genome evidence.** The human genome was decoded partly in hope of finding the secret of obesity and diabetes; nothing was found that helps people struggling with weight. There are 32 genes linked to obesity in the general population, but they account for only 9% of obesity cases; even having all 32 would add at most 10 kg of excess weight. Genes change only about 2% in 20,000 years, while obesity rose from 9% to 30% since 1960 and is projected to reach 50% by 2050 if trends continue — so something other than heredity is to blame [file #29].
- **The real causes (many factors).** Over the last 10,000 years the food supply changed radically and sugar consumption rose from 20 teaspoons a year to 21 a day. Toxins (which, Hyman says, are now reliably known to promote obesity) surround us. A diet high in fat and sugar and low in fibre made gut flora toxic, sharply increasing "микроожирения" (*micro-obesity*) — weight gain linked to inflammation-causing gut bacteria. Lack of sleep and obesity-promoting viruses also contribute. Social pressure matters: we imitate the people we socialise and work with; studies he cites show that having overweight friends predicts weight gain and obesity more reliably than having overweight parents — social ties may matter more than genetic ones. "У эпидемии ожирения сотни причин" (*The obesity epidemic has hundreds of causes*), and bad heredity comes last on the list [file #29].
- **What genes do explain.** We are programmed to love sweet and fatty food and to store belly fat in response to sugar so as to survive winters with food shortages. Genes play some role but contribute only a little to the global pandemic of obesity and diabetes [file #29].
- **Examples.** China: on a trip 30 years earlier Hyman met only one overweight woman, and she was riding a bicycle; type 2 diabetes was practically unknown. Now China has more diabetics than any other country and one in five Chinese people has type 2 diabetes — an example of the destructive effect of the Western diet. The Pima Indians a hundred years ago had no obesity, diabetes or other modern chronic diseases; now they are the second-highest obesity group (the excerpt breaks off mid-sentence here) [file #29].

## Evidence and numbers

- A study Hyman cites, covering diets in 154 countries and the links between calories, sugar and diabetes: adding 150 calories a day barely raised diabetes risk in the population, but if those 150 calories came from sweet soda, diabetes risk rose by 700% [file #29].

- Strong claims about fructose — that it has no function in the body, that bodies "cannot digest" it and that it causes leaky gut — are presented by Katya Strelnikova in her summary of Lustig; the source gives no supporting data beyond the podcast [msg #28].
- Many of Hyman's claims (refuted calorie model, 700% diabetes risk, broccoli's gene effects, toxins and viruses causing obesity) are the book author's statements; the excerpt gives no study references beyond naming the 154-country study [file #29].
- The almond (160→130) and steak (1,600→750) figures are given without a named source beyond the Lustig episode [msg #28].
- The Hyman excerpt ends mid-sentence about the Pima Indians; the rest of myth two is not available [file #29].
- Galasheva's approach explicitly avoids fixed portions because restriction raises the emotional need for sweets — a contrast with rule-based guidance such as "give sugar only 10%" [msg #170, #30].

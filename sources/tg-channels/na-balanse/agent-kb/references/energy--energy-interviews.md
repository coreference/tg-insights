# Three questions about energy (mini-interviews)

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's recurring rubric «Три вопроса про энергию к …» ("Three questions about energy to …"), in which a guest answers the same three questions: how stable her energy is on a 1–10 scale and what it depends on, what drains her energy most, and what her three main ways of restoring it are. Six interviews are covered (Masha Yolkina, Masha Manaeva, Masha Tsegelnik, Olya Malyuk, Vika Rarog, Lena Zhuravleva). Everything below is the guests' personal experience, not general recommendations from the channel. [msg #233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]

According to the launch post, the rubric exists to give more attention to the channel's second main theme — energy — and to introduce readers to interesting women; the stated aim is to talk about «что помогает поддерживать ресурс, что его забирает, собираем лучшие инструменты для восстановления» (what helps maintain one's resource, what takes it away, and to collect the best tools for recovery). [msg #233]

## Core ideas

- **Sleep is the most-named foundation.** Five of six guests explicitly put sleep (quantity, quality, or a sleep schedule) at the base of their energy or name poor sleep as the top drain; Tsegelnik puts it bluntly that no amount of "pumping" energy can trick a lack of sleep. [msg #233, #279, #312, #614, #657]
- **Energy is cyclical, and dips are normal.** Several guests tie energy to the menstrual cycle and to seasons; Tsegelnik frames falls in energy as a necessary phase before renewal rather than a failure. [msg #233, #279, #312, #531]
- **Uncertainty, chaos, unmade or doubted decisions and hanging tasks drain energy; planning and clarity restore it.** Yolkina, Tsegelnik and Rarog each describe this, with planning as the antidote. [msg #233, #312, #614]
- **Doing what you do not want to do drains; doing what drives you charges.** Malyuk (long imbalance of "have to" over "want to") and Zhuravleva (energy falls when doing what she no longer wants) make this point; Zhuravleva adds that driving activities can energise even through 10 hours of contact with people. [msg #531, #657]
- **Movement, nature and walking are the most-cited restorers**, followed by solitude/quiet, and — for some guests — trusted conversations with close people. [msg #233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]
- **Overstimulation (noise, crowds, bright screens, phones) drains sensitive and introverted people**, who protect themselves with earplugs, greyscale mode, solitude and phone-free time. [msg #233, #312, #531, #657]
- **Self-observation is the meta-skill**: noticing body signals, analysing what one actually does, and regular reflection are how the guests learned what works for them. [msg #279, #531, #614, #657]

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### The three-questions-about-energy self-audit (rubric format)
Purpose: a fixed interview template the channel uses with every guest; read as a method, it is a three-question audit anyone can run on their own energy. [msg #233]
1. **«Оцени стабильность своей энергии по шкале от 1 до 10? От чего это зависит?»** (Rate the stability of your energy on a scale of 1 to 10. What does it depend on?) — gives a number and forces naming the factors behind it (in the guests' answers: sleep, cycle, season, stress, health, planning, type of work, age). [msg #233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]
2. **«Что отбирает больше всего энергии?»** (What takes the most energy?) — identifies the main drains (noise and crowds, uncertainty, lack of choice, forced effort, imbalance of obligations, chaos, poor sleep, poor lifestyle basics). [msg #233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]
3. **«Три главных способа восстановления энергии?»** (Three main ways of restoring energy?) — collects the person's top restorers. Yolkina notes it is hard to pick only three. [msg #233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]

Numbers given by guests in answer to question 1: Tsegelnik ≈8 on average; Malyuk 6–7; Rarog 7–8; Zhuravleva 7–8. Yolkina describes her energy as «довольно стабильный» (fairly stable) when conditions are met but gives no number; Manaeva calls hers «очень нестабильна» (very unstable) without a number. [msg #233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]

### Masha Yolkina's routine (highly sensitive person)
Masha Yolkina — mindfulness teacher and embodiment facilitator — describes a set of conditions and protections. [msg #233]
1. **Protect sleep.** Her energy level depends first of all on the amount and quality of sleep, so she tries not to sacrifice it for other things. [msg #233]
2. **Track the menstrual cycle in the calendar.** Energy also depends on the day of the cycle; she enters the first and last weeks of the cycle into her calendar in advance. [msg #233]
3. **Keep stress at a manageable level — including positive stress.** Her energy is fairly stable if she sleeps enough, follows her cycle and has «посильное количество стресса (как негативного, так и позитивного)» (a manageable amount of stress, both negative and positive). [msg #233]
4. **Shield against noise and crowds.** As «человек с высокой чувствительностью» (a person with high sensitivity), noisy crowded places and loud sounds take the most energy. Her countermeasures: she almost never goes to events with many guests, wears earplugs on public transport, and plans rest breaks after any events and meetings. [msg #233]
5. **Remove uncertainty and hanging items.** Uncertainty, «подвисшие дела и разговоры» (pending tasks and conversations) also drain her, so she plans her day and tasks in Google Calendar and tries not to postpone important discussions. [msg #233]
6. **Restorers (more than three):** sleep; lying with closed eyes (especially after a long time at the computer); yoga nidra; a hot shower; massage, including self-massage; walks in nature; and at least one day a week with no tasks at all — better two. [msg #233]

### Masha Tsegelnik's three restorers
Masha Tsegelnik (CTO-2 at OzonTech, coach, mindfulness organizer) gives a numbered list. [msg #312]
1. **The base: sleep, food, movement — plus cheat codes.** She calls sleep, nutrition and movement «база» (the base): «Сколько не качай энергию, недостаток сна не обмануть» (however much you pump up your energy, you can't trick a lack of sleep). But there are «чит-коды» (cheat codes), for example the NSDR (non-sleep deep rest) protocol. [msg #312]
2. **Silence and greyscale mode on the iPhone.** It is important for her to be in contact with herself, which is hard amid external noise, so she always carries earplugs. A recent find: greyscale mode on the phone, to remove unnecessary bright stimuli. [msg #312]
3. **Breathing practices** — and any other practices that give connection with oneself. Her favourites have a complex rhythm and breath holds; they act «как мягкая перезагрузка системы» (like a soft reboot of the system). (!) INCOMPLETE: she does not specify which breathing techniques, rhythms or hold durations. [msg #312]

### Olya Malyuk's movement rotation and want/have-to balance
Olya Malyuk (36, solo entrepreneur, mother of two) defines energy and then describes her system. [msg #531]
- **Definition.** She calls energy «физический и эмоциональный настрой двигаться вперед, продуктивно работать и проявлять интерес к жизни во всех ее сферах» (the physical and emotional disposition to move forward, work productively and take interest in life in all its areas). Her rating is 6–7, so she considers herself «довольно нестабильна» (fairly unstable). [msg #531]
- **Mechanism: physical fatigue drags emotional fatigue.** She swings between two states, «то я на дне, то на коне» (now I'm at the bottom, now I'm on top). It depends primarily on physiology: not sleeping enough, a lot of work, the start of the cycle, no breather between main work and switching to parenting (children aged 5 and 8). Physical fatigue pulls emotional fatigue behind it — drive disappears and life looks gloomy; conversely, the better she feels physically, the more joy, optimism and desire to create and learn she feels. Hence her conclusion: it is important to be able to hear the body's signals, know what restores it and do it. [msg #531]
- **Main drain: a long skew toward "have to".** As a solo entrepreneur with many processes tied to her, some tasks are creative and nourish her (creating new products, finding and interviewing people for the team), while others are operational — uninteresting, even boring, but necessary for the business to live. Doing only necessary, uninspiring tasks for a long time exhausts her, and she gets the feeling «жизнь живет меня, а не я ее» (life is living me, not I it). She tries to keep a healthy balance of «хочу и надо» (want and have to) at work and in other areas of life. [msg #531]
- **Restorer 1 — daily movement, fixed in the morning.** A different workout every day, each with a stated purpose: running and skiing — good for the heart and unloads the head; strength training with weights at home or in the gym — to strengthen muscles; yoga — for flexibility and a calm mind; badminton — develops coordination, plus the sheer enjoyment of the game. Because movement «гарантированно восстанавливает» (restores her guaranteed), the workout is scheduled daily in the morning and almost never moved. [msg #531]
- **Restorer 2 — solitude.** She is an introvert and tires from external stimuli: talking to people, noisy places, anything demanding outward attention. The best recovery is to be alone, preferably at home, for at least a few hours without rush or plans. She notes that parents of small children will understand this too. [msg #531]
- **Restorer 3 — a walk with a friend.** Although everyday contact with people tends to drain her, a trusting conversation on a walk with a close friend fills her up and «возвращает к жизни» (brings her back to life). Several friends live 15 minutes' walk away, and they meet often to support each other or discuss current matters. [msg #531]

### Lena Zhuravleva's low-energy planning, detox day and retreat
Lena Zhuravleva (47, psychoanalytic therapist and executive coach, founder of HFLabs/DaData, mother of three). [msg #657]
- **Energy grew with age after a change of activity.** At 47 she expected to have less energy but rates it 7–8. It became much greater when she changed occupation (now a practising psychoanalytic therapist and coach) and as her children grew up. When she got tired of the CEO role, her energy noticeably dropped. Her general observation: energy falls when she does what she no longer wants to do. [msg #657]
- **Reflection as the main tool.** Therefore «время на рефлексию – главное» (time for reflection is the main thing). She regularly writes for about 15 minutes whatever comes to mind and goes to therapy; this helps her stop, hear and understand herself. [msg #657]
- **Driving activities charge, even if long.** Energy appears when doing what drives you: she runs a film club with difficult films and sometimes talks with people for 10 hours — and does not get tired but gets charged. [msg #657]
- **Main drain: poor sleep.** At 20 she could skip sleep and work the next day; now she can't — she needs to sleep enough. [msg #657]
- **Living in low-energy mode.** She has learned to live in a low-energy mode: when she knows there is little energy today, she plans the day differently and may cancel or postpone something. [msg #657]
- **Restorer 1 — walking.** Anywhere, but especially in a forest; afterwards she reliably feels better. On average she walks 12,000 steps. [msg #657]
- **Restorer 2 — a day for herself.** She does only what she wants: if she wants to lie down all day, she lies down; if she wants to go to friends, she goes. [msg #657]
- **Restorer 3 — digital detox day.** She does not go near gadgets all day. If an idea to do something or call someone comes up, she writes it down on paper. She lies, walks, reads paper books. [msg #657]
- **Extra: a sanatorium as a retreat.** When she feels she is getting tired, she goes to a sanatorium for 3–4 days; for her it works as a retreat. The key condition is no phone. On the first day she feels periodic impulses to grab the phone, but then the brain calms down. [msg #657]

## Detailed insights

### Masha Manaeva: seasonal energy and the "banal" drains
- **Energy is very unstable and tied to season and cycle.** Manaeva says her energy is very unstable; this year she made self-exploration her focus, wanting to understand better what gives and what takes away her strength. Energy definitely depends on the time of year and the cycle. She feels good in snowy winter, spring and early summer; by the end of summer energy drops and she wants to go into autumn hibernation. In her view, January–July are the most active months, and from August it is time «собирать урожай» (to "harvest") the efforts made in the first half of the year. [msg #279]
- **The drains are known — people look for other causes.** She calls this banal: «Мы все знаем, что забирает у нас больше всего сил, но зачастую ищем какие-то иные причины» (we all know what takes the most strength from us, but often look for some other reasons). Her list: poor nutrition; the phone; a wrong sleep schedule; lack of sport or everyday physical activity; and, in her case, lack of mental (brain) activity. [msg #279]
- **Under-challenge drains too.** If things are too easy for her and she isn't solving hard problems, over time she becomes sluggish and inactive. [msg #279]
- **Her three restorers:** (1) a walk in nature — alone or with her fiancé; «Природа + движение — самый быстрый и приятный способ восстановиться» (nature + movement is the fastest and most pleasant way to recover); (2) reading; (3) a hot bath, which she takes every other day. [msg #279]

### Masha Tsegelnik: cyclical energy and the cost of indecision
- **Momentary vs retrospective view.** In the moment it often seems to her that her energy jumps a lot, but looking back she sees she has much more energy now than a year ago or earlier — about 8 on average, and it has become noticeably «плотнее» (denser). [msg #312]
- **"We are not machines."** She likes the idea that falls in energy are normal: it is cyclicity, like the change of seasons — there must be a moment of decline and stillness for a new birth to happen. [msg #312]
- **Drain 1 — lack of choice and doubt about a decision made.** When a choice is made and she accepts it, it immediately becomes easier to focus. [msg #312]
- **Drain 2 — squeezing out more when rest is due.** What empties her a lot is the urge to squeeze «ещё немножечко энергии» (a little bit more energy) out of herself when, properly, she should simply rest. [msg #312]

### Vika Rarog: noticing what you actually do, and planning as a support
- **Reassessing one's own energy.** She once considered herself a low-energy person and admired people who seemed to manage everything — work, friends, family, hobbies, sport. Then she analysed how much she actually does and realised she is one of those people. Her lesson: «Главное — замечать то, что с нами происходит, и не обесценивать даже маленькие результаты» (the main thing is to notice what is happening to us and not devalue even small results). [msg #614]
- **What energy depends on.** She rates it 7–8 on average for the current period. First of all it depends on health. Planning also helps: when she knows exactly what to do and why, energy seems to be allocated to those tasks and she becomes «машиной, которую не остановить» (an unstoppable machine). [msg #614]
- **Main drain — chaos in tasks and in the head.** She likes everything maximally transparent and clear; this calms her. Periods with a lot of uncertainty oppress her, and she feels the oppression physically. The fix is again having a plan: «пусть он будет неидеальным и поменяется сто тысяч раз, но пока он есть, он послужит мне опорой» (let it be imperfect and change a hundred thousand times, but while it exists, it will serve as my support). [msg #614]
- **Restorer 1 — sleep as the axis of life.** Her life is built around her sleep schedule, since student days: no cramming exam tickets overnight — everything is learned in advance, and the night before an exam is for sleeping. Now she guards sleep from all disturbing factors to regularly get her norm of 7–9 hours. [msg #614]
- **Restorer 2 — movement.** Tennis, home workouts on a mat, and walking her dog 3 times a day. This helps unload worries from the head and recharge with «гормонами удовольствия» (pleasure hormones). [msg #614]
- **Restorer 3 — communication with people.** Being with close ones and having a place to discuss important topics gives her a charge of inspiration and a desire to start something new. [msg #614]

### Patterns across the six interviews
- **Social contact cuts both ways.** For Yolkina (crowds) and Malyuk (everyday communication) people drain; yet Malyuk's walk with a close friend, Rarog's time with close ones and Zhuravleva's 10-hour film-club conversations charge them. The distinction the guests draw is between noisy/obligatory contact and trusted or driving contact. [msg #233, #531, #614, #657]
- **Rest days without tasks** appear twice: Yolkina's one to two task-free days a week and Zhuravleva's "day for herself". [msg #233, #657]
- **Hot water** appears twice: Yolkina's hot shower and Manaeva's hot bath every other day. [msg #233, #279]
- **Earplugs** appear twice: Yolkina on transport, Tsegelnik always carrying them. [msg #233, #312]
- **The phone as a drain** appears in Manaeva's list of drains, Tsegelnik's greyscale mode and Zhuravleva's detox day and phone-free sanatorium. [msg #279, #312, #657]

# Learning to rest

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's teaching on *how* to rest so that energy actually comes back: a scheduled rest formula across five time horizons, matching the type of rest to the type of energy that was spent, the transition buffer after work, aftercare after intense events, a guilt-free "low-power" mode for exhausted days, unplugged vacations, the DRAMMA model of quality vacation, and Katya Strelnikova's own evolution toward balanced trips and retreats. According to the source, many people (Katya included) were never taught to rest systematically, so evenings and weekends "smear" into neither work nor rest, and lying down often fails to restore anything [msg #131, #135, #277]. Katya frames rest as an inseparable part of an integrated approach to energy, wellness and flourishing [msg #131].

This part adds one post to the topic: Katya Strelnikova's reflection on actress Tilda Swinton's public career pause, used as a real-life case of a **sabbatical** — a long, conscious break from one's main work. It extends the existing topic (which teaches rest at the scale of hours, days, weeks, quarters and the 2–3-week yearly vacation, see *Basic formula of rest and self-care* in part 1) to a longer horizon: stepping away from a career for an open-ended period. According to the source, the case matters because it disproves the fear that pausing at the peak destroys a career, and it lets Katya state her own definition of success [msg #666].

## Core ideas

- **Rest has to be planned like everything else.** Rest and self-care are inseparable parts of energy and wellbeing and should be in the calendar, scheduled in advance, at least until they become iron habits (see *Basic formula of rest and self-care*) [msg #131].
- **Rest must match what was spent.** We have four kinds of energy (physical, emotional, cognitive, spiritual) and should replenish each according to how it was used; lying on the couch with a screen after a day at the computer keeps draining cognitive energy (see *Four types of energy*) [msg #135].
- **Rest is a return to balance, not the opposite of work.** Dasha argues the balance to seek is "life–work–rest", and rest is a whole system of recovery (sleep, food, movement, time alone, pauses, change of activity), not just evenings and vacations [msg #277].
- **You cannot rest while needs are left outside the door.** After hours of externally focused work you need a buffer to notice your state and needs, otherwise you slide into cheap dopamine (see *Post-work buffer*) [msg #132].
- **No quality result without quality rest.** Like athletes' training cycles, a phase of taking from the body for fast results must be followed by a recovery phase [msg #140].
- **New impressions are not rest.** In new places the psyche does not unload; you switch but do not switch off [msg #140].
- **Exhaustion is often about life, not rest.** If life and work do not meet your needs, no number of Maldives trips will help; sometimes the fix is minimizing what tires you most at work [msg #277].
- **Good rest is balance, not extremes.** Katya's year of experimenting showed her that rest is neither chasing impressions nor lying flat, but listening to herself and combining effort, beauty, slowness, conversation and solitude [msg #392].

- **Rest is not a reward but part of the process.** Citing a chapter of the book *Rest: Why You Get More Done When You Work Less*, Katya states that rest in the sabbatical sense "не награда, а часть процесса" (*is not a reward, but part of the process*): it does not destroy a career but creates space for something new [msg #666].
- **A pause at the peak did not ruin a top career.** Tilda Swinton announced a break after a major award; when Katya checked the outcome more than a year later, nothing bad had happened — the career simply continued on Swinton's own terms [msg #666].
- **Success as permission to slow down.** Katya defines success not as running after the next achievement but as being able to afford not to hurry, not to prove anything and not to work for the public — "просто пожить" (*just to live*) [msg #666].
- **Pauses help texts as well as people.** Katya says a text, like a person, sometimes needs a pause, and calls this her signature approach to blogging [msg #666].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Basic formula of rest and self-care
Purpose: Katya shared this with a client whose request was "to learn to rest" and who had never approached rest systematically; it was a revelation to the client that "вообще-то существует базовая формула отдыха и заботы о себе" (*actually there is a basic formula of rest and self-care*). Katya notes she herself once had to learn to rest. The formula has five time scales (as on the card: 1 hour a day, 1 day a week, 3 days a month, 1 week a quarter, 2–3 weeks a year); Katya "fantasized" how each could look in practice and invited readers to share their ideas. She admits she is good at short-term rest but still struggles with the quarterly week and takes it as her growth point [msg #131, img #131].

1. **1 hour a day.**
   - *Movement*: 30 minutes of walking, running or a workout.
   - *Relaxation and reflection*: 15–20 minutes of meditation, breathing or journaling.
   - *Hobby*: 10–15 minutes doing something you love — reading a book, drawing or anything else. [msg #131]
2. **1 day a week** (options):
   - *A day for yourself*: schedule only what you like — hobby, time with family or friends, a trip into nature, the spa.
   - *A day without plans*: just wake up and live.
   - *Digital detox*: one day without social networks and gadgets, to rest from information noise. [msg #131]
3. **3 days a month** (options):
   - *Mini-vacation*: a short trip somewhere nearby.
   - *Self-development*: a training, retreat or master class — "но не по работе, пожалуйста" (*but not work-related, please*).
   - *Staycation*: stay home and plan great activities you have long postponed — not what you "have long needed to do" but what you "have long wanted". [msg #131]
4. **1 week a quarter** (options):
   - *Travel*: a more distant trip than the monthly one, e.g. to another city or even country.
   - *Self-development*: a retreat or active holiday where you can fully disconnect and recover.
   - *Staycation*: if travel is impossible, stay home with minimal obligations and maximal self-care. [msg #131]
5. **2–3 weeks a year**: a long vacation or retreat to truly recharge and rest from work and routine [msg #131].

Common mistake implied by the source: treating rest as something that happens by itself; Katya's rule is that until rest is an iron habit it must be scheduled in advance [msg #131].

### Four types of energy and how to replenish each
Purpose: explains why "lying down to rest" often fails, and what to do instead. Katya's observation: she regularly runs into (and observes in other people) the problem of lying down a whole evening or day off and have no more strength afterwards; she says you did not need to be a health coach to sense something is wrong with the "lie down after work" approach. Drawing on a burnout livestream, she lists four kinds of energy and the principle "восполнять эти виды энергии стоит в соответствии с их расходом" (*these types of energy should be replenished according to how they were spent*) [msg #135].

1. **Physical energy** — literally strength in the body (what people usually call energy). Spent by physical actions; just living also spends it — more than 60% goes on metabolism. *Replenish* (when you walked or trained a lot): sleep, lying down, maybe a bath, maybe even with magnesium salt [msg #135].
2. **Emotional energy** — obtained from positive emotions; if life is a chain of events with a minus sign, there is no energy to expect. *Replenish* (when you were doing what you really did not want to do): do something that gives you real pleasure ("кайфово") [msg #135].
3. **Cognitive energy** — the "space" in the head and cognitive resource. Filling the brain with reels, Zoom calls, email and books without a break drains it, and "начинаем тупить, как браузер, переполненный вкладками" (*we start to lag like a browser overloaded with tabs*). *Replenish* (when you loaded your brain): move, remove screens, meditate or breathe [msg #135].
4. **Spiritual energy** — obtained from meaningful actions and important matters; doing what you do not believe in or what is not important takes energy away. *Replenish* (when you did what was needed, but not by you): do something you consider important — it gives meaning to the day [msg #135].

Worked example: you sat all day at a computer (not carrying sacks), straining eyes and brain, so the main load fell on cognitive energy. If you then lie on the couch and pick up a screen again — "а мы возьмем: ну кто лежит на диване без соцсетей, ютюба, сериала или книги на худой конец" (*and we will: who lies on a couch without social media, YouTube, a series or at worst a book*) — you keep overloading your head and keep drawing energy instead of restoring it [msg #135].

Katya's personal practice under this model [msg #135]:
- Focus on sleep, movement every day, balanced food.
- Screen-free time in the morning (breathing, meditation, walk, workout, breakfast), at lunch (lunch, an NSDR practice, self-massage) and in the evening (walk, going to the beach, dinner); sometimes she just lies with eyes closed for two minutes.
- She always leaves time between computer tasks to stand up, stretch, zone out, drink water, hug her husband, stare at one point.
- If she does something "unpleasant", she always plans something that will cheer her up afterwards.

### Post-work buffer (why rest does not work after intense work)
Source: a forwarded post by Max Rodin summarising a discussion with Dasha [msg #132].

The problem: after a burst of work it is time to stop, but you cannot leave the flow — it is evening, nothing constructive happens, you are no longer working, yet time leaks away while you sit in your thoughts or on the phone [msg #132].

The mechanism: rest, recovery and any constructive (resource-giving) activity are directly tied to satisfying needs. We often work contrary to what the body needs and the nervous system wants, out of "надо" (*must*) for external circumstances — which, in a sense, is normal. But to meet needs you have to feel the body and know your needs in the moment. If for 3–6 hours you juggle tasks in the habitual external locus of control ("мне надо идти на звонок или считать табличку" — *I need to go to a call or work on the spreadsheet*), attention is there, not in the body and needs. Leave the needs "behind the door" and they keep straining you; you cannot rest [msg #132].

The method — a buffer after work tension and external attention:
1. **Notice your state** first ("а какой я сейчас? что я чувствую?" — *what am I like now? what do I feel?*).
2. **Notice the needs**, including deferred ones — not only physiological ("попить давно пора" — *it's long past time to drink*) but subtle emotional ones ("вообще-то мне грустно сегодня" — *actually I'm sad today*).
3. **Act in accordance with them**, choosing the activity smartly for the current need ("что мне наиболее полезно сейчас" — *what is most useful to me now*): sometimes recovery means moving in the gym, sometimes not moving; if there is fatigue — lie down; if anxiety — soothe yourself; if grief — cry.
Only this frees space for real switching, attention to the next arising needs, and recovery. Without it, after a hard work stretch ("после херачки") you will most likely get stuck in some cheap-dopamine activity and will not rest at all. The author calls pause → attention to state → need-matched activity the optimal flow for spending an evening after work constructively [msg #132].

### Aftercare list
Definition: aftercare (literally "care after") is the practice of caring for oneself after an intense or emotionally charged experience. Its goal is to lower stress and give oneself time and space to process and integrate the experience, especially when it was emotionally or physically demanding. Katya notes the concept originally came from the BDSM community, but she likes using it for any intense experience [msg #157].

When: she now always schedules aftercare after events she runs herself and after any intense event, e.g. after four hours of study. Duration and format depend on the length and intensity of the event; on average she needs 1.5–2 hours, sometimes more [msg #157].

Her list, compiled from analysing many years of experience [msg #157]:
1. Talking with people, if ideas or thoughts remain that she wants to discuss after the event.
2. Reflecting on the experience — she often notices a need to share it right away (in stories or on Telegram) or simply write it in notes.
3. A cool shower.
4. Lying in silence and "тупить в телефон" (*zoning out on the phone*).
5. A relaxing body practice (calm meditation or yoga nidra).
6. A daytime nap, if the event was in the morning or afternoon.
7. Food — after intense events she always very much wants to eat and drink.
8. Not planning any intense events afterwards.
9. A walk in a quiet place.

Each person's list can differ; any actions aimed at slowing down, processing the experience and recovering fit [msg #157].

### Velvet Pulls mode (Бархатные Тяги)
Context: Katya took her daughter Kira to the dentist on a Monday morning, which crushed her "like a concrete slab" (childhood traumas), and still had to teach at the Higher School of Economics in the evening and be an energetic, interesting presence for students. She managed, but was depleted. So she activated "режим Бархатные Тяги… Это когда ползёшь на минималках пока энергия не восстановится" (*Velvet Pulls mode… when you crawl along at minimum until energy recovers*) [msg #83].

What she is allowed in this mode [msg #83]:
1. Not making the bed.
2. Staying in pajamas.
3. Replacing a workout with stretching.
4. Not cooking.
5. Not doing any extra tasks.
6. Moving everything that can be moved in the calendar.
7. Getting stuck in social media.
8. Writing in a journal.
9. Not brushing her hair.
The list is open ("И так далее…"), and the key condition is doing all of it "без угрызений совести и самобичевания" (*without pangs of conscience and self-flagellation*). Her expectation: most likely she will be back in shape the next day and live the rest of the week fully [msg #83].

### Five reasons we cannot rest (Dasha's analysis)
Source: forwarded from the channel Даш, инсайт дашь (*Dasha, give an insight*). Dasha noticed frequent complaints that work swallows everything, though on inspection pure work time is 8–10 hours, while the rest of the time set aside for rest gets "smeared": "Ни поработал, ни пожил, ни отдохнул, только еще больше устал" (*Neither worked, nor lived, nor rested — only got more tired*). Having been in this vicious circle herself, she broke the concept of rest down [msg #277].

1. **We confuse what rest really is.** Rest is often seen as the opposite of work, as in work-life balance, as if you should minimise work volume and intensity. But rest is the process of returning the system to a balanced state and recovering to a work-capable and life-capable state. So the balance to seek is not "life–work" but "life–work–rest" [msg #277].
2. **We wrongly think rest is mainly evenings, weekends and vacations.** It is a whole recovery system; turning off the laptop or boarding a plane to warm places is not enough. The system is trivial, we just neglect it [msg #277]:
   - Sleep at consistent hours, 7–9 hours (Dasha needs 7, plus catching up on sleep several days in a row once a quarter).
   - Nourishing healthy food, but without strict restrictions, on a schedule (her focus: regularity and lowering psychological food restrictions).
   - Moderate physical activity of different formats (she admits she is "not right" here, training every day and often hard; now adding more yoga and stretching).
   - Time without a phone and alone with yourself ("это самое крутое, что стало у меня работать!" — *this is the coolest thing that started working for me*).
   - Pauses during the day to increase awareness (still mastering meditation; mini-reflections already in place).
   - Change of activity and creative pursuits (she added music and drawing).
3. **We don't know which rest actually restores us and from what.** We get tired not from work as such but from many factors; it is critical to learn which resource we depleted. Example: our reserves of attention, concentration and decision-making power are spent daily; if we overused them today, reduce their use in free time and sleep well. We also lack a list of rest options for different cases — if a bell rang "Everyone rest urgently!", would you sleep, watch a series, scroll Instagram? There is no single answer; it differs for everyone. She adds Pavlov's criteria of rest (see below) [msg #277].
4. **If we get tired, it is better not to rest more but to minimise what tires us most at work.** Track energy and fatigue levels during the day depending on tasks and events. Her example: at Yandex she was badly worn out by weekend pings (the delivery business never sleeps). She built a duty system: someone in the team was always on watch in all chats to put out fires, and if she was critically needed they messaged her privately or called. This let her disconnect without anxiety, compensating the team with extra days off later [msg #277].
5. **Being without strength is less often about incomplete rest; more often it is about an incomplete life.** If your life and work do not please you, do not meet your needs and you do not feel happy, "хоть каждый месяц летайте на Мальдивы, лучше вам не станет" (*even if you fly to the Maldives every month, you won't get better*). It is critical to understand what ideal life you see for yourself, what is in it and what fills you. Getting tired is normal; not being happy, engaged, active and whole is the worst [msg #277].

### Pavlov's criteria of rest
Dasha cites physiologist Pavlov's criteria of rest: presence of pleasure; absence of responsibility, competition and result-orientation ("наличие удовольствия, отсутствие ответственности, конкуренции и нацеленности на результат") [msg #277].

### Training cycles analogy (micro / meso / macro cycles)
Used by Marina Sidorich (forwarded post) as an argument for switching off fully on vacation. Professional athletes divide training into three cycle types [msg #140]:
1. **Microcycles** — usually coincide with weekly cycles.
2. **Mesocycles** — usually coincide with a monthly cycle.
3. **Macrocycles** — last from 3–6 months up to 1 year.
Within microcycles load and rest must alternate, as well as higher and lower loads. The positive effect of training is achieved only if one or several "working" microcycles are followed by an unloading microcycle — rest that restores the body's functional capacities and allows adaptation processes to proceed most effectively; otherwise there is a threat of physical and nervous exhaustion. The author's inference: intellectual workers are probably not very different from athletes, so they should plan recovery just as carefully [msg #140].

### DRAMMA rest model
Shared as a "reminder" card from the Daily Reminder channel; the post notes the model does not depend on finances or season [msg #330, img #330].
1. **Detachment, Relaxation (DR)**: turn off notifications; arrange not to be disturbed about work; spend more time in nature; more sleep and physical activity.
2. **Autonomy (A)**: choose yourself what to do on vacation; find time to be alone; sometimes decide to do something spontaneously — let that be part of the plan.
3. **Mastery (M)**: learn a new skill (example: a local cooking master class); do small challenges (examples: find the oldest building in the city, climb a hill, learn 40 new words).
4. **Meaning (M)**: learn the history of the place; write in a journal what this vacation means to you; discuss what you saw with family or friends.
5. **Affiliation (A)**: share impressions; play board games; talk to other people (example: sign up for a shared dinner). [img #330]

### Balanced trip formula
Katya's description of her ideal rest: not extremes, but listening to herself and finding balance — "Не убегать от жизни в сериалы или бесконечные поездки, а оставлять время почувствовать ее вкус" (*Not escaping life into series or endless trips, but leaving time to feel its taste*). Components she combines: see beauty; strain the muscles; warm up in a sauna after a hike; grab impressions but also luxuriate in a slow morning; spend several hours in attentive conversations with her husband, then read a book alone with a mountain view. Different situations can be substituted depending on the type of trip [msg #392, #418].

### Sabbatical as a conscious pause
Purpose: a model for long-horizon rest — what to do when the regular rest formula is not enough and one needs time away from the career itself. Katya draws it from the book *Rest: Why You Get More Done When You Work Less*, which she says devotes a whole chapter to sabbaticals — "осознанным паузам, которые брали и берут топовые художники, ученые, предприниматели" (*conscious pauses that top artists, scientists and entrepreneurs have taken and still take*). (She mentions she had cited this book earlier in a different context.) [msg #666]

Components of the idea as presented in the source:
1. **The pause is conscious.** It is a chosen break, announced and taken on purpose, not a collapse or forced sick leave. Swinton's case: she "берет паузу в актерской карьере" (*is taking a pause in her acting career*) on her own initiative [msg #666].
2. **The content of the pause is ordinary living.** Swinton's stated plan was to take time to live: to be at home in Scotland, to sleep in her own bed and to work on other projects. Her quote as given by Katya: "Мне нужен перерыв. Немного отдохну и подумаю, какими будут следующие сорок лет" (*I need a break. I'll rest a little and think about what the next forty years will be like*) — i.e. the pause also serves as time to reflect on the long-term direction [msg #666].
3. **It is taken despite the pressures to keep running.** Katya contrasts the decision with the usual reflexes — instead of chasing the next role, fearing competition and fearing to "упустить момент" (*miss the moment*), Swinton "разрешает себе ПОЖИТЬ" (*allows herself to LIVE*). The post's title, «Тильда Суинтон разрешила» (*Tilda Swinton allowed herself / gave permission*), frames the sabbatical as an act of self-permission [msg #666].
4. **The pause creates space for the new, and work resumes.** Per the book, rest of this kind "не разрушает карьеру, а создает пространство для нового" (*does not destroy a career, but creates space for something new*). Swinton's outcome confirmed it: she came back with a new film, went to Cannes, and in the autumn is due on the London stage in a role she first played in 1988 [msg #666].

Example and its lesson: Swinton was 64, with forty years in film and an Oscar behind her, and had just received the Golden Bear at the Berlinale when she announced the break. Katya's lesson from the outcome: "Карьера не рассыпалась, а просто продолжилась на ее условиях" (*The career did not fall apart, it simply continued on her terms*) [msg #666].

## Detailed insights

### Unplugged vacations: fear, arguments, and why they work
Marina Sidorich (forwarded in Katya's channel) knows how scary it is to go on a vacation where you won't work: it feels like the world will collapse, everything will break, or you'll come back to a pile of messages that can never be cleared. Everyone's fears differ but they are all about one thing — fear of letting go of control. She writes not from enlightenment but as one more dialogue with herself, trying to negotiate a vacation [msg #140].

Her argument for turning off chat notifications: energy and strength must be rebalanced — if there is a phase where she takes from her body for some fast result, there must also be a phase where she recovers; skip the second and the first will not be as good. "без качественного отдыха не может быть качественного результата" (*without quality rest there can be no quality result*). The athletes' cycle analogy supports this (see *Training cycles analogy*) [msg #140].

A humorous follow-up screenshot: Marina replies that unplugged vacations are great — after her last 5-day vacation without contact she quit and went into health coaching ("после последнего отпуска без связи 5 дней я уволилась и ушла в хэлс коучинг") [msg #141, img #141].

### New impressions are not rest
Marina's lesson: in new places the psyche does not unload, so rest does not happen. Even when she flies to Bali but stays in a new district or hotel, the first days go on exploring and absorbing new information — she can switch over but not switch off and exhale. Because her work involves much stress and novelty, she needs a familiar routine on vacation to slow down and recover. To be filled with something (impressions of a new country, city or art) you first need to recover, which is rather lazy rest in a familiar place; if you go for new emotions when depleted, fatigue after returning to work will likely increase. Better still: don't let yourself reach that state at all, and take days off so that vacations can be about filling up with new emotions and inspiration [msg #140].

Katya's own version of the same pattern: for a long time her rest was in extremes — either chasing impressions (new places, people, activities), after which she needed another vacation (she once went to Turkey on vacation after a trip around Baikal), or falling into total inaction, lying flat. Then she stopped resting altogether — "найди себе работу по душе и не будешь отдыхать ни дня" (*find a job you love and you won't rest a single day*, an ironic twist on the proverb). That is where her research into rest began at the end of the previous year. After a year with 8 long weekends (3–4 days each) and 2 vacations (1–2 weeks each), she concluded it is not about extremes but about listening to herself and finding balance (see *Balanced trip formula*). She continues learning to make rest part of life and to understand in what volume, form and frequency she needs it — and says she now feels rested ("Отдохнувшей я себя чувствую") [msg #392].

### Retreats and curated trips as balanced rest
Katya recommends two trips that she says fit her picture of ideal rest perfectly (the picture from her earlier post: beauty, muscles on a hike then sauna, impressions plus slow mornings, conversations with her husband then a book alone), and where she would go herself if she were in the European part of the world [msg #418]:
- **Authentic camp in Çıralı, Turkey** by Lesha Ivanov and Sveta Shedina (whose course she took and loved) — about authenticity and working with parts (IFS) in a picturesque, soulful place; she says she has never heard a bad word about Çıralı, where "the air itself" sets a slow, engaged vibe; the program promises transformative rest. Day structure: morning body practice → breakfast → training on authenticity and IFS (individual and group) → lunch, walks, sea → evening practice and training again (learning to access your life energy faster) → dinner by the fire and conversations under the stars. Around it: hiking, yachting, sea, mountains. [msg #418]
- **NES Experiences on Madeira, Portugal** by Dasha Shulga and partners — not tour operators but "кураторы отдыха" (*rest curators*): small groups, everything built so that "сама поездка была практикой баланса" (*the trip itself is a practice of balance*): movement and impressions by day, a chance to exhale in the evening. The 7-day program: hiking along levadas and forests, surfing on wild beaches, canyoning and swimming in waterfalls, bike tours in the mountains, volcanic pools, ocean sunsets, food and wine where locals eat. The key feature: adjustable intensity — want more active, take cycling, surfing, canyoning; want softer, keep hiking, beach, rest. Katya: this kind of rest charges energy rather than sucking it out — "Баланс, как я его вижу" (*Balance as I see it*). [msg #418]

### Wellness on a city trip: Dubai
Katya's notes from a trip to Dubai, which she approached with doubts about whether she could keep her step count and eat well; having lived in Bali and the US she has formed lifestyle standards, and Dubai unexpectedly met them [msg #551]. Transferable observations:
- Gyms and pools inside residential buildings are the norm there; having lived in such buildings in the US and Brazil, she says this really changes your relationship with sport — "В любой непонятной ситуации за две минуты можно оказаться на коврике, беговой дорожке или шезлонге" (*in any unclear situation you can be on a mat, treadmill or lounger in two minutes*).
- Fresh vegetables and fruit are available; Careem delivery works like Yandex (the fast version brings essentials in about 20 minutes).
- They cooked a lot at home — a bonus of "non-hotel" rest.
- Staying between World Trade Center and Financial Center metro stops is not the most restful area but is 10–20 minutes by car from everything she describes.
Her list of wellness activities: long walks and runs on Kite Beach or La Mer Beach (beaches have lockers, so you can come with a bag and change of clothes); a day at Dubai Creek (great on the waterfront at sunrise and sunset; where she would live if she moved); an evening in DIFC (multi-level outdoor spaces, restaurants, contemporary art); Alserkal (Dubai's "Artplay" with a factory vibe, restaurants, shops, galleries, an art-house cinema, part built by Rem Koolhaas, reminiscent of Moscow's Garage); a walk or bike ride along the canal with tea at Orto cafe at the start or end; City Walk (new views of Burj Khalifa, a spa or an empty lululemon, a picnic in the "Dubai Central Park" first, landmark The BARN Coffee); One Central (small area with restaurants and a cinema, reachable on foot from the Museum of the Future if you want a step-heavy walk); wakeboarding and kitesurfing (not Bali, but she tried it for the first time and loved it); cycling on the DX Bike track (free infrastructure with showers and lockers, bike rental, rides right into the city centre); yoga plus spa at Villa Sage and Samadhi (yoga studio + spa with sauna/steam/plunge + café; she preferred Sage; the spa is included with a class; check that it is a women's day); and eating well, e.g. Fish Market Jumeirah 1, where you pick fish and seafood and they cook it as you ask. Not yet tried: hiking, the desert, the desert cycling track, beach clubs, sunbathing (she is not a fan), and fashionable places like Aura (book far ahead) [msg #551].

## Evidence and numbers

- A study by Ernst & Young (cited by Marina) found that for every additional 10 hours of vacation employees took, their year-end performance improved by 8% [msg #140].
- Adam Galinsky (Columbia Business School) conducted numerous studies linking travel and creativity: "Зарубежный опыт увеличивает как когнитивную гибкость, так и глубину и интегративность мышления, способность устанавливать глубокие связи между разрозненными формами" (*Foreign experience increases both cognitive flexibility and depth and integrativeness of thinking, the ability to make deep connections between disparate forms*). In one study, creative directors of elite fashion houses who travelled abroad more often and truly engaged with and immersed in the local environment produced creative lines more consistently [msg #140].
- Daily Reminder cites the statistic that peak vacation enjoyment comes at the 43rd hour, i.e. by the end of the second day [msg #330].
- More than 60% of physical energy goes on metabolism (Katya) [msg #135].

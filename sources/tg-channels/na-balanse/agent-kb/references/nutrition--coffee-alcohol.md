# Coffee, caffeine and alcohol-free

## What this covers

This topic gathers everything the channel "На балансе" teaches about coffee and about drinking without alcohol: Katya Strelnikova's personal caffeine rule and her five facts about timing, dose, tolerance and sugar; her re-check of the belief that coffee with unsweetened milk spikes glucose, illustrated by six pairs of continuous-glucose curves from Glucose Goddess; a guest post by Marina Sidorich debunking four coffee-and-energy myths; and Katya's six-plus years without alcohol with a tasting of five non-alcoholic Sauvignon Blancs and a recommendation of Zero Point non-alcoholic beer [msg #57, #58, #234, #497, #499].

Why it matters according to the source: coffee is treated as an energy tool that can either raise or drain energy depending on when, how much and with what you drink it — "кофе может и поднимать и забирать энергию" (*coffee can both raise and take away energy*) [msg #57]. Alcohol-free drinks matter for people who want to keep the ritual of a festive glass without the alcohol [msg #497, #499].

This part covers one long personal-experience post (rubrics #pov_набалансе and #личныйопыт_набалансе), written when Katya Strelnikova had gone seven years without alcohol. It explains why she drank (to escape her life, not for the taste), how she actually stopped (a three-month experiment), how she spends parties on water, what she cut out of her life along with alcohol, the cosmetic effects, alcohol as social contract and marketing, her overall verdict on non-alcoholic drinks, and what she got in return [msg #745].

Why it matters according to the source: Katya presents life without alcohol "не как жертва, а как очень выгодная сделка" (*not as a sacrifice but as a very profitable deal*). Her main point is that letting go of one dependency "через понимание, а не через запрет" (*through understanding, not through a ban*) lets you start seeing your other dependencies too [msg #745].

**How this updates the existing topic file:**
- **Duration updated.** The earlier post (#497, December 2025) said 2,300 days, i.e. more than six years. This post says seven years and dates the start to September 2019. The two are consistent [msg #745].
- **Fills an earlier (!) INCOMPLETE.** In #497 Katya did not explain how she stopped drinking or got used to sober parties and only hinted at a future post. This post gives the method (the three-month experiment, below) and how she handles parties (water, and the party itself as the treat) [msg #745].
- **Non-alcoholic drinks, extended.** In #497 she had found decent non-alcoholic wine only in Russia and had not searched Europe. Now she reports alternatives even in Serbia and a whole non-alcoholic shop in Barcelona. Her overall verdict is that most are grape juice with ambitions, though some are worth drinking for the taste [msg #745].
- **Non-alcoholic beer, extended.** #499 told how Zero Point sent beer to Bali when she complained there was none. Here she explains its role: at first she clung to non-alcoholic beer, and the lack of it on Bali taught her that water is enough [msg #745].

## Core ideas

- **Coffee is double-edged for energy.** A sweet cappuccino sipped from morning to night is a way to treat yourself, not to wake up; a leisurely "кайфово с утра  посидеть с чашечкой кофе" (*it's lovely to sit in the morning with a cup of coffee*) habit, in Katya's view, robs you of energy in working hours. Full rules in *Five facts for getting energy from coffee* [msg #57].
- **Caffeine as a deliberate tool, not a default.** Katya drinks decaf by default and uses caffeine only when she really needs a boost [msg #57].
- **Sugar and high-carb milks turn coffee into a dessert.** Sugar and some milks (oat, skim) cause glucose spikes and therefore take energy away; quality black coffee is the choice for those who want energy from coffee [msg #57, #58].
- **Unsweetened whole milk or cream may not spike glucose at all** — Katya overturned her own belief after looking for evidence [msg #58].
- **Coffee does not add energy, it masks fatigue** — the guest author's thesis; the real source of energy is self-care [msg #234].
- **Life without alcohol is sustainable and the need for a regular glass fades;** non-alcoholic wine and beer can serve festive occasions [msg #497, #499].

- **She drank to escape, not for the wine.** Alcohol fuelled an existential crisis. Once she no longer wanted to escape her life, she stopped missing it [msg #745].
- **"I just like the taste" usually covers something else**, unless you are a sommelier [msg #745].
- **Parties work on water.** If an evening is dull without a glass, the problem is not the glass [msg #745].
- **Alcohol is glue.** It held together things that would not hold on their own. When it went, what was left felt spacious, not empty [msg #745].
- **Not drinking also works as a beauty treatment**: less puffiness, plus a 2019 study on facial aging [msg #745].
- **The urge to drink is shaped by social contract and advertising**, which is why non-alcoholic alternatives are useful [msg #745].
- **Stop through understanding, not a ban.** Done this way, it shows you your other dependencies and makes you freer [msg #745].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Katya's personal caffeine rule
**Origin.** Katya quit coffee and went through "жесточайшего синдрома отмены в 4 дня" (*the most brutal 4-day withdrawal syndrome*). She then "почистилась 5 недель" (*cleaned herself out for 5 weeks*) — five weeks without caffeine — and after that worked out a rule for herself [msg #57].

**The rule:** "Всегда пить декаф, за исключением ситуаций, когда реально нужно взбодриться" (*Always drink decaf, except in situations when you really need a boost*) — and then take caffeine "прямо как таблеточку" (*just like a pill*), 15–20 minutes before you need the effect. She adds that she needs a coffee boost "далеко не каждый день" (*far from every day*) [msg #57].

**Why:** by keeping caffeine for the moments that need it, the boost is used deliberately rather than burned on habit or pleasure (the post's framing contrasts "seeking a treat" with "seeking a boost") [msg #57].

### Five facts for getting energy from coffee without side effects
Purpose: Katya's list of facts to take into account "если хотите получать энергию от кофе, а побочные эффекты не получать" (*if you want to get energy from coffee but not the side effects*) [msg #57].

1. **First cup 1–2 hours after waking, not immediately.** On waking, blood cortisol rises naturally, which helps you feel alert. Caffeine should not be mixed into that window; otherwise you can set up a sharp energy crash around lunchtime [msg #57].
2. **Finish coffee before 17:00, preferably earlier.** Full metabolism of caffeine takes 8 to 14 hours, so late coffee can hit sleep hard — "Ну а о какой энергии может идти речь без нормального сна?" (*And what energy can we talk about without normal sleep?*) [msg #57].
3. **Above 2–4 standard cups a day, tolerance develops and the effect fades.** A standard cup is 240 ml. Not overdoing it matters also because excessive consumption leads to insomnia, nervousness and other side effects [msg #57].
4. **Tolerance develops anyway, so take regular breaks from coffee.** Fully removing tolerance takes at least a month, but abstaining for one week is already enough to feel the effect [msg #57].
5. **Coffee with sugar (and some milks, e.g. oat or skim) takes energy instead of giving it,** because it causes glucose spikes. "Качественный черный кофе — выбор тех, кто ищет в кофе энергию" (*Quality black coffee is the choice of those who seek energy in coffee*). This point is refined in the next framework and in the curves [msg #57].

### How to have coffee with milk and keep your energy
Context: while writing #57 Katya again wondered how coffee with milk affects glucose spikes and, as a consequence, energy. She admits she was sure that even plain coffee with milk and no sugar raises glucose, for two reasons: it was taught on her course ("доверяй, но проверяй" — *trust but verify*), and it seemed logical, since milk contains lactose, a disaccharide that breaks down to glucose. When she went looking for studies she found no evidence for this; on the Instagram profile of Glucose Goddess she saw that coffee with (not skimmed) milk or (not skimmed) cream "может скачков глюкозы не вызывать" (*may not cause glucose spikes*). She stresses that everything is individual and depends on many factors — even coffee without milk can cause a glucose spike. Problems start when you add sugar or begin "experimenting" with milk: "превращаем напиток, дающий энергию, в десерт, энергию забирающий" (*we turn a drink that gives energy into a dessert that takes energy away*) [msg #58].

The rules ("Что делать, чтобы и с молоком, и с энергией?" — *What to do to have both milk and energy?*) [msg #58]:
1. **Do not add sugar or other sweeteners** to coffee with milk [msg #58].
2. **Choose milk and cream that are not skimmed.** Reason given: sugar is often added to skimmed products to compensate for the missing fat and make them "tastier" [msg #58].
3. **For plant milk, prefer nut milks** — almond, coconut, cashew and similar. Plant milks made from high-carbohydrate foods (oat, rice) "точно бомбанет вам глюкозу" (*will definitely blow up your glucose*) [msg #58].
4. **Read labels; don't buy if there is added sugar** [msg #58].
5. **Drink even black coffee after a meal,** especially when you have slept badly [msg #58].
6. **Jessie's extra tip (Katya not yet convinced):** Jessie also advises not drinking coffee together with high-carbohydrate food — but Katya admits "тут даже у меня пока сердце дрогнуло" (*here even my heart has wavered for now*), i.e. she is not ready to adopt it [msg #58].

Additional factor Katya found surprising: according to Jessie, sleep and PMS also influence the reaction to coffee (see the curves below) [msg #58].

### Glucose Goddess coffee curves (six paired comparisons)
Katya attached six chart cards from @glucosegoddess. Each shows a continuous-glucose line in mg/dL from "eating time" to "+2 hours"; most cards mark a "baseline", a "spike +30" line (above +30 mg/dL is the red "spike" zone, below is green) and a "+60" level. Values below are read from the curves and are approximate [img #58, #59, #60, #61, #62, #63].

1. **Same black coffee, two people (Alan vs Jessie).** "Alan drinks a coffee": glucose rises from baseline to just under the +30 spike line, then drops below baseline, then a second small bump. "Jessie drinks a coffee": only a small, gentle bump. Lesson in the post: reactions are individual — even coffee without milk can spike glucose in some people [img #58] [msg #58].
2. **Coffee with 2 tsp brown sugar vs 2 tsp white sugar.** Both curves rise above the +30 spike line into the red zone and then fall below baseline (a dip after the spike). The brown-sugar curve is not lower than the white-sugar one — both produce a clear spike [img #59].
3. **Coffee with skim milk vs whole milk** (Stonyfield Organic Fat Free Milk vs Stonyfield Organic Whole Milk). Skim milk: a moderate bump (roughly half-way to +30) followed by a noticeable dip below baseline. Whole milk: essentially flat around baseline. This is the visual basis for "choose milk that is not skimmed" [img #60].
4. **Vanilla latte vs coffee with whipping cream** (absolute scale 80/110/140 mg/dL). The vanilla latte (Starbucks iced) rises from about 80 to about 105 mg/dL and returns around 2 hours later; coffee with whipping cream stays flat at roughly 80–85 mg/dL [img #61].
5. **Vanilla latte during PMS vs usually.** During PMS the same latte rises above the +30 spike line and is followed by a dip below baseline labelled "cravings"; usually it stays below the +30 line with only a slight dip after. Lesson: PMS amplifies the glucose response to the same drink [img #62] [msg #58].
6. **Whole milk cappuccino after bad sleep vs normally.** After bad sleep the curve rises noticeably (still under +30) and then dips below baseline; normally the cappuccino gives only a small bump. Lesson: poor sleep amplifies the response — which is why rule 5 says to drink coffee after food, especially after a bad night [img #63] [msg #58].

### Four myths about coffee and energy (guest post by Marina Sidorich)
Guest author Marina Sidorich (rubric #здоровая_Мару) frames coffee as "утро, ритуал, спасение в сложные дни и… источник ложных надежд" (*morning, a ritual, a rescue on hard days and… a source of false hopes*) and separates truth from myths and cognitive distortions [msg #234].

1. **Myth 1: "Coffee is energy in a cup."** According to the author, coffee does not add energy; it only "switches on" the brain by blocking fatigue receptors. You feel alert, but your body's resources do not increase. Her analogy: "Это как включить яркий свет в комнате — вы видите лучше, но мебель осталась той же." (*It's like turning on a bright light in a room — you see better, but the furniture is the same.*) [msg #234]
2. **Myth 2: "The more coffee, the more alertness."** Not quite: after the second or third cup the effect may reverse — excess caffeine overloads the nervous system, causing irritability and anxiety. The body is effectively shouting "Мне нужен отдых, а не кофе!" (*I need rest, not coffee!*) [msg #234]
3. **Myth 3: "A morning without coffee is not a morning."** The author suggests that what feels like needing coffee may actually be sleep deprivation. A sip of coffee can perk you up but will not replace full sleep, a warm-up, a balanced breakfast and a glass of water to "wake up" the body [msg #234].
4. **Myth 4: "Coffee helps cope with fatigue."** "Кофе — это временный костыль, а не решение проблемы." (*Coffee is a temporary crutch, not a solution to the problem.*) Real fatigue goes away when you revise your habits: more movement, more water and food that sustains energy levels — protein, fats and complex carbohydrates [msg #234].

**What to do if coffee is your love:** drink it consciously — not as an automatic refill at lunch break, but as a small ritual: good coffee, a pleasant setting and maximum enjoyment. Closing thesis: "настоящий источник энергии — это не кофе, а забота о себе" (*the real source of energy is not coffee but self-care*) [msg #234].

### The three-month no-alcohol experiment (how Katya stopped)
Purpose: a way to stop drinking without a vow. Katya rejects the addict framing: "Я не «завязывала»" (*I didn't "quit"*) [msg #745].

1. **Ask the question as an experiment.** As an adult she had never tried living without alcohol, so she simply asked herself: "а что будет, если не пить три месяца?" (*what will happen if I don't drink for three months?*). It is a time-limited, curious test, not a lifelong ban [msg #745].
2. **Go the full three months without drinking** [msg #745].
3. **Test again afterwards and watch your body.** After the three months she drank a bottle of beer at a friend's place in Tel Aviv. The next morning she looked in the mirror, took a headache pill and realised that alcohol "мне это больше не подходит" (*doesn't suit me anymore*). The decision came from her own experience, not from a rule [msg #745].
4. **Keep checking without committing.** Since then she sometimes tries a sip and still has not wanted more. She leaves the door open: "Не знаю, захочу ли я когда-нибудь обратно" (*I don't know whether I'll ever want to go back*) and "Зарекаться не буду" (*I won't swear off it*) [msg #745].

Why it works, in Katya's view: letting go through understanding rather than prohibition is what makes you start noticing your other dependencies [msg #745].

### Five reframes from seven alcohol-free years
These are the realisations that surprised Katya, each replacing a belief she held before she stopped [msg #745].

1. **"Life without alcohol will be bland" → she misses neither alcohol nor that life.** She used to expect Friday to become an average Tuesday, parties to feel like wakes, and herself to become the friend who sits with juice and leaves at ten. She jokes that this did happen, except she leaves at 8 because by 9 she likes to be in bed. What surprised her is something else. In September 2019 she thought losing alcohol would take away lightness, fun and the right to not think about anything for at least one evening. Only the last one really went. Her diagnosis: "я пила не потому, что любила вино, а потому, что из моей жизни хотелось выйти" (*I drank not because I loved wine, but because I wanted to get out of my life*). She was in an existential crisis, and alcohol was "топливом, на котором эта конструкция еще держалась" (*the fuel on which that construction was still holding together*). Now she no longer wants to escape her life [msg #745].
2. **"I liked the taste" → the taste was learned.** She once thought there was no life without New Zealand Sauvignon Blanc and Pfefferer. Now even expensive wine "бьет в нос спиртягой" (*hits the nose with raw spirit*). She tried it twice in a year. Her mechanism: few people like their first sip of alcohol; people get used to it. Over years the brain links the taste with the Friday exhale and the feeling "я взрослая и разбираюсь в вине" (*I'm an adult and I know wine*). Hence: "«Мне просто нравится вкус» почти всегда упаковка для чего-то другого" (*"I just like the taste" is almost always packaging for something else*), with the caveat "если вы не сомелье" (*unless you are a sommelier*) [msg #745].
3. **You can just drink water at parties.** At first she held on to non-alcoholic beer. Then she moved to Bali, where there was none (she wonders whether it has appeared since). She found she could drink water all evening, dance, chat, and it was fine. If you want something special: lime, mint, tonic, a beautiful glass. But if the party, the people and the music are good, "это и есть «вкусненькое»" (*that is the "treat"*). Her test: "А если без бокала скучно и пресно, дело не в бокале" (*And if it's boring and bland without a glass, the problem isn't the glass*) [msg #745].
4. **How much excess there was → alcohol as glue.** She went to parties to forget herself. Some acquaintances that seemed like friendships turned out to be drinking companions. "Алкоголь был клеем, который держал вместе то, что само по себе не держалось" (*Alcohol was the glue that held together what didn't hold on its own*). When the glue went, the excess fell away, and life became "не пусто, а просторно" (*not empty but spacious*) [msg #745].
5. **She can't unsee the social contract and the marketing.** A celebration means a glass. Adulthood means a glass. So do a date, a hard day, good news and bad news. We absorb this from childhood, from parents and from screens that always show sunsets, clinking glass and carefreeness. She suspects that half her desire to drink was about getting, at least for one evening, into "ту жизнь из рекламы" (*that life from the ads*). Such rituals are hard to uproot because nobody films beautiful ads for water (though, she jokes, they are starting to). That is why she values non-alcoholic alternatives: the glass stays in your hand, "просто без побочек" (*just without the side effects*) [msg #745].

A sixth surprise, the cosmetic effect, is under *Alcohol-free as a beauty treatment* below [msg #745].

### Gains ledger of an alcohol-free life
Katya lists what she got "взамен" (*in return*), "Слишком много" (*too much*) to want to go back [msg #745]:
1. **Money** that used to drain away on bars and on "бутылочку к ужину" (*a little bottle with dinner*) [msg #745].
2. **Weekends without a hangover** ("выходные без отходняка") [msg #745].
3. **A routine** ("режим"); she now likes to be in bed by 9 [msg #745].
4. **Regular exercise** [msg #745].
5. **Normal eating** [msg #745].
6. **The main gain: seeing other dependencies.** When you let go of one dependency through understanding rather than prohibition, you start to see the others. Her examples are the phone, sweets and work. She says this makes her freer, which serves her goal "быть свободной, счастливой и процветающей" (*to be free, happy and prosperous*), so that she can help others be free, happy and prosperous [msg #745].

### Alcohol-free as a beauty treatment
- Personal observation: she no longer wakes up so puffy ("такоооой отекшей") [msg #745].
- A 2019 study the author cites (on smoking, alcohol use and facial aging in women; a large multinational, multiracial cross-sectional survey, according to its linked title) found that women who drank 8 or more servings a week had more under-eye bags, wrinkles, drooping mouth corners and visible blood vessels. Katya glosses 8 servings a week as "это чуть больше бокала в день" (*a little more than a glass a day*) [msg #745].

### Non-alcoholic drinks: an updated verdict
- Alternatives exist widely now: even in Serbia, and in Barcelona she visited a whole non-alcoholic shop [msg #745].
- Verdict: "Большинство, честно говоря, виноградный сок с амбициями" (*Most, honestly, are grape juice with ambitions*). But some are drinks you want "ради вкуса" (*for the taste*) [msg #745].
- Their function: they keep the ritual of a glass in your hand without the side effects. This matters because, as she argues in reframe 5, the drinking ritual is reinforced by culture and advertising [msg #745].

- (!) CONTRADICTION (soft): #57 calls quality black coffee the choice for energy, while #58 notes black coffee can also spike glucose in some people and recommends drinking even black coffee after food [msg #57, #58].
- (!) CONTRADICTION (soft): #57 places the tolerance/side-effect threshold at more than 2–4 standard cups (240 ml) a day; the guest post #234 says the effect may reverse already after the second or third cup [msg #57, #234].
- Mechanism claims differ in framing: Katya's post links coffee's energy effects to cortisol timing, sleep and glucose spikes; the guest post says caffeine blocks "fatigue receptors" and adds no resources. Neither cites a study [msg #57, #234].
- Jessie's advice not to drink coffee with high-carb food is reported but not adopted by Katya [msg #58].

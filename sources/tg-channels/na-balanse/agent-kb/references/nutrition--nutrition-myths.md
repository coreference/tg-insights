# Nutrition myths and evidence-based guidelines

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's evidence-based corrections to popular nutrition beliefs: a two-part interview with gastroenterologist Natasha Suvorova debunking six myths about diets, gluten, milk, fruit, supplements and liver "detox"; a point-by-point digest of Norway's updated dietary guidelines (August 2024); and Andrew Huberman's argument that oral health is a core pillar of health, with his practical tooth-care rules. The author frames the myth series as a response to the fact that "в последнее время появилось множество противоречивой информации о питании и здоровье ЖКТ, которая часто не имеет научного обоснования" (*lately a lot of contradictory information about nutrition and gut health has appeared, often without scientific grounding*) — the goal is "не попадаться на крючок" (*not to get hooked*) by such claims [msg #151].

## Core ideas

- **Prefer research-confirmed eating patterns over fashionable diets.** DASH and Mediterranean diets have proven benefits; keto gives only short-term weight loss and may raise cardiovascular risk (see *Myth 1*) [msg #151].
- **Most "food is harmful" claims apply only to diagnosed subgroups.** Gluten is a problem for people with celiac disease or gluten sensitivity, lactose for people with lactase deficiency, fruit for people with a sensitive gut — and even then the response is individual (see *Myths 2–4*) [msg #151, #154].
- **Supplements and detoxes are not harmless or necessary by default.** Supplements are not clinically tested and can be contaminated or toxic to the liver; a healthy liver detoxifies itself (see *Myths 5–6*) [msg #154].
- **Current official guidance trends toward "less meat, more plants".** Norway's 2024 guidelines cap red meat at 350 g/week and build the diet on plant foods (see *Norway's guidelines*) [msg #204].
- **Oral health is a whole-body issue, and caries is about acidity, not sugar per se.** Oral bacteria can affect the nervous and cardiovascular systems; teeth remineralise when oral pH is kept favourable (see *Huberman's 7 oral-health rules*) [msg #56].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Six nutrition myths debunked (gastroenterologist Natasha Suvorova)
A two-part series (parts 1 and 2) in which the channel interviewed Natasha Suvorova, a gastroenterologist at an evidence-based medicine clinic. Each myth is stated, then corrected by her [msg #151, #154].

**Myth 1: "The keto diet is good for your health" ("Кето-диета полезна для здоровья").** According to Suvorova, the DASH diet and the Mediterranean diet are the ones confirmed by scientific research: they have been shown to reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease, obesity, diabetes and cancer. Other diets such as keto give short-term weight-loss results, but can increase the risk of cardiovascular events because of their high content of harmful fats. In the long run, keto is not more effective than balanced eating or a simple calorie deficit. Her recommendation: always discuss your diet with a dietitian physician or nutritionist to choose the optimal type of eating for you and to avoid a reduction in dietary diversity ("избежать снижения пищевого разнообразия") [msg #151].

**Myth 2: "Gluten is harmful to everyone" ("Глютен вреден всем").** Many people believe gluten harms the body and causes bloating, diarrhoea and abdominal pain. Suvorova says this applies only to people with celiac disease — about 1% of the world population — and celiac disease must be confirmed by blood tests and a biopsy. There is also non-celiac gluten sensitivity, in which a person can have symptoms without true celiac disease. But even a genetic predisposition does not mean 100% gluten intolerance. What to do: discuss all symptoms with a doctor, keep a food diary and track your reactions [msg #151].

**Myth 3: "Milk forms mucus in the gut" ("Молоко образует слизь в кишечнике").** The widespread belief is that milk causes mucus in the intestine and in the ENT organs (ear, nose, throat). Scientific studies since 1964 have not confirmed this hypothesis. Some people do feel thick, viscous saliva after dairy, but this is due to the organoleptic properties of milk (its texture and taste), and has nothing to do with the mucous membranes. Milk is a valuable source of calcium. People with proven lactase deficiency can consume up to 24 g of lactose per day without developing symptoms — though "здесь все индивидуально" (*here everything is individual*) [msg #151].

**Myth 4: "Eating fruit is harmful" ("Фрукты есть вредно").** The myth holds that fruit is harmful because of its fructose. In fact, Suvorova says, fruits are among the most beneficial sources of fibre, which people often under-eat. If bloating does not bother you, there is no need to limit fruit. If you have a sensitive gut, you can follow the principles of a low-FODMAP diet — excluding or reducing foods high in fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides and polyols. Practical swap: instead of apples and grapes, eat blueberries, strawberries and citrus fruits to avoid unpleasant symptoms [msg #154].

**Myth 5: "All supplements are absolutely safe" ("все БАДы абсолютно безопасны").** Unlike medicines, supplements (БАДы) do not go through strict clinical trials, so their safety and efficacy are often unknown. Studies show that the actual content of some supplements differed from the declared composition and could contain admixtures of drugs, hormones and even mouse droppings. Case from Suvorova's own practice: a patient took a supplement to improve memory and developed elevated liver enzymes, which led to serious consequences. Cases of drug-induced hepatitis after supplement use are known. Although some supplements may be safe, it is always better to consult a doctor before using them and to check information on specialised resources such as the LiverTox website (Ливертокс) [msg #154].

**Myth 6: "The liver needs a detox" ("Печени нужен детокс").** The liver itself performs detoxification, plus a great many other useful functions: it stores (deposits) vitamins, metabolises hormones and produces bile. Suvorova calls it "своеобразной фабрикой для организма" (*a kind of factory for the body*), and when it works normally it needs no additional cleanses. Detoxifying therapy is carried out only in proven liver failure — for example in viral or alcoholic hepatitis; in that case toxins accumulate in the body, which can lead to neurological consequences. The main task remains a healthy lifestyle and avoiding factors that can harm the liver: limit alcohol, follow the principles of rational eating, get vaccinated against viral hepatitis. If you have problems with the liver, gallbladder or pancreas, consult a doctor to determine the exact cause and treatment approach [msg #154].

### Norway's updated dietary guidelines (August 2024)
The author describes these as Norway's updated dietary recommendations published in August 2024, "самые свежие на сегодняшний день" (*the most recent to date*), headlined "меньше мяса, больше растений" (*less meat, more plants*). The points she highlights, all twelve [msg #204]:

1. **Red meat:** the maximum amount of red meat has been lowered to 350 g per week [msg #204].
2. **Plant base:** the foundation of the diet should be plant foods — from vegetables to legumes and whole grains [msg #204].
3. **Nuts and seeds:** eat 20–30 g of unsalted nuts per day (roughly a small handful) and add various kinds of seeds to the diet [msg #204].
4. **Vegetables, fruit and berries:** 500–800 g per day, of which vegetables should make up at least half [msg #204].
5. **Potatoes:** part of a healthy diet, but not counted in the vegetable category [msg #204].
6. **Juice:** 100 ml of freshly squeezed juice a day is acceptable, but only together with food; for children, juice intake should be limited [msg #204].
7. **Whole grains:** include whole-grain foods in at least 2 meals every day [msg #204].
8. **Fish:** 2–3 times per week (300–450 g in total), at least once oily fish (for example salmon) and once lean fish (for example cod) [msg #204].
9. **Legumes instead of meat:** replace the meat component of dishes — for example in soups and stews — with beans and lentils [msg #204].
10. **Dairy:** part of a healthy diet if it is lower-fat. Recommended 3 servings daily, for example yogurt + a glass of milk + 2 slices of cheese. People with lactose intolerance can choose suitable alternatives [msg #204].
11. **Sweets:** a healthy, varied diet has room for sweets too, but they are recommended in limited amounts and for occasions [msg #204].
12. **Food culture and shared meals:** food is part of culture and tradition; eating together is an important part of healthy habits, especially significant for children [msg #204].

### Huberman's 7 oral-health rules
Source and rationale: in a podcast episode on oral health, Dr. Andrew Huberman placed oral health among the main pillars of psycho-emotional and physical state, alongside sleep, nutrition, exercise and stress management. The reason: the mouth hosts a rich microbiome, and the bacteria that cause gum recession can penetrate further into the body, with unpleasant consequences for the nervous and cardiovascular systems [msg #56].

The mechanism behind the rules: "Сахар особо не виноват" (*sugar isn't really to blame*). Sugar itself does not cause caries; the bacteria that feed on it and release acid do. An acidic oral environment promotes demineralisation of teeth, while an alkaline one promotes restoration — remineralisation. So the main goal of dental health is to maintain the pH balance at which saliva can restore enamel, reversing the formation of caries [msg #56].

What "uncle Andrew" (дядя Андрей) advises [msg #56]:
1. **Never skip brushing in the evening** — at night we have less saliva, so the teeth are more vulnerable [msg #56].
2. **Floss before bed** [msg #56].
3. **Rinse your mouth after eating** [msg #56].
4. **Breathe through your nose, especially during sleep** [msg #56].
5. **Keep "clean" gaps between meals** so that the right pH is maintained in the mouth (this ties to the pH mechanism above) [msg #56].
6. **Do not use mouthwashes that contain alcohol** [msg #56].
7. **Visit the dentist twice a year** [msg #56].

The author closes by urging readers to get their teeth checked and to bring their children along [msg #56].

## Detailed insights

### How the gastroenterologist's advice repeats across myths
A recurring rule across the myths is to replace self-diagnosis with medical assessment: discuss your diet with a dietitian or nutritionist (Myth 1), discuss symptoms with a doctor and keep a food diary (Myth 2), consult a doctor before taking supplements (Myth 5), and see a doctor for liver, gallbladder or pancreas problems (Myth 6). Diagnoses such as celiac disease and lactase deficiency are treated as things that must be "proven" (by blood tests and biopsy for celiac disease), not assumed [msg #151, #154]. The series invited readers to send further "truth or myth" questions on nutrition and gut health [msg #151, #154].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- Suvorova's claim that keto raises cardiovascular risk "из-за высокого содержания вредных жиров" (*because of the high content of harmful fats*) is hedged with "могут" (*may*); no specific study is named [msg #151].
- The supplement-contamination findings ("исследования показывают" — *studies show*) and the "since 1964" milk-mucus research are cited without naming the studies (!) INCOMPLETE: specific studies not identified in the source [msg #151, #154].
- The 24 g/day lactose figure for people with lactase deficiency is qualified by Suvorova herself as individual [msg #151].
- The Norwegian guidelines are presented as the author's selection of "интересными моментами" (*interesting points*), not the full guidelines [msg #204].
- (!) UNCLEAR: in the Norwegian guidelines, red meat is capped at 350 g/week while legume substitution is encouraged; the source does not say whether processed meat is included in the cap [msg #204].
- The oral-health rules are Huberman's recommendations as retold by the author; the post does not detail the evidence behind each rule (e.g. why alcohol-containing mouthwash or mouth breathing is harmful) (!) INCOMPLETE [msg #56].

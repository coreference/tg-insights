# Relocation and staying stable through moves

## What this covers

Two personal-experience posts from Katya Strelnikova, written about one month after she and her husband moved to Belgrade (her fourth emigration in six years, second in one year, after the US and then a Spanish residence-permit refusal). The first post explains, point by point, why Belgrade suits her better than the US, Spain/Europe or Moscow, and what the downsides are. The second describes her rider (райдер), the portable set of routines, objects and habits that she says lets her change countries without losing her footing, and names the privileges that make it work. The source frames this as personal experience (both posts are tagged #личныйопыт_набалансе, "personal experience"), not universal advice; her summarizing thesis is that you take yourself wherever you go, but place still matters [msg #734, #737].

## Core ideas

- **You take yourself with you, but place still decides.** Inner stability travels with you, yet the external environment (familiarity, pace, costs, safety, food, distance to family) strongly shapes how well she feels. See *Six reasons Belgrade feels better* [msg #734].
- **Stability moves with you if your way of life moves with you.** Her explanation for coping with frequent emigration is that she moves "not alone, but with my (husband and) way of life": countries change, the supports stay the same. See *The relocation rider* [msg #737].
- **Resilience is built over years; packing takes minutes.** The deepest item of the rider is mental practice (daily meditation, impermanence practice, self-compassion), which takes years to build but then goes anywhere [msg #737].
- **Comfort, not challenge, is her growth condition.** She says she does not like challenges and "drowns" in them; to flourish she needs comfort, which is why lower financial pressure and an unhurried culture matter so much to her [msg #734].
- **The rider rests on a foundation of luck.** No children, control over her own schedule, a financial cushion, a husband who handles immigration paperwork, and moving only to places they have already visited and liked [msg #737].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Six reasons Belgrade feels better (Katya's place-fit criteria)
Purpose: her account of why, one month after arriving, she feels as if she has "lived here all my life", and what a place gives her that the US, Spain and Moscow did not. She stresses that it is "Для меня. По крайней мере, пока." (*For me. At least for now.*), and that she does not know whether it is her trained experience of moving or Belgrade itself; the best description she has is "город для жизни" (*a city for living*). She calls the move a "психологический и физический ретрит" (*psychological and physical retreat*) sent by the universe, after the decision a year earlier to leave the US and Spain's refusal of a residence permit that summer. General pluses she lists before the six points: comfortable prices, tasty food, lots of great people, Russian or English spoken almost everywhere, and everything close: she walks almost everywhere in the city even if it takes 45–50 minutes, and nature and mountains are an hour or two by car (she has already been hiking with people from a Telegram chat and was going to a spa hotel with friends) [msg #734].

1. **It is familiar ("Здесь знакомо").** She immediately reads a familiar context: cigarette butts on the pavement, panel blocks, the "Slavic gaze", Cyrillic script, birch trees, grandmothers at the market, a language you do not know but still partly understand. Why it matters: "Мозгу не приходится тратить кучу сил на адаптацию и привыкание" (*The brain doesn't have to spend a lot of energy on adaptation and getting used to things*) [msg #734].
2. **You don't have to try hard ("Здесь не нужно стараться").** To live in the US "надо очень хотеть и очень стараться" (*you have to want it very much and try very hard*); there is a specific culture of achievement and the American dream, and she and her husband are, apparently ("or temporarily"), "too old for this shit". Europe fit ideally in this sense (a place to go and "live out" life) but it did not work out, and Serbia's vibe is no worse. "Полако" (slowly) is heard at every step; lunch can last a couple of hours, people budget an hour for coffee with a friend, and nobody rushes. She ties this to her long-standing thesis that rest is not a reward for productivity but a physiological necessity: here it is simply a way of life and nobody needs to explain it. Her husband's joke: with their vibe they should definitely be admitted to the EU [msg #734].
3. **She (almost) doesn't worry about money ("Здесь я (почти) не тревожусь о деньгах").** Her cost comparisons for living the way that is comfortable and joyful for her: Spain about 1.5 times more expensive than Belgrade ("life a bit to grow into"), Moscow about twice, the US more than twice (and there she was "going mad with anxiety"). The thought "умру под мостом" (*I'll die under a bridge*) visited her daily in the US, fairly often in Barcelona, and is a very rare guest in Belgrade. Her reasoning: "А я не люблю челленджи, я в них тону. Мне, чтобы цвести, нужен комфорт." (*And I don't like challenges, I drown in them. To flourish, I need comfort.*) [msg #734].
4. **She feels safe ("Здесь я чувствую себя в безопасности").** In the US she felt unsafe on many levels (financial included). In a country where anyone may have a gun she feels not calmer but more scared, constantly in an "alert" state; she recalls being told on arrival in San Francisco not to leave valuables in the car. She mentions the well-known robbery problem in Barcelona. In Belgrade friends laugh at her because, as a person with PTSD, she guards her iPhone anxiously; a friend in a public sauna, asked where her phone is, answers she doesn't know, probably on a chair over there. For Katya this is still extremely unusual [msg #734].
5. **The food is incredibly good ("Здесь невероятно вкусные продукты").** She calls the product quality in Serbia mind-blowing; compared with the US and Moscow, Spain was good too, but she does not remember salmon or raspberries tasting like this. Their favourite pastime is going to the market (one "like from childhood, with jeans on a piece of cardboard"), buying something, being pleasantly shocked by the prices, then slowly savouring "another masterpiece" at home. She is happy to live where kajmak is sold and eaten everywhere [msg #734].
6. **She doesn't feel cut off ("Здесь я не чувствую себя отрезанным ломтем").** In the US she permanently felt torn away from everything dear to her: a flight to Moscow took 20 hours "if lucky"; from Europe about 10 hours; from Belgrade there is a direct 3-hour flight. This matters because her mother is in Russia and she plans to visit; knowing her mother is a few hours away rather than a day of travel is "a completely different feeling" [msg #734].

**The downsides she names** ("И да, минусы тоже есть", *And yes, there are downsides too*):
- Smoking: the most tangible for her; people smoke a lot and everywhere, including cafés and restaurants.
- Difficulties with Schengen visas (with the aside "but where don't Russians have them now?").
- Not the most popular airport: no direct flight to Bangkok.
- Electronics and deliveries are not simple; Amazon does not work in Serbia.
- Financial instruments are not as luxurious as in Europe or the US ("a problem for future us").
- Belgrade is probably not for those seeking a world-class cultural programme or career heights; she describes herself as more of a quiet type ("тихой сапой").
Her verdict on these: "Но если бы вы знали, как мне на это все похер, вы бы заплакали." (*But if you knew how little I care about all this, you'd cry.*) [msg #734].

**Conclusion.** She had a similar state on Bali, which she still considers the best time of her life; she is afraid to "jinx" it but wonders whether she can come close to it again. Since arriving "мне все хочется" (*I want to do everything*), which is not always the case for her; she notes that she is a different version of herself in different countries (a topic she plans for a separate post). Her signature line: "куда бы ты ни ехал, ты берешь с собой себя. Но место все же решает." (*wherever you go, you take yourself with you. But place still decides.*). She hopes for a three-year residence permit, and adds that if it is refused she no longer doubts they will cope "in every sense" [msg #734].

### The relocation rider (portable supports for staying stable through moves)
Purpose: the answer to the question of why she copes with frequent emigration. Context: Claude, after analysing her energy diary three weeks after the move to Belgrade, told her "Ты неплохо держишься для второй эмиграции за год" (*You're holding up pretty well for a second emigration in a year*). She did not get ill or bedridden; she got a bit lost and lost inspiration, but says that happens to her without emigration too. Over six years she has lived in Moscow, Bali, Java, Istanbul, Miami, Raleigh, Barcelona and now Belgrade (not counting moves within places); this year Spain refused them a residence permit in summer and then a Schengen visa, so they packed and moved to Belgrade. Clients and friends tell her this is unusual, especially without "losing your mind". Her secret: "переезжаю я не одна, а со своим (мужем и) образом жизни. Меняются страны, а опоры остаются те же." (*I move not alone but with my (husband and) way of life. Countries change, but the supports stay the same.*) The rider has ten items, following the post's headings [msg #737]:

1. **Working with the mind ("Работа с той самой кукухой").** She calls this the main item. What: she has meditated every day for the past year and has practised since 2018. How: she keeps taking programs by the teacher Lobsang (most recently Cultivating Emotional Balance); she practised impermanence for a year and believes that is why she got through the Spanish residence-permit refusal "without hysterics"; she treats herself with compassion rather than criticism when something goes wrong. Why: "Чемодан я уже могу собрать за сорок минут… А вот устойчивость собирается годами, зато потом едет с тобой куда угодно." (*I can already pack a suitcase in forty minutes… But resilience is assembled over years, and then it travels with you anywhere.*) [msg #737].
2. **A normal relationship with food ("Нормальные отношения с едой").** What: she trained herself to eat a clear, simple set of foods that can be bought in any country: tomatoes, cucumbers, greens, bread, eggs, salmon. Why: no hunting for special cottage cheese, cheese or sauce across every supermarket of a new city. Caveats: she says this was neither easy nor quick given a history of an eating disorder (рпп в анамнезе); in some places these foods are less tasty and cost more, while in Belgrade she really enjoys her product list [msg #737].
3. **Home workouts ("Домашние тренировки").** What: since 2021 she trains at home in Anastasia Lunegova's club. How: she carries small balls and resistance bands, and on arrival immediately buys dumbbells, a foam roller and a Pilates ball. Why: she doesn't need to find where to go right away, coordinate schedules or overcome a language barrier; "Я просто всегда знаю, что мне делать" (*I just always know what to do*) [msg #737].
4. **Sport hobbies that are easy to find anywhere.** What: padel, Pilates, yoga, hiking; every city has good places, studios and coaches. How: she explores them gradually, after the home routine is in place. Why: it gives the feeling of integrating into a community [msg #737].
5. **Spacious, bright, quiet and cosy housing.** What: they pay enormous attention to housing and look for flats with enough light and space. Cost: sometimes they pay more than they'd like. Why: they rent not only a home but an office, since both work from home. Rule learned "the hard way": "экономить на месте, где проводишь 90% времени, плохая идея" (*saving on the place where you spend 90% of your time is a bad idea*) [msg #737].
6. **Rituals that are easy to reproduce anywhere.** The list: meditation, microcurrents, face massage, dry-brush massage, skincare, a bath, a book with a cup of coffee, a TV series, studying. She always gets up one to two hours earlier than her husband (and goes to bed earlier): "Это мое время, и его не отменяет никакой переезд" (*This is my time, and no move cancels it*) [msg #737].
7. **Acquaintances around the world.** What: she has acquaintances and friends in almost every part of the world, some met online, and she keeps meeting new people (a "non-obvious benefit of the blog"). How: first thing, she writes to all locals she knows and asks where to live, where to go, how things work there, and their favourite wellness places [msg #737].
8. **Small household items carried along.** The list: a light alarm clock, a pillow, an aroma lamp, pyjamas, favourite slippers, a laptop stand and lamp, a tripod. Why: "Эти мелочи превращают чужую квартиру в мою за один вечер" (*These small things turn someone else's flat into mine in one evening*) [msg #737].
9. **Fast search for basic places.** What: in every new city during the first month she finds a bakery, a coffee shop and tea shop, a grocery, a Korean cosmetics shop, a pharmacy with collagen (which requires trying and testing), and similar. Why: so as not to feel anxiety when something runs out and marketplaces like Ozon and Amazon do not work (Amazon doesn't work in Serbia, and in Spain its selection was "disgusting") [msg #737].
10. **A vacation for a romance with the city ("Отпуск для романа с городом").** What: for the second time she takes vacation for the first two weeks after a move. Trade-off: on one hand she "loses vacation" (and, for self-employed people, money); on the other she has never regretted it. Why: while her head is not occupied with work she has time to calmly set up daily life, wander, zone out and "start a romance" with the new city. She walks a lot and loves exploring space; this is how she makes herself fall in love with a city and finds places even her local friends don't know [msg #737].

Note: the post gives no count for the rider; in the source text items 3–6 run together without line breaks, and the ten-item numbering above follows its bold-style headings [msg #737].

### Foundation under the rider (preconditions that make it work)
She stresses the rider works because there is a foundation under it, and "я знаю, как мне повезло" (*I know how lucky I am*) [msg #737]:
1. **No children.**
2. **She sets her own work schedule.**
3. **A financial cushion.** "We're not multimillionaires", but they have a reserve "for a step left and right": to pay for housing in a new country, pay helpers with documents, buy a coffee machine, set up daily life and keep money for a rainy day.
4. **Her husband technically handles most immigration questions**: in the US they lived on his visa, in Spain they applied via his startup, in Serbia via his sole proprietorship (ИП).
5. **They only move to places they have already visited and liked** — a rule adopted after the US. How they choose a place she says deserves a separate post.

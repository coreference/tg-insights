Part 6 of the «На балансе с…» series (August–November 2026) — three interviews with Zhenya Datsko (mentor, balancing chaos and order), Nastya Veselko (personal-finance expert, balance as "a great day") and Anya Kovaleva (coach and entrepreneur, balance as making a choice and accepting its consequences): how each defines balance and the concrete routines for calendar blocks, mood-based training, chronotype-based days, sleep, call grouping, relocation and permission to rest.

## Core ideas

- **Perfect, even balance is not achievable; rebalancing is.** Almost every guest rejects equal distribution: Ermakova does not believe in long-term "ideal balance" [msg #39]; Malybaeva says doing everything simultaneously and evenly is impossible [msg #53]; Kasay says even distribution is only realistic with a couple of life areas [msg #118]; Brish says it is impossible to keep up with everything [msg #129]; Ella Mills says she cannot find balance in everything [msg #130]. The working model is to deliberately prioritize one or two areas for a period and then switch (see *Four burners theory*).
- **Balance is personal and must be defined by you.** Luppova: what is a skew for one person is balance for another [msg #76]; Keilman: "Баланс — разобраться, что для тебя баланс" (Balance is figuring out what balance is for you) [msg #97]; Maslovskaya: balance is a choice [msg #115].
- **Losing balance is part of balance, and low energy is normal.** Keilman: dropping out of balance is part of balance [msg #97]; Kasay: she does not try to restore energy every time it dips [msg #118]; stress is described as a normal reaction to big change and a condition of growth [msg #53, #118].
- **Resource first.** Guests tie everything else to sleep, food, body practice and inner contact: nothing good gets done "не в ресурсе" (out of resource) [msg #53]; the "sheet" of balance stretches more easily when you have slept and eaten [msg #129]; quality sleep and contact with oneself are named the most important things [msg #105].
- **Self-criticism drains energy; self-acknowledgement and leniency restore it.** Recurrent across Luppova, Keilman, Maslovskaya and Brish [msg #76, #97, #115, #129].
- **Protected time slots and hard rules work better than willpower.** Almost every guest has a non-negotiable morning, a meeting-free day, a no-work weekend, a screens-off time or a fixed lunch [msg #39, #53, #76, #97, #105, #115, #118, #130].

- **Balance is a verb, not a state.** Several guests reject a static balance: Grabovich says life is about balancing, like juggling on a tightrope; Fedorova and Beloglazova contrast the noun «баланс» with the verb «балансировать»; Kronewald notes nature has only two days of equilibrium per year; Kuts calls balance a process of reflection, search and choice [msg #142, #155, #163, #177, #194].
- **Blend instead of separation.** Nikulina and Polishchuk prefer work-life blend; Fedorova prefers work-play balance. All still keep some deliberate switch-off rule [msg #134, #169, #155].
- **Balance is visible over a longer horizon.** Grabovich: not at the level of a moment but of a week; Fedorova: rhythm can be complex and only visible from a distance, so she uses seasons [msg #142, #155].
- **Sleep is non-negotiable for almost everyone** (7–8 h, ~8–10 h, 9+ h, before 22:30, before midnight) [msg #134, #142, #147, #155, #177, #182, #194].
- **Know your personal imbalance signals and act on them** — irritability, anxiety and fidgeting, demanding care from a partner, autopilot, anger/apathy, body sensations in the solar plexus [msg #147, #155, #169, #177, #182].
- **Rest must be permitted and planned**, against inherited beliefs that rest is laziness [msg #134, #155, #163, #177].
- **Ask for help and build a support team** — therapists, coaches, doctors, nannies, partners [msg #134, #163, #177].
- **Lasting change takes years and repetition**, so repeated wellbeing ideas are not wasted [msg #195].

- **Constant balance is not the goal; dynamic balance is.** Almost every guest describes balance as something that moves: a seesaw you keep swinging (Shedina), airing a room (Zharikova), "equilibrium imbalance" (Mikhailova), surfing energy channels over a wide time scale (Bichevskaya). Demanding constant balance of oneself is itself called a form of over-achieving that contradicts balance (Abibullaeva) and a waste of energy (Nikolaeva). [msg #208, #232, #256, #214, #244, #218]
- **Balance is personal, learned by experience, and ruined by comparison.** Polozhentseva says you must "get your licence" through experience, not from one book or podcast, and that comparing your pace to others' derails you. [msg #205]
- **Sleep is the most repeated foundation.** Polozhentseva sleeps 7–8 hours, Nikolaeva 8–10, Babushkina keeps her sleep schedule even at festivals, Chernysheva lacks only settled sleep to reach 10/10, and several guests treat irritation as a signal to go to sleep. [msg #205, #218, #249, #226, #214]
- **Know your personal overload signals.** Guests name concrete markers: irritation with people (Bichevskaya, Abibullaeva, Nikolaeva), wearing the same jeans and hoodie (Zharikova), eating a pie fast between sessions (Zharikova), asking oneself "am I happy?" less often (Babushkina). [msg #214, #244, #218, #232, #249]
- **Design the environment so healthy actions happen automatically.** Put lunch into the calendar, pick a kindergarten within walking distance, go to a studio across the street, cook in batches. [msg #208, #218, #244]
- **Solitude, silence and the body are the main recovery channels.** Walks alone, banya, massage, yoga, dancing, a shower in the dark, time in nature. [msg #205, #208, #214, #218, #232, #249]
- **Seek help without shame.** Weekly psychotherapy, gestalt therapists, coaches, EMDR, "helping specialists" recur as a way to keep going. [msg #208, #226, #256, #276]
- **Self-acceptance over self-criticism.** Being a friend to oneself rather than an evaluator, accepting imperfection, accepting one's real values (e.g. wanting to earn a lot). [msg #218, #244, #205]

- **Balance as a fixed, equal state does not exist; it is a dynamic process.** Almost every guest rejects the "10/10 in every sphere" ideal: Sonya Smyslova says it exists only on paper, Maryana Onysko compares it to happiness you cannot stay in, Sasha Arest compares it to life on Mars, Lena Degtyar believes in conscious imbalance, and Ira Oganesyan says "we are not in balance, we are balancing" [msg #298, #339, #374, #387, #524].
- **Balance is seen over time, in periods and seasons.** Guests describe balance retrospectively (five burnt years then five recovery years), as seasons (immigration, business, family, creativity), as rotating focus, or as dependent on the life period and children's ages [msg #298, #339, #374, #453, #524].
- **Balancing means choosing and giving up.** With 24 hours in a day, one must decide the current priority and release the rest; choosing one thing almost always means refusing another [msg #387, #524, #320].
- **Harmony comes from meaning and self-knowledge, not equal distribution.** Feeling in harmony "when I know why", hearing oneself, and knowing one's values are repeatedly named as the real basis [msg #298, #304, #524].
- **Social expectations and the "best version of yourself" create pressure; balance itself can become another source of anxiety.** Guests describe dropping conventional success and "correct" motherhood scripts; Polina Perusheva warns against pursuing balance with perfectionism [msg #298, #374, #524].
- **Body basics and small rituals sustain energy:** sleep, sport without fanaticism, walking, closing vitamin deficits, limiting alcohol, phones and Instagram [msg #304, #320, #374, #417, #453].
- **Support systems matter:** therapy (as hygiene or a life-long sport), coaching, friends ("tribe"), journals and ChatGPT for emotions [msg #304, #320, #339, #387, #453].
- **Burnout gives early grey signals before red ones** — the channel author's own framework from her Life Blend Talks interview [msg #430].

- **Balance is internal, not external.** Alena Bocharova calls it inner stability, Lena Sdobnikova a steadiness "that does not need to be searched for", Ira Evdokimova a sense of agency — the feeling you will cope with anything. [msg #527, #611, #664]
- **Balance is dynamic, not a fixed point.** Nelli Nedre: balance is the skill of losing equilibrium and getting back "on the tightrope"; Varya Semenikhina: constant tuning to a changing system; Polina Kadkina: different seasons of life need different things; Ira Evdokimova: periodically lacking balance is normal. [msg #624, #595, #561, #664]
- **Imbalance has personal early-warning signals.** Irritation and tears (Alena), snapping at parents (Polina), inability to calmly settle tired children (Lena), being unable to get out of bed (Nelli), sleep and food going off track when living against one's values (Ira). [msg #527, #561, #611, #624, #664]
- **Recovery is a skill that grows in importance with responsibility and age.** Varya Semenikhina argues each career step costs more energy and that recovery depends on how you are built inside, not on external comforts. [msg #595]
- **Structure carries the parts of life that are not in focus.** Fixed routines (sleep times, meals, steps, weekly bath, family day) let guests rest on habits while their attention is elsewhere. [msg #561, #611, #624]

- **There is no balance, only balancing.** Datsko: balance is the process of searching for it, like standing on a balance board — you freeze for a fraction of a second and then recalibrate again; it never happens that every sphere is perfect at once [msg #681].
- **Balance is a felt result of a varied day.** Veselko: balance is when you go to bed and think «классный сегодня был день» (what a great day it was today); a day needs variety or she "fades out" [msg #721].
- **Balance is choosing and accepting the cost.** Kovaleva: what exhausts us is often not the choice itself but the unwillingness to make it and to accept what it requires giving up; balance means enjoying what you chose without guilt, shame, envy or regret [msg #746].
- **Structure should flex to the person, not the reverse.** All three guests build forms that fit their nature: booked-but-flexible blocks and floating workouts (Datsko), concentration-based task blocks and honest needs (Veselko), call grouping and portable routines (Kovaleva) [msg #681, #721, #746].
- **Live by your real chronotype and sleep need.** Datsko discovered she is a lark and rebuilt her life around it; Veselko gets up early and treats sleep deprivation as the hidden cause of bad moods; Kovaleva starts work at 10–11 and sleeps 9–10 hours [msg #681, #721, #746].
- **Do not force yourself when energy is low.** Veselko no longer "gathers her strength" or pours coffee into herself; Kovaleva takes on fewer tasks and allows herself to be in a low state, treating joy at cancelled plans as a tiredness signal [msg #721, #746].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Four burners theory (Alena Ermakova)
Purpose: a way to think about balance without pursuing equal attention to everything. Ermakova says the concept of the "four burners" ("четырех конфорок") is closer to her than "ideal balance" [msg #39].
1. **The stove has several burners** — money, family, leisure, health and similar areas of life [msg #39].
2. **They cannot burn with equal strength.** "У плиты электричества для полноценной работы хватает только на две" (The stove has enough electricity to fully run only two) [msg #39].
3. **Uniform flame = mediocrity.** If you light all burners evenly, you will not succeed at anything and will be "середнячком" (a middling performer) [msg #39].
4. **Balance = timely switching of power.** The point of balance is to change burner power at the right time: these two now, others later, another one can be put on full blast but quickly turned off again [msg #39].
Her current application: money, projects and travel are "burning" most; family is always at the highest level; therefore hobbies and self-care have collapsed. Example: she skipped the gym in Istanbul several days running — not from laziness but because she enjoyed working so much she did not want to stop, having just returned from a holiday full of sport and rest and having missed "sitting and inventing things" [msg #39]. Her goal for the year: learn to balance the burners so they do not sag when she is away from home, since she spends about 60% of the year travelling [msg #39].

### Lex Fridman six-part morning-pages practice (Alena Ermakova's day-balancing practice)
Purpose: at the start of each day, use morning pages to choose "что сегодня в приоритете, и какие конфорки будут гореть ярче" (what is the priority today and which burners will burn brighter). She borrowed this specific practice from Lex Fridman, whose podcasts and lectures she listens to regularly. It has six parts [msg #40]:
1. **Rules or prohibitions** — are there any rules or bans I want to keep today? [msg #40]
2. **Gratitude** — whom do I want to thank and for what? [msg #40]
3. **Long-term goals and dreams** — over a 5–25-year horizon [msg #40]
4. **Short-term goals and dreams** — for example until the end of the day, month, year [msg #40]
5. **Today's actions** — what do I want to do today? [msg #40]
6. **Principles** — from which principles will I act today? Her example: "я буду действовать из любви и благости и не разорву всех к херам" (I'll act from love and kindness and won't tear everyone to pieces) [msg #40]
Why it works, per Ermakova: each time she decides herself what the day is about, what she wants to think about, what will happen by the end of the month and year. What she highlights and prioritizes is "как будто **даю себе обещание, что я на это не забью.**" — like a promise to herself not to drop it; this sets the mood for the day [msg #40].

### Alisa Luppova's "ideal balance" template
Luppova defines balance as managing to invest an adequate amount of time and effort into the important areas of life, with "adequate" being individual [msg #76]. Her ideal, itemized [msg #76]:
1. Five working days.
2. One of them without meetings (in practice Friday — "Золотое правило про встречи: пятница без встреч", the golden rule: meeting-free Friday, used to give proper time to whatever cannot be done between calls).
3. No work on weekends.
4. 4–5 varied workouts per week.
5. A cultural outing and meeting friends and children at the weekend.
6. Sleep 7–8 hours.
7. At least 15,000 steps.
8. Three meals at the same times, without overeating late at night.
Why she invests effort: in balance she has more energy to do what she wants; she is active and interested in many things, which takes strength [msg #76]. On steps: in Barcelona 15,000 steps are easy; in Moscow it was harder (freezing rain, cramming the impossible into one day). She suggests a more relaxed "Catalan vibe" helps — not scheduling meetings back to back, so you can walk calmly from point A to point B [msg #76].

### Olga Malybaeva's "out of resource" recovery and prevention protocol
Premise: "Все мои сферы деятельности и роли постоянно стремятся к дисбалансу" (all my areas and roles constantly tend toward imbalance); her role is to bring them back with conscious actions. She "knows clearly" she will do nothing good when out of resource; she has hit zero, and then the top priority is getting out [msg #53].
1. **Recognize the state.** Being out of resource feels like being in a noisy room: you keep doing things and no longer understand what or why, just trying to cope with the flow. Then it is very important to stop [msg #53].
2. **Write a journal.** She sits down and writes what she feels, what worries her and why, what she really wants. This helps break the closed loop; she says she does not linger in the "non-resourceful" state and catches it very fast [msg #53].
3. **Release stress through the body when needed.** Massage and sport; this removes the "глухоту" (deafness) and she starts hearing her body again [msg #53].
4. **Prevent hitting zero.** Meditation (almost) every day; tracking the state in which she goes to sleep and wakes up; journaling; working with a coach and a psychologist [msg #53].
5. **Discipline.** She recently gave up alcohol and stopped eating red meat; regular meals, greens and vegetables and no snacking strongly help her stay in a basically good energy state [msg #53].

### Grounding by narrating the present (Alexandra Kalacheva)
Purpose: to ground herself and leave fantasies, anxieties and thoughts about the future. Method: say aloud what is happening to her right now, e.g. "Я сижу, подушка мягкая, у меня побаливает нога, я чувствую как рука держится за ручку, и она шероховатая, я чувствую запах…" (I'm sitting, the cushion is soft, my leg aches a little, I feel my hand holding the handle, it's rough, I smell…). Result: she can notice, for instance, that she is hungry and go eat — shifting focus [msg #85].

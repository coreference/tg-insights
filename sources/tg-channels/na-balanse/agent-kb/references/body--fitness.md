# Fitness independence and sustainable movement

## What this covers

This topic gathers what the channel teaches about keeping movement in your life regardless of gym membership, location, schedule or mood: where long-term motivation comes from, Katya Strelnikova's concept of "fitness independence" and the concrete steps behind it, the honest claim that workouts never become fully automatic, evidence-checked advice on post-workout recovery, the argument that endurance training builds career-relevant traits, and lifelong-fitness principles from Jane Fonda. According to the source it matters because movement is framed as a lifelong part of health and of an active old age — Katya plans to live actively "minimum to 100, better 120" — and because most people's attempts at sport end after a couple of months when built on the wrong goals [msg #42, #229].

## Core ideas

- **Motivation must be long-term and internal.** Motivation borrowed from "this is how it's done", from what others like, or from social-media ideals does not carry you far; the coach advises assuming sport will be part of your life until the end and finding your own reason for it (see *Gavrish's three principles*) [msg #42].
- **The wrong goals explain repeated quitting.** Katya started gyms, yoga, CrossFit, stretching, tennis and badminton hundreds of times and always quit within a couple of months because her goals were appearance, trendiness or external motivation; things changed only when the goal became being "fitness-independent" — doing movement for herself, with pleasure, without coercion [msg #229].
- **Fitness independence = want + know + be able.** Wanting and choosing to move in any circumstances, knowing which kinds of movement fit those circumstances, and having the possibility to do them (see *Definition of fitness independence* and *Steps*) [msg #229, #230].
- **Workouts never fully become automatic — and that is normal.** Because the brain economises energy, resource-intensive activity is never treated as a "great idea"; what gets easier with time is ignoring the self-sabotage, not the effort itself [msg #238, #239].
- **Recovery rests on basics, not gadgets.** Sleep, nutrition, water, protein and rest work better than any expensive procedure; ice baths, cryo chambers and similar are optional bonuses [msg #322].
- **Endurance trains character, not just muscles.** Katya, drawing on the book *Rest* and studies she links, argues that endurance training improves the brain, transfers stress tolerance to life and builds self-belief [msg #477].
- **Health in old age is laid down in youth.** Jane Fonda: in youth we take health for granted, but that is when the foundation is built; keep the body flexible and strong and the heart healthy with cardio [msg #285].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Fitness coach Yuri Gavrish's three principles for starting and not quitting
Purpose: to find motivation that actually lasts, presented in an interview post by Marina Sidorich titled «Годовой план можно сделать только за год» ("A yearly plan can only be done in a year"). The framing: often the hardest part of taking up sport is finding motivation; motivation because "that's the norm" or "others like it" won't take you far, and neither will an inadequate striving for social-media ideals [msg #42].

1. **Accept your body in its current state.** Start with «принятия своего тела в его текущем состоянии» (accepting your body in its current state), understanding your own features and limitations. Why: this creates realistic expectations. Accept that no super-changes in the body will happen within a month — or even a year. The older we are, the more we have to invest for a smaller return. Yet in any case the body and your state will become noticeably better — «И этому надо радоваться!» ("And that is something to be glad about!") [msg #42].
2. **Distribute the load correctly.** «годовой план можно сделать только за год» (a yearly plan can only be done in a year): sport is a gradual increase of load and the organism needs adaptation. A second benefit of going gradually: it lets you exhale and observe the process, which makes it easier to notice what you like most — the club, the trainer, how you feel and your mood, your energy level after workouts — and so what will motivate you most [msg #42].
3. **Define your long-term motivation.** Assume from the start that sport will be part of your life until the end, and ask why. Examples the coach gives: wanting to live longer, actively and with enjoyment; getting rid of bad habits, because sport disciplines; or a concrete passion — running wine marathons in Bordeaux, taking part in amateur padel-tennis or powerlifting competitions [msg #42].

### Definition of fitness independence (three components)
Katya Strelnikova says she coined the term «фитнес-независимой» ("fitness-independent") after noticing that her activity survived constant change of circumstances. Her definition, verbatim: «В любых условиях и обстоятельствах хотеть и выбирать двигаться. Знать, какие варианты движения тебе подходят в этих условиях и обстоятельствах. И иметь возможность их сделать.» (In any conditions and circumstances, to want and choose to move. To know which movement options suit you in these conditions and circumstances. And to have the possibility to do them.) [msg #229]

1. **Wanting and choosing to move** — motivation that is internal and holds in any setting.
2. **Knowing your options** — a repertoire of movement that fits whatever conditions you are in.
3. **Being able to do them** — practical access and capacity.

She explicitly states that this goal "is not about achievements, muscle definition, overcoming, community and fun. It is only about your state and health" — and asks "isn't that the main thing?" She discusses with clients that cultivating fitness independence is one of the most important goals on the path to health and self-care, and stresses that even someone who currently does no workouts at all "can end up where I am" [msg #229].

### Steps to fitness independence (announced as six, seven listed)
Katya's post is titled «Шесть шагов к фитнес-независимости» (Six steps to fitness independence) but lists seven items, the seventh added "on a hint from the comments". These are the steps that helped her get there and help her stay there [msg #230].

1. **Understand why you train.** What training gives you; revise your goals. This refers back to her diagnosis that she used to set the wrong goals (weight loss, splits, a defined body, fashionable sport, community, fun, forgetting herself, external motivation — often subconsciously, "covered with care for health") and replaced them with moving for herself because she needs and enjoys it [msg #230, #229].
2. **Change your perception of what a "workout" is.** For example, a purposeful walk at a confident pace now counts as a workout for her, because it is movement she chooses in order to work her body and improve her state [msg #230].
3. **Keep a repertoire of favourite workouts within easy reach.** Hers: the Nike Training and Nike Running apps and Anastasia Lunegova's workouts; she also regularly tries new options [msg #230].
4. **No attachment to workout duration.** If there are only 5 minutes (or only the desire for 5 minutes), it will be 5 minutes — «а там посмотрим» ("and then we'll see") [msg #230].
5. **No attachment to workout type.** If she feels her body is already "clogged"/tight, she does stretching or yoga instead of strength training, even if strength was planned [msg #230].
6. **No attachment to time of day.** Today she may have time in the morning, tomorrow at lunch, the day after in the evening. When planning a day she always thinks ahead about where movement can be integrated [msg #230].
7. **Emphasis on everyday activity** (added from comments). Wherever possible walk, bend, reach, stand a little longer at the stove or sink — she perceives this not as a chore but as good load for the body [msg #230].

### Recovery methods compared — ice bath, cryo chamber, banya
Expert: Ekaterina Khomenko, movement and nutrition specialist. Context: workouts should give energy, but after a break or when you overdo it, the next morning the body may feel "on strike" and getting out of bed feels like a new workout. She separates what really helps from "a pretty wrapper with doubtful effect" [msg #322].

**Ice bath** — "the hardcore method".
- Protocol: immerse legs or arms in water with 20–40% ice, no longer than 5–7 minutes (unless you are a "walrus", i.e. a winter swimmer); do it within 30 minutes after the workout.
- Pros: tones muscles; reduces inflammation and fatigue; costs zero roubles and is available at home.
- Cons: can cause cramps or hypothermia; immersion above the waist risks the genitourinary system; contraindicated with joint problems; effectiveness not proven ("water conducts cold, but... no miracles").
- Verdict: suits the experienced and careful — "or those who want to impress their household" [msg #322].

**Cryo chamber (криокапсула)**
- Protocol: exposure to cold down to −130° for 2–3 minutes; can be done within 24 hours after the workout.
- Pros: powerful anti-inflammatory effect; works well for immunity; gives a release of endorphins, light euphoria and a "second wind".
- Cons: comparable in effect to an ice bath but many times more expensive; cools worse than water.
- Verdict: an excellent recovery option if you want a feeling of "luxury" and vigour, but it does not replace the basics [msg #322].

**Banya (bathhouse/sauna)** — "beloved classic, with caveats".
- Protocol: if you lost a lot of fluid during the workout, better postpone it; use the banya on a rest day.
- Pros: relaxes; improves microcirculation; helps remove lactic acid.
- Cons: fat is not burned in the banya ("alas"); the main weight loss is water; with heart problems — use caution; "removing toxins" («выведение шлаков») is a myth not supported by science.
- Verdict: excellent for relaxation, but sleep and nutrition influence recovery more [msg #322].

### Simple recovery basics available to everyone
Khomenko's "simple and working methods" [msg #322]:
1. **Hot bath or shower** — add sea salt, magnesium, essential oil; relaxes and improves circulation.
2. **Light exercises the next morning** — bends, rotations, squats; even 5 minutes of movement makes it easier.
3. **Massage or self-massage** — with hands, a roller or a brush; important to direct the movements along the lymph flow.
4. **Quality sleep and nutrition** — "sounds boring, but works better than any expensive spa". Without recovery muscles don't grow; only fatigue accumulates.
5. **Pressure chamber, Indiba, endosphere, cryo — can be added as a bonus.** But first the base: routine, water, protein and rest.

### Soreness rule — when to train and when to rest
- If after intense load there is light pain in the body the next day that passes after a warm-up, you can train, just a bit more gently.
- In other cases you can give the body 2–4 days and everything will pass.
- Don't hurry to spend money on a cryo chamber if you ignore balanced nutrition and sleep 5 hours; start with basic things, and the rest is "by desire, possibilities and... the whim of the soul" [msg #322].

### Four mechanisms by which endurance training is said to help career
Katya Strelnikova's argument, which she frames around running but believes applies to all endurance activity (cycling, swimming, rowing). Trigger: the book *Rest: Why You Get More Done When You Work Less*, which describes how aerobic endurance training affects purposefulness, concentration and even career success; her summary: «выносливость — не только про мышцы, но и про характер» (endurance is not only about muscles but also about character) [msg #477].

1. **Brain renewal via irisin and BDNF.** Harvard scientists (a study she links) found that during prolonged cardio (e.g. running or swimming) muscles produce the protein irisin, which stimulates the brain to produce BDNF, a neuron growth factor. This helps the brain "renew": memory, attention and ability to learn improve. Running turned out to be especially effective — it stimulates neurogenesis, the formation of new nerve cells (second linked study) [msg #477].
2. **Physical endurance trains cognitive endurance.** When you train the body to withstand load, the brain learns to do the same: hold focus, tolerate boredom, not give up at the first resistance. "Roughly speaking, running 10 km and finishing a big project are processes of the same type." Murakami wrote that finishing a book is closer to physical labour than to inspiration, and that he runs marathons because they help him keep concentration and not "fall apart over the distance" [msg #477].
3. **Retuning the nervous system to stress.** Running and other endurance training (linked article) reconfigure the nervous system, teaching the body to withstand stress and recover faster — "a training camp for the psyche". We create controlled short shake-ups; the organism learns to recognise that this is not danger but load, and that it copes. Over time the skill transfers to life: negotiations, deadlines, moves [msg #477].
4. **Endurance builds self-belief.** When we regularly cover a distance, even a short one, the brain records «я могу» ("I can"); this quiet inner "I can" then appears everywhere — at work, in relationships, in life (linked study) [msg #477].

### Jane Fonda's training and ageing principles
Katya rewrote Jane Fonda's W magazine interview (given on the release of the VR program The Routine Reboot Challenge) as if the conversation were with her. Fonda, 80, Oscar winner, is described as the first fitness influencer: her 1982 video cassette "Jane Fonda's Workout" opened home workouts — previously considered a purely male prerogative — to women worldwide and even influenced fashion (the 80s leggings with leg-warmers) [msg #285].
1. **Train daily when at home, alternating load:** upper body one day, lower body the next.
2. **Key word — slower:** she does the same exercises as before, just performs them more slowly.
3. **Favourite cardio — walking;** depending on the weather a treadmill or exercise bike instead.
4. **Age is no barrier to new technology:** «Я думала, что слишком стара, чтобы быть частью виртуальной революции, оказалось это не так.» (I thought I was too old to be part of the virtual revolution; it turned out not to be so.) You don't need to understand technology to enjoy a workout.
5. **Ageing depends on health, built in youth:** when you age, everything depends on how healthy you are; in youth we take health for granted, but that is exactly when the foundation for the future is laid.
6. **Body flexible and strong, heart healthy:** heart health requires cardio.
Her (non-fitness) New Year resolutions: judge less, be kinder and show more empathy [msg #285].

## Detailed insights

### Katya's movement history: activity that survives every change of circumstances
Five years before the post Katya practically did no sport, or did it unsystematically. Four years before she started running regularly. Two years before, her activity was (almost) daily surfing and regular home yoga. Three months before it was tied to a fitness club (strength training and group yoga). Then she lived "literally in the forest"; now she has moved back to a city without knowing where she'll settle in the next 3–6 months. Her observation: it seems there is less activity, but it hasn't disappeared — she still walks and runs, does short yoga and strength sessions of 10–30 minutes. This persistence across changing settings is what she named fitness independence [msg #229].

### The turning point: "what if I force nothing and just enjoy it?"
In quarantine in Jakarta in early 2021 she realised she no longer wanted to depend on anything or anyone, and wanted to simply do something — even a little — for herself because she needs and likes it. Her question: «А что если ничего не заставлять себя делать, а просто получать удовольствие? Что из этого выйдет?» (What if I don't force myself to do anything and just enjoy it? What will come of it?). 3.5 years later she has done 800 workouts in the Nike apps alone — about 229 training days a year and about 19 days a month, "only in two free apps" [msg #229].

Her current state: no more doubts or resistance — «Я просто делаю то, что хочу, когда хочу. И могу.» (I just do what I want, when I want. And I can.) And she can almost every day because after any workout she feels great — morally (satisfied and proud of herself) and physically (a bright surge of energy and good mood). A second reason: she plans to live actively at least to 100, better 120; if she already "creaks like a worn saddle" and doesn't get off the couch, the probability of a magnificent old age "somewhere in the Alps with poles in hand" tends to zero [msg #229].

### Why workouts never become automatic
Katya wrote this to correct the impression that after becoming fitness-independent training is "hyper-easy" for her. Her thesis: «некоторые. привычки. никогда. не станут. автоматическими.» (some. habits. will never. become. automatic.) — "a scary thing, especially from a coach" [msg #238].
- She pushes back on the popular claim that more than 40% of behaviour is automatic and that practically any habit can be taken into or out of autopilot ("if it hasn't happened to you, you just don't know how to cook them"). "Not so fast": habits that require serious resources don't always fit that category [msg #238].
- Mechanism (her logic): on one hand, the brain is geared to save energy — that explains automatisms. On the other hand, the brain is geared to save energy — so any resource-intensive activity that doesn't help survival in the moment or reproduction won't be considered a great idea. Translation: workouts will most likely forever stay in the category «надо бы немножко поднапрячься» ("I should strain a little"), especially in low-energy situations [msg #238].
- Case: her mother, 71, has always more or less exercised (morning exercises, skiing) yet constantly says how hard it sometimes is to push herself out to the pool, to Pilates, even to do exercises at home. Katya, 38, regularly training for several years, catches herself doing the same [msg #238].
- What does get easier: effort isn't needed every single time, and what becomes easier is «уговорить себя не реагировать на этот предательский самосаботаж» (persuading yourself not to react to this treacherous self-sabotage) [msg #238].
- Lesson: if you've trained for "…teen months or years" and still constantly have to make an effort, nothing is wrong with you — «Вы просто человек.» (You are just human.) Supporting example: in a podcast episode on controlling the inner voice, Andrew Huberman admits he too regularly has to motivate himself, though he has done sport since 16 [msg #238].
- A forwarded subscriber reply confirms it: her colleague, a lifelong athletic "jock" who runs 1–2 times a day, honestly says that every time he feels lazy and has to force himself, and the main thing is to start the workout. She (running slower than toddlers learning to walk, a kilometre at most, which is a huge win for her) says the thought helps her understand that it is normal, everyone has it, they somehow cope, and she can too [msg #239].

### Running and Katya's own trajectory
Katya was prompted by Murakami's story — he became a writer only at 33 after owning a bar and attributes this to running. People are surprised by her systematicness, resilience and ability to "hit one spot no matter what"; her mother called her "so stubborn" — and she notes it wasn't always so. In 2020, at the peak of an existential crisis, she started running regularly; in 2021 she understood what she actually wants — "coincidence?" She hedges: her changes started before running became constant, but whether they'd have happened at this scale is "an open question". She suggests she keeps going, not giving up, changing her life again and again and believing she can succeed in a new profession on her own terms perhaps because every time, even when she doesn't want to or seems to have no strength, she takes one more step and a small effort over herself [msg #477].

### Lunegova's program and the reality behind a fitness business
Katya has trained with Anastasia Lunegova since March 2021, when she decided in Jakarta to become fitness-independent and, through a friend, found Lunegova's "Здоровое тело" (Healthy Body) program; she has since completed it twice more and is now in the club "Легкость и сила" (Lightness and Strength). Her verdict: she grunts, puffs and curses Nastya, «Но, блин, после этих тренировок реально чувствую себя супер.» (But, damn, after these workouts I really feel super.) [msg #470]

She listened to the PR-Агент Купер podcast episode "Fitness and business: what's behind building an online sports product", where Lunegova — founder of a large online platform with a team of 20+ — speaks not in the glossy language of "successful success" but honestly about strategy, mistakes, system and team, and about combining the work of trainer and entrepreneur without giving up on life. Katya says the episode "sobered and invigorated" her, and names five things she found useful to learn from it:
- what stays behind the scenes of "healthy" online products — her one-word answer: a lot of work («очень много работы»);
- how not to lose the balance between "I'm a trainer" and "I'm an entrepreneur";
- how to build a team that holds quality;
- which strategies work now and which don't;
- how to stay in contact with a huge community.
Her takeaway: it is important to have examples of honest, sustainable business nearby rather than magical "million-rouble launches" [msg #470].

# Burnout: prevention and exit

## What this covers

This topic gathers everything the channel taught about burnout: what it is made of (four kinds of energy, a finite resource), how to measure how close you are (a 16-question test), the red flags and their counter-moves, why passion for work is a common entry route, how a "get sick to rest" fantasy is a symptom, and how Katya and guest experts exited burnout and prevent it now. According to the source it matters because, as with any "illness", preventing burnout is easier than getting out of it — Katya needed to quit her job and spend more than two years restoring her health and psyche [msg #127].

## Core ideas

- **Energy is a finite resource, not a bottomless well.** One's state and energy must be replenished regularly so they do not run out [msg #127].
- **There are four kinds of energy, not one** — physical, emotional, intellectual and spiritual — and all of them have to be watched; physical is the base (see *Four kinds of energy*) [msg #127].
- **Burnout = prolonged deprivation of all kinds of energy; the antidote = regaining the ability to feel.** If I understand what I want, what is happening to me and why, I will take care of myself (see *Recipe vs antidote*) [msg #127].
- **Prevention is easier than exit; exit is only "through".** Once burnout has happened, the only way is through it, in small, patient steps; if the test says you are scorching, start now and seek a specialist if you can't [msg #127].
- **Passion is a frequent entry route.** Burnout often starts with a crazy love for one's work; what looks like "high engagement" is often already a signal of lost balance, and unmanaged enthusiasm turns work into a form of addiction (Olga Sorina) [msg #451].
- **Rest fantasies are symptoms.** "It would be nice to get sick so I can rest" — and even a romantic thought about a vacation — signals exhaustion that likely started much earlier [msg #236].
- **Boundaries, self-knowledge and therapy are the recurring levers** in both guest stories and Katya's own experience [msg #207, #377].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Four kinds of energy
Purpose: a model for what "energy" consists of, used to diagnose where you are depleted. Katya and Masha Tsegelnik presented it at the stream "how not to burn out" (the stream announcement promised to discuss what energy consists of and how to replenish it, to share personal experience, and how to take care of yourself so burnout doesn't happen) [msg #125, #127].
1. **Physical energy** — sleep, nutrition, movement. It is the *base*: without normal sleep, food and movement it will be hard to restore the other spheres, "because on a chemical level we won't be able to" [msg #127].
2. **Emotional energy** — irritation/anxiety levels, time with loved ones, enjoyable activities, noticing one's own merits [img #127].
3. **Intellectual energy** — focus, strategic vs "firefighting" work, time for reflection, planning and creativity, not working evenings/weekends [img #127].
4. **Spiritual energy** — spending time and energy on what you do best, love most and consider truly important; deciding from your own goals rather than demands; positively influencing the world [img #127].
Katya adds a direction-of-travel framing: through our actions, habits and deeds we are always moving somewhere — either toward premature death or toward flourishing: «Решать вам.» ("It's up to you.") [msg #127].

### Burnout proximity test ("Тест: Насколько близко выгорание?")
Purpose: check your "level of scorching". Made by the authors of "На балансе", based on the Harvard Business Review article by Tony Schwartz and Catherine McCarthy. How to use: answer 16 yes/no statements, count the "yes" answers, look at the result, then see which sphere sags the most **and start with it** [img #127].

Physical energy [img #127]:
1. I regularly don't sleep 7–8 hours and often wake up tired.
2. I often skip breakfast or make do with whatever is at hand.
3. There is little movement in my life.
4. I rarely take breaks from work and often eat lunch at the computer.

Emotional energy [img #127]:
5. I often feel irritation and anxiety («Я часто чувствую раздражение и тревогу»).
6. I don't have enough time for family and loved ones, and when I'm with them I'm often distracted.
7. I have no time for activities that bring me pleasure.
8. I rarely notice my achievements and feel pride and gratitude.

Intellectual energy [img #127]:
9. It's hard for me to concentrate on one thing; I'm easily distracted.
10. Most of the day I "put out fires" instead of doing something strategically important.
11. I don't devote enough time to reflection, strategic planning and creativity.
12. I work evenings/weekends and often check email on vacation.

Spiritual energy [img #127]:
13. I don't devote enough time to what I do best and what I love most.
14. I don't spend most of my time and energy on what I consider truly important.
15. My decisions are driven more by demands than by understanding my own goals.
16. I make no effort to positively influence the world and the people around me.

Scoring — number of "yes" answers [img #127]:
- **0–3** — you are in excellent condition.
- **4–6** — you are still resourced, but there is already something to think about.
- **7–10** — you clearly have a resource deficit; burnout is close.
- **11–16** — "Oops, it seems you are already in crisis."

What to do with the result: if you took the test and realised you are scorching, don't wait — start right now. Katya says she has no doubt you know what to do; if you don't know, can't, doubt or don't believe in yourself, go to a specialist: «Не затягивайте, умоляю.» ("Don't put it off, I beg you.") [msg #127].

### Recipe for burnout vs antidote to burnout
The core theses from the stream with Masha Tsegelnik [msg #127]:
- **Recipe:** arrange for yourself, for a prolonged time, deprivation across all kinds of energy. Katya adds: «я точно знаю, я так делала» ("I know for sure, I did it that way").
- **Antidote:** learn to feel, return to yourself the capacity to sense (чувствование), find time for reflection. The mechanism: «Если я понимаю, что я хочу, что со мной происходит и почему это так, я буду заботиться о себе.» ("If I understand what I want, what is happening to me and why, I will take care of myself.")
- **Pauses as the delivery mechanism:** life and the calendar need pauses — unfilled moments with free attention; these are exactly the opportunity to feel.
- **If burnout has already happened:** the only way is "through" (Katya uses the English word, "as we say here in the US") — in small steps, patiently.
- **Main advice:** believe that your life can be different, even if you are currently turned 180 degrees away from routines and practices of healthy energy, and imagine that life.

### Red flags of burnout and what to do instead (Mila Plyushch)
Guest author Mila Plyushch (CMO at Yandex, author of the blog "Невыгоревшие") lists the red flags she failed to notice for too long before burning out, each paired with what she does now [msg #207]:
1. **Red flag: thinking only about work**, at any hour of day and night. It still happens, but when it drags on for months or years — «привет, уголек» ("hello, ember"). **What she does:** techniques like in meditation — when she catches herself chewing "work mental gum" after hours, she calmly stops it without judgment, telling herself the thought can wait for working hours: «наш блестящий ум никуда не денется» ("our brilliant mind won't go anywhere") and will produce an equally good solution as the one it generated "in the toilet at 3:36 a.m." [msg #207].
2. **Red flag: constant overwork.** It usually creeps in gradually: working hours drift a bit into the evening; "I'll do an important presentation on the weekend"; "I'll answer a couple of urgent messages on vacation" — and that becomes the norm. **What she does:** sets boundaries — both for herself and for other people. Declining an after-hours meeting (especially with the boss) used to seem wild; now she has the skill: «Извини, в 20 не могу - давай назначим в рабочее время, пожалуйста» ("Sorry, I can't at 8 pm — let's schedule it during working hours, please"). She still catches herself to avoid explaining reasons ("I need to pick up my son"), because a valid reason can even be wanting to lie on the couch and stare at the ceiling: «Нерабочее время нам на то и дано, чтобы не работать.» ("Non-working time is given to us precisely so as not to work.") [msg #207].
3. **Red flag: tolerating what she didn't like** — accepting toxic self-talk and a team culture of relationships and communication alien to her as a given; crying in the toilet after meetings and scolding herself for being so sensitive. **What she does:** gets to know herself — what categorically doesn't suit her versus what she can change her attitude to — and takes back responsibility for choices: she consciously chooses whether to stay or leave. This is far calmer than living in the state of «оно само со мной так происходит, а я просто страдаю тут бедная» ("it just happens to me on its own, and I'm just poor me suffering here") [msg #207].
4. **Red flag: ignoring her needs** — going to work sick, not noticing she had needed the toilet for three meetings in a row or had skipped two meals; work was more important than everything, even the need for rest. **What she does:** studies herself and now knows when she needs rest (or lunch, or the toilet). At the point of full burnout only a **4-month sabbatical** (prolonged rest without work) helped, but when you monitor yourself regularly, recovery is easier [msg #207].

Mila's key caveat: many of these counter-moves would not have been possible without **regular psychotherapy**, which helped her find inner supports, feel out her boundaries and learn to defend them — she "won't tire of recommending" it [msg #207].

### Balancing enthusiasm and burnout (Olga Sorina)
Context: Katya says burnout often begins with crazy love for work and that this was likely her case — when she joined advertising after reading Beigbeder, she was so gripped by excitement that she wanted to work all the time, answer emails until 1 a.m., skip lunches, weekends and vacations: «Так я начала брать силы в долг сама у себя.» ("That's how I started borrowing energy from myself on credit.") [msg #451].

Olga Sorina's mechanism: the paradox is that what looks like "high engagement" is often already a signal of losing balance; unmanaged enthusiasm makes work a form of addiction (she says WHO is seriously considering workaholism on a par with other addictions). You do something useful, get recognition, feel meaning — but gradually stop breathing, eating, resting, living outside work. This happens because work has everything needed: a goal, a result, feedback — a clear scheme for getting joy and satisfaction. Life outside work lacks such clear confirmations of being needed and such vivid feedback; we simply live with other people who have their own wants, needs and demands of us. In life you must invent for yourself why you do something, where to find joy, and how to feel "good enough" without external evaluation and without always being useful to others [msg #451].

What helps keep the balance [msg #451]:
1. **Track the moment engagement becomes unmanageable.** If it is hard to tear yourself away, that is not a sign of passion but a warning bell. Trying to stop triggers rationalisations: "if I finish now, tomorrow will be easier", "people will look at me strangely if I drop it unfinished", "if I keep indulging myself and resting a lot, things will stall", "you're not that tired, work is more important". All are ways of convincing yourself to continue, built on fragile grounds and ignoring long-term consequences. Remedies: ask loved ones to help you finish on time, or schedule enjoyable fixed-time activities right after work — theatre, meeting friends, a workout.
2. **Create interest outside work.** Not necessarily a hobby — anything that also engages and gives a sense of aliveness, perhaps relationships with friends and loved ones. Ask yourself where your joy is and whether you feel satisfaction and interest in your life as a whole. The questions are hard, but without answers you cannot rebuild the work situation in the long term.
3. **Learn to be "useless".** Sometimes it is important to "be" rather than "do"; allowing yourself uselessness is a form of self-care. Many treat themselves as a function, so when they aren't performing a role they immediately feel wasted time, loneliness, meaninglessness. Try "useless" time doing what you like even without much sense in it — at least 5–10 minutes a day.

Her definition: «Баланс — когда мы вовлечены, но не растворяемся в работе.» ("Balance is when we are engaged but don't dissolve in work.") Balance is not rigidly allotted time; we can never build a static system. Those prone to over-engagement must constantly watch that they don't drown in work emotionally and physically; the boundaries between work and personal life will always shift one way or the other, and the task is to stay attentive to them — «Быть включенными в работу, но при этом живыми» ("To be switched on at work, yet alive") [msg #451].

### Katya's energy experiments to avoid burning out (March 2025)
Context: after her energy diary showed her energy that year was "so-so", Katya took steps to feel better — "and it seems I even do" [msg #299]:
1. **Changing sleep habits — successfully.** Her latest realisation: her sleep problems are largely in her head, so she is now rebuilding her beliefs about sleep, not only changing external habits [msg #299].
2. **Getting up 30 minutes earlier** to plan the day and do strategic tasks before meetings start. She noticed planning gives her energy and makes her feel more conscious and fulfilled. It was hard for about 2 weeks during the switch; now "super" [msg #299].
3. **Planning less.** She no longer tries to squeeze anything between meetings. She does less, but doesn't lose energy on switching or the feeling "I can't get anything done" [msg #299].
4. **3–5 minutes of sensory deprivation before/after client meetings.** She feels it reboots her. Additionally she goes to a sensory-deprivation capsule twice a month for an hour-long floating session [msg #299].
5. **Putting gadgets away when there are many meetings (especially back to back).** On low energy she badly wants to grab the phone or turn on a podcast — which exhausts her even more. By willpower she takes herself for a walk without headphones and phone; around minute 10 it becomes very good, and by the end she feels boundless gratitude to herself and wants to live, even if before it seemed life was no picnic [msg #299].
6. **Testing the "basic rest formula"** (1 day a week, 3 days a month, a week each quarter). Finding: "1 day a week" doesn't suit her — she needs 2 full days off; "3 days a month" is her option (tested in February, will do monthly); she has scheduled "a week per quarter" and wrote the post from her first such vacation [msg #299].
7. **Monday as an [almost] meeting-free day.** Without a day set aside for creativity, strategy and admin, none of it happens given her number of calls and the (lack of) energy after them. A couple of 30-minute meetings remain; she will not add new ones [msg #299].
8. **Not reading comments, let alone replying.** You can drain a bucket of energy there. She also started an "Instagram-check diary" — now she hardly wants to go there, only reaching for it at very low energy [msg #299].
9. **Not seeking, watching or reading new content until she has read the old.** Consuming new content eats energy [msg #299].
10. **Sunday as a day without plans.** Previously she allowed herself to start the morning with a series, stay in bed two hours or stare at the ceiling only once she had driven herself to the point she couldn't get up. Now she does it every Sunday: nothing planned for the first half of the day, "go with the flow". «От планов и решений тоже нужно отдыхать.» ("You also need rest from plans and decisions.") [msg #299].
11. **Testing new day "repertoires".** Her habitual routine of "afternoon magic" (a couple of hours of rest after lunch, then working 15:00–18:00 with fresh energy) proved not ideal — rest often killed her desire to continue. New variant: work the same 2–3 hours right after lunch and put all rest afterwards. It turns out productive and she isn't more tired; she will keep observing [msg #299].

Main conclusion: she "touched her boundaries in terms of workload" and realised she does not want to live with that many meetings [msg #299].

### Two questions for exiting a meaning crisis
Katya's account of what got her out of the burnout and existential crisis of 2018 and helped her "reinvent herself" — turning habits around 180°, changing profession and country [msg #377]:
- A meaning crisis needs two things: (1) noticing what is going on in your head, and (2) understanding where it came from and why it is not the truth — otherwise how do you allow yourself to change it. A third: concrete steps toward transformation. **Therapy** helps with the third; **contemplative practices and philosophy** with the first two. This combination pulled her out.
- At her "point of no return", what was important yesterday abruptly lost weight while new meanings hadn't yet appeared. Two questions started pulling her out: «Что у меня в голове прямо сейчас?» ("What's in my head right now?") — the domain of mindfulness practices; and «Откуда берутся мои мысли?» ("Where do my thoughts come from?") — the domain of philosophy. Together they helped rebuild her meaning apparatus so as to «снова хотеть жить свою жизнь, а не жизнь по инструкции» ("want to live my own life again, not a life by the instructions").
- **Lenses** (an idea from the book she is reading, «Перевал в середине пути» — "The Middle Passage"): heredity, sex, culture and family environment are lenses through which we look; in the first part of life (some people until the end) we live in a reality formed by these lenses rather than from our own essence. If meaning is lost, the first step is to acknowledge the limitations of these lenses, since a crisis is often about a divergence between true nature and instilled culture.
- Transformation is not an event but a process — in her case a way of existing. She still needs to understand how she thinks, what limits her, where she sets her own frames: «Без этого развитие не едет.» ("Without this, development doesn't move.")
- What developing "philosophical intellect" gives, per Katya: the ability to see your own premises, tolerate uncertainty, untangle the mess of «надо/принято/ожидают» ("must/customary/expected") so as to choose your own living «хочу/важно/верю» ("want/important/believe") — an upgrade in depth without which surface changes may not stick. She also states that this type of growth always precedes financial growth.

## Detailed insights

### "Get sick to rest" as a symptom
Katya regularly hears clients say: «Иногда я думаю, что было бы классно заболеть, чтобы немножко отдохнуть» ("Sometimes I think it would be great to get sick so I could rest a little"). She naively thought she was no longer in that league — then on the 6th day of bed rest realised that a few days before falling ill she had been thinking exactly about rest, not in that wording but "definitely 'it'" [msg #236].

The sequence she reconstructs: early the previous week she fantasised about Christmas holidays and a vacation; she already felt overheated, and (crediting herself — she notices this much earlier than before) decided to "ease off the horses" and make Thursday and Friday short days. She did so with variable success: in the freed-up time she did not really rest but «набивала себя новым опытом и впечатлениями» ("stuffed myself with new experiences and impressions"). Now she has been "resting" for almost a week — though, she notes, rest would go better in a healthy state [msg #236].

Lessons she draws [msg #236]:
- The romantic thought about a vacation was a symptom — «Я задолбалась» ("I'm fed up / worn out"). Symptoms most likely appeared much earlier, and the thought may have lived in her head long before it became intrusive enough to notice.
- Filling freed time with new experiences is not rest.
- Doing reasonably well at work/life balance is not the same as work/rest balance, at which she still does poorly.
- Her response: make "rest research" one of her themes for 2025 — learn to hear herself and the symptoms in time, understand what kind of rest is needed and when, and what restores and fills her.

### Katya's burnout history
- Katya's past burnout required her to «уволиться с работы и больше двух лет восстанавливать здоровье и психику» ("quit my job and spend more than two years restoring my health and psyche"); she literally couldn't do or even want anything. Her point to readers: you can simply not let it get that far by starting to change your life little by little right now [msg #127]. In the stream announcement she describes it as having to "pump herself back" for two years [msg #125].
- She dates the burnout and crisis to 2018, and the exit involved changing habits, profession and country [msg #377].
- Its origin, in her telling, was passion for advertising work after reading Beigbeder (see *Balancing enthusiasm and burnout*) [msg #451].
- She herself, as a burned-out person, took a course with career guide Olga Polishchuk ("and you see what became of me") [msg #255].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The test is the channel's own adaptation of an HBR article, not a validated clinical instrument; the source gives no validation data [img #127].
- The WHO statement about workaholism is Olga Sorina's claim; no source is given [msg #451].
- Duration of recovery varies across stories: Katya needed more than two years (and quitting her job); Mila needed a 4-month sabbatical; Olga Polishchuk's framing mentions "half a year" of rest as the typical assumption [msg #127, #207, #255].
- Katya's experiments are personal n=1 observations still in progress ("I'll keep observing"); "1 day a week" of the basic rest formula did not fit her [msg #299].
- Several recommendations (Olga Polishchuk's workshop, Humanity course, Olga Sorina's group) came with commercial promotion; in #377 Katya is a paying participant of the course she recommends [msg #255, #377, #451].

## Coaching practice and client stories

### Core ideas

- Burnout recovery can become a profession. Katya calls herself a burnout veteran who now professionally helps others not get there; after two years of recovery she changed field after 35 [#263]. Detail in *Career change story*.
- Balance is internal, not an equal split of spheres. Keeping an artificial "equality" of life areas is not the point; finding your own rhythm that supports your inner state is. From the outside a balanced life may even look unbalanced [#263].
- Physical resource comes before growth. Growth requires both resource and a growth mindset, but physical strength is first; people with low energy or in burnout say they can no longer dream [#263].
- Same desire, different paths. Like Tolstoy's happy families, clients phrase problems differently but share one desire — to be satisfied with themselves, their energy and life — yet there are no universal recipes; each has their own optimal point of application of force [#538].
- Goals are researched before they are acted on. In Katya's contracts each goal is first turned into an exploration of the client's own patterns, and only then into concrete steps and implementation [#538, #539, #540].
- Fixing health often transforms other areas. Clients frequently say that something fixed in the health sphere transformed other spheres of life [#538].

- Stable energy is the goal, not peak energy. The case ends with "ожидания ниже, энергия выше" ("expectations lower, energy higher"): the state evened out "без эйфории и без провалов" ("without euphoria and without dips") [#735].
- You need a map of "good" before you can move toward it. Defining in the client's own words what a good and a not-good state are comes first; "Без такой карты непонятно, куда идти" ("Without such a map it's unclear where to go") [#735].
- Tracking is a training tool, not a supervisor. The energy diary exists to build the habit of noticing; once the habit exists, daily entries are no longer needed [#735].
- Energy can be physiological, not a matter of willpower. "Иногда энергия — это не про силу воли, а про ферритин" ("Sometimes energy is not about willpower but about ferritin") [#735].
- Experiments instead of rules. Behaviour changes are framed as tests whose effect the client observes, not as commandments [#735].
- The main result is calm understanding, not new habits. Katya stresses that the key outcome was peace that comes from understanding cycles, stages and changing meanings [#735].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Coaching contract goal structure (Goal → Research → Concrete steps → Implementation)
Purpose: Katya says she always concludes a contract with each individual client ("мы всегда заключаем контракт"); the goal is written down at the start, in the client's words, and is turned into a research plan before any action is prescribed [#538]. The ten verbatim client goals she shared (transcribed in full below as examples A–J) all use the same numbered section 4.2 of the contract, with these four parts:

1. 4.2. Цель (Goal). One or two sentences in the client's own terms of what they want — often an outcome plus a felt state (e.g. "научиться отдыхать", learn to rest; "чувствовать, что живу", feel that I am alive). The goal describes a state or capability, not a metric [#538, #539, #541].
2. 4.2.1. Исследование (Research). A lettered list (a, b, c…) of questions the client will investigate about themselves: what the topic means for them personally, what currently helps and hinders, which attitudes, habits and beliefs interfere or support, what fills and what drains energy, the current situation versus an optimal picture. This step embodies the "no universal recipe" principle: the plan is derived from the client's own data [#538, #539, #543].
3. 4.2.2. Выработка конкретных шагов (Working out concrete steps) "в соответствии с п 4.2.1" — steps and plans are built on the basis of the research findings. In one contract this is expanded into a plan of action: which habits and actions to eradicate and which to introduce, and what resources are needed to reach the goal [#540, #547].
4. 4.2.3. Внедрение изменений и шагов (Implementing the changes and steps) in accordance with 4.2.2 — the client puts the steps into practice [#538, #546].

Note: the "clarity" contract card shows only the goal and the research block; steps 4.2.2–4.2.3 are not shown on that card [#545].

#### Five ways clients formulate goals
Katya groups the year's goals (about 40 individual clients) by how they sound; some seem very down-to-earth and pragmatic, others "philosophically far out" — and she explicitly invites the latter too ("с такими тоже приходите") [#538].
1. Rationally and clearly — "I want a system of nutrition / movement / health that will support me"; or find the resource and time to do what I want and what matters to me; or learn to structure the day — so as not to keep endless questions about body, food, health, weight and fears in one's head [#538].
2. Exploratively — figure out where energy goes and what it really depends on; what works and doesn't work for me; what prevents a harmonious relationship with coffee, sweets, the smartphone [#538].
3. Philosophically — what does it mean to feel that I live? Live beautifully and consciously. Or explore "clarity" to get a clear vision of oneself [#538].
4. Psychologically — regain control over one's life, or acquire inner supports [#538].
5. From exhaustion — then the goal sounds like: regain energy for life during the day, because right now "я труп, ничего не могу" ("I'm a corpse, I can't do anything") [#538].
What unites them all: everyone wanted to feel better, and believed it was possible [#538].

#### Early burnout signs progression
Katya's description (interview) of how burnout creeps in unnoticed, in roughly this order [#263]:
1. At first it seems you are just tired; getting up in the morning is very hard.
2. Then you don't want to go to work and can't remember when anything there last interested you.
3. More and more "не хочу", "не могу" ("I don't want", "I can't") and irritation at colleagues and meetings appear.
4. Concentration becomes something "из Красной книги" (from the Red Book, i.e. an endangered species), while procrastination flourishes.
5. Even favourite activities stop bringing joy — not only at work.
6. It becomes harder and harder to dream and to want anything.
7. Instead there is often an urge to forget oneself with a glass of wine and to "treat yourself".
The key, she says, is to notice that something has changed: ask "Do you feel now the way you used to?" If not, it is worth investigating [#263].

#### Two components of growth
Asked for advice to everyone who wants growth, Katya says movement requires two components: resource and growth mindset ("ресурс и мышление роста"). Physical strength still comes first. Her observation: people with low energy or in burnout say, without exception, that they can no longer dream. Her recommendation: let yourself rest, recover, fill yourself up — and then growth will certainly happen [#263].

#### Ira's case structure (Given → Goal → What we did → Result)
Katya presents the case in four blocks; the same blocks are a template for reading her coaching work.

1. Дано (Given) — the starting point. Ira came with a request she herself called vague: "Как было — больше не могу и не хочу, а как нужно — еще не знаю" ("The way it was — I can no longer and don't want to; how it should be — I don't know yet"). Background: designer, many years in consulting, emigration; endless business trips and projects where it was impossible not to tear oneself apart ("не получается не порваться"); burnout, burnout leave, redundancy, a close person's serious illness, a new job. Important: she did not start from a blank page. Over a couple of years she had already done a lot — tennis, dancing, functional training, an hour before sleep with nothing to do, work with a psychologist. Yet energy "жила своей жизнью" ("lived its own life"), from an almost manic high to a complete down. Thoughts swung the same way, from "go to the sea and work as a waitress" to "but what about the career, why did I invest in all of it". On top came habitual beliefs: "Должна ходить, мочить всех и фонтанировать" ("I must walk around, crush everyone and gush with energy"), "Как будто я недостаточно сделала за день" ("As if I haven't done enough in a day"), "Уже разогналась — не буду же заканчивать" ("I've already picked up speed — I'm not going to stop now") [#735]. Lesson implied by the case: good habits (sport, psychologist, wind-down hour) were already in place and still did not stabilise energy — the missing piece lay elsewhere.
2. Goal. Formulated together: to carry out a research of what to do and not do to feel stably good, and to learn to notice grey flags rather than red flags, "когда уже поздно" ("when it is already too late"). (The full contract wording is example F in the topic file.) [#735]
3. What we did — six methods, each written out below as its own framework, in the order given: map of good/not good; energy diary; discovering chronic under-recovery; experiments; four kinds of energy; grey-flag practice. The post marks its list of methods as unfinished with a trailing ellipsis bullet; the remaining methods are not disclosed [#735].
4. Result — written out under *Outcomes of the case* in Detailed insights [#735].

#### Map of "good" and "not good" states
Purpose: the first step of the work — describe what "good" and "not good" look like specifically for this client, so that there is a direction and a reference for noticing deviations. Katya: without such a map it is unclear where to go [#735].
- Ira's "good": waking up with the desire to live and create, falling asleep without torment, and if tired then it is a pleasant tiredness [#735].
- Ira's "not good": getting irritated at people, wanting TV series and sweets, feeling "socially drained" [#735].
The descriptions are concrete, observable behaviours and sensations, not abstract ratings — which is what makes them usable as early markers later [#735].

#### Energy diary
Purpose: find out what one's energy actually depends on, and build the habit of noticing.
1. Base entry: three times a day note the colour of the "батарейка" (battery) [#735].
2. Add context variables later: sleep, day of the menstrual cycle, day of the week were added afterwards [#735].
3. Review together: coach and client went through the diary jointly and looked for dependencies [#735].
4. Stop when the habit forms: "Дневник — не надзиратель, а способ развить привычку замечать" ("The diary is not a supervisor but a way to develop the habit of noticing"). Once the habit appeared, daily entries became unnecessary [#735].
Result in this case: the diary highlighted chronic under-recovery (next section). One of the client's image cards reframes the colour scale itself: perhaps yellow is her green — it is impossible to be at peak energy all the time; it is more like surfing [#735].

#### Discovering chronic under-recovery (iron)
The diary showed chronic under-recovery. Ira had known before about her low iron and had periodically dealt with it, but now she saw how strongly it affects her state. Katya's conclusion: sometimes energy is not about willpower but about ferritin. After the work Ira knows that her energy depends on iron, sleep and the cycle, and she keeps an eye on "the base" ("следит за базой") [#735]. Note: the source gives no lab values, doses or treatment details — only the recognition of the link [#735].

#### Experiments instead of rules
Instead of rules, they set experiments — changes whose effect Ira could observe. The examples given:
1. Go to bed at 10 o'clock five days out of seven (not every day) [#735].
2. Watch how alcohol affects sleep, and move drinking to the daytime — Katya reminds readers she usually recommends drinking in the morning/earlier in the day rather than in the evening ("обычно рекомендую выпить с утра") [#735].
3. Do not take the phone to bed [#735].
4. Board games instead of social media [#735].
5. A morning walk with the dog [#735].
The source closes the list with "И т.п." (and the like) — (!) INCOMPLETE: further experiments are not named [#735]. The outcome on alcohol: Ira now knows how it affects her, which Katya notes "было неприятно признавать" ("was unpleasant to admit") [#735].

#### Four kinds of energy
They worked through four kinds of energy. Named or implied in the post: physical, emotional, social and meaning-based (смысловой опоры, the support of meaning). Two lessons Ira drew [#735]:
- One can have physical strength yet have no emotional or social resource at all.
- Physical, emotional and social energy may all seemingly be there, but without a meaning-based support everything starts to sag.
(!) INCOMPLETE: the post does not define each kind further or describe how to restore each one; the fourth is named only as "смысловой опоры" [#735].

#### Grey-flag practice
Learning to notice grey flags meant three moves [#735]:
1. Feel it in advance — sense the decline before it becomes acute.
2. Say to yourself "на сегодня well enough" ("well enough for today") — stop for the day.
3. Lower the load tomorrow — plan a lighter next day.
Plus a rule: do not take on extra at a peak ("Не набирать лишнего на пике") — the high phase is exactly when over-commitment happens, which is what produced the swings to a down [#735]. The client's card gives a concrete trade-off example: if she went to a party today, tomorrow she won't write the year's summary [#735]. This applies the *Grey flags vs red flags of energy* idea in the topic file.

### Detailed insights

#### Career change story
Katya worked in marketing for many years and changed profession about three years before the January 2025 interview; by then she had done 700+ hours of consultations and helped 100+ clients, according to the interviewing channel [#263]. After leaving full-time work she recovered for two years: morally there was total emptiness and loss of meaning, and physically she was literally suffocating ("буквально задыхалась"). The hardest part was to believe that "this too shall pass" ("пройдёт и это"), not give up and keep "digging". A couple of years of therapy, courses on reinventing herself and mindfulness practices led her to believe that changing field after 35 is not such a bad idea. She admitted to herself that she loves learning, discovering and experimenting — preferably with topics applied to her own life: health, recovery, energy, self-acceptance — and dared to ask: what if someone paid for all this? She then learned about the health and wellness coach profession, talked to people in it, chose training and found first clients [#263].

#### Balance: research findings and personal experiment
- Katya ran a study of balance among women leaders. Main conclusion: before she started talking about balance she held an illusion of some evenness and of being able to combine everything "correctly". The conversations showed that what matters is not holding an artificial "equality" of spheres but finding one's own rhythm that supports the inner state; balance and harmony are about what is inside, while from outside it may even look like imbalance [#263].
- Personal experiment: after burnout she pursued balance as fiercely and self-sacrificingly as she once worked. Before New Year she counted how much time went to work versus "everything else" — it turned out 50/50. Yet external balance turned out not to be what she needed for inner harmony. She describes herself as an ambitious person who was once simply very tired; her current goal is not to keep a formal balance but to (ecologically) step out of it — to test "non-linearity", finding a way to invest more in development and achievement without harming her physical state [#263].

#### No universal recipe: the point of application of force
With each new client Katya sees more clearly that although everyone strives for the same thing, each has their own path and there are no universal recipes. At any given moment each person has their own optimal point of application of force — sleep, food, movement, rhythm, relationship with oneself or with others, permission to rest, getting out of the endless "надо" ("must"). If you hit exactly that point, almost everything else becomes easier; clients often say that something fixed in health transformed other spheres too [#538].

#### Client case: Natasha Gulyaeva's testimonial
Natasha Gulyaeva, a kundalini guide and Katya's client, wrote that she always thought she was someone who likes to cope alone, then realised there is no need to do it "alone" when there are great specialists-guides who can make the path more pleasant, fun and even faster. What Katya helped her with [#594]:
- she allowed herself to choose and move at her own pace;
- she put her state first (sometimes that means cancelling, rescheduling, going away to rest);
- she understood that without recovery "further" simply doesn't work;
- she saw work as a system rather than an eternal survival marathon;
- more clarity: where she is going and what really matters to her;
- more freedom in life, work and relationships.
Most important, in her words: less struggle and more support inside [#594].

#### Outcomes of the case
Katya's summary: "В итоге ожидания ниже, энергия выше" ("In the end, expectations are lower, energy is higher"). Specific outcomes [#735]:
- The state evened out, with neither euphoria nor dips.
- Sleep normalised.
- Ira knows her energy depends on iron, sleep and the cycle and monitors these basics.
- She knows how alcohol affects her (unpleasant to admit).
- She can skip a party, whereas she used to go by default.
- At Christmas she did not gather guests but went away alone, because she wanted to. (The source closes this list with "И т.д.", meaning further unnamed changes.)
- She gained the strength to think long-term. She decided to stay in her profession but change the format of work: no business trips and a more flexible schedule — resolving the earlier swing between "become a waitress by the sea" and "what about my career".

The main result, in Katya's framing, is not new habits but calm that comes from understanding. Ira's words: "Появились силы на эксперименты и понимание, что не все в жизни навсегда. Есть циклы энергии, профессиональные этапы, меняющиеся смыслы. Иногда нормально просто сохранять силы, никуда не бежать и не спешить чего-то достигать. Не обязательно получать все и сразу: можно проходить через изменения и откаты и двигаться в своем темпе" ("There was strength for experiments and the understanding that not everything in life is forever. There are energy cycles, professional stages, changing meanings. Sometimes it is normal just to conserve strength, not run anywhere and not hurry to achieve something. You don't have to get everything at once: you can go through changes and setbacks and move at your own pace.") [#735]

## Dictionary of change: paired concepts (Словарь изменений)

### What this covers

"Словарь изменений Кати Стрельниковой" ("Katya Strelnikova's dictionary of change") is a rubric of posts each built around one pair of concepts that people confuse. Every post opens with the same framing: the rubric is "О словах и понятиях, которые люди путают друг с другом. И дихотомии, которая чаще всего делает изменения невозможными." ("About words and concepts that people confuse with each other. And the dichotomy that most often makes change impossible.") [#673, #688, #707, #723, #738]

This topic covers five pairs: control vs choice, self-pity vs self-compassion, pleasant vs necessary, expectations vs faith, and refusal vs moderation. Each post gives definitions, explains how the "wrong" twin works, contrasts the two with paired example sentences, and ends with self-test questions. Per the author, the pairs matter because the wrong twin of each pair looks like the right one but traps people in cycles of tension, breakdown and guilt. The posts also refer back to an earlier first post of the series, "Насилие VS Усилие" ("Violence vs Effort"), which is not part of this topic's messages. (!) INCOMPLETE: the content of "Violence vs Effort" is not in the provided source. [#673, #688, #707, #723, #738]

### Core ideas

- The wrong twin imitates the right one. In each pair the counterproductive concept (control, pity, the pleasant want, expectations, refusal) looks like discipline, care or a goal, which is why it is so easy to confuse. Each pair is explained in full in *Frameworks* below. [#673, #688, #738]
- Culture teaches the wrong twin from childhood. Katya repeatedly blames the culture we grow up in, with its "pull yourself together", "patience and work grind everything" and "don't feel sorry for yourself", for making control and harshness seem like the adult way. [#673, #688]
- The test is always consequences and direction, not momentary feeling. Across the pairs the deciding question is where an action leads: does it make things really better later, does it lead where I want to be, does it answer "why do I need this". [#688, #707, #723, #738]
- Coercion produces rebellion and relapse. Control, bans and rigid expectations create tension and deficit, which end in rebellion, breakdown, guilt and a new round of harshness: a swing that does not stop until we step off it ourselves. [#673, #738]
- Real adulthood means understanding, not enduring. Choosing rather than controlling requires growing up in the sense of figuring out what is important and what we truly want, not learning to endure and suppress desires. [#673]

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Control vs Choice (Контроль VS Выбор)
Background. Katya says this pair was confused in her own head for a long time. We are born into a culture of control and hear from childhood: «Возьми себя в руки» ("Take yourself in hand"), «Соберись, тряпка» ("Pull yourself together, you rag"), «Мало ли чего ты хочешь» ("Who cares what you want"), «Терпенье и труд все перетрут» ("Patience and work grind through everything"). It feels as if adults know how to do this, since they tell us so. With this background, when something does not work, we draw the "logical" conclusion: not enough control, be harsher, restrict, oblige and monitor ourselves even more. [#673]

Control — definition and mechanism. "Контроль — попытка управлять собой через принуждение." ("Control is an attempt to manage oneself through coercion.") We decide how things should be and then force ourselves and life to match that picture. At the base of control is distrust of oneself, one's body and the process, as if saying to oneself: «если я тебя отпущу, ты все испортишь» ("if I let you go, you'll ruin everything"). Hence the need to hold, watch and never relax. Katya links this to a childhood illusion that somewhere there is an adult who holds everything in their hands and can do literally anything "if only they pull themselves together". But in her view life does not adjust, the body does not adjust, and we ourselves do not adjust. "И чем сильнее мы пытаемся продавить свое «как надо», тем меньше слышим, что происходит на самом деле" ("And the harder we try to push through our 'how it should be', the less we hear what is actually happening"). [#673]

The control–rebellion–breakdown cycle. Katya describes the loop step by step:
1. Constant struggle with oneself and life creates tension. [#673]
2. Tension leads to inner rebellion, for example when all you want is to lie down, or to eat only sweets for lunch out of spite ("назло кондуктору"). [#673]
3. Rebellion leads to a breakdown (срыв). [#673]
4. Then everything starts again, because the conclusion drawn is that even more control is needed to make it work next time. [#673]

Choice — definition and mechanism. "Выбор — способность действовать из понимания, а не из принуждения. А также понимание последствий выбора" ("Choice is the ability to act from understanding rather than from coercion. And also understanding the consequences of the choice"), for example that something will have to be given up. We do not force ourselves; we consciously decide because we see the connection between the action and what matters to us. At the base of choice are trust and contact with oneself: «я знаю, чего хочу, замечаю, что со мной происходит, и двигаюсь, опираясь на это» ("I know what I want, I notice what is happening to me, and I move relying on that"). When we choose, the struggle disappears: there is no need to force ourselves because we are doing what we decided. Flexibility appears: if something does not work, we do not blame ourselves for lacking willpower but investigate what needs to change for it to work. [#673]

Paired examples.
- Control: «Нельзя есть после шести» ("You must not eat after six"). Choice: «Я замечаю, что если ем поздно, плохо сплю, поэтому выбираю ужинать раньше» ("I notice that if I eat late I sleep badly, so I choose to have dinner earlier"). [#673]
- Control: «Надо ходить в зал три раза в неделю» ("I have to go to the gym three times a week"). Choice: «Движение дает мне энергию, поэтому я ищу, какое движение мне подходит» ("Movement gives me energy, so I look for the kind of movement that suits me"). [#673]

How to tell control from choice — 3 self-tests.
1. The "skip once" test. Ask yourself what will happen if I do not do "…" once, or recall such a situation from the recent past. If guilt washes over you and "everything is lost" ("все пропало") — it is control. If you calmly think "ну ок, завтра продолжу" ("okay, I'll continue tomorrow") — it is choice. [#673]
2. The body-feeling test. Control feels like tension, "I have to pull myself together". Choice feels like calm and clarity, even before something difficult. Katya asks: are you joyful and calm? Or do you think with disgust about what you will have to do, while everything inside contracts? [#673]
3. The "why do I need this" test. Ask «зачем мне это» ("why do I need this") and listen to the answer. «Потому что надо», «так правильно», «стыдно иначе» ("because I must", "that's the right way", "it would be shameful otherwise") — control. «Потому что мне от этого лучше», «я так хочу» ("because it makes me feel better", "I want it this way") — choice. [#673]

Growing up. "Чтобы выбирать, а не контролировать, нужно по-настоящему повзрослеть." ("To choose rather than control, you need to truly grow up.") Not in the sense of learning to endure, force yourself and push your desires aside, but the opposite: figuring out what is important, what suits you, what you really want and why. Katya says she often sees content claiming that being an adult is boring and hard, and she disagrees. At the start of work she tells every client «Мы будем делать только то, что вы захотите» ("We will only do what you want"); clients do not believe it and are later very surprised. When we understand what is important to us and why, there is no need to control ourselves: it is enough to do what we want and not do what we do not want, although this is not always pleasant and sometimes not easy. [#673]

#### Self-compassion vs Pity (Жалость VS сострадание)
Background. Katya says self-sympathy and self-care are complicated in "our culture": «То пятилетка за два года, то Иисус терпел и нам велел» ("Either the five-year plan in two years, or 'Jesus suffered and told us to'"), «Не надо себя жалеть» ("Don't feel sorry for yourself"), «Соберись» ("Pull yourself together"). This is "буквально вода, в которой мы плаваем с детства" ("literally the water we swim in since childhood"). For many people (for her too, for many years) the very idea of self-care sounds suspicious. The real problem, she says, is a mess in our heads: most often we confuse self-care and self-compassion with pity and indulgence. In recent years she increasingly sees both compassion and pity in herself, and does not shy away from calling the latter «токсичная жалость» ("toxic pity"), because it is what pushes her towards decisions that do not lead to a better state and flourishing, but rather to greater suffering. [#688]

Self-compassion — definition. "Самосострадание — видеть, что тебе сейчас сложно и плохо, и выбирать позаботиться о себе так, чтобы стало лучше." ("Self-compassion is seeing that you are having a hard time right now and choosing to take care of yourself so that it becomes better.") Really better, not for a second. Examples Katya gives: get up and do morning exercise because you know you will feel more energetic afterwards; go to bed at ten because you need strength tomorrow; make a salad instead of eating a bun because in an hour you would be hungry and angry again. And sometimes self-compassion is allowing yourself not to do something, accepting the inevitable, giving up the "convulsions" (отказаться от конвульсий). What makes it compassion is that it is chosen from the place of «мне это сейчас правда нужно» ("I really need this now"), not «я же такая бедная-несчастная, надо себя пожалеть» ("I'm so poor and miserable, I should feel sorry for myself"). [#688]

Pity — definition. "Жалость — выбирать то, что кажется облегчением в моменте, но на деле уводит еще дальше от желаемого состояния и целей." ("Pity is choosing what seems like relief in the moment but actually leads you even further from the desired state and goals.") Its typical voice: «Ты так устала, полежи с сериалом» ("You're so tired, lie down with a series") — never mind that you won't fall asleep afterwards; «Ой, ты так не выспалась, не делай зарядку» ("Oh, you didn't get enough sleep, skip the exercise") — never mind that it is precisely the exercise that gives energy; «Ты уже такая голодная, съешь булку» ("You're so hungry already, eat a bun") — never mind that in an hour you'll be even hungrier. In the moment it feels like care; in fact it is indulgence that has to be paid for. [#688]

The caring-mother test. Katya asks the reader to think: would a caring mother endlessly feed her daughter ice cream and let her skip school at the first "I don't want to"? Perhaps you do that with your own children? [#688]

Why they are confused. Both start from the feeling «мне тяжело» ("this is hard for me"). "Жалости можно дать Оскар за то, как талантливо она мимикрирует под заботу." ("Pity could be given an Oscar for how talentedly it mimics care.") [#688]

4 differences Katya identifies.
1. Aftermath. After an act of self-compassion things really get better. After pity it always gets worse — in an hour, a day, a week. [#688]
2. The question asked. Self-compassion asks «Что мне сейчас действительно нужно?» ("What do I really need now?"). Pity asks «Что меня быстро успокоит?» ("What will calm me down quickly?"). [#688]
3. Movement. Self-compassion moves you forward, sometimes through discomfort, but always in the right direction. Pity gives temporary relief but leaves you in the same place with your problems. [#688]
4. Inner figure. Self-compassion is the caring adult inside you. Pity is the inner child who rebels and demands a candy. [#688]

Consequences prompt. If it is still hard to tell them apart, Katya's tip is to always remember consequences and ask: "То, что сейчас хочу сделать, приведет меня туда, где я хочу быть? Или это временное облегчение, которое сделает хуже?" ("Will what I want to do now lead me where I want to be? Or is it temporary relief that will make things worse?") [#688]

#### Pleasant vs Necessary (Приятное VS Необходимое)
Origin. At her practicum "В этот раз получится!" ("This time it will work!") the group discussed what "I want" means, and Katya noticed this links directly to the previous post on pity and compassion, so she continued the theme. [#707]

Premise. "Мы, люди, вообще-то незамысловато устроены: хотим, чтобы было хорошо, и не хотим, чтобы было плохо." ("We humans are actually simply built: we want things to be good and don't want them to be bad.") The only problem is that we constantly confuse sensual pleasure in the moment with the real "good" — the state in which it is truly great to live. [#707]

Two kinds of "want".
1. The pleasant want — about pleasure. Momentary, sensual: eat a cake, cancel a workout, get stuck in the phone, treat yourself here and now, switch quickly, feel sorry for yourself. [#707]
2. The necessary want — about depth. Not a momentary whim but a sincere, urgent desire that leads to long-term happiness and flourishing. [#707]

Personal example: massage. Katya goes for massage and it is often unpleasant for her. A client of hers said «массаж для меня — не отдых» ("massage is not rest for me"), and Katya agrees. She plays padel, which tightens her right arm and shoulder; she runs and walks a lot, which tightens her calves and thighs. When the masseuse works these places she wants to slide off the table or at least whimper. But she has no doubt she needs massage, because without it she would not feel free and great in her body and could not keep enjoying sport. It is the necessary thing that "I want" even when it is unpleasant. [#707]

Necessary does not equal unpleasant. "необходимое не равно неприятное" ("necessary does not equal unpleasant"). This is not about "the more you suffer, the more useful it is". Massage is her example of the necessary that is sometimes unpleasant, but the necessary is very often pleasant: getting enough sleep, eating tasty and filling food, walking in good weather, hugging someone close — all are "кайф" (a delight). Pleasantness is simply not the main criterion. The necessary is defined not by whether it is pleasant but by where it leads us; sometimes the path is unpleasant, sometimes very pleasant, and both are normal. [#707]

Spinoza: freedom as recognized necessity. Katya cites her favourite quote of Benedict Spinoza: "Свобода — это осознанная необходимость." ("Freedom is recognized necessity.") We are used to thinking freedom means doing "what we want" — and it does, but wanting has different meanings: «Хочу тортик» ("I want a cake") and «Хочу быть здоровой» ("I want to be healthy") are different kinds of wanting. In her reading, Spinoza says that the slave of (pleasant) desires is not free: he jerks after every "want" like a marionette pulled by strings. "По-настоящему свободен тот, кто понимает свою истинную природу и свои настоящие потребности — и осознанно выбирает то, что ему действительно нужно." ("Truly free is the one who understands his true nature and real needs and consciously chooses what he really needs.") Not under the stick, not through violence against oneself, but from deep understanding that this leads to the life he wants and the state in which he flourishes. "Осознать необходимость — значит перестать быть заложником приятного." ("To recognize necessity is to stop being a hostage to the pleasant.") Real freedom, and real pleasure too, live in that conscious choice, because "нет ничего приятнее, чем жить жизнью, которая тебе по-настоящему подходит" ("there is nothing more pleasant than living a life that truly suits you"). [#707]

How to tell them apart — 3 signs.
1. Volume and persistence. The pleasant shouts loudly, stamps its little feet and demands immediately. The necessary speaks quietly but does not go away. [#707]
2. Aftertaste. The pleasant gives quick relief and an aftertaste of «ну зачем» ("why did I do that"). The necessary sometimes requires effort but leaves behind «это было правильное решение» ("that was the right decision"). [#707]
3. Time frame. The pleasant is about now. The necessary is about us as a whole. [#707]

The pleasant is not the enemy. Cake, a TV series and a day on the sofa can also be necessary. The point is not always to choose the "useful", but to see what exactly we are choosing, why, and where it leads us. [#707]

#### Faith vs Expectations (Вера VS Ожидания)
Why we swap faith for expectations. Katya constantly sees, in her personal experience and her practice, people trying to replace faith with expectations. Honestly, believing is scary: faith has no guarantees, deadlines or numbers, only a direction and a readiness to keep walking without knowing how much further. Expectations give an illusion of certainty and control. «Минус пять килограммов» ("Minus five kilograms") sounds clearer than «я просто буду сбалансировано есть, больше двигаться и высыпаться, а тело само найдет свой вес» ("I'll simply eat in a balanced way, move more and sleep enough, and the body will find its own weight"). [#723]

Related sayings of hers. She often says that before "just do it" you need "just believe it". A phrase clients hear from her with enviable regularity: "Disappointed? That's what you get when you have expectations". She has also previously written about the "похмелье от ожиданий" ("expectation hangover"). [#723]

Her tongue-in-cheek formula. "Формула успеха: Верить, что дойдешь, и при этом не представлять, куда именно и когда." ("Success formula: believe that you'll get there, while not imagining exactly where and when.") [#723]

Faith — definition. "Вера — внутреннее знание, что я двигаюсь в важном для меня направлении и дойду, даже если пока не понимаю, как именно и когда." ("Faith is the inner knowing that I am moving in a direction important to me and will get there, even if I don't yet understand exactly how and when.") Regardless of what obstacles come. It is about direction, not a point on the map; about what is in my power: my actions, my persistence, my readiness to try again. [#723]

Expectations — definition. "Ожидания — конкретная картинка будущего." ("Expectations are a concrete picture of the future.") With numbers, deadlines and form. They are almost always about things that only partly depend on us: how the body will respond, what people will say, how circumstances will turn out. When reality diverges from the picture, expectations say «У меня не получается / Со мной что-то не так / Это не мое» ("It's not working for me / Something is wrong with me / This isn't for me") — insert your own version. [#723]

4 differences, with paired examples.
1. Direction vs a dated result. Faith is about direction and trusting oneself; expectations are about a concrete result by a concrete deadline. Expectation: «Минус пять килограмм за месяц» ("Minus five kilos in a month"). Faith: «Я выстраиваю питание и движение, которые мне подходят. Тело придет к своему весу» ("I'm building eating and movement that suit me. The body will come to its weight"). [#723]
2. What I do vs what should happen. Faith relies on what I do; expectations rely on what should happen. With the same "minus five kilos in a month" example, the number on the scale depends not only on me: on the body itself, the current lifestyle, the environment and similar factors. The faith version is the same as above. [#723]
3. After a failure. Faith looks for a new path; expectations look for someone to blame. Expectation: «Больше не ем сладкое» ("I don't eat sweets anymore") — then she eats cake at a friend's birthday and immediately: «Я слабовольная» ("I'm weak-willed"). Faith: «Я выстрою здоровые отношения со сладким» ("I'll build a healthy relationship with sweets"). Overate candies? Instead of criticism and verdicts, questions: «Что меня к этому привело? Как избежать этого в будущем?» ("What led me to this? How can I avoid it in future?"). [#723]
4. Energy. Faith gives energy to continue. Expectations take it away: first on tense waiting, then on disappointment. Expectation: «В следующем месяце буду заниматься три раза в неделю» ("Next month I'll train three times a week") — missed two days, and that's it, «опять не смогла» ("failed again"). Faith: «Я стану человеком, который занимается спортом регулярно. Сколько это займет, столько и займет» ("I'll become a person who exercises regularly. However long it takes, it takes") — missed two days, went on the third. [#723]

"Believe not in success, believe in reality". Katya was reading Pema Chödrön's book «Как жить и как умирать» ("How to Live and How to Die" in Russian translation) and found her teacher's saying, which in Katya's opinion explains the whole confusion: «Верь не в успех. Верь в реальность» ("Believe not in success. Believe in reality"). Belief in "success" (a very concrete picture of the future) is exactly expectations: confidence that everything will work as we want and on our terms. But success cannot be relied on: sometimes things go as we want, sometimes not. Reality always happens, and it can be counted on. If we stop perceiving every refusal and failure as a personal vendetta against us, a surprising freedom appears: to be equally open to the desired and the undesired. Katya says this seems to be the deepest form of faith. Her summary line: "Не «у меня все получится», а «что бы ни случилось, я с этим справлюсь и пойду дальше»." ("Not 'everything will work out for me', but 'whatever happens, I'll cope with it and move on'.") [#723]

Self-test question. "Я понимаю, куда иду, и верю, что дойду? Или ожидаю, что будет именно так и именно к этому сроку?" ("Do I understand where I'm going and believe I'll get there? Or do I expect it to be exactly like this and exactly by this deadline?") [#723]

#### Refusal vs Moderation (Отказ VS Воздержанность)
Origin. A thought from the newsletter @justakindreminder inspired the post: moderation (воздержанность), they write, is not asceticism at all but the skill of managing one's desires and passions. [#738]

Katya's position and the refusal swing. "Я против аскез, марафонов и отказов от чего-то" ("I'm against asceticism, marathons and giving things up"). She says this played a bad joke on her many times ("привет 20 лет рпп" — "hello, 20 years of eating disorder"), and both from her own experience and years of health-coaching practice she has observed in full colour how they end. Her examples: 30 days without sugar, and on day 31 a whole box of candies at once; two weeks without alcohol, then "I deserved it" and a hangover for the whole weekend; a social-media detox after which you scroll until 3 a.m. Then the familiar path: rollback, guilt, "I have no willpower", and a new marathon from Monday. "Качели, которые не заканчиваются, пока мы сами с них не слезем." ("A swing that doesn't stop until we ourselves get off it.") [#738]

Refusal — definition and mechanism. Refusal is an external promise of "never again". It is held by willpower and fear («если разрешу себе хоть чуть-чуть, все пропало» — "if I allow myself even a little, all is lost"). Instead of figuring out our relationship with something, we simply remove it from life. "А потерпеть, увы, ничего не проясняет." ("And enduring, alas, clarifies nothing.") Instead it launches deficit thinking: the forbidden becomes the most desired, and we are back on the "holding on / breaking down" swing. [#738]

Moderation — definition. Moderation is the skill of managing one's desires: noticing them, weighing them, understanding their origin and choosing the measure — how much I really need for it to be enough. It is held not by prohibition but by understanding: "я знаю, зачем мне это, сколько мне в радость и где начинается «во вред»" ("I know why I need this, how much of it is a joy to me, and where 'harmful' begins"). [#738]

Why they are confused. Both look like "I'm restraining myself", but they work differently. [#738]

5 differences, with paired examples.
1. "Never" vs "how much I need". Refusal: «Больше не ем сладкое» ("I don't eat sweets anymore") — then a friend's birthday, and in go half a cake, those cookies over there and a box of Raffaello ("so as not to get up twice"). Moderation: «Я не отказываюсь от сладкого. Но выбираю десерт, который люблю и который правда хочу. И ем его с удовольствием, а не с чувством вины» ("I don't give up sweets. But I choose a dessert I love and really want. And I eat it with pleasure, not guilt"). [#738]
2. Control vs choice. Refusal rests on control; moderation on choice (linking to the first pair above). Refusal: «Удаляю инстаграм навсегда» ("I'm deleting Instagram forever") — and then can't tear away for 4 hours. Moderation: «Я захожу в инстаграм, когда мне что-то нужно, а не когда мне скучно» ("I go on Instagram when I need something, not when I'm bored"). [#738]
3. Deficit vs relief of tension. Refusal creates deficit; moderation relieves tension. Refusal: «Никаких сериалов по вечерам» ("No TV series in the evenings") — then a binge till morning despite an important meeting. Moderation: «Посмотрю одну серию и пойду спать, потому что завтра хочу проснуться бодрой» ("I'll watch one episode and go to bed because tomorrow I want to wake up fresh"). [#738]
4. Fear vs understanding. Refusal grows from fear; moderation from understanding. Refusal: «Не держу дома орехи, а то съем всю пачку» ("I don't keep nuts at home, or I'll eat the whole pack"). Moderation: «Орехи стоят на полке. Я знаю, что горсти мне хватает. А если однажды съем больше, ничего страшного не случится» ("The nuts are on the shelf. I know a handful is enough for me. And if one day I eat more, nothing terrible will happen"). [#738]
5. Avoiding vs answering "why do I need this". Refusal avoids the question; moderation answers it. Refusal: «Больше никакого вина после работы» ("No more wine after work") — and then… (left to the reader's imagination with a grin emoji). Moderation: «Что я пытаюсь снять этим бокалом? Усталость? Тревогу? Может, мне нужен не бокал, а выходной?...» ("What am I trying to relieve with this glass? Fatigue? Anxiety? Maybe I need not a glass but a day off?"). [#738]

The exception: truly addictive substances. "Смуты добавляет, что есть исключение." ("Adding to the confusion, there is an exception.") Things that really cause addiction — alcohol, nicotine, substances and anything that messes with our dopamine — and she explicitly says sugar does not count ("нет, сахар не в счет"). For people prone to addiction, measure simply does not work there: one glass for them is not "one glass" but a ticket back into bondage. Katya says she unfortunately knows what she is talking about ("на чем я только ни сидела" — "what haven't I been hooked on"): she has not drunk for more than seven years and has not smoked for more than six. But even this is not refusal for her in the sense of "forbidden, I endure". She worked out why she needed it, what it costs her, and why she should not indulge in "чуть-чутем" ("just a little bit"), and chose to live without it. [#738]

Self-test questions before giving something up. "Какие у меня отношения с тем, от чего я хочу отказаться? Что я на самом деле ищу в этом? И как еще я могу это получить?" ("What is my relationship with the thing I want to give up? What am I really looking for in it? And how else can I get it?") [#738]

### Detailed insights

#### How the pairs connect
- Refusal vs moderation explicitly reuses control vs choice: difference no. 2 is "refusal rests on control, moderation on choice", and the "why do I need this" question appears as a test in both (control test 3; moderation difference 5). [#673, #738]
- Pleasant vs necessary grew out of pity vs compassion: Katya noticed the "what does 'want' mean" discussion "напрямую перекликается" ("directly echoes") the pity/compassion post. In both, the pleasant/pitying option is momentary relief that leaves things worse, while the necessary/compassionate option may involve discomfort but leads where you want. "Feel sorry for yourself" is even listed as one of the pleasant wants. [#688, #707]
- Faith vs expectations reuses sweets and training examples close to those of refusal vs moderation ("I don't eat sweets anymore" + friend's birthday cake; "three times a week" training), showing the same rigid vow failing under the expectations lens (self-blame "I'm weak-willed") and under the refusal lens (binge). [#723, #738]
- Expectations, like control, are described as giving an "illusion" of certainty and control, and both lead to self-blame when reality does not comply. [#673, #723]

#### The cultural and personal background
Katya frames the wrong twins as learned: control from the "culture of control" we are born into, harshness towards oneself from Soviet and religious messages ("five-year plan in two years", "Jesus suffered"), where self-care sounds suspicious. She presents herself as having been inside these confusions: control vs choice "долго путалась у меня самой" ("was confused for a long time in my own head"); she sees "toxic pity" in herself; she lived 20 years with an eating disorder and past addictions. Her lessons are offered from that lived experience plus her coaching practice. [#673, #688, #738]

### Practical playbook

All self-test questions from the rubric in one place (each explained in its framework above):
- Control vs choice: "What happens if I skip this once?"; "Does this feel like tension or calm clarity?"; "Why do I need this?" [#673]
- Pity vs self-compassion: "What do I really need now?" vs "What will calm me quickly?"; "Will this lead me where I want to be, or is it temporary relief that will make it worse?" [#688]
- Pleasant vs necessary: is it shouting and demanding now, or quiet but persistent; will it leave "why did I" or "that was the right decision"; is it about now or about me as a whole? [#707]
- Faith vs expectations: "Do I know where I'm going and believe I'll get there, or do I expect exactly this by exactly this date?" After a slip, ask "What led me to this? How to avoid it in future?" instead of blaming. [#723]
- Refusal vs moderation: "What is my relationship with this? What am I really looking for in it? How else can I get it?" [#738]

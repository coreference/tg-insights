## Tools and reflection practices

### What this covers

This file collects every concrete reflection instrument the channel offered: a monthly couple ritual (Life Dinner), an exercise for defining core values, the permissions wheel for self-care, a single-area wheel of balance for health, the weekly Plus / Minus / Next log, New Year and year-results reflection questions, and a gentler "start the year from your state" question set. It also contains a guest expert's rules for learning without overload and the author's own detailed year-results case (what planning, public reporting and energy tracking taught her). According to the source, these tools matter because change and self-care "begin not with actions but with permissions", because clarity about values removes doubt, and because regular reflection lets the brain see what really works — turning self-criticism into experiments [#166, #139, #366].

### Core ideas

- Self-care starts with permissions, not actions. Before you can sleep enough, rest or set boundaries you must give yourself permission to do so; the permissions wheel makes the gaps visible (see *Permissions wheel*) [#166].
- Wheels are for spotting growth points, not for scoring 100. Both the permissions wheel and the wheel of balance (which "many specialists hate") are not meant to be filled to the maximum; the aim is to see the main points of growth, and each tool can be adapted to oneself [#166, #168].
- Clear values speed decisions and remove FOBO. Knowing 3-5 core values lets goals and intentions be realized faster, in harmony and without fear of better options (see *Core values definition*) [#139].
- Regular, light reflection changes behaviour by itself. Writing weekly what gave and took energy makes the brain see what works; after the fifth "minus" entry the behaviour starts to change, and the "Next" column converts self-blame into experiments (see *Plus / Minus / Next*) [#366].
- A plan is a sketch, not a reflection of reality. The author's year-results case shows that most plans may go unfulfilled while the year is still a success; a vector is enough [#502].
- Start the year from state, not from a goal list — otherwise there are goals but no strength or inner agreement with them [#506].
- Learn slowly, rarely and with meaning — learning needs time, low stress and limited volume (Elena Tikhomirova) [#369].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Life Dinner (monthly couple reflection)
Purpose: Marina Sidorich presents Life Dinner as a coaching practice "used by many entrepreneurs" for keeping long-term relationships healthy. The rationale given: research (she links a Harvard Gazette piece) shows that harmonious relationships with close people are directly linked to health and well-being; long-term relationships are not easy, crises happen, and in those moments it is important to talk [#117].

Format: once a month, over dinner, partners share feelings and experience of the past period, give each other feedback and discuss further mutual goals. Communication at this dinner "should be kind, open and supportive" — «Общение в этот момент должно быть добрым, открытым и поддерживающим» (Communication at this moment should be kind, open and supportive) [#117].

The question set has three blocks:
1. Personal assessment of the past month (each partner about themselves):
   - What could I have done better? (Что я могла бы сделать лучше?)
   - What am I proud of? (Чем я горжусь?)
   - What is troubling me? (Что меня тревожит?)
   - What worries me about the future? (Что меня беспокоит в будущем?) [#117]
2. Assessment of the past month in the relationship (together):
   - What did we notice in each other and what do we like?
   - What did we learn in the relationship?
   - How could we improve the relationship? [#117]
3. Personal goals for the new month:
   - What do I want to achieve in the next 4 weeks? The author stresses it is important to discuss this aloud in order to connect personal and shared priorities [#117].

Bonus suggestion: the monthly dinner is also "a great excuse" to discover new places for romantic meetings and attunement with each other once a month [#117].

#### Core values definition (against FOBO)
Purpose: for people who face an important choice, often doubt, and suffer from FOBO (fear of better options) and FOMO. Marina Sidorich describes herself as «человек сомневающийся» (a doubting person) and says understanding her key values helps her be sure she is making the right life decision: «Когда есть ясность, цели и намерения воплощаются быстрее, в гармонии и без чувства FOBO» (When there is clarity, goals and intentions are realized faster, in harmony and without the feeling of FOBO) [#139].

Steps:
1. Freewriting. Take a sheet of paper and start writing everything that matters to you in life. Do not think, do not correct, write chaotically until thoughts and words run out. Variant for ChatGPT users: the technique can be done inside the chat; the AI will analyse the chaos and return an ordered list [#139].
2. Review a list of values — the author links a values list proposed by authors of works on motivational interviewing (motivational counselling). Choose 3-5 values without which your life is impossible. Completion criterion: you should feel a sense of completeness — nothing needs to be added or removed [#139].
3. Use the helper questions to narrow down the choice:
   1. Which of these values evokes the strongest emotions?
   2. Which value shows up most often in my decisions and actions?
   3. Without which value can I not imagine my life?
   4. Which of these values supports my emotional and physical well-being?
   5. Which values matter most in relationships with loved ones and friends?
   6. Which value reflects my ideal of self-realization?
   7. Which value helps me cope with difficulties and stress?
   8. Which value is most important for my inner world and harmony?
   9. Which of the values is most consistent with my past experience and lessons? [#139]
4. Check how manifested the values are now. Once the 3-5 values are defined, think how much they are present in your life now. Ask: How do I feel when I live in agreement with my values? How did I feel when reality went against my guiding points? What decision can I make to strengthen the manifestation of my values? [#139]

Author's worked example (Marina Sidorich's three "coordinates"):
- Family — gives her support and a sense of safety, inspiration, joy and meaning; «Семья — это мой дом» (Family is my home).
- Personal well-being (well-being, deep health) — emotional stability and energy to fully enjoy life; she needs to be resourced herself in order to take care of the family.
- Freedom — freedom of movement, the ability to choose and be in any place without restrictions, the ability to choose her field of activity and her surroundings [#139].
Lesson she draws: from these three ideas she knows how to develop, which projects to choose, what to leave aside, what lifestyle to lead and where to live; «Лучшее, что происходило в моей жизни, вписывается в систему этих трех координат» (The best things that happened in my life fit into the system of these three coordinates) [#139]. Katya Strelnikova independently names freedom as her own key value and uses it to explain why her plans are sketches (see *Year results case*) [#502].

#### Permissions wheel for self-care (Колесо разрешений для заботы о себе)
Origin and purpose: the permissions wheel is a tool from Transactional Analysis; it visually shows how a person is doing with permissions in different areas of life and highlights growth points. Katya Strelnikova saw it in Sasha Mkhitaryan's channel and together they made a version for self-care, because «забота о себе — комплексный процесс, который, как и все остальное в этой жизни, начинается не с действий, а с разрешений» (self-care is a complex process which, like everything else in this life, begins not with actions but with permissions) [#166].

Mechanism, with the author's examples of permission → behaviour:
- To get enough sleep and find time for sport, you need to allow yourself to prioritize health.
- To rest instead of overeating sweets, you need to allow yourself to feel your needs.
- To decline a work meeting in favour of lunch, you need to allow yourself to have boundaries.
- To love yourself, you need permission to know that you are OK [#166].

Structure (from the card, [#166]): a circle with concentric rings scaled from 0% at the centre to 100% at the edge, divided into four quadrants of three permissions each — 12 segments in total. You rate how much you currently give yourself each permission:
1. Self-perception (самовосприятие):
   1. *Быть собой* — to be yourself.
   2. *Знать, что со мной всё в порядке* — to know that I am OK.
   3. *Принимать восхищение и похвалу* — to accept admiration and praise.
2. Self-compassion (самосострадание):
   4. *Совершать ошибки* — to make mistakes.
   5. *Прощать себя* — to forgive yourself.
   6. *Иметь границы* — to have boundaries.
3. Health (здоровье):
   7. *Приоритизировать здоровье* — to prioritize health.
   8. *Интересоваться улучшением здоровья* — to be interested in improving your health.
   9. *Давать себе время на восстановление* — to give yourself time for recovery.
4. Needs (потребности):
   10. *Чувствовать потребности* — to feel your needs.
   11. *Удовлетворять потребности* — to satisfy your needs.
   12. *Хотеть больше* — to want more. [#166]

How to use it: fill each segment to the level that reflects your current permission. As with the wheel of balance, the goal is not to fill all scales to a hundred: «Цель — увидеть главные точки роста» (The goal is to see the main points of growth). The tool is meant to be customized — keep only what is relevant and add something more important for you. The author invites readers to ask: what is missing from this wheel, and what is missing for you to take good care of yourself? [#166]

#### Single-sphere wheel of balance for health
Context: Varya (forwarded from her channel Fizkulturny kod) likes to begin her year in September and, despite being in a slump (no time, sport and procedures on pause, after pregnancy and birth), drew herself a healthy-lifestyle plan for September. Her principle: «если не можешь бежать, то можно хотя бы «полежать в сторону цели»» (if you cannot run, you can at least "lie in the direction of the goal") [#167]. She always uses the wheel of balance tool, reviewed bullet-journal ideas and her old notebooks, and realized she missed hand-drawn trackers [#167].

Katya Strelnikova's comment: the wheel of balance is often hated, «Но как будто не все умеют его готовить» (But it seems not everyone knows how to cook it); she liked the "elegant" use of the tool for a single sphere — people rarely go beyond the standard format — and the clear, visual explanation. She asks readers what a "five" would mean for them in these categories [#168].

Step 1 — choose the zones of one sphere and score each on a 5-point scale. Varya's health version (she stresses it is her version; take what is relevant to you):
1. Sport 2/5 — regular moderate load on different muscle groups.
2. Everyday activity 3/5 — moving rather than sitting.
3. Nutrition 2/5 — regularity, not forgetting to eat, a balanced plate so as not to make up for lack of energy with sweets.
4. Recovery 1/5 — sleep, massage, muscle release with a roller, sauna (banya).
5. Care 2/5 — face masks, brushes, facial massages.
6. Medicine 1/5 — a check-up plus appointments with all regular doctors after pregnancy and childbirth [#167].
The photo shows these six axes drawn by hand in a notebook as a hexagonal spider diagram, each axis divided into five marks and the scores coloured in [#167].

Step 2 — for each zone write:
1. What a 5 means in my understanding (the definition of the target). The photo shows the start of this for sport: a heading 1) Sport, training, regularity 2/5, followed by a handwritten line saying that 5 means 2 swimming-pool sessions, 2 … (stretching sessions, partly legible) — (!) INCOMPLETE: the rest of the definition is cut off in the image [#167].
2. What I can do in the next 3 days for each zone — always the simplest actions, and she puts them straight into the week's to-do list.
3. Which 1-2 zones most need attention.
4. "Bingo!" — are there zones whose growth will pull the other zones up? She had not found one yet, but thinks in her case it is moving sport to the part of the day when her daughter is with the nanny (previously she trained in the morning before work) [#167].

Step 3 — review in 3 months. Her attitude to low scores: «сначала ты на коне, потом ты под конем» (first you are on the horse, then under the horse) — it happens, and will happen again, so with "healthy excitement" she thinks about what she can improve under an unstable schedule of life and sleep, and will re-score in three months [#167].

#### New Year reflection — three simple questions
Context: whether or not you celebrate New Year, the long holidays are a good time for a small (or big) reflection — at minimum a few minutes to think about what worked and didn't work in the past year and what we want to take into the next one. Katya Strelnikova offers, "as coaches", three simple questions [#250]:
1. What helped and what hindered me in being in balance in [the past year]? Write a list of 5-10 items.
2. What gave and what took my energy in [the past year]? Write a list of 5-10 items.
3. What is important to start / stop doing to be in balance and with energy? Look at the lists above; find which actions or habits unite them and write them out. Then choose one — the most important — and keep your focus on it first [#250].
(!) UNCLEAR: question 3 in the source literally says "in 2024" (the year just ended), although by context it refers to the coming year [#250].
For help with question 2 she refers to the channel's earlier full guide on what gives and takes energy (content not reproduced in this message). She also suggests using the holidays to finally read what you had no time for: the year's interviews, everything under the personal-experience tag (which she finds most interesting), and the channel's rubric post [#250].

#### Plus / Minus / Next
Source: a tool from the book *Tiny Experiments* by Anne-Laure Le Cunff, founder of the Ness Labs community and an ambassador of "life as an experiment" (Katya considers herself one too) [#366].

How Katya uses it: for tracking her state and energy. During the week she enters into a table in Notion what she did "in plus", what "in minus", and what she wants to do about it next [#366]. The three columns:
1. Plus — everything that charged you with energy during the week.
2. Minus — actions or circumstances that drained your strength.
3. Next — concrete changes for the next week [#366].

Why she likes this format:
1. Every week the brain sees what really works and what doesn't. Even if she does nothing with this information, by the fifth time of writing something into Minus it starts to change — «лень уже бумагу марать» (you're already too lazy to keep wasting paper on it) [#366].
2. Specificity: not an abstract "low energy" but a precise «пропустила утреннюю прогулку → тревога и раздражение с утра» (skipped the morning walk → anxiety and irritation from the morning) [#366].
3. Next follows Minus immediately, so instead of «ругать себя» (scolding yourself) you «делать вывод и экспериментировать» (draw a conclusion and experiment) [#366].

What the book says (book page, [#366]): Plus Minus Next is simple, but is based on centuries of practical wisdom — the ancient Greeks spoke of praxis, present-day researchers call it reflection in action, and in everyday terms it is thinking on your feet. The method works because it is fast, flexible and future-focused: filling the weekly page should take no more than about five minutes — a few bullet points and done; let the mind flow without forcing any structure beyond the three columns. The column that should take the most time is "Next": take time to reflect on what happened the past week to decide where to focus the following week [#366]. The page shows a real example from the Ness Labs community: Plus — the time difference helps maintain "always on" boundaries; constant joy thinking about where I'm at now, my partner and a new friend; excitement about a new "Learn to Grow" project; the manager gave constructive feedback on my lead magnet. Minus — Thursday and Friday were not as good, got interrupted a lot and didn't progress as much; it was much easier in summer to wake up with the sun; didn't complete budgeting for next month; got into a stressful argument with the catering contractor because of poor communication. Next — block out time to complete the budget; study more JavaScript (45 minutes on Tuesday and Thursday); it's birthday week, but want to make sure things are wrapped by Friday, looking forward to the staycation; keep paying attention to catering for the event and consider switching providers if issues persist [#366].

Second book page ([#367], p. 19 of the book): Le Cunff's own story behind the experimental mindset. After leaving a former path she attended workshops, read books for pure pleasure and freelanced to maintain income, felt "like my old self again", her curiosity kept leading back to how the human brain works, and she returned to school to study neuroscience "without a grand plan", just to explore, learn and grow. Inspired by the experimental mindset of scientific training, she asked herself (the passage Katya underlined in the photo) what experiment she could run on her own life that would bring an intrinsic sense of fulfilment whatever the outcome. Because she loves writing, she made a pact with herself to write and share 100 articles in 100 workdays on mental health at work, creativity and mindful productivity. Sharing daily was terrifying at first ("I felt naked"); her only anchor was the pact itself; she resisted the urge to clarify her end goal and focused only on showing up, leaning into self-compassion when life got busy, taking notes and journaling, and playing with formats. She finished the 100 articles and her newsletter grew steadily [#367].

Katya's summary of the book: it is about "an experimental way of change and growth" instead of linear SMART goals, and about why it is important to finally become interested in your own life — to learn to be curious and go into "I don't know" with interest instead of anxiety. In essence it is an instruction on turning life into a series of «радостных экспериментов» (joyful experiments) rather than a race to possibly unreachable peaks [#366].

Katya's own filled-in example (her Notion table, [#368]), row by row:

| Plus | Minus | Next |
|---|---|---|
| Very many meetings with dear people | Often have to choose against myself and my energy because I want to hug everyone | Start cancelling client meetings; decide not to earn all the money but let myself get inspired before the second half of the year |
| On Monday I chose myself: stayed lying at my mother's (didn't even talk to her) and watched a series, because it was necessary | Practically nowhere to fit formal workouts | Take a vacation for the last 2 weeks of June without pangs of conscience |
| Went to the hairdresser and the dentist | Almost not meditating | At least 2 formal workouts this week |
| Still squeezed in a massage once a week | Reading little | Meditate at least 3 times |
| Ordered presents for my mother | A rush of client meetings — 4-5 a day (good that there are only 4 working days, but after such work I need to be revived) | Make Sunday a day without plans |
| Was invited to a podcast but asked to come back at the end of June because I have no resource now | Her Welltory app already often shows the orange-red zone and suggests easing off | — |

Lesson visible in the example: Next items are small, concrete and directly answer the Minus entries (cancelling meetings, a guilt-free vacation, minimum numbers of workouts and meditations, a plan-free Sunday) [#368]. Long-term result: in 2025 she kept the Plus / Minus / Next diary weekly in her closed group and reported monthly, which she credits for ending the year more resourced (see *Year results case*) [#501].

#### Year results — from simple to "luxurious"
Context: Katya Strelnikova understands people who say "leave me alone with your year results" — she doesn't celebrate New Year herself. But the previous year she summed up the year for the first time and also planned for the first time, and moving along the plan during the year «меня нереально поддержало» (supported me unbelievably), especially in turbulent moments. Not everything planned happened, but she told herself adequately: «это год, когда мы вообще смотрим, а что реально, а как возможно, а что умещается» (this is the year when we just look at what is real, what is possible and what fits) [#478].

Her "luxurious" programme (she devotes part of all December to it):
1. Do an inventory of plans: something moves to the next year, something simply goes away («пойдет лесом», goes to the woods) — "and that is normal" [#478].
2. Fill in a year-results notebook (one recommended earlier in the channel) [#478].
3. Open all last year's notes and reflect on what kind of (wonderful) person she was, what worked and what didn't, and why [#478].
4. Do YearCompass (a free year-reflection and planning booklet) and look at last year's one; she promised to share the Russian PDF [#478].

Simpler ideas that make reflection and planning easier and also more enjoyable (she did or planned to do all of them):
1. Simplest: listen to the podcast episode «Как подвести итоги года без стресса?» (How to sum up the year without stress?), from what she calls her favourite podcast ever [#478].
2. Olya Polishchuk's webinar "How to set year goals from yourself so as not to become someone else in the new year". The thought Katya liked most: «цели не надо придумывать, их надо сартикулировать и записать» (goals don't need to be invented, they need to be articulated and written down). Olya builds both the webinar and her career re-invention lab on an experimental approach, which Katya says is close to her own; she took that lab in 2020 and recommends it warmly [#478].
3. Radmila Khakova's session "Итоги отЛичного года" (results of an exCellent/personal year — a pun on «отличный» excellent and «личный» personal). Radmila has her own method for doing results in a relaxed, fun way: participants look through photo galleries and use the creative method of artist Natasha Polyakova, which makes it easy to focus on the desired difference «было / будет» (was / will be) and keep that focus throughout the year [#478].

#### Starting the year from state, not a goal list
Thesis (forwarded from Sasha Mkhitaryan's channel «Со всеми все окей»): we are used to entering the new year with set-ups — define goals, set tasks, decide what to achieve. Often the list appears before we ask ourselves how we actually live now and what state we are in. «В итоге цели есть, а сил и внутреннего согласия с ними нет» (As a result there are goals, but no strength or inner agreement with them) [#506].

The holidays give a rare pause to start differently — with attention to yourself, your rhythm, a sense of freedom and aliveness in everyday life. The questions can be answered gradually, returning to them during the holidays and the whole year:
1. How do I want to feel on most days of this year?
2. In what rhythm do I feel truly good now, and which rhythm do I endure out of habit or expectations?
3. Where in my life is there freedom, and where am I living on autopilot?
4. What in my everyday life gives me energy, and what takes it away?
5. What would I like to free myself from, even if I don't yet understand how?
6. What does living my own life mean to me?
7. «Если убрать слово «надо», какой выбор стал бы очевидным?» (If you remove the word "must", which choice would become obvious?) [#506]

How to hold them: do not formulate ideal answers or turn this into yet another task for the year. Sometimes it is enough to let the questions be nearby and observe what thoughts and feelings they raise. Such a start rarely looks impressive, but often turns out to be the most sustainable [#506].

#### Elena Tikhomirova's principles of slow, meaningful learning
Context: Katya admits that in March she asked her community to remind her she had promised herself not to buy any more courses this year — and immediately bought three (only already planned ones), because there are so many useful courses. So the channel asked Elena Tikhomirova (author of «Обучение со смыслом», "Learning with meaning", and the channel «Живое обучение», "Live learning") when it is appropriate to start new learning, how to calculate one's strength and keep balance when learning is not one's only activity [#369].

Her diagnosis: «Обучений стало слишком много» (There is too much learning on offer now) — courses, trainings, marathons, intensives everywhere — which causes FOMO and an inferiority complex. But the brain cannot absorb everything we want, and the body cannot live in constant stress. Learning is necessary, but not the way we think [#369]. Her principles:
1. Learn slowly. The brain needs not just to perceive information but to link it with what is already known, analyse, try to apply and draw conclusions. This requires resource for openness to the new and for "digesting" what enters the head [#369].
2. Learning needs time. Before formal learning, look at your calendar first: if it does not have 3-4 hours per week for calm study, better postpone the idea until better times. Time for learning must be allocated systematically. Her example: she studies on Fridays — the whole Friday has been devoted to learning for many years [#369].
3. Time does not find itself. Decide what you will give up during the learning period, and agree on it in advance with yourself — and maybe with those around you. Otherwise: you have a course webinar but nobody to walk the dog or pick the kids up from kindergarten; you choose kids and dog, postpone learning, then scold yourself that "it didn't work again" and try to catch up under stress [#369].
4. Stress and learning are incompatible. When we are anxious and nervous, the brain switches off a number of processes, including those responsible for learning (at least for learning that is not vital). That is why we stop remembering properly, read and listen to the same thing ten times, and cannot solve problems at all [#369].
5. Choose the period with the least stress. For her it is January: low work activity, the children's term has just begun, everything is calm. Summer does not suit her: she starts rushing about, wanting to go to the river, walk, read a detective novel and spend time with the children rather than sit with books. Don't enrol in a course when the children have exams, you are finishing an important project and have 33 other things to do [#369].
6. There should not be a lot of learning. The brain needs time to process, and our heads are already full. Let it be one course a year, but attentive, thoughtful, immersive. You will feel its effect and a pleasant sense of achievement; it will pull books, lectures and podcasts after it. Little and slow, but with results — far better than a lot with the feeling that you understand nothing and keep up with nothing [#369].
7. The most valuable learning is the one you want to dive into — when you catch and follow your interest, which is only possible going slowly and thoughtfully. Often courses aren't even needed: sometimes it is 5 pages or 10 minutes a day. It is about trusting your own interest and meaning, not about a toxic urge to complete every course in the world [#369].

### Detailed insights

#### Year results case (Katya Strelnikova, 2025)
Why she does it: she has nothing against those who skip year results, but for her it worked uplifting («духоподъемно») for the second year in a row. In early December — partly under the stress of moving countries — she felt she had "failed the year"; after collecting, assessing and weighing everything she concluded «я молодец» (I did well), deliberately not saying "great" because it would not be sincere. Lesson: the felt sense of a year is unreliable until you actually collect the evidence; by late December the feeling flipped from "I did nothing" to "I could have done less" [#501, #502].

Theme of the year: her 2025 theme was «нетоксичное развитие» (non-toxic development). Her written intention for 2025, shown on the card, answered "How to make 2025 my best year?": I want to be rested, full of resource, ideas and strength; I want a stable income and its systematic growth, the possibility to travel (and rest) to a greater extent; I want to do responsible, high-quality practice, meaningful content where I speak about what matters to me, new products, more experiments [#501, img #501]. Looking forward, she wants to devote the next year to satisfaction and gratitude, whose deficit she felt acutely, because without them her mission — «стать свободной, счастливой и процветающей, чтобы помогать другим становиться свободными, счастливыми и процветающими» (to become free, happy and prosperous in order to help others become free, happy and prosperous) — cannot be fully realized [#501].

Practice 1 — financial goals with public monthly reports, and multi-level planning. She set financial goals and wrote public reports every month; planned monthly, weekly and daily in several directions; summed up and reflected once a day / week / month / quarter / year. Background: on an authentic-blogging course she had put the topic of money into her red zone (a topic she never wants to discuss) and then realized this reluctance and fear limited her — «Если боюсь говорить про деньги — как их зарабатывать?» (If I'm afraid to talk about money, how can I earn it?). Public reports helped her reflect concretely every month, notice trends and draw conclusions; now she feels calm in such conversations — it feels like another block has fallen apart [#501]. Result as data: she received $40,567 against a planned $36,000, met the plan on 1 December, more than doubling income, which a year earlier she could not imagine; she jokes that she should have set it higher, but notes that it does not work that way. Only three months had income above $5K, but they showed it was possible — as coaches would say, a stretch (растяжка) [#501]. What regular planning revealed: her throughput capacity, constraints, planning errors, tasks she wants and doesn't want to do — which helps her move forward with a more adequate view of her strength and of what is really interesting and important to her. She devotes all of January to planning the next year [#501].

Practice 2 — energy diary. She kept an energy diary almost every day, reported quarterly, experimented with sleep, recovery and rest, and strictly tracked days off and holidays. She had never managed to do this systematically before. What worked: (1) a public promise — saying on the blog she would keep this focus (as with finances); (2) a closed group where she kept the Plus / Minus / Next diary weekly and reported to everyone once a month. Result: despite a larger workload and moving to another country, she ended the year in a more resourced state than a year earlier; she saw energy waves, understood what affects what, and allowed herself to sometimes be tired «без истерики и вины» (without hysteria and guilt). She plans to continue to track her patterns and energy movements [#501].

Practice 3 — professional growth ("said goodbye to impostor syndrome"): finished an American course in Advanced Health Coaching and Behavioral Change, coached in English, had several supervision sessions with excellent results, logged about 400 practice hours with 40 clients (topics: nutrition, physical activity, self-acceptance, energy and rest, clarity, self-satisfaction, self-discovery, permissions), mentored 8 helping professionals [#501].

Other things done in the year (her full list): lived by a forest and took up birding; visited new places in the USA and Serbia (Belgrade); started tracking expenses (and was shocked); created funds into which a percentage of income goes automatically; started building a financial safety cushion; wrote about 250 posts (and no fewer stories); took part in 5 podcasts and 3 projects; tried Threads and TikTok — «но растёт то, куда идет внимание» (but what grows is where attention goes); tested lots of beauty gadgets; went to a cosmetologist for the first time and chose a routine; took a course on sales and on IFS; went deeper into a philosophical intelligence course; at the end of the year started a daily meditation on impermanence (75 days so far); developed entrepreneurial thinking; worked with an accountant, a reels maker, an editor and hired an assistant; removed papillomas from her neck; read 41 books [#502].

What did not work: when she opened the 2025 plan and marked unfulfilled items red, the page turned almost completely red — and she calls this «хороший знак и точка роста» (a good sign and a growth point). Not done: did not experiment with formats; did not create original projects (secret wish: a meditation album with a musician); did not find a hobby; did not stop compulsively checking social media; did not finish sorting out nutrition; did not find supervision in Russian; did not decide on further education or save for it; did not meet a coach regularly; did not test new approaches to planning; did not surf or snowboard; did not assimilate in the US (and is glad not to have spent resource on it); did not run ads; did not start making reels regularly; filmed little and made no self-portraits; did not rebuild her schedule around circadian rhythms; did not sort out investing; did not go to Resonance. When planning the next year she will take the important, let go of what doesn't resonate, and get to know herself better [#502].

Conclusion: «План — не отражение реальности. А набросок.» (A plan is not a reflection of reality. It is a sketch.) — especially for someone whose key value is freedom. She often chooses what inspires her in the moment and does not pour goals and tasks into concrete; when there is a vector and an understanding of where she is going, that is enough. «План ради плана мне не нужен» (I don't need a plan for the sake of a plan) [#502].

#### Reading as reflection: three books and quotes that most influenced the author in 2025
Method: Katya does not just read — she makes notes in the book, then goes through it again and copies them out, so all good quotes and her own thoughts stay in her notes; at year end she went through these notes to pick her three main books [#503].
1. Jiddu Krishnamurti, «За пределами известного» (Beyond the Known). She discovered him only in 2025 and found that roughly every thought resonated. Quote: «Смотрите на себя. Наблюдайте себя. Исследуйте себя» (Look at yourself. Observe yourself. Explore yourself) — because in our present state we will create a monstrous world, and wherever we go, even to the Moon, Venus or Mars, we carry ourselves there; «Изменитесь сначала сами!» (Change yourselves first!); and «Это не учение, не весть. Вы можете это делать сами, если захотите.» (This is not a teaching, not a message. You can do it yourself if you want to.) [#503]
2. Oliver Burkeman, «4000 недель» (Four Thousand Weeks), long recommended by Maxim Koshulinsky of the wellness media @justakindreminder, which she calls her favourite Russian-language wellness media; she says it was exactly what she — and everyone — needed to read. Quote: by stopping making exorbitant demands of a "correctly lived" life, you can consider other ways of using your limited time meaningfully, and understand that the things you already do «более важны, чем вы предполагали» (are more important than you assumed) — you had simply been subconsciously devaluing them [#503].
3. Lobsang Tenpa, «Радикальное спокойствие» (Radical Calm). She met Lobsang in 2018 and only now read the book; she calls it the simplest and clearest way to understand what "meditation" is — and how much more it is than "working with concentration and stress relief", although it helps with those too. It inspired her to deeper study and practice: three-day retreats, the philosophical intelligence course, 75 days of impermanence meditation, three more Buddhist books (a fifth in progress). Quote: «Не относиться к себе слишком серьезно и проявлять к себе мягкость и сострадание: принять свои ограничения, но неуклонно желать себе измениться к лучшему.» (Don't take yourself too seriously and show yourself gentleness and compassion: accept your limitations but steadfastly wish yourself to change for the better.) [#503]

## Inner work: self-worth, shame, parts

### What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's posts on the psychology of the relationship with oneself: how to soften the inner critic through self-compassion, how self-worth differs from self-esteem, how shame is "architected" to block self-care, what basic self-care actually consists of, and how Katya Strelnikova works with her inner parts through IFS-based self-leadership. It also includes her account of anxiety under uncertainty and the Buddhist ideas (impermanence, the two arrows) that reframed it, and a literary excerpt (Pelevin) she links to IFS. According to the source, these matters "напрямую влияет и на баланс, и на энергию" (directly affect both balance and energy), and a positive inner dialogue is "крайне важен для снижения тревожности и улучшения общего физического самочувствия" (extremely important for reducing anxiety and improving overall physical well-being). [#116, #92]

These posts extend the topic's existing treatment of the inner critic (Neff's four-step writing practice in #92, the critic as one of the seven forms of the "architecture of shame" in #416, and the critic that "changes its words" in #323). They add: Katya Strelnikova's own history of a critic that held back her weight loss, her relationship with movement and her career change; a three-stage cycle explaining *why* self-criticism stalls change; a concrete compassionate-voice script; a four-item route of resources (test, two books, a course) with her verdicts and the science she cites; and a later, applied account of how self-acceptance and tolerating discomfort let her publish imperfect work. Why it matters, per the source: "невозможно построить что-то новое на фундаменте из самобичевания" (it is impossible to build something new on a foundation of self-flagellation), and self-compassion, not criticism or willpower, is what she says actually changed her life. [#676, #677, #727]

### Core ideas

- We are kinder to others than to ourselves, and that gap can be closed by practice: address yourself as you would a good friend; the more often you practise self-compassion, the more natural it becomes (see *Self-compassion writing practice*). [#92]
- Self-esteem without self-worth looks like success outside but feels like failure inside; full, happy living needs both (see *Self-esteem vs self-worth*). [#116]
- Shame has an architecture — seven recurring patterns (escapism, inner critic, insufficiency, perfectionism, toxic independence, devaluation, "I must earn it") that keep us from being ourselves and from caring for ourselves. It cannot be uprooted quickly, but it can be recognised, and a new "architecture in which you can simply be" can be built slowly (see *Architecture of shame*). [#416]
- Self-care is constantly confused with other things (grooming, laziness, procrastination, selfishness), for six named reasons; basic self-care is a concrete list of daily-to-monthly actions (see the checklist and *Six reasons*). [#149, #150, #145]
- A person is much more than one part. Parts are spectra, not monoliths; conflict arises when their radical expressions collide; when you can see who is speaking, you can do something with it (see *Parts map* and *Part-conflict pattern*). [#323, #528]
- The critic does not vanish, it changes its words: even after "working through" it, it may return in subtler forms that do not block action but clutter the head and drain energy. [#323]
- Anxiety under uncertainty is physiology, not proof of failure; the deeper cause, in Katya's case, was resistance to change and the "second arrow" of her own thoughts (see *Impermanence and two arrows*). [#435]

- The critic cannot simply be dropped, but it can be noticed and disbelieved. "Сейчас я знаю, что просто отказаться от критика невозможно. Но можно научиться замечать его слова. И ему не верить." (Now I know it is impossible to simply give up the critic. But you can learn to notice its words. And not believe it.) This consistently echoes part 1's observation that the critic does not vanish but changes its words (#323). [#676]
- Self-criticism produces a closed loop, not change — inadequacy → pressure → rebellion → back to inadequacy (see *Three-stage vicious circle*). "Никто не способен долго ехать на топливе ненависти к себе." (Nobody can travel long on the fuel of self-hatred.) [#676]
- There is a second, compassionate voice, and using it is a skill learnable at any age (see *Compassionate voice*). Katya's own self-compassion test score is her evidence that it is trainable. [#676, #677]
- The critic is an evolutionary and neural default, not a personal defect — understanding this stops you criticising yourself for having a critic (see the four-step route). [#677]
- Self-compassion, not criticism or willpower, is what works long-term, because when you treat yourself with care you cannot do yourself harm. [#677]
- Self-acceptance is the same skill as seeing beauty in imperfect things, turned toward oneself; the critic can remain while perfectionism retires (see *Six self-observed skills*). [#727]

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Self-compassion writing practice (Kristin Neff)
Purpose / when to use. Posted by Marina Sidorich in the rubric #осознанность_набалансе (tools for self-reflection and positive thinking) as "Как перестать себя ругать и справится [sic] с внутренним критиком?" (How to stop scolding yourself and cope with the inner critic?). The practice is attributed to K. Neff, "пионера в исследованиях самопринятия и самосострадания" (a pioneer in research on self-acceptance and self-compassion), and is meant to help you become aware of the negative inner voice and develop a positive dialogue and self-acceptance. The rationale given: research shows we are usually kinder to others than to ourselves, while a positive inner dialogue matters for lower anxiety and better physical well-being. The authors themselves set aside a notebook, a pen and calendar time for such practices and invite readers to do the same. It is a written exercise (each step begins with ✍️ — write it down). [#92]

1. Recall a loved one in trouble. Remember the last time someone close to you got into an unpleasant situation or felt bad. Write down what you would do and say to them, and pay attention to the *tone of your voice*. This sets the baseline of how you naturally treat others. [#92]
2. Recall your own difficulty. Remember a time when you were struggling — possibly it is happening now. Write down what you would do and say to yourself, again noting the tone of voice. [#92]
3. Compare and ask why. Was there a difference between how you reacted to the loved one and to yourself? If so, ask yourself why it happens, and write down what makes you treat others and yourself differently. [#92]
4. Imagine the change. Write down what could change if you spoke to yourself the same way you speak to your loved one. [#92]

Ongoing application. When something in life does not go to plan, address yourself the way you would address a good friend, husband or child, and observe what comes of it. "Чем чаще практиковать сострадание к себе, тем естественнее и привычнее оно станет" (The more often you practise self-compassion, the more natural and habitual it becomes). [#92]

#### Self-esteem vs self-worth
Origin. Katya came to this "(банальную)" (banal) idea she had never thought about while reading Jamie Kern Lima's book «Worthy». She notes that in Russian the closest phrase would be "чувство собственного достоинства" (sense of one's own dignity), but for her that wording has other associations, and jokes that "Самоценность — такая хрень, что даже автокоррект подчеркивает" ("Self-worth" is such a thing that even autocorrect underlines it). The accompanying voice podcast is not available in the export. [#116]

1. Self-worth (самоценность) — "глубокое внутреннее убеждение, что я ценна и заслуживаю уважения, любви и принадлежности, независимо от внешних достижений, социального статуса или одобрения других людей" (a deep inner conviction that I am valuable and deserve respect, love and belonging, regardless of external achievements, social status or other people's approval). It means being valued as I am — I do not need to pretend, play along or please to receive it. Self-worth "просто есть, и не меняется, что бы ни происходило" (simply is, and does not change, whatever happens). [#116]
2. Self-esteem (самооценка) — "как я оцениваю себя на основании качеств, навыков и успехов" (how I evaluate myself on the basis of qualities, skills and successes). Because it is tied to external factors that change often, it too can fluctuate. [#116]
3. The imbalance. Usually we invest all our time and attention in pumping up self-esteem and achievements, but "самооценка без самоценности кажется успехом снаружи, но внутри ощущается как провал" (self-esteem without self-worth looks like success on the outside but feels like failure inside). [#116]
4. Both are needed. To be maximally fulfilled and happy you need both; the higher both indicators, the more whole, emotionally and physically healthy and happy we feel. Katya calls this the path to self-acceptance, authenticity and happiness. [#116]

#### Six reasons we confuse self-care with something else

#### Architecture of shame («Архитектура стыда: что мешает о себе заботиться»)
Purpose. Katya's model of how shame blocks self-care, written after the podcast «Что они скажут» with Dasha, where she shared personal experience of how shame gets in the way of caring for oneself. The architecture of shame "создается, чтобы мы случайно не начали быть собой" (is built so that we don't accidentally start being ourselves) — and instead keep staying within convention. She frames it playfully: "Напишите, сколько покемонов вы собрали" (Write how many Pokémon you've collected), i.e. how many of the seven forms you recognise in yourself. Each form is given with how it sounds or looks, how it gets in the way, and the result. [#416]

1. Escapism (Эскапизм).
   - *Forms:* workaholism, addictions, over-eating/under-eating, endless relationships/break-ups and the like.
   - *How it blocks:* real desires and needs are not recognised; we live in flight rather than in contact.
   - *Result:* we don't hear fatigue and irritation until we break down; we don't understand what we actually want — because we always live by someone else's programme; we forget about the body until it screams with pain. [#416]
2. Inner critic (Внутренний критик).
   - *Sounds like:* "Ты опять облажалась" (You screwed up again), "Серьезно? Вот это ты называешь прогрессом?" (Seriously? You call this progress?), "Ты могла бы сделать лучше" (You could have done better).
   - *How it blocks:* every undertaking ends up under fire; we either don't act at all or start with anxiety and finish with burnout.
   - *Result:* we postpone self-care — "сначала надо похудеть, привести себя в порядок" (first I need to lose weight, get myself in order); we don't go to the gym — "все там прокачанные, а я как мешок" (everyone there is buff and I'm like a sack); we don't try new practices because "все равно заброшу" (I'll quit anyway). [#416]
3. Insufficiency (Недостаточность).
   - *Sounds like:* "Я не как все" (I'm not like everyone), "Мне чего-то не хватает" (Something is missing in me), "Я слишком …" (I'm too …).
   - *How it blocks:* any movement requires a preliminary "fixing".
   - *Result:* we don't allow ourselves to hear our true needs, to be "just ourselves" — because "меня такой не полюбят" (nobody will love me like this); we postpone self-care — "не заслужила" (I haven't earned it); we don't let ourselves rest just like that — only as a reward for results. [#416]
4. Perfectionism (written "Перперфекционизм" [sic]).
   - *Sounds like:* "Если не сделаю идеально — зачем вообще начинать" (If I won't do it perfectly, why start at all), "Если отдохну — все развалится" (If I rest, everything will fall apart), "Я должна быть лучше всех, чтобы иметь право на место" (I must be better than everyone to have the right to a place).
   - *How it blocks:* inability to rest, overload, devaluing one's own successes.
   - *Result:* we don't allow ourselves to "do enough" — more is always needed; even health becomes a project: perfect eating, perfect habits and no flexibility; we rest with a sense of guilt. [#416]
5. Toxic independence (Токсичная независимость).
   - *Sounds like:* "Я сама справлюсь" (I'll manage on my own), "Просить — стыдно" (Asking is shameful), "Лучше не делиться, чем быть непонятой" (Better not to share than to be misunderstood).
   - *How it blocks:* no access to support; we carry everything on ourselves.
   - *Result:* we don't go to therapy or coaching because "сама должна справляться" (I should cope myself); we don't ask for help even when we can no longer cope; we hold on until we break down into illness. [#416]
6. Devaluation (Обесценивание).
   - *Sounds like:* "Ну ничего особенного" (Well, nothing special), "Да мне просто повезло" (I just got lucky), "Да это вообще ерунда" (It's nothing at all).
   - *How it blocks:* no sense of satisfaction with life, no sense of progress; successes don't consolidate.
   - *Result:* we constantly feel we "еще ничего не достигли" (haven't achieved anything yet); we don't praise ourselves for real steps and successes; we don't celebrate small wins, and energy drains away. [#416]
7. The belief "I must earn it" (Установка «Я должна заслужить»).
   - *Sounds like:* "Отдых только после результата" (Rest only after results), "Нельзя просто так" (You can't just do it for no reason), "Я должна быть полезной" (I must be useful).
   - *How it blocks:* self-care provokes anxiety, as if you were breaking a rule.
   - *Result:* we don't allow ourselves what we want or what would give pleasure and energy, even when we can; we shame ourselves for any pleasure; we live in "deferred life" mode: "еще чуть-чуть — и тогда смогу" (just a little more — and then I'll be able to). [#416]

#### Working with shame — what to do
1. Accept the timescale. "Стыд не искоренить быстро и навсегда" (Shame cannot be eradicated quickly and forever), but you can start to recognise it and slowly build a new architecture "в которой можно просто быть" (in which you can simply be). [#416]
2. First step: learn to hear the voice of shame. Distinguish when we act from love and understanding vs from fear; from trust in ourselves vs to prove something; from contact vs from flight. [#416]
3. Next: gradually "unstick" from it and practise sufficiency ("Дальше потихоньку разлепляться с этим и практиковать достаточность"). [#416]
4. Where to start — three routes:
   - *Therapy:* "Ну конечно" (Well, of course).
   - *Self-inquiry:* keep a journal; notice in which moments shame switches on, how it sounds, which beliefs stand behind it; try rewriting those beliefs from the position of a caring adult ("от лица заботливого взрослого").
   - *Coaching:* Katya invites people to come explore and change with her (a self-promotional item; booking link dropped).
   She closes: "Это не быстрый путь, но свобода быть собой того стоит" (This isn't a quick path, but the freedom to be yourself is worth it). [#416]

#### Katya's self-leadership parts map («Само-лидерство: Карта частей»)
Context. Katya took the first part of a self-leadership intensive by @talkauthentic (based on IFS); it was among her goals for the year. She has been "talking with her parts" in one way or another since 2018, when she went into psychodrama and then transactional analysis; IFS is "тоже про части, но немного с другого бока" (also about parts, but from a slightly different angle). Her request for the course: "Выделить наконец легальное время на знакомство со своими внутренними частями и рефлексию" (To finally set aside legitimate time to get to know my inner parts and to reflect). Her motivation: she often found herself after something destructive had already happened (lying down watching a series, overeating chocolate, choosing suffering or violence against herself) without fully understanding what triggered it; or facing far too much resistance out of nowhere; or doubting whether some tasks really deserve so much strain. Within the two weeks of the intensive she made many discoveries, and they "все еще долетают" (are still arriving). [#323]

Key shift. She used to think of parts as whole chunks (Critic, Child, Parent — the classic transactional-analysis triad); now they "раскололись как спелый арбуз — из слипшейся мешанины появилась ясность" (split like a ripe watermelon — clarity emerged from the stuck-together mush). Each of her five main parts has several faces, "same-same, but different". She pictures the main parts as herself in different periods of life, and caught their shades through music tracks she once played to death — the map is built in the Freeform app as a "карта частей с плейлистом" (parts map with a playlist), with a song attached to sub-parts (e.g. on the Kaprizulya card: "Can't Buy Me Love" by The Beatles, "Where Is The Love?" by Black Eyed Peas, "Another Love" by Tom Odell, "Ask the Mountains" by Vangelis, "Home Again" by Michael Kiwanuka, and a track titled 5 Причин). #### Part-conflict pattern
Thesis: "Каждая большая часть — спектр, и может быть вполне себе нетоксичной и даже полезной. Конфликт случается, когда сталкиваются радикальные проявления частей" (Every big part is a spectrum and can be quite non-toxic and even useful. Conflict happens when the radical expressions of parts collide). Katya's four examples of such collisions, each a chain: [#323]
1. When Big Lover of Plans overdoes it, the Controller switches on — the Spontaneous Creator can't get a word in, "Да и хй с ним" (Oh, screw it) arrives — and everything goes down the drain. [#323]
2. Conversely, when the Spontaneous Creator runs too wild, Big Lover of Plans suffers and tends to switch on the Controller (and then the same chain follows). [#323]
3. When the Disciplined Kitty pushes the gas too hard on wellness routines, and Гуляй, рванина feels it isn't fun enough, Сгорел сарай (The barn burned down) wakes up. [#323]
4. When Katya the Almighty "рвет рубаху, стоя на стульчике" (*tears her shirt standing on a little chair* — i.e. grandstanding), a trembling Scaredy-pants crawls out, often retreating into Under the blanket. [#323]

How she uses it. In moments of unpleasant emotion or anxiety she asks herself "а что тут за конфликт" (what's the conflict here?), looks for the parts that have fallen out over something, and helps them negotiate. She admits it doesn't work perfectly yet — "это ведь только начало" (this is only the beginning) — and keeps observing and collecting cases. [#323]

#### Hearing parts in solitude (time "without content" but "in contact")
What. A year after the intensive, Katya reports she has become "спокойнее, устойчивее и добрее к себе" (calmer, more stable and kinder to myself). The most important change, she thinks, was an "убойным комбо" (killer combo) of three things: (1) she began to genuinely prioritise time alone with herself; (2) she realised she is much more than one part; (3) she began to hear her "тяни-толкаи" (push-me-pull-yous) very clearly. [#528]

How. She knew formally that she needed time "без контента" (without content) but "в контакте" (in contact). Her journal entry from the intensive: she feels unity with herself most often alone, in nature, or in a practice such as floating, Kundalini or a workout — "Как будто все части просто на нейтралке — никто никуда не тянет. Я просто есть" (As if all the parts are simply in neutral — nobody pulls anywhere. I simply am); nowhere to hurry, nothing to do, no one to be. [#528]

Why. This time is not only rest and "reboot" — it is a space where it becomes audible what exactly is happening to her. Alone, the inner push-me-pull-yous stop merging into the hum of "со мной что-то не так" (something is wrong with me) and "нужно срочно что-то делать" (I urgently need to do something), which used to lure her into days of fruitless anxiety. Now she clearly hears parts that used to be stuck together and indistinguishable. "Когда ты видишь, кто говорит, с этим уже можно что-то делать" (When you see who is speaking, you can already do something about it). [#528]

Example — her opposing pairs: [#528]
- a part sure that *if I don't do more, I won't be loved*;
- another whispering *if I do more, it doesn't count*;
- one that wants to be invisible;
- and next to it, one that strains toward the stage;
- one part strives for lightness and freedom;
- another is still sure that nothing will come without hard grind.

Result. Before, all this was experienced as chaos, doubts, relapses and even illness; now as a normal inner conflict that can be seen, named and worked through rather than swept "под внутреннюю кровать" (under the inner bed). Her biggest upgrade: "не становиться какой-то новой, а наконец начать слышать тех, кто уже внутри" (not becoming someone new, but finally starting to hear those who are already inside) — and only then moving forward. [#528]

#### Brain as a prediction machine, impermanence and the two arrows
Context. Katya describes an acute stress period: her husband lost his job at the end of July, meaning loss of the main income source and a new emigration; anxiety went "х1000". Whenever life becomes calm she forgets how the brain works and naively thinks "Какая я уже проработанная" (How worked-through I already am) — and the stress proved otherwise. She is coping much better than in "previous episodes", but body and psyche show there is room to grow; a 6-week laboratory on "Philosophical intelligence" exploring Buddhist philosophy helped her see where. [#435]

1. Mechanism of anxiety. "Мозг — машина прогнозирования" (The brain is a prediction machine). When the future holds too many unknowns, it sends a request for certainty; getting no answer, it marks the situation as dangerous and launches the stress response — "бей, беги или замри" (fight, flight or freeze). At that moment body and mind are aimed not at development but at survival. [#435]
2. Her reactions (an inventory she invites readers to compare with theirs): fairly gloomy tunnel thinking; an "echo of war" in the form of a harsh controller and critic waking up; possible headache; rapid heartbeat; difficulty breathing; loss of focus; an urge to get stuck in content; difficulty letting go of thoughts even in calm states (on a walk, floating, in bed); sleep problems (wild dreams, waking in the night and not getting back to sleep); loss of inspiration (much less desire to film and share). [#435]
3. Rational understanding doesn't override physiology. Even when she rationally knows everything will be fine and there is no scenario in which they won't cope, "физиология остается физиологией" (physiology remains physiology) — sleep breaks down, inspiration leaves, thoughts jump. People with a "подвижной психикой" (mobile psyche), as she was called since childhood, feel this especially vividly. [#435]
4. Principle of impermanence. Everything changes — any form, feeling, situation, state. "Мы страдаем не из-за перемен, а из-за того, что сопротивляемся им и цепляемся за стабильность, которой в природе вещей просто не существует" (We suffer not because of change, but because we resist it and cling to a stability that simply does not exist in the nature of things). [#435]
5. Concept of the two arrows. The first arrow is the pain itself (a fact, event, loss). The second is how we finish ourselves off with that pain — with thoughts, analysis, "а что если" (what if), "почему именно я" (why me). "Стреляет уже не жизнь, мы сами в себя тыкаем этой стрелой" (It's no longer life shooting — we jab ourselves with this arrow). [#435]
6. Applied insight. Her anxiety turned out to be caused neither by the emigration (it hadn't even begun) nor by money (their lifestyle hadn't changed yet), but by two things: resistance to change and "непереносимой печали по будущей утрате" (unbearable sadness over a future loss) — i.e. not yet knowing how to be in uncertainty without trying to control everything. Conclusion: "дело не в обстоятельствах, а в непринятии принципа непостоянства и тыканья в себя своими мыслями на этом фоне" (it's not the circumstances, but non-acceptance of the principle of impermanence and jabbing myself with my thoughts against that background). [#435]
7. Her practice going forward. She decided to devote the next year to exploring and practising impermanence — learning to let go of control and stop fighting for everything to be "понятно и навсегда" (clear and forever). She added a daily meditation on impermanence to her tracker (2 days done at the time of writing). The lab's format: each week one Buddhist concept — listening to explanations, meditating, reflecting and discussing in a group. [#435]

#### Three-stage vicious circle of the inner critic
Context. Post title: "«Жирная уродина, у которой ничего никогда не получается»" ("A fat ugly woman who never succeeds at anything") — "Мотивационные лозунги, которые НЕ помогли мне меняться" (Motivational slogans that did NOT help me change). Katya explains that she reacts relatively calmly to online hate ("ну ладно, спокойнее многих" — *well, calmer than many*) because she had already said all of it to herself. Examples of her own critic's lines: "Ты недостаточно ходишь" (You don't walk enough), "Можно было съесть салат вместо пасты" (You could have eaten salad instead of pasta), "Все уже давно похудели" (Everyone lost weight long ago), "Куда тебе в хэлс-коучи, ты себя видела?" (What business do you have being a health coach — have you seen yourself?), "Сначала надо закончить вот этот курс" (First you need to finish this course), "Пора уже разобраться с белком и силовыми" (It's time to finally sort out protein and strength training). [#676]

She listened to this voice for about 20 years and took it for truth, and her changes stalled; only in the last 10 years did she gradually learn to make it quieter, "И движение пошло" (And movement started). While she listened to it, every breakdown became a verdict ("ты слабая, у тебя нет силы воли, никогда не получится" — *you're weak, you have no willpower, it will never work*); every missed workout became proof that "я не спортивный человек" (I'm not a sporty person); every thought of a career change turned into "кто ты вообще такая, чтобы кого-то коучить?" (who are you even to coach anyone?). She thinks this is part of why her path of change took about 8 years. She now recognises the melody "ты недостаточно хороша" (you're not good enough) from a single note, and describes the process literally as three stages (she invites readers to add anything she missed): [#676]

1. Stage 1 — Feeling of inadequacy, "со мной что-то не так" (something is wrong with me). All self-improvement is built not from "я хочу" (I want) but from "я плохая, надо исправить" (I'm bad, I need fixing). Nothing sustainable can be built from this place, because the goal becomes not "become better" but "stop being bad". There is no joy in it: even when something works out, we do not enjoy the achievement — we only stop feeling terrible for a second, and the feeling returns instantly because the critic has not gone anywhere. [#676]
2. Stage 2 — Excessive pressure. It has to be perfect and no other way; it is always "недостаточно" (not enough). Fixation, perfectionism and comparisons switch on. "Мы не можем не мочь. Мы всегда должны, вне зависимости от обстоятельств." (We can't not be able to. We always must, regardless of circumstances.) This exhausts the nervous system and inflames the inner relationship with oneself ("привет вашему несчастному внутреннему ребенку" — *greetings to your unhappy inner child*). After a while comes Stage 3. [#676]
3. Stage 3 — Inner rebellion. Body and psyche say "с меня хватит" (I've had enough). A breakdown, apathy and rollback begin — "сгорел сарай, гори и хата" (*the barn has burned, let the house burn too*, i.e. all-or-nothing giving up). Katya went through this dozens of times — with food, workouts, any habits — and each time concluded she simply hadn't tried hard enough. And the circle closes: back to Stage 1. [#676]

Lesson. Change powered by self-hatred cannot last; the loop itself, not lack of willpower, is why changes stall. (Cross-reference: the phrase "Сгорел сарай" also names one of Katya's Kaprizulya sub-parts in her part-1 parts map, #323/#324.) [#676]

#### Compassionate voice vs critic voice — four moves
What. "Хорошая новость: голос критика — не единственный, который может звучать в вашей голове." (Good news: the critic's voice is not the only one that can sound in your head.) There is another one — compassionate — "Тот самый, которым мы так легко говорим с близкими, но не умеем с собой" (the very one we so easily use with loved ones but don't know how to use with ourselves). This restates, in Katya's words, the premise of Neff's writing practice in part 1 (#92). [#676]

Replacement phrases (compassionate ← instead of critic): [#676]
- "Сегодня было тяжело, понятно, почему не получилось" (Today was hard, it's understandable why it didn't work out) instead of "Ты опять облажалась" (You screwed up again).
- "Одна пропущенная тренировка — не приговор" (One missed workout isn't a verdict) instead of "Ты никогда не изменишься" (You'll never change).
- "Что тебе сейчас на самом деле нужно?" (What do you really need right now?) instead of "Соберись, тряпка" (Pull yourself together, you wimp).

The four moves behind them: [#676]
1. Acknowledge that it is hard ("Признаем, что сложно").
2. Separate the situation from self-worth ("Отделяем ситуацию от самоценности") — a failed day says nothing about your value (links to the part-1 distinction between self-worth and self-esteem, #116).
3. Look for how to help, not what to blame ("Ищем, чем помочь, а не в чем обвинить").
4. Act from care, not guilt ("Действуем из заботы, а не вины").

"Good news two": it is a skill ("это навык"), learnable at any age. [#676]

#### Four-step route to a constructive relationship with the inner critic
Purpose. Follow-up post: "Как победить внутреннего критика и наконец-то похудеть / завести блог / сменить профессию / пойти на танцы / подставьте свой вариант" (How to defeat the inner critic and finally lose weight / start a blog / change profession / go dancing / insert your own). Diagnostic: if "недостаточно", "не то", "куда тебе", "надо было лучше" (not enough, not right, who are you to, should have done better) sound inside constantly, you are most likely treading water — whatever you want to change, "воз и ныне там" (the cart is still where it was), although you have approached it more than once and are tired of rehashing it. The goal is not to destroy the critic but to build "конструктивные отношения" (constructive relations) with it so it does not stop you moving forward. Katya invites readers to share other materials. [#677]

1. Start with a test to see where you are now. Kristin Neff's site has a self-compassion test, which Katya calls great. Her result: 4.05 out of 5, with an average of 3.0; several years earlier it would probably have been about 2. She regrets not finding the test earlier, so she has no documented "before/after" ("придется вам поверить на слово" — *you'll have to take my word for it*), but says the difference is colossal. Lesson she draws: "самосострадание не врожденная черта, а навык, который тренируется" (self-compassion is not an innate trait but a skill that is trained). [#677]
2. Read Russ Harris's «Ловушка счастья» (The Happiness Trap) — to stop blaming yourself for having a critic. Criticising yourself for being self-critical is "совсем тупо" (really dumb) — though she admits she did it. Harris's explanation, in Katya's two-word summary: the brain is evolutionarily tuned not for happiness but for survival. It scans for threats, compares us with others and fixates on failures — "Это не баг, а фича" (It's not a bug, it's a feature). Our ancestors survived precisely because they were anxious and self-critical; whoever thought "I'm great, everything's fine" relaxed and died. "Мы — потомки паникеров." (We are descendants of panickers.) Realising this is half the job: the critic stops being "our problem" and becomes just the background noise of an ancient mechanism. [#677]
3. Watch Kelly McGonigal's course The Neuroscience of Self-Compassion — to believe it can be trained. In the course McGonigal dissects criticism from a neurobiological angle. Per Katya's account: the critic lives in the brain's passive-mode (default) network, which switches on as soon as we are without a task — in the shower, in a traffic jam, before sleep, i.e. at rest. Neither the network nor the critic can be switched off, but the brain can be trained to switch over. Katya cites research (a study linked in the post) that after eight weeks of self-compassion practice people's self-criticism drops significantly, and MRI scans show neural connections restructuring: connectivity with regions responsible for self-regulation strengthens, and connectivity with the amygdala (the centre of anxiety and threat) weakens. "можно в буквальном смысле поменять свой мозг" (you can literally change your brain). [#677]
4. Read Kristin Neff's «Сочувствие к себе» (Self-Compassion) and do all its practices. Neff was the first to introduce the concept of self-compassion into science and broke it into three pillars (see *Neff's three pillars*). The book is full of exercises; Katya says she developed all this for years "вслепую" (blindly), whereas here is a ready-made system. Bonus for English readers: Neff's site has a whole section of free meditations and exercises — nothing needs to be bought to start; "Просто зайдите и попробуйте одну практику сегодня" (Just go there and try one practice today). [#677]

Her conclusion after years: "именно самосострадание, а не критика и не сила воли, изменило мою жизнь по-настоящему" (it was self-compassion, not criticism and not willpower, that truly changed my life). Mechanism: when you treat yourself with care, you simply cannot do yourself harm — you don't lose weight through starvation but feed yourself balanced food; you don't force yourself to move but look for what brings joy; you don't pretend a job suits you but allow yourself to try. This turned out to be "единственным, что реально работает вдолгую" (the only thing that really works in the long run). [#677]

#### Kristin Neff's three pillars of self-compassion
As relayed by Katya, who names the pillars without elaborating on them: [#677]
1. Kindness to oneself instead of judgement ("доброта к себе вместо осуждения").
2. Recognising that imperfection is a common human experience, not a personal catastrophe ("признание, что несовершенство — общий человеческий опыт, а не личная катастрофа").
3. Developing mindfulness ("развитие осознанности").

#### Six self-observed skills of sharing imperfectly (self-observation through a practicum)
Context. After a three-week Visual (photography) practicum, Katya writes "Предлагалось понаблюдать за осенью, а получилось понаблюдать за собой" (We were invited to observe autumn, but it turned out I observed myself), and concludes, half-jokingly, that she is "destined to be a blogger". The five reasons are mostly inner-work skills, the last three directly about self-acceptance and the critic: [#727]

1. She shoots content whether or not she posts it — every day, in quantity, so there is always something to post even in the busiest week. She was not always like this: it is learned and becomes habitual through the number of repetitions. [#727]
2. She finds it easy to see beauty. Her top strength in Seligman's model, year after year, is appreciation of beauty. She is not an artist and "art" doesn't move her much; she doesn't need to convey a deep thought or evoke strong emotions — "мне понравилось, посмотрите и вы" (I liked it, you have a look too) is enough, which she calls the formula of blogging. [#727]
3. She sees beauty in different things. She doesn't need a perfect picture — and knows that for many the main barrier is "у меня некрасиво, нечего показывать" (mine isn't pretty, nothing to show). She used to need dazzling beauty too; now she can melt with tenderness before a prefab panel block, peeling paint or a crack in the asphalt. After moving to Belgrade (brutal panel blocks, peeling façades, graffiti, dog poop and dead pigeons), she can't stop shooting, whereas a couple of years ago she would have decided it was ugly and been upset. Lesson: the eye is trainable. [#727]
4. She accepts herself and posts even when she doesn't like herself. "Тот же навык, только направленный на себя" (The same skill, only directed at oneself). The practicum suggested "любить себя по частям" (loving yourself in parts), which Katya says she has long done, in life and with clients: "Не обязательно нравиться себе целиком в каждом кадре. Достаточно увидеть, что в нем есть что-то живое и настоящее." (You don't have to like yourself entirely in every frame. It's enough to see that there is something alive and real in it.) [#727]
5. She said goodbye to perfectionism and can tolerate discomfort. Every day she posted her two frames and thought how untalented they were — and often heard back that they were great. "Мой критик никуда не делся. Но я научилась выдерживать дискомфорт и делиться неидеальным и недостаточно талантливым." (My critic hasn't gone anywhere. But I learned to tolerate the discomfort and share the imperfect and not-talented-enough.) "критик есть, а перфекционист уже курит в сторонке" (the critic is here, but the perfectionist is already smoking on the sidelines). [#727]
6. She weaned herself off trying too hard ("Отучила себя стараться"). Last summer she noticed she was always trying too hard even when it wasn't required, and decided to stop; now she doesn't need perfect preparation — "Херак-херак и в продакшн" (slap-dash and into production). Her photography rule: at least five shots (more often 10) per picture; out of five imperfect ones there is almost always one she likes. [#727]

Note: the post announces "reasons" without a fixed count; the source lists six bold items above (shooting daily, seeing beauty, seeing beauty in different things, self-acceptance, goodbye to perfectionism, not trying too hard). She adds a seventh candidate in passing: reflecting on events ("переосмыслять события") is "тоже скилл" (also a skill) that should be added to the list — the post itself "fell out of her" when she sat down to reflect on the practicum. [#727]

### Detailed insights

#### The critic changes its words rather than disappearing
Katya was sure she had worked through her critic before her career change, but it turned out "он просто теперь говорит другими словами" (it just speaks in other words now). Earlier, "по методичке" (by the textbook), it said "У тебя не получится!" (You won't succeed!) and blocked everything new at the root — that version she did deal with. Now it says things like: "нельзя идти на подкаст, если ты не готовилась часов восемь" (you can't go on a podcast if you haven't prepared for about eight hours), "такого контента и так куча, надо что-то особенное" (there's already loads of content like this, you need something special), "ща они тебя раскусят" (they're about to see through you), "говоришь про деньги — денег не будет" (talk about money — there won't be any money), "че-то ты выбиваешься из своих же целей" (you're falling behind your own goals) — "and all that about too much and too little". It no longer blocks actions, but it so clutters the head that she finds it frightening to imagine how much energy would be freed if all this were worked through. The same critic and controller resurfaced as an "echo of war" under the stress described in #435. [#323, #435]

#### Golden shadow

She first voiced it aloud at a quarterly review with her coach and was so frightened that she felt it was too early to talk about it publicly. (!) #### "This isn't like me" — the danger of over-identifying with one part
Once you hear the parts, it becomes easier to notice "это НЕ я (полностью), это всего лишь части меня" (*this is NOT me (entirely), these are only parts of me*). Previously she could fall into one part and think "просто я такой человек" (I'm just that kind of person) or "на меня это непохоже" (that's not like me). From both her personal practice and her professional work with parts and identities, she sees how dangerous it is to get carried away with one part while ignoring the others — not only because of inner conflict. When we say "это на меня не похоже", we often mean "это не вписывается в мой привычный образ себя" (this doesn't fit my habitual self-image); then any change turns into an endless struggle: you can want the new as much as you like but unconsciously sabotage everything that doesn't match the chosen role. [#528]

#### Pelevin's "fourth": the observer beyond the inner prosecutor and advocate
Whenever she writes about IFS, Katya always recalls her favourite chapter 8 of Pelevin's Chapaev and Void (Чапаев и Пустота). The excerpt she shares (a dialogue between Volodin and Shurik): the inner prosecutor (who can "arrest" you, like Raskolnikov) and the inner advocate (who tries to get you off) are both "какая-то моя часть" (some part of me) — "сам на себя дело завожу и сам себя отмазываю" (I open a case against myself and get myself off). Volodin asks him to imagine that even when the prosecutor has arrested you and all the advocates have failed, there remains "кто-то четвертый, которого никто никуда не тащит" (some fourth one whom nobody drags anywhere) — neither prosecutor, nor accused, nor advocate. This fourth cannot be seen or even felt ("Никто" — *Nobody*), yet "этих прокуроров и адвокатов нет. Да и тебя, в сущности, тоже. Уж если кто-то и есть, так это он" (these prosecutors and advocates don't exist. Nor do you, essentially. If anyone exists, it's him). And to get to that state, "ничего делать не надо. Как только ты что-нибудь делать начинаешь, сразу дело заводится" (you don't need to do anything. As soon as you start doing something, a case is opened right away). Katya does not interpret the excerpt; the parallel to her own description of parts "на нейтралке" (in neutral) where "Я просто есть" is drawn here only as an archival cross-reference (!) UNCLEAR whether she intends this link. [#529, img #529, #528]

Personal note with a lesson about turning points: after a 2017 performance at Praktika theatre based on this book she "так прооралась" (cried/screamed it out so hard) that she then read all of Pelevin in a year and watched all of Didenko's productions in Moscow. Writing this, she realised that although she always dated the start of her transformation (after which her life turned 180 degrees) to 2018, the count may actually begin from that winter-2017 play "после которой уже не вернулась" (after which I never came back).

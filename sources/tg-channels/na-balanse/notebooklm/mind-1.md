## Emotions as energy

### What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's posts on emotional regulation viewed as energy management: Lisa Feldman Barrett's theory that emotions are constructed by the brain and depend on the body's resource "budget", her five ways to manage emotions, a step-by-step approach to cognitive reappraisal, Katya Strelnikova's personal daily routine and her experiment with embodiment practices, and Paul Gilbert's three emotion-regulation systems as presented by a guest psychologist. According to the source it matters because unexpressed anger, accumulated resentment and endless anxiety drain energy, and because an imbalance between the internal regulation systems — not only between work and rest — decides whether a person has the strength to live [#73, #591].

### Core ideas

- Your emotional state depends on your resources. Marina observes that the better she sleeps, the more balanced she eats and the more physically active she is, the happier and calmer she is; so emotional state — and therefore relationships with loved ones — depend on the amount of resources in body and head (see *Brain body budget*) [#70].
- Emotions are constructed, so they are more controllable than thought. Per Barrett, emotions are not predetermined reactions; the brain creates them from past experience, cultural influences and the balance of physical resources — so a person has more control over emotional reactions than was previously believed [#70].
- Managing emotions means managing energy. Katya's thesis: unexpressed anger, resentment and anxiety drain energy, and mood swings decide for you what you will do today (see *Emotions drain energy*) [#73].
- Reappraisal is a trainable skill. Cognitive reappraisal starts from awareness, needs precise naming of emotions and positive recategorization, and requires practice and support (see *Three-step approach to cognitive reappraisal*) [#106].
- The body can show what the mind misses. Embodiment practices surfaced patterns and decisions Katya had only been "chewing over" in the background (see *Embodiment self-experiment*) [#243].
- Chronic tiredness comes from living only in red and blue. Modern life alternates threat and achievement, and even rest is driven by achievement; without the green safety system the nervous system does not recover (see *Three emotion-regulation systems*) [#591].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Brain body budget (Lisa Feldman Barrett)
Barrett, as Marina presents her, uses a metaphor of the brain as a financial director who allocates resources — for example glucose, oxygen and water — that are needed for survival and well-being. She calls this the "brain body budget". "Если у вас дефицит бюджета тела, вы можете чувствовать усталость или стресс." (If you have a body budget deficit, you may feel fatigue or stress.) Marina uses it to explain her own experience: on the same situation with her child — even one that looks funny from the outside — she can react anywhere from calm and patience to irritation and rudeness, and she can snap "совершенно на пустом месте" (out of nowhere); the difference is how well-resourced her body and head are at that moment [#70].

#### Barrett's five ways to increase the body budget and manage emotions
Purpose: what Barrett proposes to increase one's "brain body budget" and control emotions better. All five, as presented by Marina [#70]:
1. Mindfulness and meditation (Осознанность и медитация). Recognizing and acknowledging your emotions is the first step to controlling them. The point is to understand your feelings and inner state without judgment or evaluation. Mindfulness helps you stay open to your emotions and respond to them more constructively [#70].
2. Cognitive reappraisal (Когнитивная переоценка). Revising how you perceive the situation or event that triggers the emotional reaction. Instead of reacting automatically to a stimulus, you can become aware of and rethink your behavior. The reasoning: "Если наши реакции — это программный код, который записан на прошлом опыте, его можно изменить." (If our reactions are program code written on past experience, it can be changed.) The how-to is expanded in the next framework [#70].
3. Maintaining a healthy lifestyle (Поддержание здорового образа жизни). Physical health — quality nutrition, good sleep and regular exercise — has a direct influence on emotional well-being, because it gives the body exactly those resources the body budget runs on [#70].
4. Stress management (Стресс-менеджмент). Various relaxation techniques — breathing practices, meditation, yoga or deep muscle relaxation — lower the stress level and improve the ability to regulate emotions [#70].
5. Seeking support (Поиск поддержки). Talking with close friends, family or a helping professional. Understanding and support from others make the process of managing emotions more effective [#70].

Where to start: Marina's starting habit is to ask yourself at least once a day "А как я сейчас себя чувствую?" (How am I feeling right now?) and to analyze what really makes you irritable, sad or angry, and what, on the contrary, makes you happy, joyful and full of energy [#70].

#### Three-step approach to cognitive reappraisal
Purpose: an answer to a reader's question "Как начать работать с когнитивной переоценкой и не проваливаться вновь в автоматизм?" (How do I start working with cognitive reappraisal and not fall back into automatism?). Cognitive reappraisal is defined here as the technique of changing the perception of a situation from negative to productive, recommended by Barrett to control emotions better [#106].
1. Become aware first. To reappraise something, you first need to notice it. Mindfulness practices help — for example keeping a journal and meditation. It is useful to keep returning to yourself with the question "А как я сейчас себя чувствую?" (How am I feeling right now?) [#106].
2. Categorize emotions finely. Barrett advises developing the ability to categorize emotions in a fine-grained way: instead of a generalized "я расстроен" (I'm upset), make your feelings specific — for example "я разочарован" (I'm disappointed) or "я огорчен" (I'm saddened/grieved). Expanding your emotional vocabulary helps here (the post linked an emotion-vocabulary chart image; (!) INCOMPLETE: its contents were not provided in the source) [#106].
3. Recategorize in a positive key. One of the key methods is recategorizing emotions, i.e. reappraising them positively. Example: if you feel anxiety before an important event, try to perceive it as excitement and anticipation — according to the post this reduces the negative physical sensations and improves performance [#106].

Supporting rule: like any skill, cognitive reappraisal needs practice. Discussing your thoughts and feelings with loved ones or a specialist gives additional support and feedback [#106].

#### Katya's daily emotional-hygiene minimum
Context: Katya says she is genetically very emotional and impulsive ("вы бы видели мою маму" — you should see my mom); in the past, in fits of emotion, phones, plates and whatever was at hand flew into walls. Therefore she has been in therapy for about eight years and practices formal meditation and related practices — once episodically, for the last two years daily, "даже в самолете, даже после операции на ногу, даже если не смогла спать" (even on a plane, even after leg surgery, even if I couldn't sleep). Her reason for not skipping: if she gives in to the inner voice saying "и так тяжело, можно без этой тягомотины" (it's hard enough already, I can skip this drag), it will be even worse [#73].

Her daily minimum [#73]:
1. 20 minutes of breathing practices right after waking up.
2. 10 minutes of Non Sleep Deep Rest practice an hour after lunch — a way to recharge energy in the middle of the day (she promised to describe it separately; (!) INCOMPLETE: the NSDR method itself is not explained in this post).
3. Mindful routines — brushing teeth, exercise, dry brushing, shower, face massage, eating without the phone, done with concentration on the body and on the actions; she tries to fit all her routines into this format.

#### Three emotion-regulation systems (Paul Gilbert)
Source: a guest text by psychologist-philosopher Katya of the Balabanova channel, presented by Katya Strelnikova as "a balance you have not thought about". Usually balance is understood as "outside" — between work and rest, family and career, self and others. The alternative view: the problem may be "inside" — the three emotion-regulation systems, which determine how we feel and whether we have strength to live, may be out of balance [#591].

Basis: "Мы млекопитающие." (We are mammals.) Our nervous system still lives by evolutionary rules formed long before offices, deadlines and social networks. British psychologist Paul Gilbert described three main emotion-regulation systems everyone has; for convenience they are colored [#591]:
1. Red — the threat system. Anxiety, mobilization, defense. In the lioness image: a lioness living her best life notices strange lions on the pride's territory; the threat system activates — a surge of adrenaline and cortisol, rage, defense of the territory. She drives them away; her action is driven by the motivation to avoid harm [#591].
2. Blue — the drive system. Interest, seeking resources, achievement. The lioness needs to feed herself and her cubs, goes hunting and kills an antelope. Focus on getting the resource, activity, concentration; lots of dopamine and energy directed at the result [#591].
3. Green — the safety system. Calm, reliability, recovery. After the whole family eats the antelope, the lioness lies down to digest: the "rest and digest" state switches on. She may lick her cub, snuggle up to her lion, simply bask in the sun. This system has much softness, calm and contact; wounds heal, tissues regenerate, immunity normalizes, the hormonal system is regulated [#591].

The story is framed as "типичный день одной львицы" (a typical day of one lioness) [#591].

Diagnosis of modern fatigue: for many modern people life has turned into alternating the red and blue systems — anxiety and achievement. Even moments of rest are often driven by achievement motives. The problem is that the nervous system does not recover that way [#591].

Three-legged stool image (образ трёхногой табуретки): the guest likes this image — just as a stool works only when it has all three legs, each system evolutionarily solves its own life task, and without full rest (the green leg) the system will always be unstable [#591].

#### Three first steps to activate the green (safety) system
The guest says it is quite possible to learn to regularly give your nervous system the needed signals of safety and calm. Where to start [#591]:
1. Rhythmic calming breathing — for example inhale-exhale on a count of 5, or "2-7-8" with a breath hold in the middle [#591]. ((!) UNCLEAR: the source writes "2-7-8"; the exact phase assignment of each count is not specified beyond the hold being in the middle.)
2. Gentle self-touch — a palm on the chest, at the heart, is said to activate the parasympathetic system especially well [#591].
3. Imagining a safe place or a safe color and staying there for a while [#591].

Katya Strelnikova adds that this is a skill requiring respect and practice; the guest's channel has more concrete actions for moving from one system to another, including how to stop the "inner race" and how to use music for it ("мы уже все неосознанно это делаем" — we all already do this unconsciously) (!) ### Detailed insights

#### Emotions drain energy
Katya has always noticed how unexpressed anger, accumulated resentment and endless anxiety suck out energy, and how mood swings, instead of her, decide what she will do today. This never suited her: "не люблю я быть зависимой" (I don't like being dependent). Her post was written to expand Marina's Barrett post on the link between emotions and energy, share her experience and give advice on how to approach one's own emotions [#73].

#### Emotional stability and energy — research the author cites
Katya cites that as early as 1976 researchers found a correlation between initiative, persistence and the ability to energize oneself and others on the one hand and emotional stability on the other; she says this has since been confirmed repeatedly. For example, a 2019 study showed a positive influence of emotional stability on stress, which she says is directly connected with the ability to manage energy levels and mental health [#73].

#### Embodiment self-experiment
Katya had long wanted to try embodiment practices and joined a course of body practices called "Emotional Balance" from the Deep Mind team. Her request was "увидеть то, что я упускаю" (to see what I'm missing): despite all her existing practices she felt that her lens had blurred and there was a block in understanding herself, and "asking the body" — which she had never done — seemed sensible. The course creators say it can complement and amplify existing practices. Her written intention: she felt she had hit a wall, wanted to look at herself from the outside, see the patterns that keep her from moving forward, reach clarity and learn something new about herself to help move toward goals [#243].

She had no great expectations, so the results surprised her. Within about a month: she returned to therapy, which had been paused since summer; understood what she wants to dedicate the next year to; admitted to herself that she can't do it alone; and also started coaching [#243].

Her journal notes from the practices — which she says may sound banal but were transformative because she had never admitted them to herself and had only been endlessly mulling them in the background [#243]:
- Money (25.11): she felt she doesn't understand how to earn more money, has no strategy and no resource/time/perseverance to build one; she needs help and has to start investing in it.
- Fear of being used (12.11): her hands were cold and warmed up midway through the practice. She noticed that situations where she "могут обмануть" (may be deceived) trouble her, and that both situations were about her expectations — she fears so much that "меня используют" (I'll be used) that she doesn't see the benefits; yet it was genuinely great to meet someone and simply help, and in fact she wants people to help her just like that too.

- Anxiety about others' resentment (11.11): calmer afterwards. At the start, thoughts about a person named Ira and anxiety came again (she is not sure what she is anxious about); she recognized a trait from childhood — she feels bad when someone is offended at her, and used to consider being able to apologize even when not guilty a superpower; whether that is true needs exploring.
- Fear of "being clever" (8.11): she recalled her mother saying "не умничай" (don't be a smart-aleck), the skepticism of "Робо" (Robo; (!) UNCLEAR: the source does not say who Robo is) when she "разглагольствую" (holds forth), and the feeling from her father's lengthy reasoning ("если такой умный, почему такой бедный" — if you're so smart, why are you so poor); this made it clear why she shies away from experts and "talking heads".

Her conclusion: "И это я только пока лапкой потрогала." (And I've only touched it with a paw so far.) The body is still a half-closed book for her, she has no illusions about that, and she plans to continue the course [#243].

## Meditation and mindfulness

### What this covers

This topic gathers what the channel "На балансе" teaches about meditation and mindfulness: a guided meditation for restoring emotional energy (with its preparation instruction), Katya Strelnikova's personal experience of an 8-week happiness course measured with the PERMA test, the idea of "microdosing" mindfulness in very short doses, a meditation album recommendation, and the key ideas of Daniel Goleman and Richard Davidson's book "Altered Traits" (Russian edition: «Измененные черты характера»). According to the source, meditation matters because it changes reactions, resilience and stress recovery, and because inner habits (reactions, beliefs, defenses) are trainable just like outer behavior [#144, #209, #522, #638].

### Core ideas

- Emotions are the channel through which psychic energy is lost or gained, so emotional hygiene (clearing emotional "tails" and adding positive emotions) restores emotional energy — see *Emotional-energy meditation* [#144].
- Studying is not enough — you have to practice. Katya's main lesson from the happiness course, which she calls the most banal discovery made "for the 100,000th time" [#209].
- Little but regular beats big and rare. Even 20–60 seconds of mindful attention reduces stress and interrupts rumination; the trap is believing "if it's little, it's useless" — see *Microdosing mindfulness* [#522].
- Inner habits are trainable. Reactions, attitudes, defense mechanisms and beliefs are not a sentence or "the way I am by nature"; they are trained in a "mental gym" and become altered traits — see *Altered traits* [#638].
- The goal of meditation is to make healthy states of mind into stable traits, and its main effect, per the book, is moving closer to the best in our nature rather than better health or business productivity [#638].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Emotional-energy meditation — 7-step preparation instruction
Context: Katya had just started writing about four types of energy, and practices for restoring each began to come together. For the channel, her friend Dasha Metelskaya — a certified meditation and mindfulness trainer with six years of experience — recorded a video practice for restoring emotional energy. Dasha's framing: «Эмоции — то, благодаря чему мы можем терять или приобретать психическую энергию.» (Emotions are what allow us to lose or gain psychic energy.) The practice is described as emotional hygiene: «подчищает эмоциональные «хвосты» и заряжает положительными эмоциями» (cleans up emotional "tails" and charges you with positive emotions). The guided audio itself is in the video and has no transcribed text; only the preparation steps below are in the source. (!) INCOMPLETE: the content of the guided practice itself (what happens after step 7) is only in the video and was not available [#144].

Preparation, in order:
1. Find a comfortable, quiet place. The practice needs an undisturbed setting [#144].
2. Take a comfortable position — sitting on a chair, cross-legged, or lying down. Whatever the position, keep your back straight [#144].
3. Agree with yourself that these 15 minutes are for you. The practice lasts about 15 minutes; the explicit agreement marks this time as dedicated to yourself [#144].
4. Set an intention before starting: answer the question of why you are doing this [#144].
5. Recall two situations: one unpleasant situation whose negative charge is no more than 6 out of 10, and one very pleasant situation (it can be "all 11 out of 10"). The cap of 6/10 on the unpleasant one is part of the instruction; the source does not explain the reasoning, but these two situations are the material the practice works with — the "tails" to clear and the positive emotion to charge with [#144].
6. Take several deeper breaths in and out, exhaling all the way [#144].
7. Begin the practice (follow the recorded video) [#144].

Katya's personal context: she met Dasha in 2018 at a friendly yoga retreat in Spain; partly thanks to the meditations and yoga Dasha led there, Katya realized something was "wrong" with her, that it was time to change her life and to treat her eating disorder (РПП). She says that next to Dasha one feels as if healing, because of her bright energy [#144].

#### Microdosing mindfulness
Purpose: a mindfulness strategy for people who say they have no time. Katya, a self-described fan of "lowering the degree" and doing everything "on minimum settings" (as she already argued for steps and workouts), took the idea from a New Scientist article about microdosing mindfulness [#522].

- The barrier: many people do not start meditation or other mindfulness practices because there is "no time". Katya's answer: we do not need hours of meditation or ten-day retreats to get an effect [#522].
- The dose: per the research Katya cites, 20–60 seconds of mindful attention — 3–6 breaths in and out, a short pause — can already lower stress, interrupt ruminations and bring back a sense of contact with oneself [#522].
- The reframe: one minute of silence is rest, not "still too little to bother" [#522].
- The trap to avoid: as with steps and workouts, people fall into «если мало — значит бесполезно» (if it's little, it's useless), whereas in practice «мало, но регулярно» (little, but regularly) gives a much more sustainable result [#522].
- Where to put the doses: it is a working strategy for real people with jobs, children and projects — notice your breathing while the kettle boils; sit with eyes closed for a moment before replying in a chat; feel your body for a minute before moving to the next task on your to-do list [#522].
- The long-run effect: at some point you can no longer stop feeling — you cannot "unsee" it — without excess heroism and without raising the intensity where it is already off the charts [#522].

#### Katya's four favorite short mindfulness practices
Listed by Katya as her personal micro-practices [#522]:
1. Deep breathing with eyes closed [#522].
2. Rub your palms together until they are very hot, then place them over your eyes [#522].
3. Shake the body (тряска тела — body shaking) [#522].
4. Hug yourself [#522].

#### Altered traits — meditation as a mental gym (Goleman and Davidson)
Source: Katya's review of «Измененные черты характера» (Altered Traits) by Daniel Goleman and Richard Davidson, presented as the next book in her "easy way to start" series (after easy ways to start running and moving), with the motto «То, что практикуется, будет улучшено» (What is practiced will be improved), plus nine quote cards from the book [#638].

Components of the model:
1. Inner habits change like outer behavior. «Наши реакции, установки, защитные механизмы, верования — не приговор и не «я такая от природы».» (Our reactions, attitudes, defense mechanisms, beliefs are not a sentence and not "I'm like this by nature".) They are trained in a "mental gym" [#638].
2. Altered traits. The authors call the result "altered traits" — new characteristics that emerge from practice and stay with us outside meditation. The goal is to make healthy states of mind into the main, stable traits [#638].
3. Neuroplasticity is always happening, usually at random. Card: neuroplasticity — shaping the mind through regular experience — is present in our lives unconsciously. We spend long hours studying the screens of our devices or on countless fairly pointless goals, and meanwhile our neurons diligently strengthen or weaken the corresponding areas of the brain. Such a random "mental diet" will most likely lead to equally random changes in the "muscle of our mind". Contemplative science tells us we can care for our mind more responsibly [#639].
4. Any meditation trains psychological fitness. Card: just as regular workouts improve physical fitness, any type of meditation will to some degree strengthen psychological fitness [#640].
5. Skill follows the usual learning curve. Card: mastering any skill requires hard work at the start and gets easier with practice [#646].
6. The real aim. Card: the main impact of meditation is not improved health or business performance but coming closer to the best in our nature [#638].

### Detailed insights

#### Healthy vs unhealthy states on the map of the mind
A book card describes our psychological states as too often fluctuating within a range of compulsive desires, selfishness, laziness, anxiety and similar states (the card ends the list with «и так далее») — these are the unhealthy states on the map of the mind. Healthy states, by contrast, include equanimity (невозмутимость), mental balance (душевное равновесие), constant attentiveness (постоянная внимательность) and realistic confidence (реалистичная уверенность). Making these healthy states stable traits is the stated goal of practice [#643, msg #638].

#### Attention: noise vs signal, and the wandering mind
- Card: harmful influence spreads into our whole life. For example, the inability to filter out noise (all distractions) from signal (what you should focus on) makes it hard to identify the most important things, and so reduces our ability to remember what matters [#641].
- Card: «Блуждающий ум - ум несчастливый.» (A wandering mind is an unhappy mind.) [#644]

#### Thoughts as brief moments of mind
Card: usually thoughts subjugate us — self-disgust or self-hatred leads to one set of feelings and actions, romantic fantasies to another. But with sustained mindfulness one can have a deep experience in which self-hatred and romantic thoughts become the same thing: like all other thoughts, they are only fleeting moments of mind. We do not need to run from our thoughts during the day — they are just an endless series of short clips, previews and failed takes in the theater of the mind [#642].

#### A lighter relationship to "self"
Card: although bills still have to be paid, the more lightly we relate to the "I", the less we suffer from those bills and the freer we feel. We still look for a way to pay the bills, but without the extra load of emotional baggage. The lesson: practical problems remain, but the suffering added by self-focus can be reduced [#645].

#### Beyond the individual
Card, posed as a question: what if, by changing our mind, we could improve not only our own health and well-being but also the health and well-being of people in our communities and the rest of the world? [#647]

#### Why Katya trusts the book
- Personal effect: for several years Katya has felt meditation's influence on her reactions, her resilience and how fast she recovers after stress; rereading the book made her want to give meditation even more time and attention [#638].
- Methodological rigor: she says the authors went to great lengths to separate real effects from wishful thinking and the Hawthorne effect, used strict standards and selected only high-quality studies — rare in a field with many beautiful claims and little real science [#638].
- The authors' story: they and their colleagues began researching contemplative practices when the scientific community regarded them with open skepticism, and literally built contemplative neuroscience with their own hands, risking their reputations. Katya's lesson: what science currently considers proven or rejected is not forever; science changes, and it is good that some people go where others do not dare [#638].

#### What the happiness course taught Katya
- Skepticism overcome: she expected to know "roughly everything" on the subject — philosophers from antiquity to now, positive psychology, and Laurie Santos's course, which she had completed twice. The course still showed happiness from a different angle; she had not seen any other source dissect it so carefully from so many angles [#209].
- Main lesson: «ИЗУЧАТЬ НЕ ДОСТАТОЧНО, НАДО ПРАКТИКОВАТЬ.» (STUDYING IS NOT ENOUGH, YOU HAVE TO PRACTICE.) [#209]
- Savoring was the practice that worked best. Over 8 weeks she tried many practices; the one that clicked most was savoring (смакование — she calls it a dumb word but says nothing describes it better). She had heard it works but had simply never done it. She discovered there is a lot in her life she can savor that makes her happy, and that savoring is exactly the living-through and feeling of life she had asked for in request 3 — so, in her words, the circle closed. (!) INCOMPLETE: the how-to for savoring is in an external Instagram post that was not provided [#209].
- Most important outcome — responsibility and agency: the course helped her take back responsibility for her state and her agency. She began more actively doing what fills her with joy and not letting it slip by, and still practices this "seemingly simple (actually very hard)" exercise. She even took on "her own Everest": learning to eat more slowly [#209].
- Her summary of who the course suits: those who want to become happier, learn to enjoy what they already have and be more satisfied with themselves and life [#209].

#### Meditation as a return to the beginning (arkhe album)
Recommending the meditation album arkhe by Dasha Beloglazova, Katya writes that when we return to our breath and bodily sensations, when we find time to listen to ourselves (even on the go), we return to the beginning — to the point where the inner and outer are attuned — in order to live not the life that just happened but the one you are able to build through new choices and a new attitude. She presents meditation as a way to practice self-regulation, build contact with oneself, learn to manage attention and relax [#482].

For 2026 she is taking up metta-bhavana (loving-kindness meditation) to immerse herself in love and gratitude. Her reason: one of her main realizations at the end of 2025 was that she does not know how to be content and grateful — and without that her mission (to become free, happy and prosperous in order to help others become free, happy and prosperous) cannot be fulfilled [#482].

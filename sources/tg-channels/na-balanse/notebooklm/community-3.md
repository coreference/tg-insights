## Channel life and announcements

### What this covers

This file covers the meta-life of the Telegram channel «На балансе» (On balance): its founding idea and the questions it set out to explore, who the authors are and how the authorship changed, how the content is organized (rubricator, quarterly best-of, monthly recaps), and the transferable knowledge embedded in otherwise "meta" posts - milestone posts, ads, podcast appearances and monthly recaps. According to the source, the channel exists to explore, as an open-ended "research", how a modern woman can stay energized, find balance and accept herself, using an evidence-based approach and personal experiments [#9, #237].

This part covers the channel's meta posts from January to June 2026: a New Year "best of 2025" collection, a roundup of the author's podcast appearances, a recommendation post about blogging, the January 2026 monthly recap, a new podcast release and a milestone post with a reading/listening list about change. Event announcements and logistics are excluded; what remains is the knowledge embedded in these posts — the author's energy audit and its conclusions, her view of where the "mood to blog" comes from, the theses she voiced in podcasts, and her own thematic map of what she considers her core writing [#504, #505, #519, #526, #592, #627]. According to the author, these collections exist so that readers can get to know her approach and "so there is something to refer to later", and because re-reading old material is worthwhile as long as one is not applying it yet [#504, #505].

### Core ideas

- The channel is a research project, not a set of ready answers. The authors explicitly say they do not always know where a topic or experiment will lead; readers are invited to contribute questions, remarks and personal experience [#9].
- Energy is a finite resource that must be actively maintained, especially after ~30. Lack of ideas, unfinished goals and "maybe it's just not for me" thinking are, in Katya's reading, symptoms of having no strength rather than of lacking talent [#172].
- Shame is an atmosphere, not just a feeling, and it blocks self-care. It pushes people toward escapism instead of care; it can give a flash of motivation but never sustainability [#411, #414].
- Body shame is learned, not innate. It is instilled through princesses, magazine covers, mother's diet and "caring" remarks [#413].
- Inner conflicts are battles for energy. Tension comes from inner discord rather than external circumstances; attention, curiosity and gratitude toward one's sub-personalities generate energy [#197].
- Different lives give different perspectives. The two founders live very different lives within the same wellness concept, which the authors present as the channel's unique feature [#237].
- Rebuilding routines after upheaval gets easier with practice, and a few tiny routines keep you afloat while the full regime is under construction [#462].

- Energy is audited, not guessed. The author reviews each month by listing what gave her energy and what drained it; in January 2026 the biggest drains were background uncertainty and money anxiety, and the biggest givers were simple, cheap, solitary, slow rituals (bath house, morning baths, solitude walks, a weekday off). See *Monthly energy audit* [#526].
- Lack of fundamental clarity makes planning heavy. When it is unclear where and how you will live, writing yearly plans is hard and uninspiring; living "without understanding what's next" is more draining than the place itself [#526].
- The mood to write is a result, not a starting point. Waiting for free time or inspiration does not produce the desire to blog; mood is contextual and appears when conditions are created. See *Mood-to-blog scheme* [#519].
- Change is about identity and experiments, not "have to". In her podcasts the author argues that sudden changes are risky because the brain perceives a "new better life" as a threat to safety, that "new life from Monday" does not work, and that changes take time [#505, #592].
- Old advice is not stale until applied. "Это не баян до тех пор, пока мы это не делаем" (It's not old news until we actually do it) — the author's justification for re-reading and resurfacing past posts [#504].

#### Architecture of shame (Katya Strelnikova)
Source: Katya's guest appearance on episode 4, season 3 of Dasha's podcast «Что они скажут» ("What will they say" - a podcast about how to stop listening to everyone except yourself and start living by your own rules), summarized in quote cards [#406, #407]. The episode covered her career "re-invention" as a process - the transition from corporate marketing manager to an independent professional in sustainable change - how the body became her main source of shame, why shame is a destructive tool for growth, and how to approach habits differently [#407]. The card bio describes her as a health coach who, together with clients, explores how health, energy, attention and self-care work «без стыда, кнута и вечного самосовершенствования» (without shame, the whip and eternal self-improvement), helping build sustainable habits and live one's own, real life [#406].

The components of her model, in the order the cards present them:

1. Origin: an early decision to be "better" in order to be loved. Katya describes herself as a little girl from a communal apartment with an alcoholic father who decided that at any cost she must be better, brighter and smarter to be noticed, accepted and loved [#410].
2. Core beliefs underneath behavior. Many of her former choices - a party lifestyle, workaholism, an eating disorder - rested on beliefs: «со мной что-то не так, я не достаточная, я не заслуживаю» (something is wrong with me, I am not enough, I don't deserve). She jokingly calls herself a "veteran" of ED, burnout and disco partying [#410].
3. Shame as an atmosphere. «Самое страшное в стыде, что это не просто чувство.» (The scariest thing about shame is that it is not just a feeling.) It is a whole atmosphere that builds a whole architecture of shame - like living in a stuffy room that is so stuffy you no longer notice it. You do not even realize you are trying to run from the deep, sticky feeling "something is wrong with me" [#411].
4. The behavioral consequence: escapism instead of care. «В этот момент ты не выбираешь заботу» (at that moment you do not choose care) - you choose escapism [#411].
5. Body shame is taught. Citing Brené Brown's research, the body is the main source of shame for women regardless of age, status or profession [#413]. Katya adds that body shame is not something you are born with but something you are trained into - through princesses, magazine covers, mother's diet, and through "care" like «Ну ты бы похудела» (well, you could lose some weight) [#413].
6. Why shame fails as a motivator. «Стыд может дать вспышку мотивации,» (shame can give a flash of motivation) but it never gives sustainability [#414].
7. The alternative: listening to yourself. Change begins with the skill of listening to and hearing yourself. «Я не проект» (I am not a project) - not a project of endless improvement. And self-care is «не награда» (not a reward) for achievements [#414].
8. Working with shame made the transition possible. Katya states that her transition in career and in life "from a party girl with an ED into a person who takes care of herself" would have been impossible had she not worked with shame [#409].
9. Marking the end of the struggle. To mark the end of her fight with ED, she gave herself a photoshoot with photographer Lyusya Zharikova: over 100 frames, and she liked every single photo - «Какая я красивая женщина» (what a beautiful woman I am) [#412].
10. The closing slogan. The final card's handwritten slogan means "live rather than be ashamed" (the OCR rendered it as «Жить, а не» plus the garbled word «стаиться»; on inspection the image shows the verb for "to be ashamed") - and points readers to the channel's post on the architecture of shame [#415].

On her career change she adds that the illusion that anything can be "once and for all" disappeared for her, and she is curious how things will transform further - but she does not romanticize the transition: «он не был лёгким, он не был» (it was not easy, it was not) linear, and it continues [#408].

The channel's quarterly best-of lists the related post «Архитектура стыда: что мешает о себе заботиться» (Architecture of shame: what prevents you from taking care of yourself) as a set of "invisible" attitudes that prevent living a healthy and happy life [#433].

#### Self-leadership through internal-conflict resolution
Context: the channel's first paid ad (marked #реклама_набалансе), for the project TalkAuthentic.io on self-leadership and authentic communication by Lesha and Sveta, whose channels, podcast and book Katya says she has long followed [#197]. The knowledge she endorses:

1. Management courses are not enough for leadership. Many want to become good managers, respected bosses and authentic leaders; Katya, "from experience", says basic management courses are insufficient. Some say talent is needed; she thinks one must first master communication with oneself [#197].
2. Inner listening enables outer listening. If you have learned to notice and resolve inner conflicts and to hear the voices and arguments of all your parts, it is much easier to hear, understand and correctly interpret voices from outside [#197].
3. All conflicts are a fight for energy - external and internal alike. She links this to a phrase the channel used before: «Образовать новую нейронную связь дорогого стоит. А у нас бюджет» (forming a new neural connection is costly, and we are on a budget) [#197].
4. Tension comes from inner discord, not from external circumstances [#197].
5. Selfhood comes through acceptance, not pursuit. One reaches one's self not by chasing it but by accepting all parts of oneself and allowing them to be [#197].
6. The energy rule: «Чем больше внимания, любопытства и благодарности к нашим субличностям, тем больше возникает энергии» (the more attention, curiosity and gratitude toward our sub-personalities, the more energy arises), and the more whole and authentic we become [#197].
7. Outcome of deep self-work: if we have deeply worked through ourselves and are transparent, ethical and open, it is hard to drag us into squabbles or conflict, we cannot be manipulated, and provoking us is useless [#197].

#### Minimal support routines during upheaval
After moving continents again (to Moscow, for a while) while launching a product, Katya describes being in "energy-saving mode", trying to assemble a new regime from fragments: she did not yet know when it felt good to sleep, walk or work, what to eat, where to exercise (in a one-room apartment), where to get massages, or how much to socialize - she had already "overdosed" on friends [#462]. What kept her going were small routines:

1. Walks of at least one hour a day [#462].
2. Reading [#462].
3. Face massage [#462].
4. Vegetables in every meal [#462].
5. Psyllium as a fiber stopgap - because while her eating regime is "under construction" she cannot get enough fiber [#462].

Her reflection: it is irritating to rebuild routines again and again, but she feels strength in "these hands" that have done it for the 5th time in 5 years, and she says unequivocally that it gets easier [#462]. She also judged that allowing herself a couple of weeks of low work around the move (23 meetings over 12 days, no new clients) was "a super-right decision" [#462]. Honest caveat: under stress she "dropped everything" - skipped her philosophy-lab meetings, read detective novels instead of smart books and watched catastrophically many series [#462].

#### Three-month income averaging for the self-employed
Katya's lesson from tracking her coaching-practice income: if you work for yourself, counting income month by month is «верный путь к вечной истерике» (a sure path to eternal hysteria) [#443]. Illustration: in September she earned $1000 below plan, in October she over-fulfilled almost twofold. The idea from Nastya Grabovich is to count income not per month but per three months; Katya sees in practice how this could smooth the anxiety, but admits she psychologically still misses the old order when the same sum arrived on the same day [#443]. Her November recap shows the anxiety persisting even while slightly exceeding the yearly plan of $36,000, because spending was high; she caught herself in low energy and despair ("will I have to stress about earnings all my life?!"), cried about the unfairness of the world, and went back to work [#462].

#### Monthly energy audit
Purpose: a recurring (monthly, tagged #итоги_набалансе) self-review in which the author records the month's context, her workload, and then sorts concrete activities and conditions into "what gave energy" (🔋) and "what took energy" (🪫), ending with one main conclusion. She notes that a hard month makes it "especially clear what really supports me and what eats energy" [#526].

Step 1 — Name the context of the month. January 2026 was the first January in 10 years she spent in Moscow; it was "not that scary, even sunny". Her observation: the most energy-consuming thing is not the place but living without understanding what's next — where she will live, how her life will be arranged, what decisions lie ahead [#526].

Step 2 — Note the effect of the context on plans. She learned that when there is no clarity at the fundamental level, yearly plans are written with difficulty and are not inspiring — but she still "ticked the box" on her 2026 plans [#526].

Step 3 — Record the workload and money picture honestly. She tracks working days, client sessions and posts written (fewer posts than usual that month), and income vs spending. She started tracking expenses only the previous year and was shocked; living "в ноль" (at zero — spending about what you earn) "existentially adds not the most pleasant questions about stability and the future" [#526].

Step 4 — List what gave energy (🔋), with the "why" for each. January's ten items:
1. Bath house (баня). Three visits, "each time like water into dry earth". Getting herself out the door can be hard, but it is worth it; the secret of success is simplicity — a bath house 30 minutes away for 3,000 rubles [#526].
2. Morning baths. "My classic": slow, with coffee, a book and a head massage; filling when there is nowhere to rush [#526].
3. Padel. She had long wanted to return to team/game sports; tennis once seemed too much hassle and she does not like complications, so she chose padel and went twice, planning to continue in February (her friends in Spain all play it) [#526].
4. Solitude. Walking alone, especially in frost and without content (no audio/feeds); silence. Nature still turns out to be far more resourceful for her than exhibitions and social activity [#526].
5. An (almost) day off on Thursday instead of Saturday, plus permission for very calm mornings, especially on days full of calls. "Paradoxically", more got done from that relaxed state [#526].
6. The "Rested" Laboratory (Лаборатория «Отдохнувшие»). Although building the product on the go was hard, the group is very supportive, inspiring and returns her interest in energy and self-observation [#526].
7. Permission to be ill without guilt. She was ill for a couple of days "without second thoughts and enjoying it"; this saved an unexpectedly large amount of resource [#526].
8. Daytime naps. She began learning to sleep in the daytime and succeeded a couple of times; without nap breaks she feels she loses a lot in the amount of energy during the day and its management [#526].
9. Home workouts. 30 minutes every morning; their energising effect was especially visible when she had to take a break from them [#526].
10. Reduced caffeine. 5 g of caffeinated coffee per day is her version — enough for energy without side effects; she mixes it with decaf [#526].

(The source introduces "what gave energy" without a count; the list as written has ten bolded items.) [#526]

Step 5 — List what took energy (🪫). January's six items:
1. Uncertainty about the future — in the background it eats more than it seems [#526].
2. Money anxiety — plus a separate tiredness from having to think constantly about getting clients instead of simply doing her work well [#526].
3. Socialising after long work meetings, and two social weekends in a row. After long meetings she needs to recover, not go for another 4–5 hours of socialising [#526].
4. No planned meals — hunger, eating close to bedtime, energy spikes [#526].
5. Late TV series after 22:00 — every time: minus sleep, minus recovery [#526].
6. Low or high atmospheric pressure — she does not understand why, but only in Moscow is she very sensitive to these swings [#526].

Step 6 — Formulate the one main conclusion. January's conclusion, "incredible and completely banal": "Я. Не. Люблю. Работать." (I. Don't. Like. Working.) — explicitly "не путать с «я не люблю свою работу»" (not to be confused with "I don't like my job"). From this she sees that a new stage is ahead where she will have to reassemble meanings, supports and the format of her life [#526].

Step 7 — Invite readers to reflect. The post ends with "А как ваш январь?" (And how was your January?) [#526].

#### Mood-to-blog scheme
Purpose: explain why people who want to blog do not write even when time appears, and what to do instead of waiting for inspiration [#519].

- The trap. People think: "Вот освобожусь — и начну писать" (Once I'm free, I'll start writing), "When it gets easier, the desire will appear", "When inspiration comes, I'll return to the blog". Reality is often different: time appears but you don't want to write; there is a window in the calendar but inside it is empty or noisy; "it seems possible, but it doesn't flow" [#519].
- The thesis. A blog needs not only time but "mood", and it does not appear by itself. "Настроение — это не стартовая точка. Это результат." (Mood is not a starting point. It is a result.) [#519]
- Not a personal defect. The problem is not that you are "not a blogger", "not disciplined" or "just not gifted"; mood is a contextual thing — it cannot be squeezed out, but you can create the conditions in which it appears [#519].
- The scheme. The author has a diagram of circles that she presents in her webinar; the image accompanying the post labels four elements: тело (body), тишина (silence), тяга (pull / draw), настроение (mood) [#519]. ### Detailed insights

#### Founding idea and authors
The channel was created by Marina Sidorich and Katya Strelnikova, who at founding had practiced health coaching for about two years - "certified health trainers" who help people change lifestyle «чтобы лечиться приходилось меньше, а кайфовать больше» (so that you need treatment less and enjoy more) [#9]. Their shared interest: a healthy lifestyle, an evidence-based approach, research and practices - ways to keep health, beauty and activity "preferably until 100, or longer if science allows" [#9]. They also share being women, with questions Google cannot answer, which became the channel's three founding questions [#9]:

1. How to be "in resource" most of the time? Where to get energy, how to manage it, and can one influence it at all [#9]?
2. How to find balance between work, family, self-care, development and other spheres? Does such a balance exist [#9]?
3. How to accept yourself without sliding into self-indulgence and laxity? Where is the boundary, and can it be learned [#9]?

At the one-year mark (December 2024) the authors say they cannot claim to have "rock-solidly" found energy, balance or themselves, but they advanced in their understanding of harmonious living, self-development, self-knowledge and practical tools for maintaining health and energy, and gathered a community of women [#237]. They re-introduced themselves: both are health coaches who studied in the same lifestyle-modification programme; they created the channel because, discussing balance, energy and self-discovery, they realized they could not always agree even with each other. They live very different lives while both following the wellness concept - Marina has two children, Katya has none and plans none; Katya lives in the US and moves a lot, Marina lives in Barcelona and is more settled; they eat, move and structure days differently. This difference, they say, makes the channel unique because it allows looking at things from different sides, and they continue the search for objectivity through heroines (interviewees), experts and personal stories [#237].

Katya's own statement of approach: a certified health coach who for the last three years has helped people change lifestyle and habits "with ease" - through finding true motivation, care and respect for oneself, and accepting imperfections and one's humanity. She believes every adult can find their «зачем» (their "why"), motivation and ways to live the life they want. She has herself changed lifestyle and profession and twice changed country, calls herself "endlessly in an experiment of improving life", and states her goal as being free, happy and thriving so as to help others become so; the channel is part of that mission [#237].

Marina's statement: over three years she lived in Asia and Europe, travelled with family, changed 17 apartments and houses, left marketing for a helping profession, had a second daughter and now lives a measured life in a house by the forest near Barcelona. She understands how hard it is for a woman to combine the roles of mother, wife and professional, constantly looks for energy and strength to stay afloat and avoid burnout, and helps other women find inspiration for self-care [#237].

In October 2025 the channel became the channel of health coach Katya Strelnikova (she had in fact been writing it for the past year while Marina was on maternity leave). Marina focuses on her own channel and keeps a column here on nutrition for balance and energy. For readers this means more content from Katya - besides interviews and posts on balance, rest and energy, also on coaching and change, developing a practice and the professional path [#440].

#### Energy as a finite resource (the «БАЗА» webinar thesis)
Katya and mentor Katya Keylman made a free webinar «БАЗА» (BASE) whose card names three blocks: energy, base state, goals [#172, img #172]. The knowledge in the announcement:

- Recognizing the symptom. Typical complaints - "I want to come up with something new but can't", "I keep setting goals and not finishing them", "I have thoughts and ideas but no resource to implement them", "why does it work for others but not for me?", "maybe I'm just not gifted" - are, Katya says, a tune she can guess from one note: «У вас. Нет. Сил.» (You. Have no. Strength.) [#172].
- Reassurance and diagnosis. You are fine: not idea-less, lazy, stupid, cursed or talentless. To overflow with ideas and implement them you need to be "in resource" and to work with your mindset [#172].
- The demo-version metaphor. «Энергия — не бездонный колодец» (energy is not a bottomless well): taking from it for free works at best until about 30, then "the demo version ends" and you must switch to a paid subscription or give up all the cool functions [#172].
- Belief about adults. The authors believe an adult, sane person can learn self-care and a growth mindset - provided they want to set and reach goals, be active, develop and flourish [#172].

#### Lessons from the October 2025 recap
- Uncertainty and sleep. After three months of "suspension" before a move (the 7th move in 5 years), getting at least some certainty brought her sleep back "into the green zone"; she had forgotten over two months what it is like to have surplus energy and want to live at all [#443].
- Less energy is not only stress - count the workload. When she tallied everything she realized she had simply worked more: 42 client meetings in a month, not counting calls with an assistant, producer, methodologist and a weekly three-hour philosophy session - "really a lot" for her [#443].
- Living from result to result. With her psychotherapist she found that finances and career are the only sphere where she still cannot enjoy the process: after making a two-month plan, instead of "hooray" she thinks "there will be no income in November because of two weeks' vacation" and "how could I earn like this every month by January". She asks herself to simply rejoice - a year earlier the same month brought only $1400 [#443].
- Young Schema Questionnaire. On her therapist's advice she took it for the first time; her top schemas were «привилегированность», «поиск одобрения» и «негативизм» (entitlement, approval-seeking and negativity/pessimism); she is still digesting the result [#443].
- A longevity role model. The 102-year-old yogi Charlotte Chopin (NYT article) teaches yoga while keeping body and mind clear. Her philosophy as relayed: move every day, never stop being interested in life, accept aging as part of the path. She eats a huge honey sandwich for breakfast, does not eat after six, does inversions, reads the newspaper and every day thanks herself for being able to do all this [#443].
- Rest from curiosity, not burnout. She is glad she can explore rest out of curiosity rather than from burnout, and even make it her work; she was preparing a group project about rest [#443].

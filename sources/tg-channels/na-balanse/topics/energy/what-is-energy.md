# What energy is and where it comes from

## What this covers

The channel's foundational ideas about personal energy: what it physically is, how to judge whether yours is "normal", and the mental models the authors use to manage it. Later posts add outside frameworks: metabolic health (Huberman with Casey Means), David Ko's Battery method, a guest explanation of male energy and testosterone, Paul Gilbert's three emotion-regulation systems, and Leonid Krol's distinction between leaking and blocked energy. Almost every other topic of the channel builds on these concepts.

Part 2 adds two posts from November 2026 to the channel's foundational energy topic. The first (#740) argues that the cause of low energy people usually do not consider is not a lack of energy but a lack of what gives it: one's own interesting, joyful, meaningful activities, postponed "until later". It brings in the four energy types of Loehr and Schwartz (the scope of this topic names "the 4 energy types", which the existing file had not yet described), a client case, a story from Matthieu Ricard, a three-step method and a rule of thumb. The second (#744, forwarded from the channel Daily Reminder) summarizes what science of the last 5-7 years says about where tiredness comes from: mitochondria and brain-body signaling [msg #740] [msg #744].

How this relates to the existing file: #744 **extends** the physiology section (*How the body produces energy*, msg #17) by saying that the problem in the modern world is not too little food but too much glucose, which makes mitochondria less efficient; it **echoes** the claim in #14 that energy cannot be measured, but adds that science has only recently begun to understand the feeling of energy. #740 **adds** the four energy types and a new cause of low energy alongside those already in the file (hormones in #573, imbalance of the three regulation systems in #591, leaking/blocked energy in #621), and it **continues** Krol's idea that energy lives in movement, goals and dreams (#621) with a practical rule [msg #740] [msg #744].

## Core ideas

- There is no "energy meter", so any assessment of energy is correlational or subjective; the norm can only be found by each person through inquiry and experiments [msg #14].
- Energy is produced physically: nutrients plus oxygen are turned into ATP by mitochondria, so food quality and oxygen directly set the level of energy [msg #17].
- Energy is not a given. Like muscles or knowledge it has to be trained and maintained constantly [msg #36].
- Energy is a currency and a vessel: goals are paid for with it, and it must be refilled before new achievements are planned [msg #178] [msg #278].
- Chronic disease is largely metabolic and lifestyle-made, and lifestyle is also the way back [msg #152].
- Low energy can be hormonal (testosterone in men), an imbalance of the nervous system's three regulation systems, or the result of suppressed feelings, and each cause needs a different remedy [msg #573] [msg #591] [msg #621].

- Often the problem is "not the absence of energy, but the absence of what gives it": years of postponing one's own interests cut off the source, which is why neither a vacation nor vitamins help [msg #740].
- There are four types of energy, not one (physical, emotional, mental, meaning), and people forget the meaning type, which often feeds the others [msg #740].
- Doing what fills you is "not a spending of energy but its source"; an hour a week already changes the balance [msg #740].
- Tiredness is not caused by a lack of food; it is shaped by glucose excess, stress, the brain's predictions about what is coming, and the presence or absence of social support [msg #744].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Four lenses for judging whether energy is "normal"

A reader asked how to understand whether their energy is normal. Katya's answer starts from the problem: "what is the norm" is a philosophical question with no single answer online, and since no "energy meter" has been invented, any evaluation is correlational or simply felt and subjective. We can look at four things, each with a built-in weakness [msg #14]:

1. **Lab tests.** Weakness: tests can be within the norm while your state is not [msg #14].
2. **Formal descriptions of the norm.** Weakness: each of us is far from the statistically average person [msg #14].
3. **Presence or absence of factors that influence energy** (seasons and light, circadian rhythms, hormonal background, neurotransmitters, age, weight, health). Weakness: the list is so long that it is unclear from which side to approach it, and unclear how exactly each factor affects me in particular, even though I know it does [msg #14].
4. **Well-being (how I feel).** It seems it should be the most accurate indicator, but what if I am so used to existing outside the norm that I started to take that for the norm? [msg #14]

Rule attached to the lenses: if you are asking the question at all, that is already a reason to doubt the "normality" of your current state [msg #14].

### Three self-inquiry questions about your energy norm

Katya answers the question "as a coach", with questions, because the norm in every area of life can only be determined by ourselves, through research and experiments. If you are ready to improve your resource, ask [msg #14]:

1. **Has my energy level changed recently?** If yes, energy has probably started leaking somewhere. Age is not an excuse: at 30, 40 or 50 you can still have stable energy [msg #14].
2. **What do my ideas of "normal energy" look like, where did I get them, and how objective are they?** Describe literally, point by point, what "energy in the norm" means to you. If the picture is a superwoman who in one day gives enough time to the children, cooks a balanced lunch, does sport, has sex, goes to work and runs her own business in parallel, question that picture and formulate a more adequate norm [msg #14].
3. **Do I know what is proven to influence a person's energy, and how much of it is integrated into my life?** To build a more or less objective picture, collect information on what really matters. If many items from that list are absent from your life, you most likely still have room to grow your resource [msg #14].

If the questions do not produce an answer, the author's suggestion is to work it through in a coaching session [msg #14]. The post ends with the note that an "ultimate guide" to what can affect energy should be compiled; the channel later published it as a PDF guide to habits that give and devour energy, covered in the topic *Energy boosters and replenishing practices* [msg #14].

### How the body produces energy (three-stage mechanism)

Marina's explanation of where strength literally comes from [msg #17]:

1. **Food becomes raw material.** Energy production starts with eating. In the stomach and intestines food breaks down into macro- and micronutrients: glucose, fats, vitamins, minerals and other useful substances. This raw material enters the bloodstream and travels to every cell [msg #17].
2. **Oxygen arrives.** The oxygen we inhale passes through the alveoli into the bloodstream and with it into each cell [msg #17].
3. **Mitochondria make ATP.** Inside the cells mitochondria, "the little power stations of our body", run several reactions with oxygen, glucose and other nutrients; the result is the ATP molecule, the fuel thanks to which we live [msg #17].

Why it matters to know: when you understand that energy technically depends on oxygen and diet, the choice in favor of walks in fresh air or balanced food becomes easier, because each step is seen as a deposit into overall well-being [msg #17].

### Morning "energetic" routine (7 steps)

Katya is often mistaken for a person who wakes up full of energy every morning and jumps out of bed; she calls this a fantasy. She is "just a human" with stress, insomnia, emotional swings and bad days, and ordinary mortals should not hope for luck but take energy into their own hands. Through study and experiments she knows what to do when she wakes up with minimal energy. These are the morning routines she never skips, even when she wakes thinking "maybe lie a bit longer", because they give a charge of energy and a stable state for the whole day [msg #36]:

1. Get up immediately [msg #36]
2. Bright light in the bathroom [msg #36]
3. A glass of water [msg #36]
4. Breathing practices [msg #36]
5. Morning exercises [msg #36]
6. A walk [msg #36]
7. A shower [msg #36]

She did not arrive at this at once: each habit entered her arsenal gradually. At the time of writing she felt a contrast shower was still missing, calling it an evidence-backed way to get energy for free [msg #36].

### Metabolic health checklist (8 blocks)

Compiled by the channel's authors from the Huberman Lab episode with Dr. Casey Means and attached to the post as a one-page checklist [img #152]:

1. **Eat whole, minimally processed foods:** vegetables and fruit at every meal; high-protein foods; probiotic foods (yogurt, sauerkraut and similar) daily; healthy fats, with fish daily; shorten the eating window to 10-12 hours [img #152].
2. **Limit or exclude processed foods:** do not replace meals with sweets, chips or store-bought convenience foods; eat no more than 25 g of added sugar a day [img #152].
3. **Include varied physical activity:** 7000 steps a day; strength training 2-3 times a week; 100 to 150 minutes of moderate cardio or 75 minutes of high-intensity cardio; a movement break every 30 minutes (calf raises, squats, push-ups and the like); non-exercise activity such as toe raises [img #152].
4. **Manage stress reactions and do not allow chronic stress:** meet a therapist regularly; practice mindfulness daily (meditation, breathing and similar); investigate and remove triggers [img #152].
5. **Do thermal activities:** banya or sauna 2-4 times a month; pour ice-cold water over yourself daily [img #152].
6. **Spend more time in nature:** walk at least 30 minutes in daylight every day; go on long nature walks 2-4 times a month [img #152].
7. **Watch your sleep:** sleep no less than 7.5 hours [img #152].
8. **Watch your sugar:** once a year test glucose and glycated hemoglobin [img #152].

### Battery method (4 zones, David Ko)

Source: an On Purpose podcast episode with David Ko, CEO of the Calm app, about how to recharge. Katya found the "Battery" concept the most useful part: energy is a vessel from which you cannot draw endlessly and into which you must pour so that there is strength for new achievements. The method divides one's state into four zones by energy and focus [msg #278] [img #278]:

1. **Full charge (green): high energy, high focus.** We feel energetic, concentrated and productive: at the peak of working capacity and creativity, any task is manageable, mood is excellent and new ideas are many. As a rule we are in this state when our lifestyle is systematically built for high energy (good sleep, balanced nutrition, enough movement). Even then we can fall out of it [msg #278].
2. **Overload (yellow): high energy, low focus.** There is a lot of energy but it is scattered or chaotic. We seem busy but productivity suffers: important things may not get done, time is wasted, ideas come with difficulty, there is less "want" and more "must". How to recover: pause, review priorities, analyze your lifestyle for needed changes, take up mindfulness practices; at the very least concentrate on one task at a time [msg #278].
3. **Exhaustion (orange): low energy, high focus.** We are concentrated but physically or emotionally depleted. We keep working on autopilot because we must, through force; we feel tired but for some reason cannot stop. How to recover: find time for rest, understand what caused the fatigue and plan the needed recovery, as much as it takes; overall review your lifestyle so as not to arrive (or arrive less often) in this state [msg #278].
4. **Burnout (red): low energy, low focus.** Complete depletion with neither energy nor concentration: loss of strength and no desire to do anything. How to recover: long rest, a review of goals, priorities and lifestyle, and seeking support, often already from specialists [msg #278].

The point of the model is to treat energy consciously and take measures to return to the full-charge zone. From mid-January 2025 Katya began tracking her own energy and noticed in advance that she is rarely in the green zone [msg #278]. Her verdict on the source book: she bought Ko's «Recharge» and found it "about nothing", advising not to buy it, while valuing the metaphor [msg #278].

### Three emotion-regulation systems (Paul Gilbert) and three safety signals

Guest text by psychologist-philosopher Katya from the channel Balabanova. The usual talk about balance means work versus rest, family versus career, self versus others; her proposal is that the problem may be not "outside" (how time is distributed) but "inside": three systems of emotional regulation, which determine how we feel and whether we have strength to live, may be out of balance. We are mammals and the nervous system still lives by evolutionary rules formed long before offices and deadlines. British psychologist Paul Gilbert described three main systems, color-coded here [msg #591]:

1. **Red: the threat system.** Anxiety, mobilization, defense [msg #591].
2. **Blue: the drive system.** Interest, search for resources, achievement [msg #591].
3. **Green: the safety (soothing) system.** Calm, reliability, restoration [msg #591].

Illustration, "a typical day of one lioness": she notices strange lions on the pride's territory, the threat system activates (a surge of adrenaline and cortisol, rage, defense of territory) and she drives them off, motivated by avoiding harm. Then she must feed herself and her cubs, goes hunting and kills an antelope: the blue system works, with focus on obtaining a resource, activity, concentration, a lot of dopamine and energy aimed at a result. Then everyone eats and the lioness lies down to digest: the "rest and digest" state. She may lick her cub, press against her lion, bask in the sun. In this green system there is softness, calm and contact; wounds heal, tissues restore, immunity normalizes and the hormonal system regulates [msg #591].

The diagnosis of constant human tiredness: for many modern people life has turned into alternation of the red and blue systems, anxiety and achievement. Even moments of rest are often dictated by achievement motives. The nervous system does not restore that way. The three-legged stool image: a stool works only with all three legs; each system solves its own life task, and without full rest the system will always be unstable [msg #591].

Three ways to start giving the nervous system signals of safety and calm [msg #591]:

1. **Rhythmic soothing breathing,** for example inhale and exhale to a count of 5, or the 2-7-8 pattern with a breath hold in the middle [msg #591].
2. **Gentle touch to yourself;** a palm on the chest, at the heart, activates the parasympathetic system especially well [msg #591].
3. **Imagining your safe place or safe color** and staying there for a while [msg #591].

Katya Strelnikova adds that this is a skill that requires respect and practice [msg #591].

### Leaking vs blocked energy (Leonid Krol)

From Krol's book «Энергия — новая валюта». The book considers more causes of absent energy; these two caught Katya because she had never thought about energy from this side. Both lead to the state of "permanently half-discharged batteries" but work differently and must be handled differently [msg #621]:

- **Leak.** Energy exists but goes somewhere: into procrastination, phone scrolling, household chores, heightened distractibility. The cause of leaks is unconscious habitual feelings, what we keep trying to suppress in ourselves while it does not actually go anywhere; that anxiety exhausts. Remedy: becoming aware of the feelings and living them through [msg #621].
- **Block.** We managed to suppress the feelings. We are effective, organized, do everything on time, but feel nothing. Energy seems to exist, but there is "not enough" of it for anything important, and nothing is particularly wanted. Remedy: learning to feel and to want (the book contains concrete exercises, which the post does not reproduce) [msg #621].

Katya's self-observation: she caught herself thinking that in recent years she has not wanted anything "that strongly", and that she should perhaps look at blocks [msg #621].

### Swinging the pendulum and a repertoire for each energy state

Two further ideas from Krol, each with a method [msg #621]:

1. **Where there is prolonged stability there is no energy.** Energy is where there is movement, goals and dreams, and those, even the very positive ones, mean uncertainty and absence of stability. The harder we hold on to stability and the more the brain tries to preserve the status quo, the less energy it gives us (lest we do something out of the ordinary). The way out, per Krol, is not to break everything and start anew but to start quietly "swinging the pendulum", at first only in imagination, by asking: who else can I be? how else can I live? [msg #621]
2. **Do not hate low-energy periods; map their cycle and assign activities to each state.** All people go through periodic rises and falls of energy; this is physiology, not pathology. Problems arise not when a decline comes but when we refuse to notice it or actively fight our "lower" states. A decline is not an enemy: it is another phase with its own logic and tasks. The solution Katya endorses: observe the periodicity of energy and states (she had kept a diary for almost 1.5 years), learn when to expect dips and rises, and create a repertoire of activities for different states. Her example: she will never sort photos or desktop files in a high-energy state, so that task belongs to low-energy days [msg #621].

### The "later" trap (why energy disappears)

Katya's diagnosis of a very common request. Her client Sasha came with a popular wish: to get her energy back, to a level «которым можно питать заводы и отапливать города» (*"that could power factories and heat cities"*). But quite quickly it turned out that «дело не в отсутствии энергии, а в отсутствии того, что ее дает» (*"the problem is not the absence of energy but the absence of what gives it"*) [msg #740].

The mechanism, as Katya describes it [msg #740]:

1. **The rule we live by.** "Work, children, home, obligations, everything 'important' first." One's own, interesting, joyful things come later: when strength appears, when I clear things up, when there is time [msg #740].
2. **Time never appears.** For some reason the time does not come [msg #740].
3. **Energy shrinks.** There is less and less energy, because for years we do not do what fills us [msg #740].
4. **The closed loop.** It becomes a vicious circle, and at some point there is no energy either for one's own things or for everything else [msg #740].

Why rest and supplements fail here: when for years we cut everything "optional" out of life, we cut out the source, and then wonder why neither vacation nor vitamins help [msg #740].

### Four types of energy (Jim Loehr and Tony Schwartz)

In the book «Жизнь на полной мощности» (*The Power of Full Engagement*) Jim Loehr and Tony Schwartz distinguish four types of energy, not one. Katya's point is that we always forget the meaning type [msg #740]:

1. **Physical energy** [msg #740].
2. **Emotional energy** [msg #740].
3. **Mental energy** [msg #740].
4. **Meaning energy** (смысловая), glossed by Katya as "why I do all this, what is truly important to me" [msg #740].

The source names the first three without further explanation; the post's emphasis is entirely on the fourth. According to Katya it is precisely the meaning energy that often feeds the others, illustrated by the saying she calls great: «Если знаешь зачем, сможешь любое как» (*"If you know why, you can handle any how"*) [msg #740].

### Three steps to starting to do what you want

Katya admits that simply starting to do what you want is not easy at all, and breaks it into three steps [msg #740]:

1. **Understand what I really want.** Not what one is supposed to want at 37, not what everyone around wants, but what I want [msg #740].
2. **Feel in the moment what I need right now.** Lie down or go for a walk? Be alone or call a friend? And check whether it leads to what I truly want, or whether it is a way to escape [msg #740].
3. **Allow myself to do it.** Without the inner "straight-A student" (внутренняя отличница) who demands that it first be earned; without guilt toward everyone and everything that is not getting attention right now [msg #740].

Katya's own case showing how hard this is: two years in a row (2023 and 2024) she set herself a single goal, «Делать, что хочу, когда хочу» (*"Do what I want when I want"*). Two years, because in the first year she did not manage to learn it. "The simplest goal in the world turned out to be one of the hardest" [msg #740].

### Rule "not later, but a little now"

The rule Katya proposes: «не "потом", а "сейчас немного"» (*not "later" but "a little now"*). She stresses the post is not a call to drop everything and go sell socks in Bali; it is simply that one's own things cannot stand at the end of the queue. An hour a week on what lights us up already changes the balance, because this is not a spending of energy but its source. She closes with two reflection questions for the reader: what are you postponing until "later", and is there room in your life now for what lights you up? [msg #740]

### Four scientific drivers of tiredness and energy

From the forwarded Daily Reminder post. Context: strangely, scientists only began to understand how the feeling of having no energy arises in recent years. The very concept of energy was almost taboo in medicine: too subjective, even esoteric. Energy cannot be measured like blood glucose; you cannot put an electricity meter to your temples. But discoveries of the last 5-7 years in mitochondrial function and brain-body signaling systems expand our picture of what makes a person energetic or, conversely, drained. Four points [msg #744]:

1. **It is definitely not a lack of food.** There are enough calories in the modern world. On the contrary, excess glucose makes mitochondria less efficient and people sluggish and irritable [msg #744].
2. **Stress raises the rate at which cells burn energy,** sometimes by 60%. That is, it literally sucks strength out [msg #744].
3. **The brain's energy budget is predictive.** The brain constantly builds predictions about how events will develop and prepares for those changes by redistributing the "energy budget". If you expect something unpleasant, you will feel tired, because the brain signals to store energy. Good news (the threat is gone) immediately brings a surge of strength [msg #744].
4. **Social environment changes felt energy.** If we understand that after spending our strength we will get support from close friends, we feel we have more energy. A person among friends is like a traveller who has taken a power bank: he worries less that the phone will run out and calmly watches YouTube videos [msg #744].

## Detailed insights

### Energy as a muscle

It is commonly accepted that to achieve something you must constantly work on projects, education, body, even relationships; strangely this approach is not extended to energy, sleep and rest (nor to happiness or self-love, which people imagine can be reached once and for all). Life is a dynamic process and so am I. The cognitive error is to believe that resource should simply be there as a matter of course. A client asked how she assesses the quality and sufficiency of her sleep answered: "Well, I go to bed every evening"; that is not enough. Resource and energy, like new knowledge or muscles, must be constantly trained, pumped and kept in tone. Some people may be lucky genetically, but the rest should not rely on chance [msg #36].

### Energy as a budget

Energy is a currency with which we pay for actions, dreams and achievements. When there is little energy, dreams become modest, goals weak and results tend to zero. We have more or less learned to handle a financial budget and no longer fantasize about buying a Prada bag or a five-star Maldives trip with an empty wallet, yet for some reason we believe that with an energy balance at zero or in the minus we can reach big goals. Before planning new achievements it is worth taking care of the "base": good physical condition and a growth mindset. Without it, it is easy to stay in place again and be disappointed in yourself. (The thought comes from a free webinar called «БАЗА» that Katya ran with Katya Keylman.) [msg #178]

### Metabolic health: notes from Huberman with Casey Means

The episode's thesis: metabolic disorders are killing humanity. Mitochondrial dysfunction leads to insulin resistance, obesity and chronic inflammation and, as a consequence, to chronic diseases, the main cause of death and the main healthcare cost in the developed world; the most striking part for Katya is that these diseases are man-made. She raises three questions: how, with such an abundance of doctors and specialties, do more and more people suffer from chronic disease; are we treating consequences rather than causes; and can one avoid getting to diagnoses at all? As a health coach she is close to the "lifestyle medicine" approach: by changing lifestyle one can prevent, control and even roll back diseases (her example: type 2 diabetes can be reversed by lifestyle interventions) [msg #152].

Thoughts she noted from the podcast [msg #152]:

- Metabolic problems underlie most chronic diseases [msg #152].
- The healthcare system focuses on treating symptoms, not removing root causes, which can only be removed through lifestyle change [msg #152].
- Lifestyle is shaped by the "environment": supermarkets packed with sugar, a sedentary way of life, chronic stress and the constant urge to cram the uncrammable into 24 hours at the expense of sleep [msg #152].
- The modern world constantly provokes fear and stress and triggers a cellular defense reaction; stress raises blood sugar [msg #152].
- Ultra-processed, nutrient-poor foods break the body's natural signaling mechanisms, disrupting satiety and metabolism [msg #152].
- Shortening the eating window or intermittent fasting (the Russian "don't eat after six") reduces insulin stimulation and promotes metabolic flexibility [msg #152].
- Weight-loss drugs do not remove the root cause of chronic disease [msg #152].
- Sitting less and moving more is a simple solution for better health [msg #152].
- Daily walking of at least 3000 steps and short muscle exercises every 30 minutes improve glucose utilization and mitochondrial function and lower the risks of Alzheimer's disease, dementia, obesity, type 2 diabetes, depression, cancer and gastroesophageal reflux disease [msg #152].
- Walking after a meal significantly lowers the glycemic response [msg #152].
- To increase the number of mitochondria and improve their function, include strength training, HIIT and endurance exercise [msg #152].
- Include NEAT in everyday tasks; for example toe raises can stimulate metabolism [msg #152].
- Cold exposure can improve mitochondrial health by stimulating them to produce more heat, which increases brown fat production and improves antioxidant defense [msg #152].
- Time outdoors and in nature improves physical as well as psychological health [msg #152].

### Male energy and testosterone (guest: Gena Lokhtin)

Katya usually writes about and for women but wanted a male view, because a partner's energy is also about our energy: when the person next to you has no resource it quickly becomes a shared problem. Gena Lokhtin's explanation [msg #573]:

- **Signs are not necessarily about love.** If you notice in a man apathy, lack of initiative, low libido, quick fatigue or irritability, it does not always mean he fell out of love or burned out. Sometimes it is simply a hormonal and metabolic pit, which cannot be cured by thoughts and conversations: it is endocrinology and needs regimen, sport and recovery. The main male hormone involved is testosterone [msg #573].
- **Why it falls.** In most men testosterone gradually declines after 30. Research he cites shows its level in men in developed countries has been declining for the last 40-50 years even at the same age and health. Add chronic stress, 5-6 hours of sleep, alcohol, no training, excess weight and constant snacking and the result is not a "difficult character" but broken biochemistry. Just one week of sleeping 5 hours a night can lower testosterone in young men by 10-15% [msg #573].
- **What low testosterone means:** less energy, less ambition, less sexual desire, more anxiety and irritation [msg #573].
- **Testosterone is not about force.** Contrary to the popular myth it does not make a man aggressive and rude. It does not create new qualities but amplifies existing ones: in an athlete competitiveness, in an entrepreneur drive and risk, in a priest conviction and inner strength, in a leader confidence and the ability to make decisions. Behavioral neuroendocrinology calls it the status hormone: it strengthens the striving to take and hold a position in a hierarchy, and the manner depends on personality and environment, sometimes aggression, sometimes generosity, leadership or responsibility. The most nervous, angry and twitchy men are often precisely those with lowered testosterone [msg #573].
- **What to do.** If there is a chronically tired man next to you (or you are that man), perhaps he has not withdrawn emotionally but simply cannot cope. Without a visit to a doctor and a lifestyle change it will be hard to come back. Sometimes the best way to "bring a man back" is to help find a good endocrinologist and motivate him toward regimen and sport [msg #573].

### Case: Sasha's year (client story)

Sasha (shared with her consent, from her own year-end post in which she tagged Katya) summarized her discovery as: «Я думала, что моя суперсила — энергия, а оказалось — креативность и любовь к новому» (*"I thought my superpower was energy, but it turned out to be creativity and love of the new"*). Katya adds that it is these that give the energy. Sasha began thinking more about what interests her and, in her own words, «перестала ссать этим заниматься» (*"stopped being scared shitless to do it"*). She started spending more time on her blog, went to study styling, started a family YouTube channel and began learning to do pull-ups, all while working full-time and with a small daughter. The lesson Katya draws: **not after the energy appeared, but instead of waiting for it** [msg #740].

### Matthieu Ricard on why we never ask the main question

Katya was reading Matthieu Ricard's «Happiness» and found a take that seemed made for Sasha's post. Ricard recalls young brokers in Hong Kong who slept in the office in sleeping bags so as to catch the close of the New York stock exchange in the middle of the night. One of them got to the sea once or twice a year, looked at the ocean and thought what a strange life he was leading, and on Monday morning started all over again. Ricard believes the problem is not a lack of priorities or courage: we get so stuck in habitual life and obligation that we do not let the main question rise: "What do I actually want from life?" And once the answer is found, time to think about how to achieve it can always be found. Katya adds, half-jokingly, that this seems to be what helping professionals are for [msg #740].

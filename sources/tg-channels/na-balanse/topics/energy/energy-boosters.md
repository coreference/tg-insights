# Energy boosters and replenishing practices

## What this covers

This file collects everything the channel "На балансе" (Katya Strelnikova and Marina Sidorich, plus forwarded and guest material) taught about practices and inputs that restore energy: functional fats (MCT oil, ghee, goat cheese), a complete guide of habits that give vs. drain energy across six life spheres, Andrew Huberman's daily routine (translated by the channel), the Russian banya, forest bathing and other contact with nature, long-distance hiking, floating and birding, plus the author's stance that a healthy, energetic life does not have to be "perfect". The source frames these as practical, mostly easy-to-adopt levers: "Есть простые вещи, которые достаточно легко внедрить в жизнь и при этом внести большой вклад в наше состояние." (*There are simple things that are fairly easy to bring into your life and that still make a big contribution to our state.*) [msg #16]

Part 2 adds one post: Katya Strelnikova's "Wellness Barcelona" list. She wrote it as a sequel to her earlier wellness post about Dubai. It covers places and activities from her two-month "велнес-воркейшн" (*wellness workation*) in Barcelona, i.e. what "помогали мне чувствовать себя топчик" (*helped me feel top-notch*). The post adds new material to the existing knowledge file: concrete sauna/ice-bath and banya formats, a way to find studios while travelling, choosing where to live by your sport, food and drink sourcing, a weekend nature escape, and a sun-protection rule. The links, the referral discount, channel cross-promo, venue prices and dinner prices are logistics or promo and are dropped. The knowledge in the post is kept. [msg #671]

## Core ideas

- **We constantly confuse what gives energy with what takes it.** The authors say we follow the brain's lead and repeat the same mistakes, "путаем то, что дает нам энергию с тем, что ее забирает" (*confusing what gives us energy with what takes it away*), resulting in energy deficit, loss of concentration and demotivation. Learning to choose actions "в копилку своей энергии" (*into the piggy bank of your energy*) is called a worthy goal for a modern woman. Full guide: see *Guide to habits that give and eat energy*. [msg #25]
- **Start with the basics.** Change always begins with fundamentals: if you sleep 4 hours, a light alarm clock or magnesium is unlikely to have an impressive effect. [msg #25] Similarly, the author separates what is "база" (*the base*) in Huberman's routine — morning light, night-time darkness — from individual preferences such as breathing practices. [msg #203]
- **Any supplement is a tool, used only when needed.** Katya stopped MCT for the same reason she limited caffeine: "любая добавка — это инструмент" (*any supplement is a tool*), to be applied only when necessary. [msg #44]
- **Nature works as medicine.** The channel repeatedly cites studies on forest time, nights in nature, hiking, looking at water, hot springs, darkness and birdsong lowering cortisol, blood pressure and anxiety — "Nature is cheaper than therapy". [msg #94, #164, #321]
- **Energy often comes from removing tension, not adding stimulation.** In the banya interview: "чтобы был прилив сил, нужно убрать напряжение" (*for a surge of strength, you need to remove tension*). [msg #161] Floating and birding are presented the same way — as resets and slowing down. [msg #302, #321]
- **Rest is not a reward but a component of life.** Katya asks whether rest is "не награда, а важнейшая составляющая жизни" (*not a reward but the most important component of life*). [msg #321]
- **Healthy life is the one that fits you, not the "correct" one.** Imperfect but enjoyable choices (a 1-minute meditation, 10 minutes of movement, dancing at a rave) can do more for health than an ideal routine you hate. [msg #525]

- **Contrast heat–cold in a structured session is the author's favourite energy practice in Barcelona.** She went to three saunas, noted that there are more, and named the guided sauna + ice-bath session at ILO her favourite. This extends the earlier banya material in this topic, where Anna Artemyeva lists repeated contrast among the rituals that raise energy. [msg #671]
- **After the banya, heat stops bothering you, which makes summer a good banya season.** Katya says people avoid the banya in summer because they fear the heat, "Ну и дурачки, потому что после бани как раз жара совсем не чувствуется" (*Well, silly them, because after the banya you really don't feel the heat at all*). [msg #671]
- **Choose where to live by the movement you love.** Runners should settle near the sea in El Poblenou. Hikers should settle at the foot of Tibidabo. [msg #671]
- **Look around, not at your phone.** She found many studios through ClassPass. She found the rest by walking the streets and looking around instead of at her phone. [msg #671]
- **A cap is not enough sun protection. You also need sunscreen and sunglasses.** [msg #671]

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Guide to habits that give and eat energy (6 spheres)
Purpose: a "полный гид" (*complete guide*) the authors built after answering the question "how do I know my energy is normal" — they realised such a guide was missing. Prepared before the holidays so readers could plan lifestyle changes for 2024 "to be at peak energy". The authors say they sifted a huge amount of information and chose the main habits that are "хоть как-то подтвержденные исследованиями" (*at least somewhat confirmed by research*), arranged by sphere, each with two columns: «Делаю, чтобы быть активной» (*I do this to be active/energetic*) and «Делаю, чтобы страдать» (*I do this to suffer*). [msg #25, file #25]

**1. Sleep (Сон)** [file #25]
Energy-giving:
- Sleep about 8 hours (±).
- Go to bed before 23:00.
- Wake up and go to bed at the same time.
- Get up with the first alarm.
- Switch on bright light in the morning and dim the lighting in the evening / use a special lamp.
- Walk in natural light in the morning.
- Read a book before sleep.
- Use blackout (curtains).
Energy-draining:
- Preferring other things to sleep.
- Going to bed after midnight.
- Living on different schedules on weekdays and weekends.
- Snoozing / re-setting the alarm.
- Sitting with a night-light in the morning and with overhead light in the evening.
- Not going outside, or going outside only in the dark.
- Looking at a phone or TV screen before sleep.
- Sleeping on an uncomfortable mattress and pillows.

**2. Movement (Движение)** [file #25]
Energy-giving:
- Doing morning exercises.
- Choosing to walk, take the stairs and similar.
- Going outside in the daytime for about 30 minutes.
- Taking breaks at least once an hour to stretch, walk around.
- Holding some meetings standing or on the move.
- Training several times a week.
Energy-draining:
- Mindlessly scrolling the phone ("туплю в телефоне").
- Choosing taxis and elevators.
- Sitting indoors all day.
- Not getting up from the work chair.
- Spending free time drinking or lying on the sofa.

**3. Nutrition (Питание)** [file #25]
Energy-giving:
- Eating according to the Harvard plate principle.
- Choosing a filling breakfast and a light dinner.
- Eating the norm of vegetables.
- Taking vitamins, minerals, supplements for raising energy.
- Checking product ingredients in the shop.
Energy-draining:
- Skipping meals.
- Snacking on the go.
- Eating sweets, chips and other fast carbohydrates daily.
- Eating fast carbohydrates on an empty stomach.
- Eating semi-finished and ready-made foods.
- Taking medications whose side effects affect energy level.

**4. Drinks (Напитки)** [file #25]
Energy-giving:
- Drinking caffeinated drinks no earlier than a couple of hours after waking.
- Drinking coffee separately from meals.
- Drinking water, a lot.
- Drinking unsweetened electrolytes.
Energy-draining:
- Coffee first thing in the morning.
- More than two cups of coffee a day.
- Coffee after lunch.
- Sweet drinks, or forgetting to drink at all.
- Alcohol.
- Store-bought energy drinks.

**5. Rituals (Ритуалы)** [file #25]
Energy-giving:
- Contrast or cold shower.
- Knowing how to breathe correctly and monitoring it.
- In the daytime doing yoga nidra, NSDR practices (written "NDSR" in the guide) or sleeping 20 minutes.
- Breathing practices for raising energy.
- Listening to invigorating music.
- Keeping order at home and at the workplace.
- Keeping a gratitude journal.
Energy-draining:
- Resting during the day by scrolling the phone.
- Smoking cigarettes or vapes.
- Taking drugs.
- Not cleaning up after yourself, living in a mess.

**6. Meanings (Смыслы)** [file #25]
Energy-giving:
- Doing what inspires you.
- Communicating with important and interesting people.
- Regularly feeling joy and laughing.
Energy-draining:
- "Тяну лямку" (*dragging the strap*) at an unloved job.
- Having no interesting and inspiring activities.
- Communicating with people who annoy or upset you.

**The 5-step practice for using the guide ("Увеличьте уровень энергии")** [msg #25, file #25]
1. **Take a notebook and pen, or a note on your phone.** The work is written, so you can see both lists side by side.
2. **Write out every item from the "Делаю, чтобы быть активной" column that you do NOT do.** These are your missing energy sources. (In the PDF version: first write the heading "Делаю, чтобы быть активной" and list them in a column.)
3. **Write out every item from the "Делаю, чтобы страдать" column that you DO.** These are your active energy drains (under the heading "Делаю, чтобы страдать").
4. **Look at both lists and mark the 5 most important items** across both.
5. **Arrange those 5 in order of priority and start working on the very first one.**
Pitfall the authors flag: always start with basics — "Если вы спите по 4 часа, световой будильник или магний вряд ли окажут впечатляющий эффект." (*If you sleep 4 hours, a light alarm clock or magnesium will hardly have an impressive effect.*) The authors also invite readers to share which practices did not give the expected result. [msg #25]

### Huberman daily routines (3 phases)
Purpose: Andrew Huberman's "Daily Blueprint" — routines he follows to be at peak energy — translated into Russian by Katya for readers whose English is imperfect. Title of the translated PDF: "Научно обоснованные инструменты для увеличения продуктивности, физического и психического здоровья" (*Science-based tools for increasing productivity, physical and mental health*). [msg #203, file #203]

**Phase 1: Waking and early morning** [file #203]
1. **Delay coffee 90–120 minutes after waking** to avoid an afternoon energy crash. Note: if you exercise right after waking, you can drink coffee before the workout.
2. **Drink at least 1 litre of water in this period**, adding a pinch of sea salt to increase electrolytes.
3. **Walk in the sun for at least 10–30 minutes** — reduces stress and anxiety and raises mood and energy for the whole day. Minimums: at least 10 minutes on a sunny day, at least 15 on a cloudy day, at least 30 on an overcast day.
4. **Do demanding mental work 1–4 hours after waking.** Note: moderate physical activity before mental work improves concentration and productivity.
5. **Place the computer screen at eye level or higher** to increase alertness.
6. **If you cannot focus in silence, play 40 Hz binaural beats or white noise.**
7. **Use breathing exercises to raise energy and concentration.** Mechanism given: this causes hyperventilation, adrenaline release and therefore increased nervous excitability. Example A: a deep inhale through the nose immediately followed by a deep exhale (active or passive) through the mouth; repeat 20–25 times, then exhale fully until the lungs are empty. Example B: hold the breath for 15–30 seconds; repeat for up to 5 minutes.

**Phase 2: Midday and evening** [file #203]
1. **Avoid fast carbohydrates at lunch; better make lunch low-carb** to avoid the midday slump.
2. **Walk 5–30 minutes after lunch** to improve metabolism, relax and get daylight.
3. **Practice NSDR for 10–30 minutes** to raise dopamine and energy.
4. **Eat a dinner high in complex carbohydrates and protein.**
5. **Walk at sunset** to set circadian rhythms and fall asleep better.

**Phase 3: Wind-down and sleep** [file #203]
1. **Dim the lights around sunset and avoid bright light.** Lower screen brightness and use filters to reduce blue-spectrum exposure.
2. **Cool the bedroom by 1–3 degrees.**
3. **Create maximum darkness before falling asleep:** switch everything off, close the curtains (ideally blackout) or wear a sleep mask.
4. **Wake at the same time (±1 hour) every morning, including weekends.** Sleeping longer than usual on weekends can disrupt circadian rhythms and lower energy overall.
5. **Use breathing to relax.** Example: take a deep inhale, then a second top-up inhale to fill the lungs maximally; exhale fully; repeat 2–3 times.

Katya's "Kind reminder" (in the post and the PDF): these tools are what suits "дядя Андрей" (*Uncle Andrew*). They seem scientifically grounded, "хотя по поводу откладывания утреннего кофе все еще дискуссии ведутся" (*although discussions are still going on about delaying morning coffee*). Some things are truly the base (morning light, night darkness); others are individual preference — there are "воз и маленькая тележка" (*a cartload*) of breathing practices. Many factors (genetics, lifestyle, work schedule) determine what fits you best. She asked readers whether the translation was worth her time. [msg #203, file #203]

### Bulletproof-coffee protocol with MCT and ghee
Purpose: the forwarded "deep mind" post presents super-healthy fats as "Gamechanger 2" — a regular intake that "ощутимо меняет" (*tangibly changes*) the writer's life by feeding body and brain. [msg #16]
1. **What:** blend black coffee in a shaker with MCT oil and ghee ("bulletproof coffee"). The writer has used decaf for more than a year. [msg #16]
2. **When:** right in the morning, before breakfast, then go to meditate — "Практика проходит лучше!" (*The practice goes better!*). [msg #16]
3. **Dose:** standard is 1 teaspoon of each oil per day; if there are no problems, gradually increase to a tablespoon. Ghee is not recommended above 1–2 tablespoons a day. [msg #16]
4. **Alternatives:** the coffee-with-butter combination seems strange at first, then you get used to it and enjoy both taste and effect; alternatively add the oils to salads, drinks or simply put them in your mouth. [msg #16]
5. **Effects reported by the writer:** the head clears quickly and strongly; an even, "organic" surge of strength; mood swings become softer; less craving for junk food (sweets and questionable snacks) during the day. [msg #16]
6. **Contraindications:** for MCT the team found none except individual intolerance. For ghee: liver and biliary tract diseases, the acute period of ulcer and gastritis, pancreatitis, neoplasms, exacerbation of chronic diseases. Disclaimer: the deep mind team has no professional nutrition experts; consult specialists before applying. [msg #16]

**MCT allergy-safe titration rule (Katya's addition):** because MCT is based on coconut oil, an allergic reaction is possible. Start not with a tablespoon, as packaging often suggests, but with a teaspoon, and increase gradually. "Я так не сделала — и у словила аллергический приступ — вам оно не надо." (*I didn't do that and caught an allergic attack — you don't need that.*) The channel adds that posts are not a recommendation to use supplements; consult a doctor or a specialist you trust. [msg #44]

### Banya-for-energy self-testing checklist (Anna Artemyeva)
Purpose: what to consider so the banya brings benefit and energy, from Anna Artemyeva, banya researcher and author of the NUDE blog, interviewed by Marina Sidorich. The authors note that after moving abroad they miss a real Russian banya most of all. [msg #161]

What the effect depends on: "Честное слово от вас и верно поставленной цели" (*Honestly, on you and a correctly set goal*): to wash, lose weight, process an emotional state, or socialise. Physiological laws exist, but how they unfold always depends on the body's overall state: sometimes a morning banya heats you so much you have no strength all day, sometimes an evening one releases so much energy you can't sleep. [msg #161]

Points to test, in her order: [msg #161]
- Globally: **What do you want from the banya?** **Did you choose the place and the people well for it?**
- Then look at: **How long will you stay?** **How many rounds (заходы) will you do?** **Will there be contrast?** **Will you finish with heating or cooling?** **Which aromas accompany you and how do they affect you?**
- In parallel, know: **What is going on with your blood pressure, and does it concern you?** **Nutrition — whether you have eaten or not.**
Conclusion: there is no right or wrong approach, but you will have to try different things many times to learn yourself. And remember: "если вам худо, дело не в бане — она лишь катализатор" (*if you feel bad, it's not the banya — it is only a catalyst*). [msg #161]

### Banya rituals for raising energy
Anna Artemyeva's list of the most useful rituals for lifting energy: [msg #161]
1. Throw horseradish infusion on the stones ("Поддать хреном").
2. Trample salt ("Топтать соль").
3. Do repeated, multiple contrast (hot–cold).
4. Attention only on what happens in the banya — no phone and no external tasks.

### How to start forest bathing (4 steps)
From a forwarded post by the "Дикие" channel, published before the long holiday weekend. [msg #94]
1. **Go into a forest or park without your phone,** at least for half an hour. If not ready, switch the phone to airplane mode and forget you took it (better not to use the camera either).
2. **Walk without a pre-planned route** — go wherever you are drawn; rest on a bench if you want.
3. **Don't hurry, breathe slowly, concentrate on what you hear and see in front of you.** Don't hesitate to touch interesting textures and bend down to smell flowers.
4. **Keep your attention on the nature around you as long as is comfortable.** For a start, 15 minutes is quite enough.

### Barcelona wellness-workation list (places and activities, with verdicts)
Purpose: the places and practices Katya used to stay energetic and feel good during two months of working from Barcelona. She is continuing a tradition of city wellness guides. Each item is given with what it is and her verdict. [msg #671]

1. **ILO Studios (sauna studio). This is her favourite of the three saunas she tried.** It runs guided ("направленной") sessions: within one hour you go into the sauna twice and sit in an ice bath twice. Her verdict: "Все четко, практично, качественно" (*Everything is precise, practical, high-quality*). She calls the instructors excellent. She attended breathwork, sauna healing (саунхилинг), Chinese gymnastics, and plain silent sessions. Sessions are mixed-gender, so bring a swimsuit. [msg #671]
2. **PAR (a luxurious banya with Russian roots).** It has a large territory, an open terrace and, of course, Russian food. Group steaming (групповое парение) runs once an hour. You can come for 1.5 or 3 hours. Summer bonus: few people come because they fear the heat, although after the banya you don't feel the heat at all. It has women's, men's and mixed days, so check before going. [msg #671]
3. **PORVAPOR (banya).** Unlike PAR, it has no open terrace or large territory. In exchange, it gives you a robe, slippers and a banya hat, and the restaurant serves black caviar. Her favourite thing there is the ice bath set against a backdrop of snowy forest. Steaming sessions are also held once an hour. It is mixed-gender, so bring a swimsuit. [msg #671]
4. **ClassPass (app for booking fitness studios).** "Куда бы я ни ехала, ClassPass всегда со мной" (*Wherever I travel, ClassPass is always with me*). She found many of the studios she attended there. She found the others by walking and looking around. The studios she liked most were Edan Studios, PILAT3S, SABDA, Casa Focco and Iron Studios. [msg #671]
5. **Padel at the Tibidabo court.** She played on four courts and liked this one best. It is new and small, in the shade in the morning, and covered in plants. A Russian-speaking instructor, Edgar, coaches there. She did not get to Nikki, which everyone praises. For people who came to Spain and can't play padel yet, or want to improve, she names two Russian-speaking options: Edgar of the Гречка channel, and Lena from ШкульСпортКлуб. The post's heading describes these as Russian-language padel trainings «и не только» (*and not only padel*). [msg #671]
6. **Running: live in El Poblenou.** It is a great district by the sea. While she lived more or less in the centre, she ran through the Gothic Quarter to Barceloneta, then along the sea to Poblenou, and back. Her caveat: there is not much else to run in Barcelona unless you like running in the city or uphill. [msg #671]
7. **Hiking: live at the foot of Tibidabo.** This area lies above the Sarrià-Sant Gervasi and Gracia districts. They lived there for a while, near Valcarca metro, and were very happy. From there you get simple but pleasant hikes with views of Barcelona. [msg #671]
8. **Food: Ametller Origen.** This shop chain is in almost all districts above the Diagonal. She was told it is the local equivalent of the Russian chains ВкусВилл and Азбука вкуса, and she agrees. It is not the cheapest, but it is not "кринжово" (*cringe-worthy*) to buy fruit and vegetables there. These can be tastier than at a vegetable stall. It also sells ready meals and fresh fish and meat. Only the market is tastier, but the market is also more expensive. [msg #671]

## Detailed insights

### MCT oil: what it is and the evidence the channel cites
MCT are pure medium-chain triglycerides. The deep mind post calls them today's most rapidly absorbed fats, a source of saturated and healthy fats said to harmonise all internal systems and, first of all, to raise endurance and give an instant charge of energy. Katya explains the mechanism in her own words: MCT is turned into energy by the body faster than ordinary fatty acids, "чуть ли не как углеводы" (*almost like carbohydrates*), which is why both athletes and businesspeople love it. [msg #16, #44, #46]
Studies the deep mind post cites: MCT intake increased serum ketone bodies and improved memory in study participants; in a similar case, participants taking MCT performed better on cognitive tests; one study describes MCT improving cognitive function and glucose metabolism in patients with mild Alzheimer's disease; and a study showed MCT may help increase sports endurance. [msg #16]
Katya's own hedge: there is still disagreement in the scientific community about MCT's effectiveness, but "на моем опыте это сработало" (*in my experience it worked*). [msg #44]

### Katya's year with MCT (personal case)

### Ghee: what it is and the claims
Organic ghee from grass-fed cows is obtained by heating milk or butter above 100 °C, which evaporates moisture and, the deep mind post says, harmful chemical pesticides. Used for millennia in Ayurveda as a therapeutic agent; it is believed to promote longevity, improve digestion and memory and strengthen the nervous system. Claimed facts: ghee contains no lactose, so it is easier to digest for lactose-intolerant people; it is said to be rich "не просто жирами, а мононенасыщенными жирными кислотами Омега-3" (*not just in fats but in monounsaturated Omega-3 fatty acids*), which improve cardiovascular function and lower "bad" cholesterol as part of a balanced diet; a study suggests ghee may have an anti-inflammatory effect. [msg #16]

### Goat cheese as an MCT source
Katya always thought of MCT as something from coconut, but in a podcast with professor Robert Lustig she heard that goat cheese contains a lot of it — "a legal excuse to eat it with every breakfast". MCT content in milk is highest in goat (23.0%) and sheep (21.8%), followed by mare (15.2%), cow (10.5%) and human (7.3%) milk. The most energy-giving MCT (C8 and C10) are found in **medium-aged goat cheese**. Goat milk also has less lactose than cow milk: if you are lactose-sensitive, as Katya is, the reaction may be much smaller ("Я и вовсе обхожусь без нее" — *I get by without one at all*). Caveat: goat cheese is fat, a very calorie-dense product that is easy to overdo; a fat portion is the size of your thumb. [msg #46]

### Forest bathing (shinrin-yoku)
Forest bathing is a trend growing since 2016 that came from Japan. In 1982 Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries introduced the term Shinrin-yoku (shinrin = forest, yoku = bathing) as a preventive measure against stress in the population. Research found that people resting in a forest had lower blood pressure, heart rate and salivary cortisol than those walking in a city. Studies of long-term effects are few, but forest therapy is already being integrated into the medical systems of Japan, Korea and Canada, since there are hypotheses it may support not only mental well-being but also memory, immunity and sleep. The trend created a profession, the forest therapist: a certified specialist who gently helps you engage all five senses — draws attention to the landscape, different aromas and birdsong, shows edible berries, suggests touching tree bark or damp earth. Sessions often include meditation and a tea ceremony at the end. How to start: see *How to start forest bathing (4 steps)*. [msg #94]

### Long-distance hiking as a "retreat" (Tour du Mont-Blanc)
Guest Karina (traveller, Panorama Suite blog) describes her first long hike. Katya frames winter as the time to plan summer adventures and lists such a hike among her 100 things to do once in a life. [msg #266]

- **Context and why:** they set out at a turning point — after two years of continuous travel and living in a motorhome and a decision to move to a new country. Karina says life on the road is stress: you change your usual way of life and lose routine (which matters), safety and stability; a year of relocation paperwork kept them in limbo. - **Preparation:** it was their first big hike; they did no physical preparation — only bought gear and packed the backpack correctly. [msg #266]
- **Insights (all nine):** [msg #266]
  1. Walking all week, you first think your own thoughts and then **let go of all thoughts, just living the experience** — "Настоящая медитация" (*real meditation*).
  2. It is not an endlessly hard climb: much of it is trails and villages, though ascents and descents daily with a backpack. You can't stop halfway; however hard the end of each day, it matters to tick off the completed stage. These challenges reveal something new and **let you know yourself better**.
  3. In a week you realise you don't need much for a happy life: one change of clothes and you still feel complete and beautiful; not fixated on food or excessive self-care — a chance to **live without anything superfluous**, connection with nature in action.
  4. At the end of each day the reward is a shower, rest and hot food, hut to hut: **strong happiness from simple comfort**.
  5. Sleeping in a tent in fresh air, to the sound of Alpine cow bells — she **sleeps very well** in a tent; very comfortable inflatable mats are available now.
  6. Being in nature lets you **look at your usual life from the outside** — ideas immediately come about what you'd like to change or do.
  7. **Living an adventure:** many tourists form a whole community of travellers, so you don't feel alone and can exchange stories, impressions and inspiration.
  8. Afterwards **a connection with the world, yourself and your body appears**, and you feel more than ever that the planet is our home.
  9. Special loves — Alpine cows, snowy peaks, glaciers, views, flowering Alpine meadows, mountain campsites, sunrises and sunsets — make you **experience real happiness**.

### Imperfect health: "drink in the morning and go to a rave"
Katya's coaching stance, prompted by a friend and client's post on going to a rave: a healthy lifestyle almost always looks different from internet advice. [msg #525]

- **Meditate for one minute:** without candles, lotus pose or heroics, because a minute you really give is better than ten you postpone for months. [msg #525]
- Sometimes you can "drink in the morning and go to a rave", because life is not a clinical trial and you are not a perfect soldier: dancing, laughter and bodily joy can do more for health than an ideal routine that makes you want to howl. A healthy life is the one that suits you, that you enjoy living and are satisfied with. [msg #525]

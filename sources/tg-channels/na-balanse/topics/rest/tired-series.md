# Series: I'm 40 and worn out

## What this covers

This is Katya Strelnikova's numbered essay series «Мне 40 лет. И я задолбался. Или почему мы все такие уставшие?» ("I'm 40 and I'm fed up. Or why are we all so tired?"), written in 2025–2026 as part of what she declared her "year of personal research of rest". It moves from diagnosis (the cultural taboo on rest, why modern life is more tiring than our parents' lives) to anatomy (what kinds of tiredness exist, what rest physiologically is, what only pretends to be rest), to self-assessment (a 15-item fatigue test, three hidden signs of fatigue), to reframing (rest as a skill and a condition of good work) and finally to a quantity — the Nagoski sisters' estimate that about 42% of our time must go to rest [msg #337, #343, #391, #448, #449, #460, #463, #476, #479, #579].

According to the author it matters because tiredness has become disguised as normal: people keep producing on autopilot, ignore early signals, and end up in the state «встала — и устала» ("got up — and already tired"), which "cuts off wings, blocks inspiration, blocks joy" and makes them stop setting ambitious goals. Her core claim is that people burn out not from load but from the absence of recovery, and that rest can be learned [msg #476, #479].

## Core ideas

- **Our attitude to rest is a historical product, not a truth.** Guilt about doing nothing was built by the industrial economy, Protestant ethic, Soviet ideology and 1990s survival; knowing this lets us choose differently and allow ourselves to rest again — see *Historical genealogy of the taboo on rest* [msg #337].
- **We tire more than our parents not because we are weaker but because we built a world without automatic stop signals** — we do ten people's jobs, constantly switch, are always reachable, have lost external closing times, and have added new kinds of fatigue without new kinds of rest — see *Five reasons* [msg #343].
- **Fatigue is of different kinds, so rest must be of different kinds.** You must first find which energy is in deficit; physical fuel does not fill an emotional hole — see *Four questions* [msg #391].
- **Rest is an active physiological process**, a switch from survival mode to recovery mode: it starts with sleep, needs the Default Mode Network (idleness without screens, plans or guilt) or non-competitive flow, and a balance of stimulation and calm [msg #448].
- **Many habitual "rests" are false friends** that switch you off without restoring you — see *13 activities that pretend to be rest* [msg #449].
- **Tiredness masks itself as the norm**; three overlooked signs (rest without effect, emotional blunting with irritability, a permanent pre-illness state) indicate allostatic load — see *Three signs* [msg #476].
- **Rest is not the opposite of work and not a reward; it is part of the cycle and a condition for work being done well. It is a skill and can be developed.** People burn out from lack of recovery, not from load [msg #479, #507].
- **About 42% of the day (≈10 hours) is physiologically needed for rest** according to the Nagoskis; time usually exists but is spent on scrolling instead [msg #579].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Historical genealogy of the taboo on rest
Purpose: to explain why, even when we have free time, we cannot use it — we feel guilt or awkwardness, or switch at once into "useful rest" (learning English on vacation, listening to a podcast while cooking buckwheat, scrolling Instagram while lying on the sofa). Katya asks: «Почему отдых — это уже не удовольствие, а проступок?» ("Why is rest no longer a pleasure but a misdeed?") and traces the answer through history [msg #337].

1. **Antiquity — rest as the highest form of life.** Ancient philosophers said repose is the highest form of life. The Greek word scholē, from which 'school' comes, meant 'free time', not a place where multiplication tables are taught. A self-respecting person was supposed to think, converse, walk and observe nature — what today would be called idleness. In Latin the word for business, negotium, literally means 'not-rest'. The lesson she draws: «Работа — это отклонение от естественного состояния, а не норма.» ("Work is a deviation from the natural state, not the norm.") [msg #337]
2. **Middle Ages — rhythmic life.** "According to some researchers", peasants worked on average 150–180 days a year; the rest was holidays, fairs, recovery and simply being. Katya contrasts this with today's 28 days of vacation (and the American 10) and jokes that Ivan the Fool who lay on the stove was perhaps not such a fool. Labour was hard, but life was rhythmic and rest had a natural place [msg #337].
3. **Industrial revolution and Protestant ethic — rest becomes a sin.** Machines started the era of productivity, output norms and time accounting. At about the same time Protestant ethic began to interpret work as the highest form of serving God. Rest stopped being a good and became a potential sin — "ideal fuel for an economy built on growth and endless production". She summarises: «даже религия переобулась» ("even religion changed its shoes") [msg #337].
4. **Soviet ideology — rest as a function of production.** Rest existed but strictly functionally: rest homes, sanatoriums and weekends were not care for oneself but a tool to restore the labour resource — the body had to rest in order to go back to building communism [msg #337].
5. **The 1990s — survival as priority.** After the USSR collapsed came total uncertainty. Survival, not self-care, became the priority; work was a cult again, now for money rather than the Motherland. Whoever works more, adapts faster and "keeps spinning" survives. Rest finally became something either "earned" or unavailable — and even when "everything" became allowed, relaxing is still frightening [msg #337].
6. **Today — the culture of endless productivity**, with three rules: (a) rest must be earned; (b) weekends must be spent usefully; (c) vacation is not relaxation but time for covert (or overt) self-development, or at worst for organising a "fair of vanity" to keep up with the Joneses at any cost. The result: we have "simply forgotten" that rest is a basic need, not a whim; an act of self-respect, not a reason for guilt; freedom, not weakness [msg #337].

Conclusion and how to use it: the current attitude is "not objective truth but the product of a particular historical, economic and cultural paradigm". We were born inside this game, but «игра — это не реальность, это соглашение» ("a game is not reality, it is an agreement"); at any moment we can ask "do I want to take part in it, or choose something else?" The environment shapes consciousness, but we have the right not to follow it mindlessly. Symptoms of the inherited culture she names: we cannot stop even when exhausted; we fear silence because we do not know who we are without tasks; we rest so that it is visible that "I am still useful". Knowing this, we can again permit ourselves to rest and learn how. Her closing question to readers: what prevents you from really resting? [msg #337]

### Five reasons we tire more than our parents
Purpose: an answer to «Ну чего ты там устала, ты же ничего не делала» ("What are you tired from, you didn't do anything") and "My mother worked from morning to night — why am I collapsing?" Katya says it is fine to hear this from a stranger but frightening to hear it from yourself or from friends and clients about themselves. She wrote the post in Moscow in her former "driven horse" state, trying to fit household and bureaucratic errands, doctors, friends, beauty procedures, blogs and work into one trip [msg #343].

1. **We do the work of ten people — voluntarily.** Twenty or thirty years ago a travel agent booked tickets, a secretary typed letters, a shop assistant found your size. The smartphone turned us into all of them at once: travel agent, cashier, typist and support service in one person [msg #343].
2. **We live in constant switching.** Computers, internet and smartphones put us in multitasking mode, but the brain has not learned this. She quotes MIT neuroscientist Earl Miller: «мы не выполняем задачи параллельно — мы безумно быстро прыгаем между ними, платя когнитивный налог каждый раз» ("we don't do tasks in parallel — we jump between them insanely fast, paying a cognitive tax every time"). Her stated consequence: the nervous system burns glucose faster, and by lunch we resemble a phone at 5% charge [msg #343].
3. **We are always reachable.** A landline could not be carried (if you were lucky enough to have one); not answering meant you were busy, and that was normal. Now an unread message is perceived almost as a personal insult; the world expects us online 24/7, and the nervous system lives in permanent combat readiness [msg #343].
4. **External stop signals have disappeared.** Shops used to close at 18:00–19:00 and on weekends, TV switched off its picture at midnight (the colourful test screen — "an express age test"), and work stayed at work. Today a laptop with an estimate sits on the kitchen table, the shop in the phone works 24/7, and work Zoom crawls into the bedroom. The external boundaries "busy / resting" have been erased [msg #343].
5. **We added new kinds of fatigue but no new kinds of rest.** Three new kinds:
   - **Cognitive burnout** (Когнитивное выгорание): the brain overheats from constant switching, the volume of information and of interactions.
   - **Sensory fatigue** (Сенсорная усталость): endless staring at screens exhausts the resource of eyes, ears and brain.
   - **Emotional overload** (Эмоциональный перегруз): a 24/7 broadcast of other people's lives and constant dissatisfaction with oneself [msg #343].

Conclusion: «Мы устаём больше не потому, что слабеем, а потому, что построили мир без автоматических стоп-сигналов.» ("We tire more not because we are getting weaker but because we built a world without automatic stop signals.") If we reclaim the right to pause, switch off and be inactive, it turns out the energy has not gone anywhere — it just needs a place and time to recharge [msg #343].

### Four questions to understand what rest you lack
Purpose: for the state «Формально отдыхаю, но чувствую себя, как загнанная лошадь» ("Formally I rest, but I feel like a driven horse"), which Katya felt constantly until, at the end of the previous year, she began to "take rest apart bone by bone". Premise: «Усталость. Бывает. Разной.» ("Fatigue. Comes. In different kinds.") By evening not only the body runs down — we are emptied emotionally, overheat our eyes or brain, sometimes lose meaning. «Если «дырка» в эмоциональном баке, бессмысленно подливать физического топлива.» ("If the hole is in the emotional tank, it's pointless to pour in physical fuel.") So first determine which energy is in the minus today. She built this from her earlier description of the Schwartz and McCarthy energy model, now reassembled into her own understanding; she explicitly does not claim it is a scientific treatise, but says it helps in practice. When to use: each time you want to rest or resting is not working [msg #391].

1. **Are my needs covered?** The base of a rested state is the layer of biological needs: sleep, food, movement. If you sleep five hours, massage and meditation will not help; if you eat sweets instead of lunch, an energy crash is unavoidable. Rest here means: «выстроить систему здоровых привычек, где ваши человеческие потребности в приоритете. И закрыты.» ("build a system of healthy habits where your human needs are a priority. And covered.") This layer was also discussed at the free webinar "БАЗА" with Katya Keilman [msg #391].
2. **What is in deficit right now?** Over months, weeks, days and hours we "grind down parts": the brain tires, eyes dry, the body stiffens, emotions burn out, self-belief fails. If you looked at a monitor all day, eyes and brain will not rest by looking at another screen; this is also why "lying down to rest" is often a bad idea. Rest here means: «вернуть именно то, что вы у себя забрали» ("return exactly what you took from yourself") [msg #391].
3. **What do I need a breather from?** Katya admits that her mother's saying "rest is a change of activity" used to annoy her, but "mom was right after all". To avoid burning out you simply need to change activity regularly; doing the same thing without a break guarantees fatigue. Her own example: she forcibly does not allow herself to work on weekends and on vacations, "even for a minute, even if I really want to". Rest here means: «давать себе передышки даже от того, что сильно любите, и «легко»» ("give yourself breathers even from what you love dearly and find 'easy'") [msg #391].
4. **How much space is there in my days?** We need time where nothing must be done, improved, replenished or fixed: watching clouds float, lazily stirring soup, listening to your breath, pausing over a book. Precisely in this state meanings and thoughts appear, the desire to live is born, and you can hear yourself. Pitfall: running from yoga to massage with a crammed head leaves you a driven horse. Rest here means: «организовывать себе островки спокойствия и тишины» ("organise islands of calm and silence for yourself") [msg #391].

Summary rule: if you do not build the base, do not organise space in your calendar, and rest with something other than what is tired, rest is unlikely to work. Exercise: ask yourself the four questions today and identify your "minus(es)" [msg #391].

### Physiological definition of rest (what rest literally is)
Purpose: explain complaints such as "I lay down for two weeks and still didn't rest", "yesterday I spent 1.5 hours in the dark waiting for a headache to pass — that was resting, right?", "I need a vacation after the vacation". Rest is not merely stopping work or lying down: «Это активный процесс, когда тело, психика и внимание переключаются из режима выживания в режим восстановления.» — the bold in the source covers only "активный процесс" ("an active process, when body, psyche and attention switch from survival mode to recovery mode"). Katya also claims we need rest for inspiration, new goals and new steps [msg #448].

Components:
1. **Sleep is the base.** Although we do not control it, sleep is a form of active recovery: the body cleans the brain of waste, processes emotions, integrates thoughts, restores tissues and tunes hormones. When sleep is short the body cannot complete these cycles; after bad sleep we feel literally biochemically unbalanced, stressed and anxious [msg #448].
2. **Idle time switches on the Default Mode Network.** When we allow ourselves to zone out, be silent, not look at a screen, plan nothing and just be, the brain switches into the DMN, which (per the studies she links) is responsible for imagination, integration of experience, awareness of meaning and the feeling "I am in my place". In these moments the brain really relaxes and insights, inner calm and creative inspiration are born. Under constant connection to content, chronic stress, anxiety and hyperfocus this network "literally goes dark" — we are not resting but spending energy. This is why she writes about islands of silence and disconnecting from gadgets and content [msg #448].
3. **No guilt.** «настоящий отдых невозможен с чувством вины» ("real rest is impossible with a feeling of guilt"). When an inner voice says "I'm wasting time" or "I could be doing something else", the brain stays in anxious control, the DMN does not switch on, and we do not rest [msg #448].
4. **Flow, but not as competition.** Sometimes rest comes in flow: knitting, gardening, cooking, drawing, walking, cleaning to music, yoga — such simple activities activate the same recovery networks, letting the brain rest from directed focus and the body enter a rhythm of safety. Pitfall: if you knit thinking "I must finish", go to the gym "to burn calories" or walk "to close the rings", it is not rest but one more project that causes anxiety and burnout. Evidence she cites: a study (in Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience, per the link) showed for the first time that in flow activity in the prefrontal cortex (including self-control areas) drops and areas linked to sensorimotor integration and attention work harder — the brain "rests" from internal control and enters a stable rhythm of focus; and another paper describes flow as a balance between dopaminergic stimulation (motivation) and a temporary reduction of the "self-monitoring" network including the DMN, creating lightness and engagement without effort [msg #448].
5. **Balance of stimulation and calm.** Understand what exactly is tired and give that a rest: after long sitting — walk; after meetings — be in silence; after intense sport — just lie down and feel your breathing [msg #448].

Literal definition — rest is: when the brain is not occupied (not with reels, planning, or controlling the situation); when there is no anxiety, guilt or regret about doing "the wrong thing"; in moments of non-strenuous rhythmic action; when we rest exactly what needs rest [msg #448].

### 13 activities that only pretend to be rest ("false friends of rest")
Purpose: a checklist of habitual ways of resting — lying with a phone, a party with 2+ glasses "or something stronger", a vacation planned with a month's worth of activities (with kids, of course) — that feel familiar just like the fatigue that no longer goes away. These "only pretend to be rest but actually hinder it" [msg #449].

1. **Stimulants** (alcohol, drugs) — let you switch off, but not rest [msg #449].
2. **Zoning out in social media** — not rest but tension, comparison, anxiety and FOMO [msg #449].
3. **Excessive socialising**, especially at big events where you must "pretend" — usually takes more energy, causing a social hangover [msg #449].
4. **Lying on the sofa** after sitting all day (unless you are at the extreme stage of exhaustion) — gives not calm but even more fatigue [msg #449].
5. **Excessive planning and management** after work, on weekends and on vacation — we tire further from decision-making and rush [msg #449].
6. **Time with children** — "100% wonderful and joyful time, no argument (though who are we kidding)", but with such sensory load and level of responsibility you can hardly rest [msg #449].
7. **Listening or watching at 2x speed** — extra acceleration, more rush and anxiety, so fatigue x2 [msg #449].
8. **Doing something "because I must", out of anxiety and fear** — whether training, study or a tasty dinner — not recovery but a form of control, which exhausts [msg #449].
9. **Resting "usefully"** — a form of control, and control, "as we've understood", will not let you rest [msg #449].
10. **Overloading yourself physically** — you switch but do not rest (when after such rest you need more rest) [msg #449].
11. **Doing several things at once** (watching a lecture or series while answering chats or staring at reels) — "a total overload of systems — alarm!" [msg #449].
12. **"Doing nothing" not from a pure heart** — when inside there is anxiety and a hurricane, you can spend even more than before instead of accumulating [msg #449].
13. **Sacrificing sleep** — "a mortal sin altogether" [msg #449].

The accompanying card sums up the principle: «ВЫБИРАЕШЬ - ЖИВЕШЬ. ЗАСТАВЛЯЕШЬ - ВЫГОРАЕШЬ. ПОКОЙ НЕ СПЕШИТ.» ("You choose — you live. You force — you burn out. Calm does not hurry.") [img #449]. Readers are invited to count "how many Pokémon" (items) they have collected [msg #449].

### Test "How fed up am I" — 15 signs it is time to rest
Purpose: written for late November, when "the world again pretends that December 31 is the last day in human history" and everything must be finished, bought, found and started while being at peak energy with a smile. Katya's point: precisely when everything accelerates, slow down and look at yourself, so there is energy in 2026 too. Scoring: 1 point for every item where you say "yes, that's me" [msg #460].

1. Sleep and weekends do not make it easier.
2. Energy collapse on weekends.
3. I want nothing except lying and looking at my phone.
4. I get irritated quickly and snap at loved ones.
5. I give myself no slack: I criticise myself non-stop.
6. I constantly crave sweets, chips, buns.
7. I often get sick.
8. Something constantly stabs, aches or throbs somewhere (tests are normal).
9. I can't fall asleep or I wake up at night.
10. No inspiration and no new ideas.
11. I want to get sick in order to rest.
12. I can't concentrate.
13. Hard to think, make decisions, even choose what to eat.
14. Nothing pleases me — even what used to give pleasure.
15. Constant guilt: "I must pull myself together", "I should have done more" [msg #460].

How to treat the result: if you recognise yourself, the first reaction is probably to scold yourself. That is very human, because lack of energy is stress, and stress produces tunnel thinking (often in dark colours). But it is "not a reason for self-flagellation but a sign". Her advice: now is the moment not to accelerate but to take care of yourself — and maybe start not a new year but a new life in which you can breathe again [msg #460].

### Three signs you need rest but don't notice (the TATT club)
Purpose: one of the main problems with fatigue is that it masquerades as the norm — we keep living on autopilot, delivering the usual efficiency and thinking "all OK", "everyone lives like this", "just hold on until New Year (no)", even when the body has long been saying "wake up, Neo, time to rest". If you recognise yourself you are in the club TATT (tired all the time): Katya reports that Western doctors have this half-joking "syndrome" for a person who is officially healthy, with excellent tests and no depression, yet everything hurts, there is no energy, and they don't want to live [msg #476].

1. **You seem to rest but feel no effect.** Sleep exists but does not restore; you took a day off and still have no energy; a free evening, and the morning is just as hard. This is not "I'm lazy", "I need to pull myself together", or "I lack discipline" — it signals life in a chronic survival mode where any recovery works with a delay or not at all [msg #476].
2. **The joyful somehow doesn't please, the irritating irritates too much.** Emotional blunting is a common sign of fatigue: an exhausted nervous system saves energy by narrowing the range of emotions, lowering the capacity for pleasure and reducing interest. At the same time things you didn't notice before irritate you, because there simply isn't enough energy to process these emotions [msg #476].
3. **You live in a constant pre-illness (or illness) state.** Coughing, dry throat, inflamed skin and mucous membranes — the vibe "about to get sick". During such periods immunity really runs on reserve: the body lacks resources either to get fully ill or to fully recover, so it hangs in between. In psychophysiology this is called allostatic load — accumulated load on the nervous, hormonal and immune systems; the organism functions but does not recover, hence "micro-symptoms" that drag on for weeks [msg #476].

Why we ignore them: not because something is wrong with us but because fatigue has become the norm — a culture where 'busy' = successful, 'exhausted' = diligent, 'resting' = (crudely) got there unfairly. We ignore early signs because we don't want to believe we are not the super-women who never tire and manage everything. The price: «встала — и устала» ("got up — and tired"); it cuts off wings, closes off inspiration, blocks joy; we stop setting ambitious goals, stop dreaming of that trip, never find time for sport, a blog, a course. The good news: living like this is not obligatory, because rest is the same kind of skill and can be learned [msg #476].

### Rest as a skill — a condition of good work, not a reward
Purpose: to correct the common confusion that rest is the opposite of work. Katya opens with a quote she read: "Work is necessary to live, but taking the time to recharge is essential for living well." [msg #479]

Principles:
1. **Rest is not the opposite of work** — not "either–or", not two competing states (she blames "the creators of work/rest balance and other dichotomies"). Rest is part of the cycle, the same biological necessity as sleep, food and movement; without it productive work is impossible — not because we are weak but because that is how the body, nervous system and brain are built [msg #479].
2. **Burnout comes from missing recovery, not from load.** Her observation from age and work with people: «люди выгорают не от нагрузки, а от отсутствия восстановления» ("people burn out not from load but from the absence of recovery") [msg #479].
3. **Rest is a skill that can be developed** — like running, singing or sex: we all "sort of know how", but it helps to level up before saying we really can. Lying on the sofa doesn't yet mean recovering; even if we can switch off, we often can't reboot [msg #479].
4. **First lesson:** «отдых — не награда после хорошо выполненной работы. А условие, чтобы работа была выполнена хорошо.» ("rest is not a reward after work well done, but the condition for work to be done well") [msg #479].
5. **Why rest is "so hard":** fatigue has become a currency. "I'm busy", "I have 7 meetings today", "I slept 5 hours" sound like medals; we wear stress, overwork and workaholism as jewellery, but it drags us to the bottom, makes us less productive, takes away joy and desires. The only way out of this "cultural hypnosis" is to reclaim the right to pause and understand that rest is not a luxury, weakness or whim but «инфраструктура хорошей жизни» ("the infrastructure of a good life") [msg #479].
6. **Build a recovery system at three time scales** — daily, weekly, seasonal — by looking at your life from outside and seeing what your state, work and achievements depend on. Rest can be practised, learned, and made structured, conscious and built into life [msg #479].
7. **Protect the time.** «Время для восстановления не освободится само собой. Его нужно выделять и защищать.» ("Time for recovery will not free itself up. It must be allocated and protected.") [msg #479]

### 42% for rest (the Nagoskis) and a sample breakdown
Purpose: a quantity for how much rest is needed. Katya ties it to "The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy", where a supercomputer computed for seven and a half million years and answered "42" to the ultimate question; she says the Nagoski sisters in "Burnout" seem to have found the meaning of 42 [msg #579].

- **The number.** About 42% of time — around 10 hours a day — is physiologically necessary for rest, as a minimum for normal functioning. Katya stresses this is the Nagoskis' own authorial arithmetic, assembled from what science says about sleep, movement, nutrition and nervous-system recovery. Ten hours is the base; for some it is mostly sleep (if you know you naturally need more than 8 hours), but usually not only sleep [msg #579].
- **Why: energy is wave-like.** We are not built for continuous concentration; energy moves from focus to diffuse "digesting" and back. When you don't resist this cycle, the brain's passive-mode network (DMN) switches on; in that state new connections form, ideas come, and what we learned and lived through is integrated — which is why the best solutions arrive in the shower, on a walk, washing dishes. «Это не прокрастинация. Это нейробиология.» ("It's not procrastination. It's neurobiology.") [msg #579]
- **Forcing non-stop concentration is self-gaslighting.** Making yourself concentrate without breaks and pretending it should be so is "not courage and not great willpower, but gaslighting, and a rejection of your humanity" [msg #579].
- **The paradox of abundance.** Living in a developed country amid abundant goods and services, we often cannot satisfy basic physiological needs without inner struggle, guilt and shame for our "laziness" or "greed" [msg #579].
- **Active rest counts.** Rest can be switching, not stopping: loading one thing while what worked before recovers — physical load after mental work, silence after socialising, movement after stillness. A client's phrase: «Отдых — это еще и спросить себя, не хочу ли я подвигаться.» ("Rest is also asking yourself whether I'd like to move.") [msg #579]
- **Sample breakdown of the 42%** (everyone's list will look different): 8 hours of sleep; 30 minutes of movement; 30 minutes of mindful eating without screens; 30 minutes of live communication; 30 minutes for something of your own [msg #579].
- **Where the time goes.** Ten hours seems small and doable (Katya says she spends much more, but she has no children), yet in her work she sees many people don't give themselves even this and then wonder why they have no energy. We often under-sleep and under-give ourselves mindful time and compensate with scrolling and watching junk: «время есть и оно тратится, но тратится не туда» ("the time exists and is being spent, but spent on the wrong things") [msg #579].
- **The Nagoskis' warning (card):** «Мы не говорим, что вы должны тратить 42% времени на отдых. Мы предупреждаем: если вы не потратите их, эти сорок два процента придут за вами, и вы не обрадуетесь.» ("We are not saying you must spend 42% of your time on rest. We are warning you: if you don't, those forty-two percent will come for you, and you won't be glad.") The card continues that they will grab you by the scruff, throw you down and stand on top of you, and you will lie sprawled, defeated [img #579].

## Detailed insights

### Katya's own case: changing every habit is not the same as knowing how to rest
Months before turning 40, Katya caught herself fed up and in the same state as 8–10 years earlier, during the active stage of her burnout — paradoxically, because over those 8 years she had changed all her habits: she started getting enough sleep, doing sport, drinking coffee only in the morning, stopped partying, drinking and smoking, left employment, changed field and became a health coach. Life changed radically, yet she was still worn out. On top of that she kept a diary, which showed she was in 'full balance', and at about the same time she wrote a post about choosing to get sick in order to rest. The lesson she drew: rest and recovery are a separate skill worth developing by 40 if she wants a long, active life, so she declared 2025 her «Годом личного исследования отдыха» ("Year of personal research of rest"), gathered the closed group "Отдохнувшие", and shared experiments throughout the year. Reported result: the experiments "seem to work" — her workload tripled and she moved to another country again, yet she has more energy than a year before [msg #463].

The series opens with the same framing: since the start of 2025 she had been actively researching rest — thinking, experimenting, speaking about it — and began to collect and systematise it in posts [msg #337]. Her own habit for the third question of the 4-question method (no work on weekends and vacations) is listed there [msg #391].

### What participants learned when rest became a skill
Katya reports spending the year near tired women — not "lazy" or "doing nothing", but those who try hard, work, think, care and develop, yet feel they have less energy than before and more demands on themselves. Nearly each had her own explanation: «Это потому что переезд / дети / возраст / гормоны / проект / стресс» ("It's because of the move / kids / age / hormones / project / stress"), "When I finish this stage — I'll rest", "I just need a proper vacation", "I could before, so I must now". What turned out to matter: rest is not a vacation, not a spa, not "sleeping enough once". «Отдых — это навык. И если его нет, жизнь очень быстро превращается в череду рывков между усталостями.» ("Rest is a skill. And if you don't have it, life very quickly turns into a series of spurts between fatigues.") [msg #507]

Participant statements she quotes from the "Отдохнувшие" group:
- Basic things — sleep, stress, rest, movement — are not bonuses but the foundation [msg #507].
- Rest stopped being a reward for feats and became part of an ordinary day [msg #507].
- "I no longer wait until it gets really bad to stop" [msg #507].
- "I stopped thinking something is wrong with me. We are all alive, and everyone has dips" [msg #507].
- "I can write 'I slept well today' — and it counts as success" [msg #507].
- «Следишь за тем, как восстанавливаешься — восстанавливаешься» ("When you track how you recover, you recover.") [msg #507]
- It's a joy to be among like-minded women who regularly look at their state [img #507].
- One participant, Olya: in the group people told her "Olya, take care of yourself", and that voice began saying it to her herself, daily [img #508].
- So much on this topic had already been read and heard, but theory and practice are always different things, and someone else's theory may not fit at all [img #509].
- "I saw the cyclicity of processes and understood it is not a malfunction but the normal design" [img #510].
- Rest stopped being a reward for feats or an "antibiotic" for when you've completely run yourself into the ground [img #511].
- Live examples helped to see one's own patterns and that we are all human [img #512].
- The group gave the possibility to feel normal, imperfect and alive [img #513].

Reported outcome (Katya's observation): none of them earned less, dropped out of life or "relaxed and blew everything" — on the contrary. «Когда критерий успеха сместился с упоротости на отдохнувшесть, жизнь стала ровнее, устойчивее и честнее.» ("When the criterion of success shifted from being driven into the ground to being rested, life became smoother, more stable and more honest.") — without heroism, strain or the constant feeling of falling short; she hedges that they are "still in the process", but there is definitely less of all that [msg #507].

What is hard for adult women, in her observation: admitting that the former rhythm no longer works; stopping proving "I still can"; allowing themselves to live not at the maximum but in contact with reality. But «разучиться жить на износ — можно» ("unlearning living to the point of wear is possible") — not over a weekend or in one conversation, but through observation, regularity and careful attention to oneself. Her stated aims for such work: notice where energy leaks; stop fighting yourself; learn to recover in ordinary life; understand how *you* in particular rest. She calls it "a problem with an asterisk", but solvable in practice [msg #507].

## Evidence and numbers

- Medieval peasants worked on average 150–180 days a year "according to some researchers"; compare 28 vacation days today and 10 in the USA [msg #337].
- Earl Miller (MIT): multitasking is fast switching that costs a cognitive tax each time; Katya adds that the nervous system uses glucose faster, leaving you like a phone at 5% by lunch [msg #343].
- Sleep-deprivation statistics in the 42% post (immediately following the Nagoski discussion): chronic sleep deprivation (short or interrupted sleep) is among factors behind 20% of serious road accidents; risk of all common diseases — cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes, hypertension, Alzheimer's, immune disorders — rises by up to 45%; lack of sleep is a more reliable predictor of type 2 diabetes than lack of physical activity; three recent meta-analyses of millions of participants found that sleeping less than 5–6 hours a day raises all-cause mortality risk by 12% [msg #579].

# Longevity and aging

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's posts on longevity: what really extends life (a guest post on biohacker Gary Brecka), the Blue Zones project and the controversy over its data, Bryan Johnson (his sleep philosophy, the Netflix film Don't Die, his podcast on "stopping making yourself worse" and his $400 million list of tools), the KamaLama life-expectancy calculator (introduced in a forward and then tested by the author on herself), a Chinese cohort study of people over 80 comparing genetics and lifestyle, and an NYU study showing that anxiety about aging accelerates epigenetic aging. The through-line, in the author's words, is that most "magic pills" are free: basic habits (movement, sleep, food, relationships, low stress) dominate, the wellness and anti-age industry sells anxiety and illusions, and the goal is to extend not age but active age while accepting one's own humanity and impermanence [msg #108, #215, #287, #288, #590, #619, #620, #622, #623].

This part adds one post, forwarded by the author from Daily Reminder, that takes on the popular anecdote «Дедушка курил и дожил до ста лет!» (*Grandpa smoked and lived to a hundred!*) and checks it against a study of centenarians in Rome. Its point is that such smoking centenarians exist but are a small minority, and that even among them continued smoking is associated with worse health. It extends the existing topic file's treatment of modifiable lifestyle factors: the Chinese 80+ cohort study already listed smoking among the "modifiable factors" that outweigh genetics, and this post supplies a smoking-specific data point from the other end — who actually reaches 100 [msg #675].

## Core ideas

- **Longevity is driven mostly by ordinary, modifiable habits, not biohacks.** Blue Zones people are long-lived not because they are biohackers but because of movement, social ties, purpose and whole foods; Bryan Johnson's $400M conclusions are sleep, water and vegetables; the 80+ cohort study found lifestyle factors outweigh genetics roughly 3:1 in risk reduction [msg #108, #619, #622].
- **Removing harm beats adding "upgrades".** Health is what you stop doing against yourself daily; the body is not a ledger where a plus covers a minus [msg #590].
- **The immortality business is old and its sellers all died.** The author admires Johnson's push against modern norms but turns skeptical when his ideas become endless sales; no scientifically proven way to stop or radically slow aging exists yet [msg #288, #619].
- **How you think about aging changes how you age.** Aging anxiety measurably accelerates epigenetic aging in women, which the author reads as scientific confirmation that the anti-age industry sells harmful anxiety [msg #620].
- **Extend active age, not just age.** The author cares about the quality of the years, which depends on the same factors as lifespan [msg #623].

- **The "smoking grandfather" is a rare exception, not evidence.** The post frames the study as showing «вероятность встретить такого дедушку (или бабушку)» (*the probability of meeting such a grandfather (or grandmother)*): among Roman centenarians, current smokers were only 2.7% — 4 people out of 148 [msg #675].
- **Most people who reach 100 never smoked, and most of the rest quit.** 83.8% never smoked and 13.5% had quit, on average at age 65 [msg #675].
- **Smoking late in life still has a cost even in survivors.** Long-livers who smoked after 65 had more chronic diseases [msg #675].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Gary Brecka's key longevity factors (guest post by Marina Sidorich)
Context: Gary Brecka, described as a biohacking expert, identified the key factors of longevity and well-being after 20 years of working with large health databases. The guest author notes these are factors "we have heard more than once" but it is useful to look at the problem from different angles [msg #108].
1. **Simple changes to the daily routine.** Small routine changes can substantially change the trajectory of a life; most causes that shorten lifespan can be removed by correcting habits and actions [msg #108].
2. **No deficiencies.** Simple changes in nutrition reduce disease risk and improve emotional state. «Отсутствие дефицитов продлевает жизнь» (*The absence of deficiencies prolongs life*) — iron and vitamin D are especially emphasized [msg #108].
3. **Oxygen = energy.** Everything we call "energy" is, per Brecka, nothing other than the amount of oxygen in the blood. Breathing practices and physical activity supply the body with oxygen and so increase energy. Conversely, with a sedentary lifestyle breathing becomes short and inefficient; without enough oxygen the body suffers at the cellular level, which leads to disease [msg #108].
4. **Movement and strong social ties (the Blue Zones lesson).** Blue Zones people are long-lived not because they are biohackers: their lives contain a lot of movement, walking and gardening, plus «прочные социальные связи» (*strong social ties*) and an understanding of their role in society [msg #108].
5. **Ordinary whole foods.** Neither keto, vegan, paleo nor raw-food diets make Blue Zones people long-lived, but «обычные цельные продукты» (*ordinary whole foods*) [msg #108].

### Gary Brecka's breathing practice ("bonus" from his personal experience)
Purpose: a "universal" breathing practice described as an energy boost equal to morning coffee [msg #108].
1. Take 30 deep breaths.
2. Then exhale as long as possible and hold the breath.
3. Start with short holds of 15–20 seconds; over time one can reach four minutes.
4. Do it every day for 30 minutes — "and the effect of morning coffee is guaranteed".
Claimed mechanism: the exercise dilates blood vessels and improves blood flow by raising the level of carbon dioxide in the blood. (!) UNCLEAR: this mechanism (CO2-driven vasodilation) sits alongside the post's earlier claim that energy equals blood oxygen; the post does not reconcile the two, and no safety caveats for multi-minute breath-holds are given [msg #108].

### Blue Zones recommendations for a long and active life
The author defines the project: Blue Zones is a study of long-lived people in five regions of the world with high life expectancy and low rates of chronic disease. Its founder, Dan Buettner, studied their lifestyle and habits, identified factors contributing to longevity and health, and popularizes these approaches as a basis for a healthy lifestyle, offering actions one can integrate into life to raise its quality and length. The project is 20 years old; the author has known it for at least five years [msg #215].
The recommendations for a long and active life she defends: 1) move; 2) eat in a balanced way; 3) eat more vegetables; 4) make your life more meaningful; 5) surround yourself with like-minded people [msg #215].

### KamaLama life-expectancy test
What it is: KamaLama is a non-profit organization giving personalized, research-based health recommendations. Its health-assessment algorithm analyzes millions of studies to identify factors affecting lifespan [msg #287]. The author later adds that it is free, has no advertising, no affiliate recommendations and no data selling, and that it selected from peer-reviewed databases only factors that show a stable link with all-cause mortality. Her verdict: «Конечно, это не идеально, но, кажется, серьезнее, чем большинство wellness-тестов в интернете» (*Of course it is not perfect, but it seems more serious than most wellness tests on the internet*) [msg #287, #623].
How it works:
1. **Take the test** — answer questions about habits and lifestyle; it forecasts how many years you will live if you do not change your habits [msg #287].
2. **Get recommendations** on what to adjust to add years of life. The report groups factors by impact — in the author's screenshot: "Negative impact 2 factors", "Can be improved 3 factors" (Omega-3 Index, -3 years), "In good range 25 factors"; also shown are a biological age and risks [img #623, msg #287].
3. **Optionally add lab data** — the author did an Omega-3 index test for the first time specifically for it [msg #623].
4. **Repeat yearly**, as the creators recommend; the author plans to make it an annual practice [msg #623].
Factor estimates quoted in the channel (algorithm outputs, not individual promises):
- Walking more than 10,000 steps daily: +10 years [msg #287].
- Regular sleep under 7 hours: −3 years; and sleep "works both ways" — both too little and too much sleep are minuses [msg #287, #623].
- Wearing a seat belt: +3 years [msg #287].
- Happy relationships and satisfaction with social connections: +3 years [msg #287].
- Stress: −3 years — «Однако стресс укорачивает жизнь на те же 3 года» (*However, stress shortens life by the same 3 years*) [msg #287].
- Alcohol starts "taking away" years after four glasses a week ("which isn't even a bottle") [msg #623].
- Riding a motorcycle: immediately −10 years [msg #623].
- LDL cholesterol: per longevity data, 2.6–3.4 mmol/L is one of the most "favorable" ranges [msg #623].
The forwarded post's closing message: take care of yourself, walk more, fall in love, and remove stress — "in the end, everything is in our hands" [msg #287].

### "Stop making yourself worse" — the agency approach (forward of a post on Bryan Johnson's podcast)
Thesis: «Главный апгрейд - перестать делать себе хуже» (*The main upgrade is to stop making yourself worse*). One can take vitamins, go to the gym and follow biohackers' life hacks, but if what destroys you remains in your life, nothing changes. Per Johnson's podcast, health is not what you add on top (gadgets, habits) — «Это то, что ты перестаёшь делать против себя каждый день» (*It is what you stop doing against yourself every day*) [msg #590].
Its components, in order:
1. **Reject the culture of compensation.** "I sleep badly — but I take magnesium. I overload myself — but then I 'recover'. I eat anyhow — but I train." The body does not work like accounting where a minus can be covered by a plus: if there is regular destruction in the system, no useful actions offset that background [msg #590].
2. **First paradox — sleep deprivation as a status symbol.** In our culture, sleeping little is almost a sign of strength (you are busy and in demand) and sleep looks like weakness. In fact, without sleep basic bodily functions switch off, from cognitive to physiological; you can look successful while already starting to break inside — "we simply agreed not to notice" [msg #590].
3. **"Healthy" habits as bypass.** Many useful habits are not about growth but about avoidance — not because cold showers or sport don't work, but because they become a way not to face the one thing you can't change. You keep eating fast food and just add one more practice on top: the feeling "I'm doing something for myself" stays, the real shift doesn't happen [msg #590].
4. **Second paradox — "everything in moderation".** Most people do not live in moderation but in oscillation between "pulled myself together" and "broke down". "Sometimes it's OK" almost always turns into "regularly, with an explanation of why it's normal" — «Это не баланс, а рационализация» (*This is not balance but rationalization*) [msg #590].
5. **It is about agency.** The conversation is not about food, sport or supplements but about agency — «Про способность делать то, что ты на самом деле выбираешь» (*About the ability to do what you actually choose*). If you eat, get stuck on screens or overload yourself daily not because you want to but because you can't stop, the question is no longer health but who is running your life [msg #590].
6. **Values, not habits.** The next level is not "which other habit to implement" but what counts as important in your value system [msg #590].
7. **Redefine longevity.** Longevity is not about "living longer" — «Это про то, чтобы перестать жить против себя» (*It is about stopping living against yourself*): a state in which you don't need to recover from your own life [msg #590].
8. **Start with one honest question and one removal.** Ask: "What of the things I do every day do I not actually want to do, but keep doing?" — and try to remove at least one such thing; that is where real change appears [msg #590].
The author's own addition: everything Johnson says here is very close to her; in her thinking about health she goes deeper inward and believes all ailments come "from nerves" — that daily violence against oneself at the level of the psyche always passes into matter [msg #590].

### Four historical "sellers of eternal youth"
Purpose: the author's lens for skepticism toward today's longevity marketing — "sellers of eternal youth" have always existed, and their fates are an ironic story [msg #288].
1. **Emperor Qin Shi Huang (260–210 BC)** — searched for the elixir of immortality, trusted Taoist alchemists, took pills containing mercury. Died of mercury poisoning at 49 [msg #288].
2. **Dr. John Brinkley (1885–1942)** — promoted transplanting goat testicles into men for rejuvenation. Died at 56, never having transplanted them into himself [msg #288].
3. **Laura Haton (1909–1973)** — sold placenta injections as the secret of eternal youth; injected clients with embryonic cell extracts. Died at 64 of natural causes [msg #288].
4. **Alexander Bogomolets (1881–1946)** — claimed one could live to 150 by treatment with a "serum of health"; in effect he promoted immune therapy against aging. Died at 65 [msg #288].
Lesson: some were ahead of their time — Bogomolets spoke 100 years ago of aging as a process that can be modulated through immunity, today a scientific direction in gerontology; Haton's placental therapy partly became a basis of cellular medicine. Their ideas «не были совсем пустыми» (*were not entirely empty*), but technology has still not caught up with their promises, and people still don't live to 150. Hence the question: is it too early to believe all this now too? [msg #288].

### The author's longevity strategy and impermanence practice
- The author's view: «самая адекватная стратегия — выстроить базу *(найти свой подходящий зож)* и практиковать мудрость и принятие своей человечности» (*the most adequate strategy is to build a base (find the healthy lifestyle that suits you) and practice wisdom and acceptance of one's humanity*) [msg #619].
- For the last year she has practiced impermanence and calls it a truly pro-age practice. Example — **analytical meditation**: for several weeks she has done a meditation in which one mentally removes from the body its warmth, fluids, gases and solidity. What remains is the understanding that everything is fleeting, illusory and may soon cease to be — and then you start to value it and want to take care of it [msg #619].

### Rome centenarian smoking breakdown
Purpose: a ready data-based reply to the "grandpa smoked and lived to 100" argument. The study, described as by Italian scientists, examined 148 residents of Rome older than one hundred; the ratio of men to women was 1 to 3 [msg #675]. The centenarians fell into three smoking groups:
1. **Never smoked — 83.8%.** The overwhelming majority of people who reached 100 in this sample had never smoked at all [msg #675].
2. **Smoked but quit — 13.5%.** These had stopped smoking, on average at age 65 — that is, they reached 100 after decades without smoking [msg #675].
3. **Smoking at the time of the study — 2.7% (4 people).** This tiny group is the real-world "smoking grandfather"; the post presents it as the measure of how likely you are to meet one [msg #675].

Additional finding: among the long-livers, those who smoked after age 65 had more chronic diseases — so even the rare smokers who reach 100 do so with a heavier disease burden [msg #675].

## Detailed insights

### The Blue Zones data controversy (author's opinion post)
The author was repeatedly "corrected" in comments that Blue Zones is built on unreliable data; the NYT even wrote an article. Her argument: the project is 20 years old, so why is it questioned only now, and why has Newman's 2019 study — which pointed to possible errors in recording ages in longevity regions — only now been remembered? She notes that study is still not published in scientific journals because it «вызывает много разногласий в научной среде» (*causes much disagreement in the scientific community*). Her answer: Blue Zones became so popular that Netflix made a documentary about it last year (she calls it excellent). She considers the criticism hype and clickbait: those writing it gain nothing but attention, basing accusations on data unconfirmed and unaccepted by the scientific community [msg #215].
What irritates her most is the (she hopes unconscious) demoralization of the values behind the project: as if people may now dismiss the recommendations for a long and active life (see *Blue Zones recommendations*) because "these conclusions are built on unreliable data". She self-ironically admits she "hyped a bit" herself, "though for a good cause" [msg #215].

### Bryan Johnson: admiration and skepticism

We must become professional sleepers*). We take work seriously — arriving on time, learning, developing, deeply respecting what we do — but treat sleep as secondary, sleeping whenever: when the series episodes run out, when work allows, when guests have left. - **Don't Die — the dilemma.** After watching the film the author confirmed her dilemma about Johnson. Many things in his lifestyle that repel the average person may in fact simply be the brain's resistance; and our brain has grown used to "not the most optimal", given that over the last 50 years chronic diseases have grown to 74% of all deaths worldwide per WHO data [msg #288].
- On the other side, when his ideas turn into «очередную бесконечную продажу» (*yet another endless sale*) — first via referrals, now "magic" olive oil and supplements 3–5 times pricier than usual — her skeptic switches on (see *Four historical sellers of eternal youth*). [msg #288].
- **The $400M list.** Johnson, who by various estimates spent about $400 million on health and longevity, tweeted his main tools for extending lifespan: sleep and recovery, drink more water, eat 5 different fruits and vegetables daily, consistency is key, and similar. The author's reaction: «Это. Все. Такой вот список за $400 миллионов.» (*That's. It. Such a list for $400 million.*) She links it to Kayla Barnes-Lentz, who after spending $300,000 declared that the main tools are free [msg #619].
- **The author's reading:** Johnson's conclusions are "perhaps the best argument against the whole wellness-marketing industry", which sells the feeling that somewhere there is that pill, gadget or special program that will make us thin, healthy and even immortal. Marketing the (illusion of) immortality is a very old business: formerly elixirs and the philosopher's stone, now NMN and hyperbaric chambers — and everyone who once sold eternal youth died. «Большинство «волшебных таблеток» — бесплатные. Это неприятно и освобождает одновременно» (*Most "magic pills" are free. This is unpleasant and liberating at the same time*) [msg #619].
- Johnson's anxiety is also cited as costly: worrying too much about aging can cost not only dollars ("as with Bryan Johnson") but years of life [msg #620].

### Aging anxiety accelerates aging (NYU study)
A fresh NYU study, which the author found in the Reminder newsletter, showed that anxiety about aging accelerates epigenetic aging in women — the fear of aging literally makes you age faster. Everyone has two ages, chronological ("passport") and biological, which don't always coincide; scientists measure biological age by changes in gene function. Women who worried strongly about their health had a biological age higher than their chronological age compared with those who worried less. In the author's words: how we think about aging changes how we age [msg #620].
Her interpretation: she has said many times that the beauty and anti-age industry sells us anxiety disguised as self-care; now there is scientific confirmation that this anxiety is not only psychologically unpleasant but literally imprints itself in the body [msg #620].
A supporting quote from *Impermanence in Plain Words* by B. H. Gunaratana and Julia Harris: «В мгновение нашего рождения мы начинаем шагать в сторону неизбежного пункта назначения. Мы рождаемся с билетом в один конец.» (*At the moment of our birth we begin walking toward an inevitable destination. We are born with a one-way ticket.*) — continuing that medicine's miracles can postpone death but sooner or later everyone's life ends in death. The author: «Жестко, зато честно и точно» (*Harsh, but honest and precise*) [msg #620].

### Genetics vs lifestyle after 80 (Chinese cohort study)
A recently published study by Chinese scientists followed 1,545 people over 80 and assessed how genetics and so-called modifiable factors affect the risk of death. Modifiable factors included smoking, alcohol use, physical activity, education level, plus things changeable by lifestyle or medication: blood pressure, cholesterol, body-mass index, depression [msg #622].
Results after several years of observation:
- People with the most favorable profile of modifiable factors had a 40.7% lower risk of death than those with the most unfavorable profile [msg #622].
- For comparison, high genetic predisposition to longevity lowered the risk of death by 13% [msg #622].
- Life expectancy after 80: 18 years in the group with good genetic predisposition and a favorable modifiable profile, vs 11.4 years in the group with less favorable genetics and the worst profile — a difference of almost 7 years [msg #622].
Caveat stated in the post: this is group statistics, not a promise to a specific person. Main conclusion: even at an advanced age, sleep, movement, control of blood pressure, sugar, weight, lipids, depression and harmful habits remain factors noticeably linked to how many years of life remain [msg #622].

### The author's own KamaLama result (age 40 → forecast 98)
- Why a number helps: seeing a life-years forecast in numbers is both sobering and calming — not "I live well enough" or "you only live once", but concretely: here I am, here are my habits, here is what they "cost". It brings you back to earth; in her case it also released anxiety because she is "already doing well". Forecast: she is 40 and will live to 98, and longer if she improves some things [msg #623].
- What she cares about is not the number (98 or 120) but what those years will be like: «продлить не возраст, а активный возраст» (*to extend not age, but active age*). Good news per the author: active age depends on exactly the same factors [msg #623].
- Biological age came out as 31; smart scales always show her current age, but, she notes, they did not see her resting heart rate (55), C-reactive protein (0.4) and glycated hemoglobin (5.2%) [msg #623].
- Cholesterol surprise: she was sure her LDL at the upper edge of normal demanded urgent action; per longevity data 2.6–3.4 mmol/L turned out to be among the most favorable ranges. Too-low LDL is "not a gift" either: the body uses cholesterol for immune defense, hormone synthesis and cell repair. Her task is not to lower it at any cost but to stay where she is — "sometimes the best action is not to act, but to live for your own pleasure" [msg #623].
- Her two growth zones: raise the Omega-3 index (shown as −3 years in the report) — now oily fish almost every day, re-check in six months; and add physical activity. Plus continue not smoking and not riding a motorcycle [msg #623, img #623].
- What impressed her most were the very clear visualizations of how different things affect lifespan (alcohol, sleep, motorcycle — see *KamaLama life-expectancy test*) [msg #623].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- (!) CONTRADICTION (within the channel): the Blue Zones data are defended in #215 as reliable against "hype" criticism; the author's defense rests on the critique being unpublished, and on the values behind the recommendations, not on new data [msg #215].
- KamaLama estimates are algorithm outputs aggregated from studies; the author herself says the tool is not ideal. The 80+ cohort study is explicitly group statistics, not an individual promise [msg #623, #622].
- The NYU study as reported concerns women only and anxiety about health/aging; the post does not give sample size [msg #620]. (!) INCOMPLETE: no numbers on the size of the epigenetic age difference are given.
- Brecka's breathing practice: oxygen-vs-CO2 mechanism tension and no safety warning for breath-holds up to four minutes (see the framework) [msg #108].
- "All ailments come from nerves" is explicitly the author's personal belief, not a cited finding [msg #590].
- The author's ambivalence about Johnson is unresolved: she supports the lifestyle message, rejects the sales, and leaves open whether he actually became younger [msg #288, #619].

- (!) UNCLEAR: the post says the study shows the "probability of meeting such a grandfather", but the figures are shares among people who already reached 100; the post gives no figure for the chance that a given smoker reaches 100 [msg #675].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: the post does not say whom the smoking-after-65 group was compared with (never-smokers, quitters or both), nor how much higher their chronic disease burden was; the study year is not given in the text [msg #675].
- Small sample: 148 people, with only 4 current smokers, and a 1:3 men-to-women ratio; the post does not break the smoking figures down by sex [msg #675].

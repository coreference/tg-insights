# Marina's nutrition column

## What this covers

A food column for energy and mood that the channel launched in January 2026, written by Marina Sidorich, a certified nutrition coach and guest author [msg #521]. It contains three self-contained lessons: calcium (why it matters especially for women and how to reach the daily amount from food), fiber (the real daily target and what actually gets you there), and calorie-tracking apps (what Marina found when she tested them on herself and how to use them without harm). The column's stance is consistently non-restrictive: attention to food culture, diet quality, hunger and satiety signals and energy level is treated as more useful than counting [msg #651].

## Core ideas

- **Calcium is a common, overlooked deficiency, and women are the main risk group.** Marina did not have "hit my calcium norm every day" among her goals either, until she consulted a nutritionist herself and received what she calls a very fair remark. Her situation illustrates two risk factors: she had just finished breastfeeding, so for the previous 1.5 years her body had been giving calcium from her diet to the child's growth and development; and at 34 the peak of bone density is already behind her. In the future, with perimenopause and menopause, loss of density only accelerates because estrogen falls, which is why it is specifically women who are in the elevated-risk group for osteoporosis [msg #521].
- **Calcium is not only about bones.** It is the most abundant mineral in the body; most of it is in bones and teeth, but it is also needed for blood clotting, muscle contraction and relaxation, heart function and transmission of nerve impulses. Marina translates this into everyday terms: calcium matters for sleep quality, muscle tone and resilience to stress [msg #521].
- **Calcium is easy to get from food.** The reference point is 1000 mg per day, and the column gives a food table to add it up (see Frameworks) [msg #521].
- **Fiber has proven long-term and short-term benefits.** According to the post it is already proven that fiber intake is associated with lower risk of cardiovascular disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer. The short-term bonuses are regular stool, stable blood sugar, lower "bad" cholesterol, help with weight control and support of the gut microbiome, and gut bacteria in turn influence energy and mood. The average recommendation for an adult is 30 g of fiber a day [msg #533].
- **"Eat more fiber" is usually misread as "add cucumbers and tomatoes".** In reality you would have to eat about 3 kg of cucumbers and tomatoes in a day to reach 30 g: one tomato plus one cucumber gives only about 3 g of fiber [msg #533] [img #533]. Other foods are far denser sources (see the cheat sheet below).
- **Calorie-tracker "norms" are just calculations, and they disagree with each other.** When Marina installed several popular AI calorie apps, every app gave a different daily calorie norm after the same questionnaire; the spread was up to 200-300 kcal a day. The apps use different formulas and nowhere explain which ones or why [msg #651].
- **The apps tended to under-feed.** Several services calculated less than 1700 kcal a day for a goal of losing 2 kg in 3 months. Marina knows that for her parameters she needs roughly 1900-2100 kcal a day to feel well, do sport and cope with two children. Her verdict on the app's number: the weight loss is guaranteed, together with the absence of energy and recovery [msg #651].
- **Counting has a psychological effect that expertise does not protect against.** This surprised her most: within a few days she noticed guilt after some meals, then the urge to "save" calories now to leave them for later, and at some point it ended with her eating a whole pack of cookies. Her dry comment: so much for controlling nutrition [msg #651].
- **Tracking does not guarantee sustainable weight loss.** Attempts to stay inside rigid limits very often end in the familiar swing: restriction, breakdown, guilt, new restriction. Behind that sits a separate question worth asking: why did I decide to lose weight at all [msg #651].
- **Trackers are not bad in themselves.** Marina does not consider calorie trackers bad; in some situations they are a useful tool, for example when you are simply curious how much your food roughly "weighs" in calories. She explicitly leaves professional athletes out of the discussion [msg #651].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Calcium food table (reaching 1000 mg a day)

Purpose: check whether your ordinary daily food adds up to the 1000 mg reference. Marina's invitation is to look at your own diet and count whether you manage to reach 1000 [msg #521]. Approximate calcium content per portion:

1. Yogurt, 200 g: about 250 mg [msg #521]
2. Hard cheese, 30 g: about 250 mg [msg #521]
3. A glass of milk or kefir: about 240 mg [msg #521]
4. Cottage cheese (tvorog), 150 g: about 150 mg [msg #521]
5. Sardines with bones, 100 g: about 350 mg [msg #521]
6. Tahini, 2 teaspoons: about 150 mg [msg #521]
7. Broccoli, 200 g: about 90 mg [msg #521]
8. A handful of arugula or spinach: about 50 mg [msg #521]

Worked example using the table's own numbers: yogurt (250) plus hard cheese (250) plus a glass of kefir (240) plus sardines (350) already exceeds 1000 mg; a day without dairy or fish with bones makes the target hard to reach from greens alone, since broccoli and leafy greens contribute only 50-90 mg per portion [msg #521].

### Five steps to really increase fiber toward 30 g a day

Purpose: move from the ineffective "add a cucumber" approach to changes that actually raise fiber intake. The steps are the ones that, in Marina's words, really help to increase fiber in the diet and approach the norm [msg #533]:

1. **Learn to cook and eat lentils, beans and other legumes.** The emphasis is on learning: legumes have to become something you know how to prepare and are used to eating [msg #533].
2. **Accept and come to love whole carbohydrates:** quinoa, brown rice, whole-grain pasta [msg #533].
3. **Add seeds and nuts** to porridges, yogurts and bowls [msg #533].
4. **Eat berries and crunchy fruit** [msg #533].
5. **Try the whole variety of vegetables,** rather than repeating the same two [msg #533].

Pacing rule: not everything at once. If you are not used to eating fiber, start little by little; otherwise you can get bloated and immediately fall out of love with fiber [msg #533]. Suggested self-check: try calculating with ChatGPT how much fiber your current diet contains [msg #533].

### Fiber content cheat sheet (image cards)

The post's image cards compare how much fiber single portions give, which makes clear why steps 1-4 matter more than salad vegetables:

- 1 tomato + 1 cucumber: 3 g of fiber [img #533]
- 1 slice of whole-grain bread: 4 g of fiber [img #534]
- 1 tablespoon of chia seeds: 5 g of fiber [img #535]
- 1 pear: 5 g of fiber [img #536]

So a single pear or a spoon of chia gives more fiber than a tomato and a cucumber together, and ten "tomato plus cucumber" portions (about 3 kg) would be needed for the daily 30 g [img #533] [msg #533].

### Four rules for using calorie trackers

Marina's own summary after her experiment [msg #651]:

1. **Do not take the app's calorie norm as truth.** It is only a calculation, and different apps calculate differently (200-300 kcal apart, often too low) [msg #651].
2. **Remember your goal first.** Ask whether you really need to lose weight or whether the problem is in something else [msg #651].
3. **Watch whether counting food makes you more anxious and fixated on eating.** Guilt after meals and "saving" calories for later are the warning signs she noticed in herself within days [msg #651].
4. **Look not only at the quantity of food but at its quality and variety,** because the calorie number says very little about health [msg #651].

## Detailed insights

### Why Marina tested trackers at all

AI-based fitness products are on a hype wave: new AI counters, trackers and "personal" health assistants keep appearing. In ordinary life Marina is not a supporter of calorie counting. But against a background of regular training and normal habits she wanted to try a classic fitness protocol, and decided to briefly be "an ordinary woman who wants to lose a little weight", downloading several popular apps [msg #651]. The experiment is therefore a first-person test by a nutrition professional, not a study.

### What research says, as cited in the column

Marina notes that this is not only her experience: studies show that regular use of calorie-counting apps is associated with a higher risk of obsessive control of food, body dissatisfaction and symptoms of eating disorders. She qualifies it: not in all people and not always, but the risk definitely exists [msg #651].

## Practical playbook

- Add up a typical day's calcium against the table and see whether it reaches 1000 mg; dairy portions and sardines with bones are the dense sources, greens the small ones [msg #521].
- If you recently breastfed, or are approaching perimenopause, treat calcium as a priority nutrient, not an afterthought [msg #521].
- For fiber, plan around legumes, whole grains, seeds, nuts, berries and crunchy fruit; count salad vegetables as a small contribution (about 3 g per tomato-plus-cucumber) [msg #533] [img #533].
- Raise fiber gradually to avoid bloating [msg #533].
- If you use a calorie app: treat its norm as an estimate, compare it with what you know you need to feel well and train, and stop or step back if you notice guilt, "saving" calories or fixation [msg #651].
- Before starting any weight-loss protocol, answer the question "do I actually need to lose weight, or is the problem something else?" [msg #651].

## Evidence and numbers

- Calcium reference intake: 1000 mg per day [msg #521].
- Fiber recommendation for an adult: 30 g per day on average; about 3 kg of cucumbers and tomatoes would be needed to reach it [msg #533].
- Fiber per portion: tomato + cucumber 3 g; slice of whole-grain bread 4 g; tablespoon of chia 5 g; pear 5 g [img #533] [img #534] [img #535] [img #536].
- Calorie apps: spread between apps up to 200-300 kcal per day; several apps set under 1700 kcal per day for "lose 2 kg in 3 months", against Marina's own known need of about 1900-2100 kcal per day [msg #651].
- Associations cited without naming the studies: fiber with lower risk of cardiovascular disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer [msg #533]; calorie-app use with higher risk of obsessive food control, body dissatisfaction and eating-disorder symptoms [msg #651].

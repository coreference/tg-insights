# Everyday food culture

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's posts on how eating looks in everyday life rather than in nutrient detail: a long self-check of healthy eating habits, the intuitive eating approach, the author's diagnosis that most "food problems" stem from eating too little and doing too much, gentle eating around the New Year holidays, the case for home cooking with a full recipe, ready-meal delivery as a solution when routines collapse, and a holiday-baking recommendation. According to the source it matters because rigid diet rules, skipped meals and self-blame drive overeating, nervous tension and low self-esteem, while practical habits (planning, cooking, eating enough) make balanced eating sustainable [msg #84, #190, #581].

## Core ideas

- **Eating well is a set of countable habits.** Marina Sidorich frames healthy eating as a checklist of 39 concrete habits across what you eat, how you eat, how you plan and how you cook/store — something you can count "on your balance" (see *Healthy eating habits self-check*) [msg #84].
- **Intuitive eating is peace with food, not permission to binge.** It means no bans and choosing genuine self-care; its key element is self-compassion, and it does not work if basic needs like sleep are unmet (see *Intuitive eating*) [msg #190].
- **The root problem is under-eating and over-doing.** Katya Strelnikova argues that overeating, sugar cravings and "no energy" are consequences; the causes are eating too little and doing too much (see *Root-cause model*) [msg #581].
- **No strict diet after the holidays.** Strelnikova (and the host of the holiday project) oppose "water and bread" diets on January 1; the return to balanced eating should be gentle [msg #19].
- **Cook at home more.** Home cooking guarantees less sugar, trans fats and salt, and cooking itself may improve mood (see *Why cook at home*) [msg #110].
- **Remove decision fatigue when routines collapse.** Ready-meal delivery can take food-thinking off your plate entirely [msg #471].

### Intuitive eating — awareness, traps and self-compassion
Purpose: Marina Sidorich's answer to the common fear "Если бы я питалась интуитивно, я бы ела одни шоколадки?" (If I ate intuitively, would I eat only chocolate bars?) [msg #190].

**Definition.** Intuitive eating is "не потакание своим ежесекундным прихотям" (not indulging your every-second whims), as the name might suggest, but a path to harmony with body and soul: a peaceful relationship with food (мирные отношения с едой), the absence of bans, and choosing genuine self-care. Sidorich says she likes the approach, but learning it is "как научиться писать левой рукой: вроде бы все то же самое, но непривычно" (like learning to write with your left hand: seemingly the same thing, but unfamiliar) [msg #190].

**Component 1 — Awareness of bodily sensations.** Awareness of physical sensations gives valuable information about your physiological and psychological state. The questions she suggests asking yourself: How do I feel right now? Am I hungry or tired? Do I need movement or rest? Do I really need something sweet, or am I bored and sad? By attuning to yourself and your body you can understand what is needed in the moment to satisfy your needs [msg #190].

**Component 2 — Recognising the traps of beliefs and rules.** Typical traps: beliefs about what I must or must not eat, what a healthy body should look like, and which foods count as healthy or harmful. Diet mentality, an image of the ideal body and judgmental thoughts about food block a true understanding of your needs and wishes. Worse, according to Sidorich, they undermine self-esteem and self-worth, create nervous tension and often lead to overeating — "Знаю по себе и своим клиентам" (I know this from myself and my clients) [msg #190].

**Component 3 — Self-compassion as the key element.** To break out of this circle you need "ключевой элемент интуитивного питания – самосострадание" (the key element of intuitive eating — self-compassion): the ability to treat yourself with kindness and understanding [msg #190].

**Component 4 — Basic needs first.** Remember self-care: if basic needs — for example sufficient sleep — are not met, "никакой гармонии с телом не получится" (no harmony with the body will happen) [msg #190].

### Chocolate-only self-experiment
Sidorich's playful answer to the chocolate fear: "Можно попробовать" (You can try). Steps: (1) eat that way; (2) honestly observe what your mood and energy level are, what happens to the body and what sensations you have; (3) compare with the sensations and emotions on days when the body gets valuable, nourishing food. The point is that observation, not rules, guides choice; the questions for observation are countless and the experience is individual [msg #190].

To assess your relationship with food, she offers a test released by intuitive eating researchers. (!) INCOMPLETE: the test itself is on an external page and its questions are not in the source [msg #190].

### Root-cause model — "you eat too little and do too much"
Purpose: Katya Strelnikova's diagnostic model, distilled from 4+ years of coaching and roughly 850 sessions. Clients most often come with dissatisfaction with their body and food, or with a feeling of having no strength for anything. After another food session with a client and a group meeting of «Отдохнувших» ("the Rested", her group), she realised "как же все просто. Не в смысле легко. А в смысле, что первопричин мало, и они очень конкретные" (how simple it all is. Not in the sense of easy. In the sense that there are few root causes and they are very concrete). Overeating, eating sweets and not doing important things are, per the post's subtitle, consequences, not the problem [msg #581].

**Cause 1 — "Если у вас нет энергии, вы слишком много делаете" (If you have no energy, you are doing too much).** [msg #581]
- *The blind spot:* people with this request usually believe they "do nothing". On closer look, some (sometimes all) of their tasks are discounted — not counted as tasks: household chores, children, servicing other people's tasks and emotions, moves and emigrations, divorces and illnesses — "это ведь не работа, не что-то великое" (that's not work, nothing great, after all). But "Тело считает все, и к вечеру предъявляет счет" (The body counts everything, and by evening presents the bill).
- *The second pattern:* preferring tasks over rest and sleep (to get more done) — because rest must be earned and the to-do list never ends; because going to bed earlier feels like weakness rather than a necessity; because "for myself" activities start only when the city falls asleep.
- *The outcome:* sincere surprise at having no energy for "important" things, followed by self-labelling: "Наверное, я ленивая. Никчемная. Надо просто взять себя в руки." (I'm probably lazy. Worthless. I just need to pull myself together.)
- *Caveat (the author's own):* lack of energy can be a symptom of serious illness or depression; she speaks about the behavioural level she works with.

**Cause 2 — "Если вы не можете наладить отношения с едой и недовольны собой и телом, вы слишком мало едите" (If you can't sort out your relationship with food and are unhappy with yourself and your body, you are eating too little).** The forms of under-eating she lists [msg #581]:
1. Skipping meals — because you're not hungry in the morning and have meetings at lunch.
2. Eating unbalanced meals — so the body constantly lacks fibre, or protein, or something else.
3. Eating portions that aren't yours, because "порции должны быть маленькими" (portions should be small).
4. Eating not until full but the amount you invented in your head.
5. Expecting the same eating behaviour on days with different workloads.
6. Not accounting for the phases of the menstrual cycle.

*The consequence chain:* the hand reaches for sweets, bread, one more portion of something tasty; you call it a breakdown and weak will, beat yourself up internally and promise it definitely won't happen tomorrow. The lesson is that the "breakdown" is a predictable result of under-eating, not a character flaw [msg #581].

### Seasonal salad with tomatoes, strawberries and tarragon oil
Recipe from Nastya's channel Chez Tsybique, posted by Marina Sidorich as an example of simple, seasonal, healthy home cooking where "вкус и эстетика всегда на балансе" (taste and aesthetics are always in balance) [msg #110].

**Ingredients, 2 servings** [img #111]:
- 4 Baku tomatoes (бакинских помидора)
- 8 strawberries
- 200 g sheep's-milk brynza (or any feta-like cheese)
- For the tarragon oil: 45 g fresh tarragon, 100 ml olive oil

**Making the tarragon oil** [img #112]:
1. Prepare a bowl of ice water — the author simply pours cold water and throws in all the ice in the freezer.
2. Drop the fresh tarragon into boiling water and count to 15.
3. Immediately lift it out with a slotted spoon and put it into the ice water for one minute.
4. Squeeze it out well, put it into a blender bowl, add the olive oil and blend "до атомной крошки (насколько это возможно)" (to atomic crumbs, as far as possible).
5. Strain through a fine sieve or cheesecloth.
6. Storage and use: keeps in the fridge up to two weeks; use at room temperature. The recipe author likes to triple the recipe and pour this "волшебное зелье" (magic potion) on literally everything, from bread to fruit.

**Assembling the salad** [img #113]: either build an arrangement like the author's, or simply chop the tomatoes and strawberries at random, crumble the brynza on top and pour tarragon oil over everything. "Форма поменяется, но суть всё та же" (The form changes, but the essence is the same).

## Detailed insights

### Why cook at home
Sidorich notes that you will find the advice to cook at home more often in any dietary guidelines: home cooking guarantees less sugar, trans fats and salt in the diet. In addition, she says research shows that the process of cooking can have a beneficial effect on the nervous system, activate dopamine release and improve mood (no specific study is named) [msg #110].

### Holiday eating (New Year)
Strelnikova spoke as a guest in Katya Trofimova's project «Новогодняя ПодГотовка», aimed at people who are lazy, short of time or "совсем не умею готовить" (can't cook at all) but still want a festive table — and don't want to be left without energy over the long holiday weekend. Her announced themes: how to find rather than lose resources ("ресурс под елочкой", resource under the Christmas tree), and how not to overeat during the feast [msg #18].

The host's description adds the lecture questions: how not to overeat on New Year's night; whether you should disrupt circadian rhythms by holding the feast strictly at midnight; and how to smoothly return to balanced eating after an abundance of mayonnaise salads and Napoleon cake. Strelnikova is described as "противник идеи, что 1 января надо непременно садиться на строгую диету из воды и хлеба" (an opponent of the idea that on January 1 you must go on a strict water-and-bread diet); instead the diet of the first days of the year should be planned as gently as possible, and the recommendations are said to apply year-round, not only on holidays. ### Ready-meal delivery when routines collapse
Strelnikova describes a period of total disruption of her routines, especially around food: every day the same quest — "Оказывается, я хочу есть три раза в день. А что есть — непонятно" (It turns out I want to eat three times a day. But what to eat is unclear). Her flat adds a challenge: two burners and a very small fridge — a kitchen designed for "people who don't eat at home". When the ready-meal service JustFood sent her a trial ration, she felt cared for; the benefit she stresses is not having to think about food endlessly: "Достал, разогрел, положил на тарелку — и красота" (Took it out, heated it, put it on a plate — and beautiful). The meals shown in her photos: a slice of spinach quiche with a leafy salad and cherry tomatoes, breaded balls with stewed vegetables (zucchini, bell pepper, broccoli) in tomato sauce, a container of peeled tangerines [img #473]; and halved bell peppers stuffed with minced meat topped with white cheese, with a side bowl of salad [img #474]. This is a sponsored/gifted product recommendation [msg #471].

The same post mentions a limited planner made by JustFood with 712 PAPERS, «Create the version you want to be», which she says is based on best practices from coaching and psychology; she was about to review her year (in several sittings, not in one go) and plan the next one [msg #471]. A page photographed from it reads "Отбросить все лишнее. Оставить главное." (Discard everything unnecessary. Keep the main thing.) — the idea being that sometimes there are dozens of things in your head and all seem important, but only with regular practice does it become visible what is truly important and what is just noise; the technique helps to find real wishes and keep the main thing. ### Sourdough holiday bread (kulich)
Strelnikova's Easter recommendation: a kulich ("куличчоне") made by Italian lievito madre technology («левито мадре») — a slow sourdough dough prepared for more than 24 hours. Her claim: the sourdough makes the dough slightly easier to digest and lowers the glycaemic index ("закваска делает тесто чуть легче для пищеварения и снижает гликемический индекс"). The crumb is described as tender, slightly stretchy and aromatic. Three variants that year: raisins with white glaze and flowers; candied orange with almond crust (said to be the constant hit); and a special one with cherry, chocolate and almond flakes. This is a commercial recommendation; the shop, address, ordering and hours are omitted as logistics [msg #582].

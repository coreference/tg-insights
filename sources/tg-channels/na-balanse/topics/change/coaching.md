# Coaching practice and client stories

## What this covers

This topic gathers what the channel says about Katya Strelnikova's own health/wellness-coaching practice: her career change from marketing after burnout, the conclusions she drew about balance, how she recognises early burnout, how individual client work is structured (a written contract whose goal is broken into research, concrete steps and implementation), the range of goals clients bring, and what clients report afterwards. According to the source it matters because almost all clients, however they phrase their request, want the same thing — to feel good about themselves, their energy and their life — and the route there is individual [msg #263, #538].

This part covers one long post (October 2026) in which Katya writes up her fifth published client case, titled with the client's words "Теперь знаю, как приходить к ясности. Как выравниваться. ## Core ideas

- **Burnout recovery can become a profession.** Katya calls herself a burnout veteran who now professionally helps others not get there; after two years of recovery she changed field after 35 [msg #263]. Detail in *Career change story*.
- **Balance is internal, not an equal split of spheres.** Keeping an artificial "equality" of life areas is not the point; finding your own rhythm that supports your inner state is. From the outside a balanced life may even look unbalanced [msg #263].
- **Physical resource comes before growth.** Growth requires both resource and a growth mindset, but physical strength is first; people with low energy or in burnout say they can no longer dream [msg #263].
- **Same desire, different paths.** Like Tolstoy's happy families, clients phrase problems differently but share one desire — to be satisfied with themselves, their energy and life — yet there are no universal recipes; each has their own optimal point of application of force [msg #538].
- **Goals are researched before they are acted on.** In Katya's contracts each goal is first turned into an exploration of the client's own patterns, and only then into concrete steps and implementation [img #538, #539, #540].
- **Fixing health often transforms other areas.** Clients frequently say that something fixed in the health sphere transformed other spheres of life [msg #538].

- **Stable energy is the goal, not peak energy.** The case ends with "ожидания ниже, энергия выше" ("expectations lower, energy higher"): the state evened out "без эйфории и без провалов" ("without euphoria and without dips") [msg #735].
- **You need a map of "good" before you can move toward it.** Defining in the client's own words what a good and a not-good state are comes first; "Без такой карты непонятно, куда идти" ("Without such a map it's unclear where to go") [msg #735].
- **Tracking is a training tool, not a supervisor.** The energy diary exists to build the habit of noticing; once the habit exists, daily entries are no longer needed [msg #735].
- **Energy can be physiological, not a matter of willpower.** "Иногда энергия — это не про силу воли, а про ферритин" ("Sometimes energy is not about willpower but about ferritin") [msg #735].
- **Experiments instead of rules.** Behaviour changes are framed as tests whose effect the client observes, not as commandments [msg #735].
- **The main result is calm understanding, not new habits.** Katya stresses that the key outcome was peace that comes from understanding cycles, stages and changing meanings [msg #735].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Coaching contract goal structure (Goal → Research → Concrete steps → Implementation)
Purpose: Katya says she always concludes a contract with each individual client ("мы всегда заключаем контракт"); the goal is written down at the start, in the client's words, and is turned into a research plan before any action is prescribed [msg #538]. The ten verbatim client goals she shared (transcribed in full below as examples A–J) all use the same numbered section 4.2 of the contract, with these four parts:

1. **4.2. Цель (Goal).** One or two sentences in the client's own terms of what they want — often an outcome plus a felt state (e.g. "научиться отдыхать", learn to rest; "чувствовать, что живу", feel that I am alive). The goal describes a state or capability, not a metric [img #538, #539, #541].
2. **4.2.1. Исследование (Research).** A lettered list (a, b, c…) of questions the client will investigate about themselves: what the topic means for them personally, what currently helps and hinders, which attitudes, habits and beliefs interfere or support, what fills and what drains energy, the current situation versus an optimal picture. This step embodies the "no universal recipe" principle: the plan is derived from the client's own data [img #538, #539, #543].
3. **4.2.2. Выработка конкретных шагов (Working out concrete steps)** "в соответствии с п 4.2.1" — steps and plans are built on the basis of the research findings. In one contract this is expanded into a plan of action: which habits and actions to eradicate and which to introduce, and what resources are needed to reach the goal [img #540, #547].
4. **4.2.3. Внедрение изменений и шагов (Implementing the changes and steps)** in accordance with 4.2.2 — the client puts the steps into practice [img #538, #546].

Note: the "clarity" contract card shows only the goal and the research block; steps 4.2.2–4.2.3 are not shown on that card [img #545].

### Five ways clients formulate goals
Katya groups the year's goals (about 40 individual clients) by how they sound; some seem very down-to-earth and pragmatic, others "philosophically far out" — and she explicitly invites the latter too ("с такими тоже приходите") [msg #538].
1. **Rationally and clearly** — "I want a system of nutrition / movement / health that will support me"; or find the resource and time to do what I want and what matters to me; or learn to structure the day — so as not to keep endless questions about body, food, health, weight and fears in one's head [msg #538].
2. **Exploratively** — figure out where energy goes and what it really depends on; what works and doesn't work for me; what prevents a harmonious relationship with coffee, sweets, the smartphone [msg #538].
3. **Philosophically** — what does it mean to feel that I live? Live beautifully and consciously. Or explore "clarity" to get a clear vision of oneself [msg #538].
4. **Psychologically** — regain control over one's life, or acquire inner supports [msg #538].
5. **From exhaustion** — then the goal sounds like: regain energy for life during the day, because right now "я труп, ничего не могу" ("I'm a corpse, I can't do anything") [msg #538].
What unites them all: everyone wanted to feel better, and believed it was possible [msg #538].

### Early burnout signs progression
Katya's description (interview) of how burnout creeps in unnoticed, in roughly this order [msg #263]:
1. At first it seems you are just tired; getting up in the morning is very hard.
2. Then you don't want to go to work and can't remember when anything there last interested you.
3. More and more "не хочу", "не могу" ("I don't want", "I can't") and irritation at colleagues and meetings appear.
4. Concentration becomes something "из Красной книги" (from the Red Book, i.e. an endangered species), while procrastination flourishes.
5. Even favourite activities stop bringing joy — not only at work.
6. It becomes harder and harder to dream and to want anything.
7. Instead there is often an urge to forget oneself with a glass of wine and to "treat yourself".
The key, she says, is to notice that something has changed: ask "Do you feel now the way you used to?" If not, it is worth investigating [msg #263].

### Two components of growth
Asked for advice to everyone who wants growth, Katya says movement requires two components: resource and growth mindset ("ресурс и мышление роста"). Physical strength still comes first. Her observation: people with low energy or in burnout say, without exception, that they can no longer dream. Her recommendation: let yourself rest, recover, fill yourself up — and then growth will certainly happen [msg #263].

### Ira's case structure (Given → Goal → What we did → Result)
Katya presents the case in four blocks; the same blocks are a template for reading her coaching work.

1. **Дано (Given) — the starting point.** Ira came with a request she herself called vague: "Как было — больше не могу и не хочу, а как нужно — еще не знаю" ("The way it was — I can no longer and don't want to; how it should be — I don't know yet"). Background: designer, many years in consulting, emigration; endless business trips and projects where it was impossible not to tear oneself apart ("не получается не порваться"); burnout, burnout leave, redundancy, a close person's serious illness, a new job. Important: she did not start from a blank page. Over a couple of years she had already done a lot — tennis, dancing, functional training, an hour before sleep with nothing to do, work with a psychologist. Yet energy "жила своей жизнью" ("lived its own life"), from an almost manic high to a complete down. Thoughts swung the same way, from "go to the sea and work as a waitress" to "but what about the career, why did I invest in all of it". On top came habitual beliefs: "Должна ходить, мочить всех и фонтанировать" ("I must walk around, crush everyone and gush with energy"), "Как будто я недостаточно сделала за день" ("As if I haven't done enough in a day"), "Уже разогналась — не буду же заканчивать" ("I've already picked up speed — I'm not going to stop now") [msg #735]. Lesson implied by the case: good habits (sport, psychologist, wind-down hour) were already in place and still did not stabilise energy — the missing piece lay elsewhere.
2. **Goal.** Formulated together: to carry out a research of what to do and not do to feel stably good, and to learn to notice grey flags rather than red flags, "когда уже поздно" ("when it is already too late"). (The full contract wording is example F in the topic file.) [msg #735]
3. **What we did** — six methods, each written out below as its own framework, in the order given: map of good/not good; energy diary; discovering chronic under-recovery; experiments; four kinds of energy; grey-flag practice. The post marks its list of methods as unfinished with a trailing ellipsis bullet; the remaining methods are not disclosed [msg #735].
4. **Result** — written out under *Outcomes of the case* in Detailed insights [msg #735].

### Map of "good" and "not good" states
Purpose: the first step of the work — describe what "good" and "not good" look like specifically for this client, so that there is a direction and a reference for noticing deviations. Katya: without such a map it is unclear where to go [msg #735].
- **Ira's "good":** waking up with the desire to live and create, falling asleep without torment, and if tired then it is a pleasant tiredness [msg #735].
- **Ira's "not good":** getting irritated at people, wanting TV series and sweets, feeling "socially drained" [msg #735].
The descriptions are concrete, observable behaviours and sensations, not abstract ratings — which is what makes them usable as early markers later [msg #735].

### Energy diary
Purpose: find out what one's energy actually depends on, and build the habit of noticing.
1. **Base entry:** three times a day note the colour of the "батарейка" (battery) [msg #735].
2. **Add context variables later:** sleep, day of the menstrual cycle, day of the week were added afterwards [msg #735].
3. **Review together:** coach and client went through the diary jointly and looked for dependencies [msg #735].
4. **Stop when the habit forms:** "Дневник — не надзиратель, а способ развить привычку замечать" ("The diary is not a supervisor but a way to develop the habit of noticing"). Once the habit appeared, daily entries became unnecessary [msg #735].
Result in this case: the diary highlighted chronic under-recovery (next section). One of the client's image cards reframes the colour scale itself: perhaps yellow is her green — it is impossible to be at peak energy all the time; it is more like surfing [img #735].

### Discovering chronic under-recovery (iron)
The diary showed chronic under-recovery. Ira had known before about her low iron and had periodically dealt with it, but now she saw how strongly it affects her state. Katya's conclusion: sometimes energy is not about willpower but about ferritin. After the work Ira knows that her energy depends on iron, sleep and the cycle, and she keeps an eye on "the base" ("следит за базой") [msg #735]. Note: the source gives no lab values, doses or treatment details — only the recognition of the link [msg #735].

### Experiments instead of rules
Instead of rules, they set experiments — changes whose effect Ira could observe. The examples given:
1. Go to bed at 10 o'clock five days out of seven (not every day) [msg #735].
2. Watch how alcohol affects sleep, and move drinking to the daytime — Katya reminds readers she usually recommends drinking in the morning/earlier in the day rather than in the evening ("обычно рекомендую выпить с утра") [msg #735].
3. Do not take the phone to bed [msg #735].
4. Board games instead of social media [msg #735].
5. A morning walk with the dog [msg #735].
The source closes the list with "И т.п." (and the like) — (!) INCOMPLETE: further experiments are not named [msg #735]. The outcome on alcohol: Ira now knows how it affects her, which Katya notes "было неприятно признавать" ("was unpleasant to admit") [msg #735].

### Four kinds of energy
They worked through four kinds of energy. Named or implied in the post: **physical**, **emotional**, **social** and **meaning-based** (смысловой опоры, the support of meaning). Two lessons Ira drew [msg #735]:
- One can have physical strength yet have no emotional or social resource at all.
- Physical, emotional and social energy may all seemingly be there, but without a meaning-based support everything starts to sag.

### Grey-flag practice
Learning to notice grey flags meant three moves [msg #735]:
1. **Feel it in advance** — sense the decline before it becomes acute.
2. **Say to yourself "на сегодня well enough"** ("well enough for today") — stop for the day.
3. **Lower the load tomorrow** — plan a lighter next day.
Plus a rule: **do not take on extra at a peak** ("Не набирать лишнего на пике") — the high phase is exactly when over-commitment happens, which is what produced the swings to a down [msg #735]. The client's card gives a concrete trade-off example: if she went to a party today, tomorrow she won't write the year's summary [img #735]. This applies the *Grey flags vs red flags of energy* idea in the topic file.

## Detailed insights

### Career change story
Katya worked in marketing for many years and changed profession about three years before the January 2025 interview; by then she had done 700+ hours of consultations and helped 100+ clients, according to the interviewing channel [msg #263]. After leaving full-time work she recovered for two years: morally there was total emptiness and loss of meaning, and physically she was literally suffocating ("буквально задыхалась"). The hardest part was to believe that "this too shall pass" ("пройдёт и это"), not give up and keep "digging". A couple of years of therapy, courses on reinventing herself and mindfulness practices led her to believe that changing field after 35 is not such a bad idea. She admitted to herself that she loves learning, discovering and experimenting — preferably with topics applied to her own life: health, recovery, energy, self-acceptance — and dared to ask: what if someone paid for all this? She then learned about the health and wellness coach profession, talked to people in it, chose training and found first clients [msg #263].

### Balance: research findings and personal experiment
- Katya ran a study of balance among women leaders. Main conclusion: before she started talking about balance she held an illusion of some evenness and of being able to combine everything "correctly". The conversations showed that what matters is not holding an artificial "equality" of spheres but finding one's own rhythm that supports the inner state; balance and harmony are about what is inside, while from outside it may even look like imbalance [msg #263].
- Personal experiment: after burnout she pursued balance as fiercely and self-sacrificingly as she once worked. Before New Year she counted how much time went to work versus "everything else" — it turned out 50/50. Yet external balance turned out not to be what she needed for inner harmony. She describes herself as an ambitious person who was once simply very tired; her current goal is not to keep a formal balance but to (ecologically) step out of it — to test "non-linearity", finding a way to invest more in development and achievement without harming her physical state [msg #263].

### No universal recipe: the point of application of force
With each new client Katya sees more clearly that although everyone strives for the same thing, each has their own path and there are no universal recipes. At any given moment each person has their own optimal point of application of force — sleep, food, movement, rhythm, relationship with oneself or with others, permission to rest, getting out of the endless "надо" ("must"). If you hit exactly that point, almost everything else becomes easier; clients often say that something fixed in health transformed other spheres too [msg #538].

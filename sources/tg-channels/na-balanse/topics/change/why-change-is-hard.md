# Why change is hard

## What this covers

This file covers how the channel explains why behavior and lifestyle changes fail and what makes them last. It includes the Transtheoretical Model of Change, used to explain "I know everything but do nothing". It also includes Katya Strelnikova's list of 11 reasons habits don't stick, the small-steps approach, the idea of a personal "enough", the realistic timeline (years, not weeks) and how to treat relapses. Further parts cover identity conflict, the state-thinking-action chain from guest author Nastya Khoreva, the difference between effort and violence against yourself, and lessons from seven women's change stories. The author's main point is that failing to change is "not about laziness, procrastination, weakness or lack of willpower". It usually comes from using the wrong tools for your stage, taking steps that are too big, expecting results too fast, or using self-punishment as fuel. Change done this way can happen "without violence against yourself, with pleasure and interest" [msg #490, #612, #616, #663].

This part covers four posts from late August to mid-September 2026. They extend the channel's account of the Transtheoretical Model (TTM), which the existing topic file already presents in full with five stages and processes of change. The new material:
- says what tool each stage needs;
- treats relapse (откат) as a stage of its own, caused by barriers rising above readiness;
- uses a fitness-app example to show how lowering barriers looks at each stage;
- reports that most people misdiagnose themselves as being in Preparation when they are still in Contemplation;
- adds a forwarded argument that short, regular micro-practices work and that long sessions are not required.

According to the source, this matters because people blame themselves ("I'm lazy", "something is wrong with me"). In the authors' view, repeated failure is "not a personal diagnosis". It comes from misunderstanding how change works and using the wrong tools for one's stage [msg #689, #693, #709, #713].

**Relation to the existing file.** The new posts **extend** the TTM section with stage-specific needs and the Preparation/Contemplation confusion. They **modify** the stage list by calling relapse a stage in its own right; the existing file has five stages, with relapses occurring inside Action and Maintenance. They **extend** "small steps" with evidence that a few minutes of practice can be enough. The "just do it" line in #689 names two prior states ("just want it", "just believe it"), while #490 in the existing file names three ("just want it", "just choose it", "just believe it") [msg #689, #693, #709, #713].

## Core ideas

- **Change is a process, not a decision.** It moves through stages, and each stage has its own tasks and tools. Doing things before you have chosen inside yourself fails. See *Transtheoretical Model* [msg #490, #612].
- **"I know but don't do" is not a character flaw.** Often you are trying to "just do it" while you are still at "just want it / just choose it / just believe it" [msg #490].
- **Small steps beat grand plans.** One small change today, with permission to go back, is more likely to grow into something big than a life overhaul on January 1 [msg #136, #616].
- **Your goal should be your sustainable "enough", not a number taken out of thin air** [msg #481].
- **Change takes years.** Katya's own lifestyle rebuild took about 8 years. For fundamental changes she says to plan a year minimum. Relapses are part of the system [msg #195, #252, #612, #653].
- **Identity matters.** New behavior first feels foreign ("Чужой внутри вас", "an Alien inside you"). Relapses seem to "prove" the old self-image [msg #612, #616].
- **Choose rather than endure; effort rather than violence.** Lasting change comes from choice and care, not from enduring, punishing or forcing yourself [msg #572, #616, #663].
- **State is the foundation.** Guest Nastya Khoreva says action is limited by thinking, and thinking depends on how you feel physically, including sleep and stress [msg #216].

- **"It doesn't work" is not a personal diagnosis.** Katya and Zhenya say that "не получается" or "не делаю" ("it doesn't work out" / "I don't do it") are not a personal diagnosis. They are "результат заблуждений о том, как происходят изменения" ("the result of misconceptions about how change happens") [msg #689].
- **Every stage needs its own tools.** Any lasting change passes through several stages, and each stage needs its own tools. Using another stage's tools is what produces "it doesn't work". See *Stage-matched tools* [msg #689].
- **Relapse comes from rising barriers.** A relapse is neither laziness nor a personal defect. It happens "когда барьеры становятся выше готовности действовать" ("when barriers become higher than the readiness to act"). The smart move is to lower barriers, not to kick yourself harder. See *Relapse mechanism* [msg #693].
- **Unresolved stages repeat.** "Если мы не проработали вопросы текущей стадии, будем на нее возвращаться снова и снова" ("If we haven't worked through the questions of the current stage, we will return to it again and again"). Success "this time" means knowing where you are and starting from there; trying harder is not the answer [msg #709].
- **Regularity can matter more than volume.** The belief that only long practice sessions help is a cognitive distortion, "the illusion of the usefulness threshold". Short practices of 1–10 minutes have measurable effects [msg #713].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Transtheoretical Model of Change (TTM): "Я все знаю, но ничего не делаю" ("I know everything, but I do nothing")
**What it is.** Katya calls TTM "модель стадий изменений, разработанная Джеймсом Прохазкой и Карло ДиКлементе" ("a model of the stages of change developed by James Prochaska and Carlo DiClemente"). It shows that change is a **process** rather than a single decision. The process has **stages**, and each stage has its own tasks, risks and supporting tools [msg #490].

**Why it matters (the author's argument).** The two statements Katya hears most often in her work are "Я все знаю, но ничего не делаю" ("I know everything but do nothing") and "я все перепробовала, но у меня не получилось" ("I've tried everything but it didn't work"). They often come together with "я — сложный клиент" ("I'm a difficult client") and "вы моя последняя надежда" ("you're my last hope"). She says neither statement is about laziness, procrastination, weakness, lack of willpower, or "something is wrong with me": "Все с вами в порядке, обещаю" ("Everything is fine with you, I promise"). One key condition for real change is to understand your stage, use the tools that fit it and take the steps that fit it. She had touched on this before when explaining why Nike's "Just Do It" slogan does not (always) work. Sometimes the problem is not what you do or don't do. It is that you try to act too early: "Часто мы пытаемся “just do it”, еще находясь на этапах “just want it”, “just choose it”, “just believe it”" ("We often try to 'just do it' while still at the stages of 'just want it', 'just choose it', 'just believe it'") [msg #490].

**Which tools work when.** In the early stages, change runs on cognitive and emotional processes: awareness, re-evaluation, working with ambivalence, making sense of things, building identity and a vision of the future, and working with values. Behavioral tools become effective only later, once the inner work is done. These are habit formulas, stimulus control, environment design and reinforcement, "все, что так любят в популярной литературе про привычки" ("everything popular habit literature loves so much"). For this reason Katya always starts her client work with "conversations". It is also why popular habit books, challenges and marathons may not work for you: "Они уже про действия, а вы, может быть, еще не там" ("They are already about action, and you may not be there yet"). Resistance and failures are not a system crash, and they are not your nature or your fate. They are a signal that you are not yet at "just do it". If you give yourself tools that fit your stage, movement becomes possible again, "без насилия над собой, с удовольствием и интересом" ("without violence against yourself, with pleasure and interest") [msg #490].

**The reframed question.** Do not ask "Почему я не делаю? Почему у меня не получается?" ("Why am I not doing it? Why isn't it working?"). Ask «На какой стадии я сейчас? И какие инструменты помогут мне подвинуться вперед?» ("What stage am I at now? And what tools will help me move forward?"). Sometimes the best step is to reflect rather than act, and to experiment rather than expect a stable streak from yourself. If something isn't working, you may simply be rushing yourself: "Это не слабость. Это человеческая психика" ("It's not weakness. It's the human psyche") [msg #490].

The five stages, as described on Katya's cards:

1. **Stage 1. Precontemplation / Отрицание (denial): «У меня и так все нормально» ("I'm fine as it is").** You don't yet know you need a change. You don't see the situation as a problem, you discount the signals, you believe nothing can be changed and that "everyone is like this", and you probably even deny it. Typical phrases: «Да, я устаю, но кто сейчас не устает?» ("Yes, I get tired, but who doesn't these days?"), «У меня вся семья диабетиков, с этим ничего не поделаешь» ("My whole family is diabetic, nothing can be done about it"), «Мой дед курил и дожил до 80 лет!» ("My grandfather smoked and lived to 80!"). Katya says such clients never come to her, but relatives sometimes bring them to other specialists, and there is a way to work even with them. **What helps here:** the specialist works on raising awareness, building agency (агентность) and showing that change is possible [img #485].
2. **Stage 2. Contemplation / Размышление (reflection): «Кажется, так больше нельзя... но я не знаю, как иначе» ("It seems I can't go on like this... but I don't know any other way").** You now see that there is a problem and allow that "it's not so clear-cut". You want change but are afraid to change anything at the same time. You can't give up the current state for many reasons, you have doubts, you suffer from a lack of belief in yourself, and you resist change "even if it seems you've already decided". Katya calls this the ambivalent stage. Typical phrases: «Я понимаю, что так дальше нельзя, но сейчас не время...» ("I understand I can't go on like this, but now is not the time..."), «Я не уверена, что у меня получится» ("I'm not sure I'll succeed"), «Я, конечно, хочу поменять это, НО...» ("Of course I want to change this, BUT..."). **Key point:** "это стадия не прокрастинации, а амбивалентности" ("this is a stage not of procrastination but of ambivalence"). It is still too early to act. It is the right time to support yourself by working on meanings, values and beliefs [img #486].
3. **Stage 3. Preparation / Подготовка: «Я, кажется, готова попробовать» ("I think I'm ready to try").** You have decided the change will happen. The open questions are how to change, where to start, what to try, and what will and won't work, and this stage answers them. You start testing in practice ("потихоньку боем проверять", "gradually test in combat") what suits *you* for this goal. You look for options, change your environment, take small steps, watch your sensations and collect data. Typical phrases: «А что если сделать так?» ("What if I do it like this?"), «На этой неделе я попробую...» ("This week I'll try..."). By trial and error you find what will become the "flagship" of your change, and you gain interest and belief in yourself as things begin to work. **This is the stage** to bring in habit books, group work, marathons, and anything else that helps you find what works for you [img #487].
   - **Important remark on stages 1–3:** "к сожалению или счастью" ("unfortunately or fortunately"), the first three stages have no clear deadline. Their length depends on how much attention you give this work, whether you work alone or with a specialist, and your "baggage". People start from different points: one person just thinks "quitting smoking is hard", another thinks "это невозможно, я не представляю себя без сижки" ("it's impossible, I can't imagine myself without a cigarette") [img #488].
4. **Stage 4. Action / Действие: «Я знаю, что делать. И делаю это» ("I know what to do. And I do it").** It is now clear what to do, it's working, and nothing is stopping you. You may still need to put in effort, but each time there is less resistance and more sense of how great it is that you're doing this. At this stage habits take root, structure appears and the environment changes. Only now has the "just do it" stage arrived, and only because you went through the first three stages patiently and consciously. A relapse can happen here, and it definitely will, maybe more than once. Katya's framing: the relapse is "sent to you" so you can become a "ninja" at getting out of relapses. The good news is that over time relapses become rarer and shorter, and getting out of them becomes easier [img #489].
5. **Stage 5. Maintenance / Поддержание: «Теперь это я» ("Now this is me").** TTM holds that after about 6 months of regularly repeating a behavior, the action becomes habitual. It no longer needs special focus, still less willpower, and it becomes part of everyday life ("and of identity, if you like"). If you are here, Katya says you have done well and can move on to the next big change. Relapses happen here too. If you worked through "the relapse material" well at the previous stage, they are less traumatic. "откат - не провал, а часть процесса" ("a relapse is not a failure but part of the process") [img #490].

### 11 reasons habits don't take
Katya discussed this in a podcast as a list of "why we resist even what we consider useful" and the mistakes that stop us from giving a new behavior a chance [msg #616]:

1. **Wrong stage of change.** We try to act while still at the stage where we need to work out "why do I need this at all? Do I really want it?" Action tools don't work until an inner choice is made (see *TTM* above) [msg #616].
2. **No meaning.** Without an answer to the question of purpose, any resistance eventually wins. That purpose must be your own and not someone else's. «Чтобы влезть в джинсы» ("to fit into jeans") and «чтобы чувствовать себя живой» ("to feel alive") are very different kinds of fuel: "Второе горит дольше" ("The second burns longer") [msg #616].
3. **Identity doesn't match.** You do the action, but inside there is a quiet "this isn't me". It is not "I'm a person who exercises" but "I'm trying to force myself to go to the gym", and these are fundamentally different states. The same goes for "I don't want to, but I have to", as in "I hate running, but I need cardio" [msg #616].
4. **The source of the change is punishment, not care.** If the change is driven by "something is wrong with me, I need to fix it", the inner child will resist. "Приживается выбор, а не наказание" ("Choice takes root, not punishment") [msg #616].
5. **Immunity to change.** This is healthy human resistance, a protective mechanism. The brain is a prediction machine, and the new is energy-costly and unpredictable for it, so it literally throws a spanner in the works out of self-preservation instinct. If you move in small steps, through interest and experiment, the defence doesn't react [msg #616].
6. **Too big a step.** Continuing the resistance theme, "everything at once" doesn't work: three workouts a week instead of one, twenty minutes of meditation instead of three, a full diet overhaul instead of one vegetable at lunch. The bigger the step, the higher the chance it won't happen on the first hard day [msg #616].
7. **Environment not organized.** Example: you want to get up an hour earlier and work out, at 7 instead of your usual 8. Instead of resolving that contradiction you just try to force yourself to both get up and train. "Среда сильнее намерения почти всегда. Сначала среда, потом действие" ("The environment is stronger than intention almost always. First the environment, then the action") [msg #616].
8. **No anchor.** The habit "hangs in the air", not tied to an existing action. "I'll meditate", but when exactly, and after what? Without an anchor the new habit needs a separate decision every day. Tie the new to the old: after coffee, before the shower, right after work [msg #616].
9. **Ideal conditions and no plan B.** "I'll start when I buy sneakers, a gym membership, when this project ends." Conditions will never be ideal. And even once you start, there is no plan for a bad day: the habit is designed for an ideal day and doesn't work on a bad one. Answer in advance: what will I do if today everything goes wrong? "Три минуты вместо двадцати — это не провал, это план Б" ("Three minutes instead of twenty is not a failure, it's plan B") [msg #616].
10. **Replacing the action with the result.** Example: weighing yourself every day instead of noting whether you ate vegetables at every meal. "На этапе формирования привычки действие и есть результат. Отмечайте его" ("At the habit-formation stage the action is the result. Track it") [msg #616].
11. **Not enough repetitions and time.** A habit forms through a number of repetitions, not in 21 days. You spent so many years building the old behavior, so why should the new one appear in a week, two, or a month? (see *Three reasons change takes long*) [msg #616].

### Three reasons change takes long ("Your problem is that you're rushing")
Katya calls this a fundamental mistake she sees again and again: "вы ждете быстрых результатов там, где их не бывает" ("you expect quick results where there are none"). She admits she does it herself. With her own promotion: she posted two reels and they didn't take off, went on two podcasts and didn't become famous, ran two ads and didn't get a stable income, and concluded "that's it, let's wrap up". When something doesn't work the first time, the inner critic switches on: «значит, я недостаточно стараюсь» ("so I'm not trying hard enough"), «значит, мне не дано» ("so it's not for me"), «значит, я просто такая» ("so I'm just like this"). She calls this perhaps the most destructive part of the story: "не сам откат, а то, что мы с ним делаем у себя в голове" ("not the relapse itself, but what we do with it in our heads"). The good news is that this is how change works: you need patience and tolerance for relapses [msg #612].

The three reasons:
1. **Change is a process.** A person doesn't jump from "I don't think about it" straight to "I do it consistently". The stages of contemplation, preparation and first actions come in between, and every stage has relapses. Going back to a previous stage doesn't mean you're at the beginning: "Вы уже другой человек, который знает больше о себе, чем раньше" ("You're already a different person who knows more about yourself than before") [msg #612].
2. **You are literally building a new brain.** Every new habit means building new neural connections. Whether a habit sticks depends on the number of repetitions, not the number of days. The old connections, the ones you used for years, don't go away; they just gradually become less active. You built your current behavior over many years, so why expect the new one in a month? Ask yourself how long it takes to repeat a new action 10, 100 or 1000 times for the behavior to settle in the brain [msg #612].
3. **You are in conflict with your old identity.** "I'm not a sporty person", "I'm a night owl", "I can't live without sweets", "I'm like this by nature": this is who you think you are. New behavior at first literally feels like "Чужой внутри вас" ("an Alien inside you"). Each relapse seems to prove to you that "такая я и есть" ("this is just who I am"). Here you need faith. To make believing easier, Katya says it has been shown that people really do change at any age (she links a PMC research article; it is not described further) [msg #612].

**Rule of thumb that follows:** "месяц, два, три — срок, чтобы попробовать. Не чтобы перестроить образ жизни" ("a month, two, three is a period to try things, not to rebuild a lifestyle"). Lengthen your horizon. For anything fundamental, such as daily routine, relationship with food, movement or stress, plan for a year at minimum. That is the time a change needs to go through all the stages, build up repetitions, relapse plenty and become part of who you are [msg #612].

**Case (client).** At the end of 2023 a client set the goal "become active", starting from an almost zero base. By 2025 she doesn't think about whether to go to a workout; she just goes. She says that resting now means asking herself whether she'd like to move, because she is now "такой человек" ("that kind of person"). Katya stresses that it looks easy, but between those two points lay months, a year, or longer of trials, errors, returns, disappointments and new attempts. Working with some clients for several years lets her watch how lifestyles and identities actually change [msg #612].

### Effort vs violence (Насилие VS Усилие)
This opens Katya's rubric "Словарь изменений Кати Стрельниковой" ("Katya Strelnikova's dictionary of change"), about words people confuse. She calls this pair the dichotomy that most often makes change impossible, and the one she points out to almost every new client. The two sound similar and sometimes feel similar, but they are fundamentally different [msg #663].

- **Effort (усилие):** "когда сложно, но ты выбираешь двигаться дальше и делать что-то, потому что хочешь" ("when it's hard, but you choose to keep going and do something because you want to"). Her examples:
  - you don't want to get up for a workout, but you do and you're glad afterwards (she adds, referring to another post, that the moment when workouts become automatic may never come);
  - a conversation is hard, but afterwards you feel lighter;
  - starting a new project is scary but interesting;
  - you sit down to write without wanting to, and 20 minutes later you can't stop;
  - you change an eating habit, the first days are hard, and after a couple of days you notice it's better.

  "Усилие требует энергии, но возвращает ее с процентами" ("Effort takes energy but returns it with interest"). There is always meaning and your own choice behind it. "Именно на усилии строятся изменения" ("Change is built precisely on effort") [msg #663].
- **Violence (насилие):** "когда делаешь что-то вопреки себе, себя ломаешь, делаешь, потому что надо" ("when you do something against yourself, break yourself, do it because you must"). It is not "it's hard for me" but "it's bad for me"; not "unfamiliar" but "this isn't me at all". You have to break yourself anew every time, and it gets harder each time, not easier. Afterwards you feel no joy, relief or satisfaction. "Насилие не строит новое поведение. Оно разрушает даже то, что есть" ("Violence doesn't build new behavior. It destroys even what exists"). It makes you want to go into total rebellion and refusal [msg #663].

They are easy to confuse because both start with discomfort. Katya says the discomfort feels very different, based on her own experience and what clients tell her. She has rich experience of effort (rebuilding her lifestyle by 180 degrees) and of violence ("20 лет РПП в ремиссии", "20 years of an eating disorder, in remission"). **Three distinguishing signs:**
1. **Effort ends:** you do it and forget. Violence doesn't end: the thoughts come back, it's always not enough or not right [msg #663].
2. **After effort you want to do it again**, maybe not right away, but you do. After violence you never want to [msg #663].
3. **Violence almost always comes from outside:** from other people's expectations, standards and images of what your life, body, career or routine ought to be. Her examples: «ешь по 1200 ккал, потому что так написано в интернете» ("you eat 1200 kcal because the internet says so"), «встаешь в 5 утра, потому что так делают “успешные люди”» ("you get up at 5 am because 'successful people' do"), «делаешь силовые, потому что так сказала Стейси Симс» ("you do strength training because Stacy Sims said so"). Effort is yours; it is born inside, from your own choice [msg #663].

**The boundary moves.** What is violence today can become effort in a month, if you work on your mindset, enter the change with a different motivation, and move gradually and with self-respect. Her example: people come to her "hating vegetables" and end up leaving restaurants that don't serve salad. It also works the other way: what began as effort can turn into violence if you stop hearing your brain's and body's signals and sink into achievement-chasing (достигаторство), control and criticism [msg #663].

**Self-check question to ask regularly while working on a change:** «Я сейчас прилагаю усилие? Или я себя насилую?» ("Am I making an effort right now? Or am I forcing (violating) myself?") [msg #663].

### Finding your "enough" (effort axis × result axis)
**Problem.** Client conversations usually start with numbers: «Я хочу весить 60 кг» ("I want to weigh 60 kg"), «Я хочу медитировать 30 минут каждый день» ("I want to meditate 30 minutes every day"), «Я хочу вставать в 6 утра» ("I want to get up at 6 am"). When you look deeper, these numbers are usually taken "out of thin air". That is why such changes are hard to keep. We set a rigid target without counting the "cost" of the change, or the impossibility of keeping it up daily for the rest of our lives, simply because in that form it doesn't fit into our life [msg #481].

**Step 1: reverse the question.** Katya asks clients to look at the goal from the other side:
- «Если я буду питаться так, как считаю здоровым и правильным, сколько я буду весить?» ("If I eat the way I consider healthy and right, how much will I weigh?")
- «Сколько и как часто мне нужно медитировать, чтобы чувствовать себя спокойнее и заземленнее?» ("How much and how often do I need to meditate to feel calmer and more grounded?")
- «Какое время подъема для меня оптимально, чтобы выспаться и чувствовать себя хорошо?» ("What wake-up time is optimal for me to get enough sleep and feel good?") [msg #481]

**Step 2: picture two axes.** Any habit, routine or lifestyle is built on an **effort axis** and a **result axis**. The card shows "Результат" (result) on the vertical axis and "Усилия" (efforts) on the horizontal axis. A rising curve has a heart marking one point on it, with dashed lines to both axes. The task is not to maximize either axis but to find the point where they meet that suits you: "Не идеальную. А вашу. Ваше достаточно" ("Not the ideal one. Yours. Your enough") [msg #481, img #481].

**What "enough" is.** It is not about being lazy. It is about a sustainable ("экологичный") level you can keep up permanently, not only in periods of inspiration, free time and unusual energy. Katya cites an idea from Scott Young's newsletter: if we were perfect economic robots, we would add 5 minutes of load each day until the benefit equalled the cost, but we are not robots. Real life is made of trade-offs:
- add morning exercise but have to get up at 5, and you lose sleep;
- cook every meal yourself but give up your walk, and you lose movement;
- save money and never allow yourself rest, and you lose joy.

So "enough" is a feeling rather than a formula: the level where you feel good, that you can really keep up, and that doesn't destroy other important parts of your life [msg #481].

**Step 3: the question that "changes everything" (and the goal of the exploration).** It is not "How do I do it perfectly?" and not even "How do I get better?". It is «Какое мое “достаточно”, которое могу поддерживать постоянно — и которое делает мою жизнь лучше, а не тяжелее, и ведет меня к моим целям?» ("What is my 'enough' that I can keep up permanently, that makes my life better rather than harder, and leads me to my goals?") [msg #481].

**Examples of different "enoughs":** three workouts for one person, one for another; 10,000 steps for one, just leaving the house for another; 20 minutes of meditation for one, a retreat once a year for another; a balanced plate at every meal for one, simply managing to eat for another. All of this is normal, because it depends on your values, priorities and life: "«достаточно» — не универсальное. Оно ваше" ("'enough' isn't universal. It's yours") [msg #481].

### Start small → get to big (six micro-steps)
This is a post forwarded from Salatshop with examples of starting with a minimal change that may grow [msg #136]:
1. **One workout a week.** You will surely find time for one session, and after it you may want to add a second.
2. **Limit screen time to one hour, at least once a week.** That day may influence your screen time on other days.
3. **A 30-second plank before breakfast.** Everyone has 30 seconds. Afterwards you may want to hold it longer, or add a couple more exercises.
4. **Start at least one meal a day with a plate of vegetables and greens.** Don't fear radical diet changes; make just one change in one meal.
5. **Talk yourself into at least five minutes of meditation.** After five minutes you may want to give it another ten.
6. **Go to bed before midnight at least once a week.** Don't give up everything you love doing at night; just try falling asleep earlier once a week.

Principle: don't aim straight for grand life-changing plans, and don't wait for January 1 or a special Monday. Try one small change today and "разрешить себе в любой момент вернуться назад" ("allow yourself to go back at any moment"). There is a good chance that starting small will get you to something big over time. An accompanying card repeats "PLAN" four times and then "ACTION" once, making the same point: too much planning, too little doing [msg #136, img #137].

### State → thinking → action chain (guest: Nastya Khoreva)
**Framing (Katya).** "Изменения — не то, что вы делаете, а как думаете и себя чувствуете" ("Change is not what you do but how you think and feel"). Many of us have wanted to change something, even started, and still returned to the old behavior. Desire and action are not enough to move; you also need strength and the right attitude. "Не получится взлететь, если энергии нет даже ползать. Не получится заненавидеть себя до любви" ("You can't take off if you don't have the energy even to crawl. You can't hate yourself into love") [msg #216].

**Khoreva's argument, layer by layer:**
1. **Action layer rests on self-image.** In the 1970s, while working as a tennis coach, Timothy Gallwey found that athletes play two games at once: one on the court and one in their heads. Khoreva says that after much research the fact stands: our self-image is always built into the specific actions we take. How do I assess the threat? Is it hard and I won't cope, or do I know I can? Can I allow myself this? Even "самая эффективная система планирования не сработает, если набор внутренних смыслов и убеждений будет тащить в противоположную сторону" ("the most effective planning system won't work if your set of inner meanings and beliefs pulls in the opposite direction") [msg #216].
2. **The real obstacle is cognitive.** The problem is not that we don't know what to do and how; the secrets lie on the cognitive level. Our "procrastinators, saboteurs and impostors" live there, and if we don't come to terms with them they won't let us go further [msg #216].
3. **Below the cognitive layer: the body.** Even dealing with those inner characters won't happen without one more aspect. A large amount of data shows that brain and body are in constant communication, even when we're not aware of it. If we don't get enough sleep, the body handles stress worse and the brain processes information and makes decisions worse, "что уж говорить о том, чтобы разбираться с какими-то там самозванцами, проще сдаться" ("never mind dealing with some impostors, it's easier to give up"). (She links a Harvard Medical School magazine article on interoception.) [msg #216]
4. **Conclusion.** What is available at the level of action is always limited by how we think, and our thinking depends entirely on how we feel. "наше состояние — фундамент для всего" ("our state is the foundation for everything"), so improving it must be made the most important task [msg #216].

**Worked example.** Someone finally gets a job they long wanted, such as a dream company or a promotion. Having overcome obstacles, they now come to the office anxious, constantly thinking about how not to make a mistake. Their inner dialogue mixes *«вдруг я не справлюсь?»* ("what if I can't cope?") and *«я недостаточно хорош»* ("I'm not good enough"). Sooner or later the uncertainty limits their actions: avoiding difficult discussions, being unable to defend their ideas, spending extra time re-checking. As a result projects stall, the work stops being enjoyable and relationships with colleagues deteriorate. That is the visible layer, which we usually notice and try to fix, often by radical means. Digging deeper shows the person was anxious not only about the work but also because of sleepless nights and constant fatigue, and because of a past negative experience with a team that hurt their self-esteem. Stress gets in the way of dealing with all this, and there's no time to rest because there's so much to do. Khoreva calls it a very rough example. The lesson is that with almost any task you eventually find that state is the foundation [msg #216].

### Stage-matched tools: "Прежде чем just do it, надо just want it и just believe it"
**Purpose.** This answers why someone who "has tried everything and quits every time", or who "has long wanted something and knows everything but doesn't do it", keeps failing. Katya says she "never tires" of repeating the point. Her playful crossed-out first version of it was "вы просто пока не умеете готовить изменения" ("you just don't yet know how to cook changes"). Her actual claim is that you most likely don't know your stage of change and are trying to use the wrong tools [msg #689].

**Principle.** "Любое устойчивое изменение в жизни человека проходит несколько стадий, и на каждой стадии нужны свои инструменты" ("Any sustainable change in a person's life passes through several stages, and each stage needs its own tools"). When we start using another stage's tools, we get "it doesn't work". The headline: "Прежде чем just do it, надо just want it и just believe it" ("Before 'just do it' you have to 'just want it' and 'just believe it'") [msg #689].

**What each situation needs.** The post lists four cases and ends with an ellipsis, so it may not be exhaustive:
1. **You don't really believe change is possible.** Then you need to "взращивать веру" ("grow belief"). Action tools come later [msg #689].
2. **You haven't fully decided.** Then "нужна не мотивация, а разрешение исследовать" ("what's needed is not motivation but permission to explore"). Pushing motivation onto someone who is undecided is the wrong tool [msg #689].
3. **You are ready to act.** Then you need "конкретный план с оглядкой на окружение" ("a concrete plan that takes your environment into account") [msg #689].
4. **You have relapsed.** Then you need "ритуал быстрого возвращения" ("a ritual of quick return"). (!) INCOMPLETE: the post does not describe what such a ritual consists of [msg #689].

**What the workshop promised participants would learn.** These items show what the authors consider the key outcomes of stage diagnosis:
- identify which stage you are *actually* at, which is "обычно это сюрприз" ("usually a surprise");
- understand why previous attempts didn't work;
- learn which tools work for your particular stage;
- choose a concrete next step that really helps you advance;
- see why a relapse is "не доказательство несостоятельности, а естественная часть процесса изменений" ("not proof of failure/incompetence but a natural part of the process of change").

The intended result is a clear answer to "Что конкретно мне делать дальше?" ("What exactly should I do next?"), and knowing why that step is what you need right now [msg #689].

**Authors' basis.** Katya and Zhenya Datsko say they have helped people change for 5+ years, "не идеально, а реально" ("not ideally, but really"). Both "sincerely love" TTM, which they call a "научно подтвержденную модель изменений" ("scientifically confirmed model of change"). They say that in their own and their clients' experience "изменения работают ровно так, как она описывает" ("change works exactly as it describes"). This is the authors' assessment, and no studies are cited in the post [msg #689].

### Relapse mechanism: barriers higher than readiness to act
**Katya's case.** She moves constantly: 6 countries in seven years, plus many islands, cities, apartments and villas. Each move does the same thing to her strength training: a relapse. Her explanation is that she relapsed not because she is lazy but "потому, что барьеры вдруг стали выше, чем моя готовность действовать" ("because the barriers suddenly became higher than my readiness to act"). The barriers she lists are a new city or district, new studios, a new schedule and a different time zone. A familiar city counts too: Moscow is her old city, but "мозг уже успел отвыкнуть" ("the brain has already got unused to it"). As a result she is no longer in Action, although it had seemed Action "wouldn't go anywhere". She has fallen several stages back, into Preparation or even Contemplation [msg #693].

**Relapse as a stage.** Katya states: "Откат — это такая же стадия изменений, как и все остальные" ("Relapse is a stage of change just like all the others"). The existing topic file treats relapse as something that happens inside Action and Maintenance, not as a separate stage. This is a change of framing. Her post also shows that a relapse can drop a person more than one stage [msg #693].

**The rule.** "Откат случается не от лени и не от нашей личной дефектности. Он случается, когда барьеры становятся выше готовности действовать" ("A relapse happens not from laziness and not from our personal defectiveness. It happens when barriers become higher than the readiness to act"). From this she concludes that "самый умный ход — не пинать себя сильнее, а снижать барьеры" ("the smartest move is not to kick yourself harder but to lower the barriers"). That means making the first step "настолько маленьким и простым, чтобы его невозможно было не сделать" ("so small and simple that it is impossible not to do it"). Clients often scold themselves with "ну сколько можно возвращаться к одному и тому же" ("how many times can you come back to the same thing"). Katya treats this self-blame as the wrong response. Her wish for readers: "пусть наши откаты становятся все короче" ("may our relapses become shorter and shorter") [msg #693].

### Lowering barriers by stage (exercise example)
Katya uses the Fitmost class-booking app to show what lowering barriers looks like at each stage. The post is a labelled advertisement (see *Caveats*). The stage logic is general:
1. **Contemplation (Размышление).** Barriers are very high and motivation is low. The head is full of "а вдруг не то, а вдруг не понравится, а вдруг зря" ("what if it's the wrong thing, what if I don't like it, what if it's in vain"). The main task is to lower the threshold of entry as far as possible: no buying a membership, no calling anyone, no signing up for anything. You open the app, see what's nearby and book a class in two clicks. The point is the shift in identity and action: "Ты уже не «думаешь о спорте», а идешь на спорт" ("You are no longer 'thinking about sport', you are going to sport") [msg #693].
2. **Preparation (Подготовка).** Here it's important to try different things and find out what works for *me* and in what form. Without an aggregator this is expensive and tedious, with a separate membership or tedious communication with a club for each trial. With easy single bookings you can do pilates, yoga and strength in one week, at different times of day, near home and near work. Then you compare, listen to yourself and choose what you really like and when. Katya notes the app lists over 4,000 venues. She checked and found single sessions through it cheaper than booking directly with the club [msg #693].
3. **Action and Maintenance (Действие и Поддержание).** Katya calls these "самые коварные стадии в каком-то смысле" ("in a sense the most treacherous stages"), because once you are into it, it seems the job is done. But the settings are easy to knock off at these stages: a move, a holiday, a rush at work, a schedule change. Boredom is another risk, which Katya says she definitely has. "И привет, Откат" ("And hello, Relapse"). Two things matter here: movement must fit into life as conveniently as possible, and you need to be able to listen to yourself and change formats to suit your state and mood [msg #693].

### Preparation vs Contemplation: the four base questions
**The finding.** Before the workshop, participants could take a free self-diagnosis of their stage. Katya reports that 70% placed themselves in Preparation, saying "я уже готова действовать, осталось чуть-чуть" ("I'm already ready to act, just a little left"). "Звучит вдохновляюще, конечно. Но not so fast" ("Sounds inspiring, of course. But not so fast"). At the meeting this turned out not to be true [msg #709].

**Why.** "Подготовку крайне легко перепутать с предыдущей стадией — Размышлением" ("Preparation is extremely easy to confuse with the previous stage, Contemplation"). We feel we are at the starting line, but we haven't yet dealt concretely with the base questions of Contemplation:
- "а зачем мне это вообще?" ("why do I need this at all?");
- "а точно ли я этого хочу?" ("do I really want this?");
- "а мое ли это?" ("is this mine?");
- "а верю ли я, что получится?" ("do I believe it will work out?") [msg #709].

If these are not answered, you are still in Contemplation, whatever you feel. The workshop gave a separate diagnostic tool to catch this substitution. (!) ### The equation analogy: understand the logic instead of brute force
Imagine solving an equation and getting a wrong result. You could brute-force it by substituting one combination, then another, then a third, and you might eventually succeed after trying every option. But that is "долго, мучительно и вслепую" ("long, painful and blind"). It is simpler to understand the logic and see exactly where you are making the mistake, which shortens the path many times over. Change works the same way. Yet "вместо того, чтобы разобраться, что не работает, мы годами ходим по кругу" ("instead of figuring out what doesn't work, we go around in circles for years"): start, quit, start again. Katya's conclusion: "«В этот раз получится» — не про стараться сильнее" ("'This time it will work' is not about trying harder"). It is about understanding where you are and starting from there, "(а не из вымышленной точки, где нам хотелось бы быть)" ("(and not from an imagined point where we would like to be)"). Typical circular changes she names are routine, food, movement, a blog and a new project [msg #709].

### The illusion of the usefulness threshold (forwarded from Daily Reminder)
**The distortion.** "мы склонны считать, что любые физические упражнения и практики приносят эффект лишь тогда, когда ими занимаешься долго" ("we tend to think that any physical exercise and practices have an effect only when you do them for a long time"). The author names it "«иллюзией порога пользы»" ("the illusion of the usefulness threshold"). The example: yoga twice a week for 50 minutes counts as great, while five minutes four times a week counts as not having practised at all. "Но факты указывают на обратное" ("But the facts point to the opposite") [msg #713].

**Evidence the post cites:**
- **Short meditation and sleep.** One analysis found that people who meditate only 5–10 minutes two or three times a week tend to sleep better than those who practise more than 30 minutes a day, five days a week [msg #713].
- **One minute of breathing.** In another study, even one minute of slow or mindful breathing reduced participants' sense of stress and the amount of intrusive rumination (навязчивых размышлений) [msg #713].
- **Breathing meta-analysis.** A meta-analysis of breathing-practice studies found no simple link between session duration and the size of the stress reduction [msg #713].
- **Surgeons' micro-breaks.** The same principle applies to warm-ups. During operations, surgeons did just 1.5–2 minutes of stretching every 20–40 minutes. On days with these pauses they had less neck and back pain, and many reported better physical performance and concentration. The operations did not get longer [msg #713].

**Conclusion and method.** "регулярность может быть важнее объема" ("regularity may be more important than volume"). The main thing is to build the habit. The simplest way is to tie the micro-practice to some regular action or event. Example: an author of an article in New Scientist does a one-minute self-compassion meditation while waiting for an espresso at the coffee machine. That means "направляешь на себя доброту и тепло, положив одну руку на живот, а другую на грудь" ("you direct kindness and warmth toward yourself, with one hand on your belly and the other on your chest"). This matches the existing file's *anchor* idea (reason 8 of the 11 reasons) and its small-steps principle, and adds evidence that the small dose itself can be effective [msg #713].

### How long change takes: Katya's own case

- Her full lifestyle rebuild took about eight years, with trials, errors, failures and disappointments, then rises, exploration and new attempts again. ### Why New Year resolutions fail
- Katya cites research by Strava on 800 million users who logged their progress: about 80% abandoned their promises by the second Friday of January, and 23% didn't get through even the first week. Since 2019, after this study, the second Friday of January has been marked as the international "Quitters Day" ("день забивальщика") [msg #252].
- She lists factors that decide whether a change succeeds:
  - magical thinking and belief in miracles;
  - the timing of the moment;
  - neglecting the process;
  - trying to change the wrong thing and/or in the wrong way;
  - not understanding what behavior is and how habits work.

  She does not expand on them in that post [msg #252].
- Her view: you can work through all this on your own, but from her own experience the most effective way is often to go to a helping professional. She herself started the year with a psychotherapist and a coach [msg #252].
- "Символизм вообще-то может быть важной частью изменений, если правильно его готовить" ("Symbolism can actually be an important part of change, if you prepare it right"). A symbolic date like New Year can help when it is used deliberately [msg #252].

### Lessons from the meeting "On the third attempt" (seven women's change stories)
- The meeting was for people who tried and quit, who know what they should do and still don't, and who are tired of marathons, challenges and motivational posts "that work for three days". Each woman told personal stories of what didn't work and why, what worked for her, and her ups, downs and relapses, "без успешного успеха и сокрытия неудобных деталей" ("without 'successful success' and without hiding inconvenient details") [msg #626].
- Katya's overall conclusion: whatever the differences between situations, changing your life is "не разовая акция, а процесс" ("not a one-off action but a process"), and it almost never goes as planned. Relapses, pauses and returns to steps you already took are "не сбой, а часть системы" ("not a malfunction but part of the system"). If you trust the process and yourself, at some point you arrive exactly where you need to be, even if the path looked nothing like you imagined at the start [msg #653].
- The main thought Katya took from each speaker:
  - **Anya Bichevskaya:** "Если смотреть на кризисы с любопытством, а не со страхом... мы по сути со своим опытом все время становимся строительным материалом для следующей версии себя" ("If you look at crises with curiosity rather than fear... we, with our experience, constantly become the building material for the next version of ourselves"). Her announced topic was how wisdom, compassion and humor free you from the grandiosity of your own personality and reconcile you with life's imperfection [msg #653, #626].
  - **Anya Fedorova:** happiness is "не какая-то мифическая двухэтажная квартира в красивом городе, а это такой обычный вторник" ("not some mythical two-storey apartment in a beautiful city, but an ordinary Tuesday"): get up, brush your teeth, pour a coffee, go for a walk, be present.   - **Natasha Gulyaeva:** "Дисциплина — это акт любви к себе... Без дисциплины нет медитации. Без медитации для меня нет никакой дисциплины" ("Discipline is an act of self-love...   - **Masha Polishchuk:** "Доброе отношение к себе — это не про самопотакание" ("Kindness to yourself is not about self-indulgence"). Her announced thesis: only kindness to yourself and the ability to bear yourself as you are give a truly solid foundation for change [msg #653, #626].
  - **Sveta Shedina:** "Изменение — это когда ты можешь снова и снова отпускать старые версии себя, чтобы освободить место для новых... ## Practical playbook

- **Diagnose before acting:** ask yourself "What stage am I at, and what tools fit it?" In denial or ambivalence, work on awareness, meanings, values and beliefs (including in conversations with a specialist). In preparation, experiment, change your environment, take small steps and collect data; this is when habit books, groups and marathons help. In action, add structure, environment and reinforcement, and practice getting out of relapses [msg #490, img #485, img #486, img #487, img #489].
- **Habit design checklist** (built from the 11 reasons): an own "why" with lasting fuel (feeling alive, not fitting into jeans); a motive of care, not punishment; a step small enough to happen on your worst day; the environment arranged first; an anchor to an existing action (after coffee, before the shower, after work); a plan B for a bad day (3 minutes instead of 20); tracking the action rather than the result; and a horizon counted in repetitions, with a year as the minimum for fundamental change [msg #616, #612].
- **Before setting a numeric target**, rewrite it as "what level gives me the feeling I want, and can I keep it up for life?" Then check what it would cost in sleep, movement and joy [msg #481].
- **During a relapse**, watch the inner critic ("so I'm just like this"). A return to an earlier stage is not a return to the start: you now know more about yourself [msg #612, img #489].

- **When you relapse:** name the barriers that rose (new place, schedule, time zone, a work rush, boredom) instead of calling yourself lazy. Then shrink the first step until it is impossible not to do it [msg #693].
- **Before declaring yourself "ready to act":** answer the four base questions (why, do I really want it, is it mine, do I believe it will work). If you can't, treat yourself as still in Contemplation [msg #709].
- **If you're undecided:** give yourself permission to explore rather than pumping up motivation. **If you're ready:** write a concrete plan that accounts for your environment. **If you don't believe change is possible:** work on belief first [msg #689].
- **For exercise in Action/Maintenance:** make movement convenient to fit into your life and allow yourself to switch formats according to mood and state, to guard against boredom and disruption [msg #693].
- **For micro-practices:** 1–10 minutes done regularly, anchored to an existing routine such as waiting for coffee [msg #713].

## Evidence and numbers

- Strava study of 800 million users: about 80% abandoned resolutions by the second Friday of January, and 23% within the first week. "Quitters Day" has been marked since 2019 [msg #252].
- TTM: about 6 months of regular repetition before a behavior becomes habitual (maintenance) [img #490].
- Repetition counts rather than days: Katya rejects the "21 days" idea and frames habit formation as 10, 100 or 1000 repetitions [msg #612, #616].
- Katya's own change took about 8 years (#195, #252, #612). Elsewhere she says 10 years (#626) or 6 years (#626, #653); see the contradiction noted above.
- Katya says it has been shown that people really change at any age, citing a PMC article that the post does not describe further [msg #612].

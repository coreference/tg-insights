# Work-life balance in practice

## What this covers

How to combine work, family and the rest of life in practice, as taught through the channel's own posts and the posts it forwards: a founder-mother's principles for combining career and three children, calendar-based planning (calendar ninja levels, the ideal week, scheduled rest, protected Mondays and Fridays), the 8+8+8 rule, a coach's critique of "work-life balance", a high-season routine of a coach-strategist, Kate Hall's argument that overwork kills agency, and the misery tax. The sources treat balance not as a fashionable term but as a necessity: without managing state and energy, people "can't carry" their roles, burn out, lose agency and end up paying for disliked work with constant compensatory spending [msg #35, #191, #212, #258].

## Core ideas

- **Balance is a matter of management, not of choosing between career and family.** The FounderWoman author says the question of choice "never arose" for her; the real question is who you want to be, and then you build work, children, education, sport and self-care into the schedule (see *Seven principles*) [msg #35].
- **The calendar is the main balancing tool.** Planning the whole day — including meals, sport, routines and "doing nothing" — lets you manage time rather than be managed by it; rest that is not in the calendar does not happen (see *Calendar ninja levels*, *Ideal week*, *Protect Mondays and Fridays*) [msg #50, #201, #394].
- **"Work-life balance" can be the wrong frame.** Coach Sasha opposes the idea because it implies work is not life; she argues instead for clarifying what "the rest of life" is, checking energy, and dropping guilt and inflated expectations of hobbies [msg #74]. A forwarded video is titled "Work-life balance is bullshit" [msg #79].
- **Structure the day into work, sleep and personal time.** The 8+8+8 rule is presented as a flexible checklist, not a rigid formula; the goal is to find a place for all components [msg #124].
- **Rest protects productivity and agency.** Kate Hall: working too hard kills creativity and big-picture thinking, and burnout is "the main killer of agency" [msg #212].
- **Doing disliked work has a hidden price.** The misery tax is the stream of treats, coffees, lunches, purchases and luxury holidays needed to keep going in a job you dislike; it questions whether staying "for the money" really pays [msg #258, #259].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Seven principles for combining family and career (FounderWoman)
Purpose: the author's answer to "the most popular question" — how to combine family and career. She frames it from her experience of running a company of thousands of people and raising three children [msg #35].

1. **There is no choice to make — define who you want to be.** The question was never "career or family" but "Кто я? Какой я себя вижу? Кем я хочу быть как личность?" (Who am I? How do I see myself? Who do I want to be as a person?). She knows she wants to be happy, fulfilled, developing, healthy, loving and loved — a leader, woman, mother and wife. Having decided this, she builds work, children, education and sport into her schedule, plus self-care "чтобы на все это хватало сил" (so that there is enough strength for all of it) [msg #35].
2. **Study productivity, time management and above all energy.** To fit in what she wants, she started early studying everything about productivity and time management, "и, главное, — энергию" (and, most importantly, energy). She says there are few tools she has not tried, and she knows what works best for her [msg #35].
3. **Don't pity yourself and don't be afraid.** Her example: in her first pregnancy, at week 5, she was hospitalised with a threatened miscarriage and put on bed rest for several months. She decided "я не буду лежать и бояться, все будет хорошо" (I will not lie there and be afraid, everything will be fine), went back to work, and drove herself to an ultrasound every day. Lesson as she states it: don't fear that your husband will leave, that you'll lose your job, don't think about how you'll cope — just do what you believe in [msg #35].
4. **Everything is management.** "все есть управление, управление собой, графиком, людьми, энергией" (everything is management — of oneself, the schedule, people, energy). Her reasoning: if she can manage a company of thousands, she can manage her priorities and raise three children. The same skills work in private life as in business: responsibility, prioritisation, delegation, and enjoying the process [msg #35].
5. **Know clearly what you will and will not delegate.** She does **not** delegate choice and whatever defines her lifestyle: choosing team members (she interviews every employee of the company), housing, design, hotels, restaurants, the children's schools. She **does** delegate implementation, paperwork and process matters [msg #35].
6. **The more you want, the more you do; the more you do, the easier it gets.** She always has many bold desires; desires become plans and plans become life. "Чем выше задираешь планку, тем больше шансов к ней прийти и «растянуть» себя" (The higher you raise the bar, the greater the chance of reaching it and "stretching" yourself) [msg #35].
7. **With children, quality over quantity of time.** The hour before bed, making lunchboxes, weekends and holidays are the moments when the children "enjoy mum". Example: one morning she was rushing to a meeting but found 15 minutes to build a Lego house and promised to "put all the little figures to bed" in the evening [msg #35].

Addendum: in your own business you have much more freedom to build the schedule — she can now pause the working day between meetings to pick the children up from school, which was impossible before (when employed) [msg #35].

### Calendar ninja levels (four stages of calendar use)
Purpose: the author of *состояние потока* (flow state) describes her evolution of calendar use; for her, the calendar is "мощный инструмент для того, чтобы жить насыщенной, но сбалансированной жизнью" (a powerful tool for living a full but balanced life) and for realising desires without sacrificing herself. Readers are invited to rate their own level [msg #50].

1. **Level 1 (one ninja) — no calendar.** Everything kept in her head, a notebook, random phone notes. Her brain was a store for a pile of information never recorded systematically [msg #50].
2. **Level 2 — calendar for appointments only.** She began entering only scheduled meetings and events she had previously remembered or tracked in notes. This noticeably improved her basic time-management skills: she "delegated" the task of remembering to the calendar, and liked it [msg #50].
3. **Level 3 — the whole day is planned in the calendar,** including time without meetings and time alone. Blocks appeared for the morning and evening routine, sport, meals and simply "потупить" (zoning out/doing nothing). Effects: she began managing her time rather than the reverse, understanding where it goes, and seeing her workload visually. Bonus: she shared calendar access with her assistant and team [msg #50].
4. **Level 4 — preparing the ideal week in advance** (see *Ideal week method*). She already had March planned at the end of February [msg #50].

### Ideal week method
What it is: "заранее спланированный календарь" (a calendar planned in advance) that she fills in according to how she wants her schedule to look — what time she wakes up, how much time goes to work tasks, when she does sport, when she meets friends. How it works: things may change along the way, and she analyses the changes afterwards. Goal: "чтобы разница между идеальной и реальной неделей была минимальна" (that the difference between the ideal and the real week be minimal) — this, for her, is what effective time management is [msg #50].

### Six points on combining work and the rest of life (coach Sasha)
Purpose: written after questions in her survey and her own memory of crawling home from work "выжатым лимоном" (like a squeezed lemon). Points 1–2 are about the framing; points 3–6 are for those already in a situation where they have to "gnaw out" time and energy for themselves [msg #74].

1. **Reject the "work-life balance" idea.** She is against it because it makes it seem that "работа как будто это не жизнь" (work is somehow not life), leaving three hours a day for life. Her (self-ironic, marked with a clown emoji) conclusion: look straight away for work that lets you live [msg #74].
2. **Define what "the rest of life" is.** Ask where, why and for what you want to add your attention outside work [msg #74].
3. **Drop the pressure that out-of-work life and hobbies must exist.** The pressing thought "otherwise I'm a damn workaholic, and that's not fancy" makes people spend their scant remaining energy on guilt that they got no further than the office today. Her view: it is actually OK that there are periods when we go all-in on one thing [msg #74].
4. **Check your energy.** Combining may be hard simply because there is no energy — "а что вы сделали для того, чтобы она была?" (and what have you done so that you have it?). If you draw energy from non-work activities, treat them as restorative therapy and put them in the schedule like a doctor's appointment — not a whim but a way to get yourself in order: "dinner with friends at 7, full stop". Pitfall from her practice: people in pre-burnout sign up for, say, a cycling club at the weekend and are surprised they didn't rest at all, "even though the bike isn't work" [msg #74].
5. **Assess resources, then restore or plan.** Once you have assessed how many resources you have for non-work activities, restore them if they are low. If resources are fine, make a **top-three list** of non-work activities you want to do in the next one to three months. Why: it saves time on deliberation and guilt, and confirms there are resources for these activities [msg #74].
6. **Lower inflated expectations of hobbies.** She sees in practice expectations of hobbies like those of a "zirconium bracelet": if the hobby didn't make me lose weight, didn't rest me, didn't take me to a new level of development, or I didn't want to turn it into work, the hobby is thrown out. Her counter: "а что по удовольствию БЕЗ ПОЛЬЗЫ? уже можно" (what about pleasure WITHOUT usefulness? it's allowed now) [msg #74].

She adds that there are also many beliefs that prevent people from relaxing — and, even worse, from working normally; in her coaching she catches them in texts and live conversations and brings them to the client. ### 8+8+8 rule with 3F / 3H / 3S
What it is: a technique of "optimal work-life balance" that divides the day into three parts — 8 hours of work, 8 hours of sleep, 8 hours for yourself. Idea: distributing time in a balanced way optimises productivity and improves quality of life. The FounderWoman author stresses it is not a rigid formula but "больше чек-лист, гибкое руководство" (more of a checklist, a flexible guide) adaptable to your needs; the main thing is to find a place for every component [msg #124].

1. **8 hours of work** — devoted to professional tasks or learning, without distractions and procrastination [msg #124].
2. **8 hours of sleep** — to recover fully and be at peak productivity [msg #124].
3. **8 hours for yourself** — the part of the day that should bring joy and enjoyment, subdivided into three categories [msg #124]:
   - **3F: Family, Friends, Faith** — the people and beliefs that support and inspire you; time with family and friends strengthens relationships and social skills [msg #124].
   - **3H: Health, Hygiene, Hobby** — activities that improve physical and mental health [msg #124].
   - **3S: Soul, Service, Smile** — actions that nourish your inner world and give a feeling of happiness [msg #124].

She closes by asking which part is hardest to plan [msg #124].

### High-season resource routine (eight practices)
Context: Olia Bastron (coach and strategist, twice an emigrant, mother of two, married 18 years) says balance for her is a necessity, not a buzzword: she attends to her state because otherwise "я просто не смогу, не вывезу свои роли" (I simply won't be able to carry my roles). Her goal: "быть человеком, который много может, потому что есть на это силы" (to be a person who can do a lot because she has the strength for it). What she does [msg #191]:

1. **Digital detox and boundaries.** Once a week a digital detox; after 21:00 she tries "very (very!) hard" to leave the phone charging in the kitchen — she admits it works only with difficulty. Why: it keeps the brain from revving up before sleep and lets the day end calmly [msg #191].
2. **Structure of the working day.** She tries not to start work before 9–10 a.m. (she has a 5-hour time difference with Europe) and finishes by 16:00–17:00; no more than 2–3 client meetings a day [msg #191].
3. **Structure of the working week.** Wednesday is a special client-free day: she quietly studies, does research, meets pleasant people and handles things she never gets to on ordinary days [msg #191].
4. **Small pleasures.** Keeping resources is not only about work: she puts recovery in the calendar in advance — massages, trips — and does not forget small moments of pleasure such as a bubble bath or a morning basket of strawberries [msg #191].
5. **Working with specialists.** She regularly works with a psychologist and a health coach; she plans to start with a neuro-integration coach so as not to fall into the "red and purple zones" (when everything becomes "фиолетово" — slang for "I don't care") and to try "sprints" on herself; and she will return to a nutritionist with whom she built nutrition for the whole family a year earlier, feeling it is needed again [msg #191].
6. **Limits are OK.** She cannot focus on everything at once, and that is normal: she doesn't always manage to make lunchboxes (then her daughter Vera eats hamburgers from the school shop) or read school chats. She accepts this as part of a conscious choice in favour of what really matters: "важно знать свою цену и не бояться не успевать всё" (it's important to know your worth and not be afraid of not getting everything done) [msg #191].
7. **Quality of life through awareness.** She tracks sleep (with an Oura ring), the balance of fibre, iron and protein in her diet, and physical activity, because this helps keep resources. From time to time she rents a hotel for a couple of nights to reset and spend time in silence and in contact with herself [msg #191].
8. **Photography as meditation.** She attended a photo club in spring, a self-portrait course in summer and resumed photo walks — observing people, the city, shadows and light — which helps her be present. For moving "from the head into the body" she uses body-practice meetups ("телозамес"), ecstatic dance and the breathewithme app [msg #191].

Her summary: "баланс — это вдох и выдох, интенсивность и расслабление" (balance is inhale and exhale, intensity and relaxation) [msg #191].

### Protect Mondays and Fridays rule
Olya Chess adopts a personal rule voiced by Lera Sergeeva in an interview: protect Mondays and protect Fridays. Lera builds her schedule so that the most active work meetings, calls and decisions fall mid-week, leaving the start and end of the week "с воздухом для себя" (with air for oneself). Olya considers it a good way to balance everything and help yourself a little [msg #201].

It combines with Olya's own rule: she knows that if she does not plan rest — literally enter "обед", "чтение", "тренировка" (lunch, reading, workout) into the calendar — she will simply work. So she now plans not only those few hours a week but also to transform Mondays and Fridays [msg #201].

### Kate Hall's anti-overwork boundaries for agency
Context: Katya highlights from Kate Hall's essay on becoming more agentic (translated into Russian by Reminder) the point Hall considers most important: don't work too hard in order to be more productive [msg #212].

Definition of agency (агентность): it is the ability to act by one's own choice and make independent decisions; the feeling and belief that our actions affect life and the world around us. Katya adds that research shows developing agency improves quality of life, because with it we tend to feel a greater sense of control and become more successful at setting and achieving goals (no specific study is named) [msg #212].

Hall's argument and rules (quoted in the post):
1. **More hours is not more productivity.** It took her almost 40 years to learn this, because instinct said more hours means more productivity — "just multiplication". It isn't: overwork, even if it gives good short-term results, kills creativity and the ability to think big [msg #212].
2. **Burnout is the main killer of agency — use lost agency as an early warning.** She learned to recognise the first signs of burnout by a drop in her agency: a "switch" clicks in her head and she realises some ideas or actions should be dropped as not working or not needed, rather than pushed to completion [msg #212].
3. **Set boundaries that once seemed shameful.** Almost every day she disconnects from the internet at 18:00; she strictly observes a Sunday sabbath where nothing with even "a taste of effort" is allowed. To some this is trivial; in some communities she belongs to, it is a "mortal sin" [msg #212].
4. **Never take advice on how much to work from someone who has never burned out.** Very few people take this as seriously, she says [msg #212].

### Misery tax (налог на страдания) and how to count it
Definition: "Налог на страдания или Misery Tax" — how much we pay for working in a job we don't like, perhaps a well-paid one. Mechanism: in that situation we constantly need something to hold on, lift our mood and "treat ourselves". So the budget fills with expensive coffee from the café by the office, endless snacks, restaurant lunches and after-work cocktails, not to mention expensive purchases, spas and lavish holidays "as if for the last time" [msg #258].

The idea's author, essayist Thomas Bevan (quoted in the post): many things we do for pleasure are really ways to cope and endure, more like "смазку для машины, чем на бальзам для души" (grease for a machine than balm for the soul). These indulgences are harmful less because of their content than because of why we resort to them: we relieve suffering on the surface because we lack the time, inclination or even courage to tackle the problem fundamentally. "Возможно, поэтому заработная плата часто называется "компенсацией"" (Perhaps that's why wages are often called "compensation") [msg #258].

Katya's position: she is not calling for asceticism or giving up life's joys. But she recalls painfully the "bottomless pit" inside when she was burned out and worked without joy; now she gets so much pleasure from work and lifestyle that she no longer needs so many external pleasures on a constant basis. Her open question: it would be interesting to calculate how much doing disliked work really costs a person, since money is usually the main argument for staying — "Но так ли это выгодно?" (But is it really so profitable?) [msg #258].

How it is applied: a reader (author of *Эстетика Блаженства* (Aesthetics of Bliss)) calculated hers and found an exact sum. She had not known the theory and called it "налог на усталость" (fatigue tax), but the thought points the same way. Her count covered only monthly spending on coffee, taxis and food; adding holidays, expensive clothes and health makes it "even worse" [msg #259]. (!) ## Detailed insights

### Coaching case: from "8th speed or nothing" to a schedule with rest
The author of the food blog *Разогрейте духовку* (Preheat the oven) wanted to get rid of the constant feeling of barely managing everything. Though she had stopped blogging for a year and a half, the freed time went to basics: falling asleep before midnight, no more weekend work, training 5 days a week — "базовый минимум, чтобы не поехать кукухой раньше времени" (the basic minimum so as not to go crazy prematurely). She still had no time for series, books, solitary walks, museums and theatres — things she needs in order to create [msg #394].

Working with Katya on her schedule revealed [msg #394]:
1. **Two states only:** "я в Москве на 8 скорости" (I'm in Moscow at 8th speed) and "я в отпуске не делаю ничего" (I'm on holiday doing nothing) — no everyday middle ground.
2. **Core problem — inability to hold focus on what matters.** Small tasks constantly arrive that "must be solved immediately" and eat all free time, so important work (a recipe not yet shot, a table setting invented two weeks earlier) waits.
3. **Agreed fix — a rest day in everyday life,** with a solitary walk without any purpose, looking around in silence, listening to her thoughts; she says her best ideas always come this way.
4. **Meals must also go into the schedule.** Breakfast is fine, but lunch she remembers around 16:00 on the way between tasks, when any junk at hand will do instead of a balanced plate.

Her goal for the following months: gradually find a schedule in which she works without burnout and has time to rest [msg #394].

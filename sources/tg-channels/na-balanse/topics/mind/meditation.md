# Meditation and mindfulness

## What this covers

This topic gathers what the channel "На балансе" teaches about meditation and mindfulness: a guided meditation for restoring emotional energy (with its preparation instruction), Katya Strelnikova's personal experience of an 8-week happiness course measured with the PERMA test, the idea of "microdosing" mindfulness in very short doses, a meditation album recommendation, and the key ideas of Daniel Goleman and Richard Davidson's book "Altered Traits" (Russian edition: «Измененные черты характера»). According to the source, meditation matters because it changes reactions, resilience and stress recovery, and because inner habits (reactions, beliefs, defenses) are trainable just like outer behavior [msg #144, #209, #522, #638].

## Core ideas

- **Emotions are the channel through which psychic energy is lost or gained**, so emotional hygiene (clearing emotional "tails" and adding positive emotions) restores emotional energy — see *Emotional-energy meditation* [msg #144].
- **Studying is not enough — you have to practice.** Katya's main lesson from the happiness course, which she calls the most banal discovery made "for the 100,000th time" [msg #209].
- **Little but regular beats big and rare.** Even 20–60 seconds of mindful attention reduces stress and interrupts rumination; the trap is believing "if it's little, it's useless" — see *Microdosing mindfulness* [msg #522].
- **Inner habits are trainable.** Reactions, attitudes, defense mechanisms and beliefs are not a sentence or "the way I am by nature"; they are trained in a "mental gym" and become altered traits — see *Altered traits* [msg #638].
- **The goal of meditation is to make healthy states of mind into stable traits**, and its main effect, per the book, is moving closer to the best in our nature rather than better health or business productivity [msg #638].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Emotional-energy meditation — 7-step preparation instruction
Context: Katya had just started writing about four types of energy, and practices for restoring each began to come together. For the channel, her friend Dasha Metelskaya — a certified meditation and mindfulness trainer with six years of experience — recorded a video practice for restoring **emotional** energy. Dasha's framing: «Эмоции — то, благодаря чему мы можем терять или приобретать психическую энергию.» (*Emotions are what allow us to lose or gain psychic energy.*) The practice is described as emotional hygiene: «подчищает эмоциональные «хвосты» и заряжает положительными эмоциями» (*cleans up emotional "tails" and charges you with positive emotions*). The guided audio itself is in the video and has no transcribed text; only the preparation steps below are in the source. (!) INCOMPLETE: the content of the guided practice itself (what happens after step 7) is only in the video and was not available [msg #144].

Preparation, in order:
1. **Find a comfortable, quiet place.** The practice needs an undisturbed setting [msg #144].
2. **Take a comfortable position** — sitting on a chair, cross-legged, or lying down. Whatever the position, keep your back straight [msg #144].
3. **Agree with yourself that these 15 minutes are for you.** The practice lasts about 15 minutes; the explicit agreement marks this time as dedicated to yourself [msg #144].
4. **Set an intention before starting:** answer the question of why you are doing this [msg #144].
5. **Recall two situations:** one unpleasant situation whose negative charge is **no more than 6 out of 10**, and one very pleasant situation (it can be "all 11 out of 10"). The cap of 6/10 on the unpleasant one is part of the instruction; the source does not explain the reasoning, but these two situations are the material the practice works with — the "tails" to clear and the positive emotion to charge with [msg #144].
6. **Take several deeper breaths in and out, exhaling all the way** [msg #144].
7. **Begin the practice** (follow the recorded video) [msg #144].

Katya's personal context: she met Dasha in 2018 at a friendly yoga retreat in Spain; partly thanks to the meditations and yoga Dasha led there, Katya realized something was "wrong" with her, that it was time to change her life and to treat her eating disorder (РПП). She says that next to Dasha one feels as if healing, because of her bright energy [msg #144].

### Microdosing mindfulness
Purpose: a mindfulness strategy for people who say they have no time. Katya, a self-described fan of "lowering the degree" and doing everything "on minimum settings" (as she already argued for steps and workouts), took the idea from a New Scientist article about microdosing mindfulness [msg #522].

- **The barrier:** many people do not start meditation or other mindfulness practices because there is "no time". Katya's answer: we do not need hours of meditation or ten-day retreats to get an effect [msg #522].
- **The dose:** per the research Katya cites, **20–60 seconds of mindful attention — 3–6 breaths in and out, a short pause —** can already lower stress, interrupt ruminations and bring back a sense of contact with oneself [msg #522].
- **The reframe:** one minute of silence is rest, not "still too little to bother" [msg #522].
- **The trap to avoid:** as with steps and workouts, people fall into «если мало — значит бесполезно» (*if it's little, it's useless*), whereas in practice «мало, но регулярно» (*little, but regularly*) gives a much more sustainable result [msg #522].
- **Where to put the doses:** it is a working strategy for real people with jobs, children and projects — notice your breathing while the kettle boils; sit with eyes closed for a moment before replying in a chat; feel your body for a minute before moving to the next task on your to-do list [msg #522].
- **The long-run effect:** at some point you can no longer stop feeling — you cannot "unsee" it — without excess heroism and without raising the intensity where it is already off the charts [msg #522].

### Katya's four favorite short mindfulness practices
Listed by Katya as her personal micro-practices [msg #522]:
1. Deep breathing with eyes closed [msg #522].
2. Rub your palms together until they are very hot, then place them over your eyes [msg #522].
3. Shake the body (тряска тела — body shaking) [msg #522].
4. Hug yourself [msg #522].

### Altered traits — meditation as a mental gym (Goleman and Davidson)
Source: Katya's review of «Измененные черты характера» (Altered Traits) by Daniel Goleman and Richard Davidson, presented as the next book in her "easy way to start" series (after easy ways to start running and moving), with the motto «То, что практикуется, будет улучшено» (*What is practiced will be improved*), plus nine quote cards from the book [msg #638].

Components of the model:
1. **Inner habits change like outer behavior.** «Наши реакции, установки, защитные механизмы, верования — не приговор и не «я такая от природы».» (*Our reactions, attitudes, defense mechanisms, beliefs are not a sentence and not "I'm like this by nature".*) They are trained in a "mental gym" [msg #638].
2. **Altered traits.** The authors call the result "altered traits" — new characteristics that emerge from practice and stay with us outside meditation. The goal is to make healthy states of mind into the main, stable traits [msg #638].
3. **Neuroplasticity is always happening, usually at random.** Card: neuroplasticity — shaping the mind through regular experience — is present in our lives unconsciously. We spend long hours studying the screens of our devices or on countless fairly pointless goals, and meanwhile our neurons diligently strengthen or weaken the corresponding areas of the brain. Such a random "mental diet" will most likely lead to equally random changes in the "muscle of our mind". Contemplative science tells us we can care for our mind more responsibly [img #639].
4. **Any meditation trains psychological fitness.** Card: just as regular workouts improve physical fitness, any type of meditation will to some degree strengthen psychological fitness [img #640].
5. **Skill follows the usual learning curve.** Card: mastering any skill requires hard work at the start and gets easier with practice [img #646].
6. **The real aim.** Card: the main impact of meditation is not improved health or business performance but coming closer to the best in our nature [img #638].

## Detailed insights

### Healthy vs unhealthy states on the map of the mind
A book card describes our psychological states as too often fluctuating within a range of compulsive desires, selfishness, laziness, anxiety and similar states (the card ends the list with «и так далее») — these are the unhealthy states on the map of the mind. Healthy states, by contrast, include equanimity (невозмутимость), mental balance (душевное равновесие), constant attentiveness (постоянная внимательность) and realistic confidence (реалистичная уверенность). Making these healthy states stable traits is the stated goal of practice [img #643, msg #638].

### Attention: noise vs signal, and the wandering mind
- Card: harmful influence spreads into our whole life. For example, the inability to filter out noise (all distractions) from signal (what you should focus on) makes it hard to identify the most important things, and so reduces our ability to remember what matters [img #641].
- Card: «Блуждающий ум - ум несчастливый.» (*A wandering mind is an unhappy mind.*) [img #644]

### Thoughts as brief moments of mind
Card: usually thoughts subjugate us — self-disgust or self-hatred leads to one set of feelings and actions, romantic fantasies to another. But with sustained mindfulness one can have a deep experience in which self-hatred and romantic thoughts become the same thing: like all other thoughts, they are only fleeting moments of mind. We do not need to run from our thoughts during the day — they are just an endless series of short clips, previews and failed takes in the theater of the mind [img #642].

### A lighter relationship to "self"
Card: although bills still have to be paid, the more lightly we relate to the "I", the less we suffer from those bills and the freer we feel. We still look for a way to pay the bills, but without the extra load of emotional baggage. The lesson: practical problems remain, but the suffering added by self-focus can be reduced [img #645].

### Beyond the individual
Card, posed as a question: what if, by changing our mind, we could improve not only our own health and well-being but also the health and well-being of people in our communities and the rest of the world? [img #647]

### Why Katya trusts the book
- **Personal effect:** for several years Katya has felt meditation's influence on her reactions, her resilience and how fast she recovers after stress; rereading the book made her want to give meditation even more time and attention [msg #638].
- **Methodological rigor:** she says the authors went to great lengths to separate real effects from wishful thinking and the Hawthorne effect, used strict standards and selected only high-quality studies — rare in a field with many beautiful claims and little real science [msg #638].
- **The authors' story:** they and their colleagues began researching contemplative practices when the scientific community regarded them with open skepticism, and literally built contemplative neuroscience with their own hands, risking their reputations. Katya's lesson: what science currently considers proven or rejected is not forever; science changes, and it is good that some people go where others do not dare [msg #638].

### What the happiness course taught Katya
- **Skepticism overcome:** she expected to know "roughly everything" on the subject — philosophers from antiquity to now, positive psychology, and Laurie Santos's course, which she had completed twice. The course still showed happiness from a different angle; she had not seen any other source dissect it so carefully from so many angles [msg #209].
- **Main lesson:** «ИЗУЧАТЬ НЕ ДОСТАТОЧНО, НАДО ПРАКТИКОВАТЬ.» (*STUDYING IS NOT ENOUGH, YOU HAVE TO PRACTICE.*) [msg #209]
- **Savoring was the practice that worked best.** Over 8 weeks she tried many practices; the one that clicked most was savoring (смакование — she calls it a dumb word but says nothing describes it better). She had heard it works but had simply never done it. She discovered there is a lot in her life she can savor that makes her happy, and that savoring is exactly the living-through and feeling of life she had asked for in request 3 — so, in her words, the circle closed. (!) INCOMPLETE: the how-to for savoring is in an external Instagram post that was not provided [msg #209].
- **Most important outcome — responsibility and agency:** the course helped her take back responsibility for her state and her agency. She began more actively doing what fills her with joy and not letting it slip by, and still practices this "seemingly simple (actually very hard)" exercise. She even took on "her own Everest": learning to eat more slowly [msg #209].
- Her summary of who the course suits: those who want to become happier, learn to enjoy what they already have and be more satisfied with themselves and life [msg #209].

### Meditation as a return to the beginning (arkhe album)
Recommending the meditation album **arkhe** by Dasha Beloglazova, Katya writes that when we return to our breath and bodily sensations, when we find time to listen to ourselves (even on the go), we return to the beginning — to the point where the inner and outer are attuned — in order to live not the life that just happened but the one you are able to build through new choices and a new attitude. She presents meditation as a way to practice self-regulation, build contact with oneself, learn to manage attention and relax [msg #482].

For 2026 she is taking up **metta-bhavana** (loving-kindness meditation) to immerse herself in love and gratitude. Her reason: one of her main realizations at the end of 2025 was that she does not know how to be content and grateful — and without that her mission (to become free, happy and prosperous in order to help others become free, happy and prosperous) cannot be fulfilled [msg #482].

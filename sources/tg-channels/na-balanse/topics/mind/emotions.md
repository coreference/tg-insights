# Emotions as energy

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's posts on emotional regulation viewed as energy management: Lisa Feldman Barrett's theory that emotions are constructed by the brain and depend on the body's resource "budget", her five ways to manage emotions, a step-by-step approach to cognitive reappraisal, Katya Strelnikova's personal daily routine and her experiment with embodiment practices, and Paul Gilbert's three emotion-regulation systems as presented by a guest psychologist. According to the source it matters because unexpressed anger, accumulated resentment and endless anxiety drain energy, and because an imbalance between the internal regulation systems — not only between work and rest — decides whether a person has the strength to live [msg #73, #591].

## Core ideas

- **Your emotional state depends on your resources.** Marina observes that the better she sleeps, the more balanced she eats and the more physically active she is, the happier and calmer she is; so emotional state — and therefore relationships with loved ones — depend on the amount of resources in body and head (see *Brain body budget*) [msg #70].
- **Emotions are constructed, so they are more controllable than thought.** Per Barrett, emotions are not predetermined reactions; the brain creates them from past experience, cultural influences and the balance of physical resources — so a person has more control over emotional reactions than was previously believed [msg #70].
- **Managing emotions means managing energy.** Katya's thesis: unexpressed anger, resentment and anxiety drain energy, and mood swings decide for you what you will do today (see *Emotions drain energy*) [msg #73].
- **Reappraisal is a trainable skill.** Cognitive reappraisal starts from awareness, needs precise naming of emotions and positive recategorization, and requires practice and support (see *Three-step approach to cognitive reappraisal*) [msg #106].
- **The body can show what the mind misses.** Embodiment practices surfaced patterns and decisions Katya had only been "chewing over" in the background (see *Embodiment self-experiment*) [msg #243].
- **Chronic tiredness comes from living only in red and blue.** Modern life alternates threat and achievement, and even rest is driven by achievement; without the green safety system the nervous system does not recover (see *Three emotion-regulation systems*) [msg #591].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Brain body budget (Lisa Feldman Barrett)
Barrett, as Marina presents her, uses a metaphor of the brain as a **financial director** who allocates resources — for example glucose, oxygen and water — that are needed for survival and well-being. She calls this the "brain body budget". "Если у вас дефицит бюджета тела, вы можете чувствовать усталость или стресс." (If you have a body budget deficit, you may feel fatigue or stress.) Marina uses it to explain her own experience: on the same situation with her child — even one that looks funny from the outside — she can react anywhere from calm and patience to irritation and rudeness, and she can snap "совершенно на пустом месте" (out of nowhere); the difference is how well-resourced her body and head are at that moment [msg #70].

### Barrett's five ways to increase the body budget and manage emotions
Purpose: what Barrett proposes to increase one's "brain body budget" and control emotions better. All five, as presented by Marina [msg #70]:
1. **Mindfulness and meditation (Осознанность и медитация).** Recognizing and acknowledging your emotions is the first step to controlling them. The point is to understand your feelings and inner state without judgment or evaluation. Mindfulness helps you stay open to your emotions and respond to them more constructively [msg #70].
2. **Cognitive reappraisal (Когнитивная переоценка).** Revising how you perceive the situation or event that triggers the emotional reaction. Instead of reacting automatically to a stimulus, you can become aware of and rethink your behavior. The reasoning: "Если наши реакции — это программный код, который записан на прошлом опыте, его можно изменить." (If our reactions are program code written on past experience, it can be changed.) The how-to is expanded in the next framework [msg #70].
3. **Maintaining a healthy lifestyle (Поддержание здорового образа жизни).** Physical health — quality nutrition, good sleep and regular exercise — has a direct influence on emotional well-being, because it gives the body exactly those resources the body budget runs on [msg #70].
4. **Stress management (Стресс-менеджмент).** Various relaxation techniques — breathing practices, meditation, yoga or deep muscle relaxation — lower the stress level and improve the ability to regulate emotions [msg #70].
5. **Seeking support (Поиск поддержки).** Talking with close friends, family or a helping professional. Understanding and support from others make the process of managing emotions more effective [msg #70].

**Where to start:** Marina's starting habit is to ask yourself at least once a day "А как я сейчас себя чувствую?" (How am I feeling right now?) and to analyze what really makes you irritable, sad or angry, and what, on the contrary, makes you happy, joyful and full of energy [msg #70].

### Three-step approach to cognitive reappraisal
Purpose: an answer to a reader's question "Как начать работать с когнитивной переоценкой и не проваливаться вновь в автоматизм?" (How do I start working with cognitive reappraisal and not fall back into automatism?). Cognitive reappraisal is defined here as the technique of changing the perception of a situation from negative to productive, recommended by Barrett to control emotions better [msg #106].
1. **Become aware first.** To reappraise something, you first need to notice it. Mindfulness practices help — for example keeping a journal and meditation. It is useful to keep returning to yourself with the question "А как я сейчас себя чувствую?" (How am I feeling right now?) [msg #106].
2. **Categorize emotions finely.** Barrett advises developing the ability to categorize emotions in a fine-grained way: instead of a generalized "я расстроен" (I'm upset), make your feelings specific — for example "я разочарован" (I'm disappointed) or "я огорчен" (I'm saddened/grieved). Expanding your emotional vocabulary helps here (the post linked an emotion-vocabulary chart image; (!) INCOMPLETE: its contents were not provided in the source) [msg #106].
3. **Recategorize in a positive key.** One of the key methods is recategorizing emotions, i.e. reappraising them positively. Example: if you feel anxiety before an important event, try to perceive it as excitement and anticipation — according to the post this reduces the negative physical sensations and improves performance [msg #106].

Supporting rule: like any skill, cognitive reappraisal needs practice. Discussing your thoughts and feelings with loved ones or a specialist gives additional support and feedback [msg #106].

### Katya's daily emotional-hygiene minimum
Context: Katya says she is genetically very emotional and impulsive ("вы бы видели мою маму" — you should see my mom); in the past, in fits of emotion, phones, plates and whatever was at hand flew into walls. Therefore she has been in therapy for about eight years and practices formal meditation and related practices — once episodically, for the last two years **daily**, "даже в самолете, даже после операции на ногу, даже если не смогла спать" (even on a plane, even after leg surgery, even if I couldn't sleep). Her reason for not skipping: if she gives in to the inner voice saying "и так тяжело, можно без этой тягомотины" (it's hard enough already, I can skip this drag), it will be even worse [msg #73].

Her daily minimum [msg #73]:
1. **20 minutes of breathing practices right after waking up.**
2. **10 minutes of Non Sleep Deep Rest practice an hour after lunch** — a way to recharge energy in the middle of the day (she promised to describe it separately; (!) INCOMPLETE: the NSDR method itself is not explained in this post).
3. **Mindful routines** — brushing teeth, exercise, dry brushing, shower, face massage, eating without the phone, done with concentration on the body and on the actions; she tries to fit all her routines into this format.

### Three emotion-regulation systems (Paul Gilbert)
Source: a guest text by psychologist-philosopher Katya of the Balabanova channel, presented by Katya Strelnikova as "a balance you have not thought about". Usually balance is understood as "outside" — between work and rest, family and career, self and others. The alternative view: the problem may be "inside" — the three emotion-regulation systems, which determine how we feel and whether we have strength to live, may be out of balance [msg #591].

Basis: "Мы млекопитающие." (We are mammals.) Our nervous system still lives by evolutionary rules formed long before offices, deadlines and social networks. British psychologist Paul Gilbert described three main emotion-regulation systems everyone has; for convenience they are colored [msg #591]:
1. **Red — the threat system.** Anxiety, mobilization, defense. In the lioness image: a lioness living her best life notices strange lions on the pride's territory; the threat system activates — a surge of adrenaline and cortisol, rage, defense of the territory. She drives them away; her action is driven by the motivation to avoid harm [msg #591].
2. **Blue — the drive system.** Interest, seeking resources, achievement. The lioness needs to feed herself and her cubs, goes hunting and kills an antelope. Focus on getting the resource, activity, concentration; lots of dopamine and energy directed at the result [msg #591].
3. **Green — the safety system.** Calm, reliability, recovery. After the whole family eats the antelope, the lioness lies down to digest: the "rest and digest" state switches on. She may lick her cub, snuggle up to her lion, simply bask in the sun. This system has much softness, calm and contact; wounds heal, tissues regenerate, immunity normalizes, the hormonal system is regulated [msg #591].

The story is framed as "типичный день одной львицы" (a typical day of one lioness) [msg #591].

**Diagnosis of modern fatigue:** for many modern people life has turned into alternating the red and blue systems — anxiety and achievement. Even moments of rest are often driven by achievement motives. The problem is that the nervous system does not recover that way [msg #591].

**Three-legged stool image (образ трёхногой табуретки):** the guest likes this image — just as a stool works only when it has all three legs, each system evolutionarily solves its own life task, and without full rest (the green leg) the system will always be unstable [msg #591].

### Three first steps to activate the green (safety) system
The guest says it is quite possible to learn to regularly give your nervous system the needed signals of safety and calm. Where to start [msg #591]:
1. **Rhythmic calming breathing** — for example inhale-exhale on a count of 5, or "2-7-8" with a breath hold in the middle [msg #591]. ((!) UNCLEAR: the source writes "2-7-8"; the exact phase assignment of each count is not specified beyond the hold being in the middle.)
2. **Gentle self-touch** — a palm on the chest, at the heart, is said to activate the parasympathetic system especially well [msg #591].
3. **Imagining a safe place or a safe color** and staying there for a while [msg #591].

Katya Strelnikova adds that this is a skill requiring respect and practice; the guest's channel has more concrete actions for moving from one system to another, including how to stop the "inner race" and how to use music for it ("мы уже все неосознанно это делаем" — we all already do this unconsciously) (!) ## Detailed insights

### Emotions drain energy
Katya has always noticed how unexpressed anger, accumulated resentment and endless anxiety suck out energy, and how mood swings, instead of her, decide what she will do today. This never suited her: "не люблю я быть зависимой" (I don't like being dependent). Her post was written to expand Marina's Barrett post on the link between emotions and energy, share her experience and give advice on how to approach one's own emotions [msg #73].

### Emotional stability and energy — research the author cites
Katya cites that as early as 1976 researchers found a correlation between initiative, persistence and the ability to energize oneself and others on the one hand and emotional stability on the other; she says this has since been confirmed repeatedly. For example, a 2019 study showed a positive influence of emotional stability on stress, which she says is directly connected with the ability to manage energy levels and mental health [msg #73].

### Embodiment self-experiment
Katya had long wanted to try embodiment practices and joined a course of body practices called "Emotional Balance" from the Deep Mind team. Her request was "увидеть то, что я упускаю" (to see what I'm missing): despite all her existing practices she felt that her lens had blurred and there was a block in understanding herself, and "asking the body" — which she had never done — seemed sensible. The course creators say it can complement and amplify existing practices. Her written intention: she felt she had hit a wall, wanted to look at herself from the outside, see the patterns that keep her from moving forward, reach clarity and learn something new about herself to help move toward goals [msg #243].

She had no great expectations, so the results surprised her. Within about a month: she returned to therapy, which had been paused since summer; understood what she wants to dedicate the next year to; admitted to herself that she can't do it alone; and also started coaching [msg #243].

Her journal notes from the practices — which she says may sound banal but were transformative because she had never admitted them to herself and had only been endlessly mulling them in the background [msg #243]:
- **Money (25.11):** she felt she doesn't understand how to earn more money, has no strategy and no resource/time/perseverance to build one; she needs help and has to start investing in it.
- **Fear of being used (12.11):** her hands were cold and warmed up midway through the practice. She noticed that situations where she "могут обмануть" (may be deceived) trouble her, and that both situations were about her expectations — she fears so much that "меня используют" (I'll be used) that she doesn't see the benefits; yet it was genuinely great to meet someone and simply help, and in fact she wants people to help her just like that too.
- **Busy without fulfilment (18.11):** she caught herself not having felt a sense of duty fulfilled in the evenings for a long time, even though she is spinning like a squirrel in a wheel — so she's probably doing something wrong; a good request to work through.
- **Anxiety about others' resentment (11.11):** calmer afterwards. At the start, thoughts about a person named Ira and anxiety came again (she is not sure what she is anxious about); she recognized a trait from childhood — she feels bad when someone is offended at her, and used to consider being able to apologize even when not guilty a superpower; whether that is true needs exploring.
- **Fear of "being clever" (8.11):** she recalled her mother saying "не умничай" (don't be a smart-aleck), the skepticism of "Робо" (Robo; (!) UNCLEAR: the source does not say who Robo is) when she "разглагольствую" (holds forth), and the feeling from her father's lengthy reasoning ("если такой умный, почему такой бедный" — if you're so smart, why are you so poor); this made it clear why she shies away from experts and "talking heads".

Other results she lists [msg #243]:
- tried embodiment practices;
- returned to journaling;
- became more attentive to her emotions and states (more often hears a formed inner voice);
- started an evening practice to switch into "in bed" mode — a long-postponed project she couldn't take on for a couple of years;
- finally started doing shaking daily — 2 minutes now consistently in her morning exercise;
- started trying practices during the day instead of switching between tasks using the phone.

Her conclusion: "И это я только пока лапкой потрогала." (And I've only touched it with a paw so far.) The body is still a half-closed book for her, she has no illusions about that, and she plans to continue the course [msg #243].

# Psychiatric medication: ADHD drugs and antidepressants

## What this covers

This topic gathers what the channel says about psychiatric medication: drug treatment for adult ADHD (atomoxetine and stimulants), a complete atomoxetine memo forwarded from the Psycho_pharmacologist channel, the problem that effective ADHD drugs are not sold in Russia, why ADHD medication is worth trying at least once, what to do when treating ADHD worsens autistic symptoms, why the wish to lower an antidepressant dose is usually stigma, how to taper an antidepressant without withdrawal, and the author's own experience of finding the right antidepressant. It also includes a Q&A session (#58) about ASD/ADHD diagnosis and support that opens with the medication question. According to the source, it matters because untreated ADHD can cost people work, relationships and even lives, and because ADHD medication "может очень (ОЧЕНЬ) сильно облегчиться" life [msg #58, #208, #615].

## Core ideas

- **Adults with ADHD do have drug options:** atomoxetine and stimulants — the latter allowed only in some countries. The author's position: people with ADHD need medication [msg #58].
- **ADHD medication should be tried at least once.** It may not suit, may not help, or side effects may outweigh benefits — then it is dropped — but the potential relief is very large (see *Three answers on whether ADHD medication is needed*) [msg #615, #696].
- **There are no pills for autism.** Medication targets ADHD; antidepressants are indicated only if there is depression or anxiety [msg #696].
- **In autism + ADHD, ADHD is treated first**, because it is considered the complicating factor; worsening autistic symptoms during ADHD treatment are a common problem to be lived through carefully rather than a reason to stop (see *Treatment order in autism + ADHD*) [msg #692].
- **Wanting to reduce a working psychiatric dose is "pure" stigma** — in the rest of medicine you take the dose that works (see *Self-check for medication stigma*) [msg #78].
- **Antidepressant tapering is paced only by the individual's reaction** — if half-tablet steps cause withdrawal, they are too fast for that person (see *Individual antidepressant taper*) [msg #853].
- **In Russia, ADHD treatment suffers from two problems:** outdated doctors who misdiagnose ADHD as bipolar II or BPD, and the absence of effective drugs such as bupropion and methylphenidate (forwarded opinion from NO STIGMA) [msg #208].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Self-check for medication stigma
**Purpose:** a "life hack" the Psycho_pharmacologist author offers to clients who ask "может мне стоит снизить дозу антидепрессантов, мне же хорошо?" ("maybe I should lower my antidepressant dose, I'm feeling fine?") [msg #78].

**The base principle:** "в медицине необходимо принимать ту дозу, которая работает" ("in medicine you need to take the dose that works") — this applies to all of medicine, not only psychiatry. Because psychiatry is stigmatized, psychiatrists get these questions far more often (the author admits she never worked in somatic medicine and does not know how often somatic doctors hear them) [msg #78].

**Argument 1 — analogies with somatic medicine:**
- *Thyroid:* a person with reduced thyroid function still has problems on a dose of 50 and the problems go away on 100. Does it make sense to go back to 50 once 100 solved the problems? [msg #78]
- *Antibiotics:* the prescription is 1000 for 7 days; after 3 days you feel better — would you cut it to 500 or even 250? [msg #78]
These feel illogical, yet in psychiatry the same reasoning seems "logical" — "Да, вот так и выглядит стигматизация области" ("Yes, this is what the stigmatization of the field looks like") [msg #78].

**Argument 2 — the "liver" objection, answered with paracetamol:** the usual justification for lowering a dose is "воздействием на печень" ("the effect on the liver"). Paracetamol is one of the drugs that affects the liver most strongly and can cause liver dysfunction when overused. The maximum daily dose for an adult is 4 g; one sachet of Fervex contains 500 mg, so it takes 8 sachets a day to reach the maximum — which does not look frightening to most people. Yet even a couple of sachets of Fervex with paracetamol make the liver work harder than taking an SSRI such as sertraline [msg #78].

**Conclusion and the test:** "желание снизить психиатрические препараты является чистой воды результатом стигматизации" ("the desire to reduce psychiatric drugs is purely a result of stigmatization"). The self-test: ask yourself "А если бы это был препарат от какого-то физического недуга, я бы стремился его снизить/отменить/не пить вообще и тп" ("If this were a drug for some physical ailment, would I try to lower it / stop it / not take it at all, or the like?"). If the answer is NO, the urge is stigma [msg #78].

### Atomoxetine prescriber memo
A memo made by the Psycho_pharmacologist author from *Stahl's Essential Psychopharmacology. Prescriber's Guide (7th edition)*, published as four image cards [img #90, #91, #92, #93].

**Drug class:** selective norepinephrine (noradrenaline) reuptake inhibitor [img #90].

**Indications:**
1. ADHD in adults and children from 6 years old (FDA-approved) [img #90].
2. Treatment-resistant depression [img #90].

**Mechanism of action:**
- Raises the level of the neurotransmitter norepinephrine, and may also increase dopamine in the prefrontal cortex [img #90].
- Blocks norepinephrine reuptake pumps (norepinephrine transporters), presumably increasing noradrenergic neurotransmission [img #90].
- Because in the frontal cortex dopamine is inactivated by norepinephrine reuptake (dopamine transporters are mostly absent there), atomoxetine can also boost dopamine neurotransmission in that part of the brain [img #90].

**How long it takes to work:**
- The onset of therapeutic effect in ADHD can be seen as early as the first day of taking it [img #90].
- The therapeutic effect can keep building for 8–12 weeks [img #90].
- If it does not work within 6–8 weeks, it may not work at all [img #90].

**Goal and duration of treatment:** the goal is to reduce symptoms of inattention, motor hyperactivity and/or impulsivity that disrupt social, school and/or occupational functioning. Continue treatment until all symptoms are under control or improvement is stable, then continue indefinitely, periodically reassessing whether treatment is still needed. ADHD treatment started in childhood may need to continue into adolescence and adulthood if clinical benefit is confirmed [img #90].

### Atomoxetine — what to do if it does not work
Five steps from the memo card [img #91]:
1. Consider adjusting the dose or switching to another drug [img #91].
2. Consider behavioral therapy [img #91].
3. Consider whether the patient is not taking the medication as prescribed (adherence problems) [img #91].
4. Consider evaluation for another diagnosis or a comorbid condition — for example bipolar disorder, substance misuse, or a somatic illness [img #91].
5. Some patients show a clear lack of benefit because they have bipolar disorder. They need additional assessment for bipolar disorder, discontinuation of atomoxetine, and a switch to mood stabilizers [img #91].

### Atomoxetine — partial response or comorbidity
Three scenarios from the same card [img #91]:
1. **Treatment-resistant depression together with ADHD:** try a different monotherapy for ADHD before adding an SSRI, SNRI or mirtazapine. Combine antidepressants with atomoxetine with caution, because theoretically this can activate bipolar disorder and suicidal thoughts [img #91].
2. **Co-occurring bipolar disorder:** consider mood stabilizers or atypical antipsychotics approved by the FDA for bipolar disorder [img #91].
3. **Partial response in ADHD:** atomoxetine can be combined with modafinil and methylphenidate [img #91].

### Atomoxetine — safety profile
**Warning:** not recommended for patients without a verified ADHD diagnosis [img #92].

**Monitoring:** it may be reasonable to monitor blood pressure and pulse at the start of treatment and while therapeutic doses are being increased [img #92].

**Why side effects happen:** norepinephrine rises not only in the target brain areas but also in the body and at receptors other than those producing the therapeutic effect (for example norepinephrine's action on acetylcholine release). This causes reduced appetite, increased heart rate and blood pressure, dry mouth, urinary retention and similar effects. Side effects usually appear acutely but often pass with time [img #92].

**Life-threatening or dangerous side effects (5):**
1. Rapid heartbeat and hypertension [img #92].
2. Orthostatic hypotension [img #92].
3. Severe liver damage (rare) [img #92].
4. Hypomania and, theoretically, induction of mania [img #92].
5. Activation of suicidal thoughts and behavior (rare) [img #92].

**Abuse potential:** atomoxetine does not boost dopamine activity in the brain's limbic areas, which theoretically explains why it has no abuse potential (does not form dependence) [img #92].

**Do not use (5 contraindications):**
1. If the patient is taking an MAOI [img #93].
2. If the patient has a current pheochromocytoma or a history of one [img #93].
3. If the patient has a severe cardiovascular disease that could worsen with a clinically significant rise in heart rate and blood pressure [img #93].
4. If the patient has angle-closure glaucoma [img #93].
5. If there is a proven allergy to atomoxetine [img #93].

**Pregnancy and breastfeeding:**
1. No controlled studies in pregnant women have been done; some animal studies showed adverse effects. Use in pregnancy requires weighing potential benefit to the mother against potential risk to the fetus. Women of childbearing age should usually stop atomoxetine before a planned pregnancy [img #93].
2. It is unknown whether atomoxetine passes into human breast milk, but all psychotropic substances are assumed to. The recommendation is either to stop the drug or to bottle-feed [img #93].

### Three answers on whether ADHD medication is needed
A subscriber with several diagnoses including ADHD came to the psychiatrist about the other diagnoses; she said her ADHD symptoms do not really bother her and she is fine being who she is. The psychiatrist replied that she is fine with herself but the people around her are not, so medication is needed. She asked whether psychiatry, unlike psychotherapy, touches what doesn't bother the patient, or whether this was unethical. The author calls it a great question that opens several areas at once [msg #615]:
1. **Do we always need medication for ADHD?** "Желательно да" ("Preferably yes"). It may not always suit or help, and side effects may outweigh the benefit — then of course the drugs are dropped. "Но всё таки хотя бы раз надо попробовать, потому что ваша жизнь может очень (ОЧЕНЬ) сильно облегчиться" ("But you should still try at least once, because your life can become very (VERY) much easier") [msg #615].
2. **Can we treat one condition and ignore another?** In principle yes, but neurodivergence and ADHD affect all areas of life and affect how other disorders run, so leaving ADHD untreated is unlikely to work. The author adds that the doctor's wording ("you're fine, others aren't") was not very correct [msg #615].
3. **Working with doctors:** earlier the paternalistic model dominated — the doctor is the expert and the patient "obeys". Now the partnership model is preferred: doctor and patient decide together what to do and what suits or doesn't — though the doctor naturally knows more of the nuances [msg #615].

### Treatment order in autism + ADHD
**Situation:** a subscriber writes that when she starts treating ADHD her autistic symptoms intensify, and when she stops, life overall gets worse; her doctor says pills won't help autistic rigidity, and she has no depression or anxiety [msg #692].

**The author's answer, step by step:**
1. **Know it is common:** in combined autism + ADHD this is a very widespread problem [msg #692].
2. **Treat ADHD first:** at present ADHD is considered the complicating factor in this combination, so clinicians first try to "tame" it [msg #692].
3. **Don't rush yourself:** when autistic traits intensify during ADHD treatment, observe what exactly is intensifying [msg #692].
4. **Keep the ADHD treatment and live through the period as gently as possible** — "не бросая СДВГ" ("without dropping the ADHD [treatment]"). In theory it should eventually get easier [msg #692].
The author adds that this is "наша борьба всю жизнь" ("our lifelong struggle"), speaking as someone who shares it [msg #692].

### Individual antidepressant taper
**Situation:** a subscriber took antidepressants for 4 years and is coming off them slowly and painfully. Two psychiatrists recommended fast reduction (half a tablet at a time) and she felt withdrawal; she left the second doctor and now splits the tablet into thirds herself, planning quarters, but fears her jewelry scale can't weigh such small amounts. She read that other countries reduce by 10% every 1.5–2 months, which needs liquid forms, and asked whether those exist in Russia. She is also tired of doctors telling her "СДВГ или РАС у вас быть не может, вы просто тревожная" ("you can't have ADHD or autism, you're just anxious") and prescribing anxiety drugs that leave her half-asleep for months [msg #853].

**The author's principles:**
1. **Difficulty is normal:** coming off an antidepressant can be hard for everyone — neurodivergent and neurotypical alike [msg #853].
2. **Pace by individual reaction only:** "Мы тут ориентируемся ТОЛЬКО на индивидуальную реакцию. Если вам слишком быстро по 1/2т , то значит вам слишком быстро" ("Here we go ONLY by the individual reaction. If half a tablet at a time is too fast for you, then it is too fast for you") [msg #853].
3. **Choose an individually comfortable reduction step and observe** how you respond [msg #853].
4. **Technique for small doses:** crush the tablet, buy empty capsules, weigh the powder and fill the capsules with the needed dose [msg #853].
(!) UNCLEAR: the author does not answer whether liquid forms are available in Russia, nor comment on the 10%-every-1.5–2-months schedule the subscriber read about [msg #853].

## Detailed insights

### Should I take pills? Matching medication to symptoms
A subscriber with diagnosed ADHD and (not yet diagnosed but obvious to her) autism was told by her psychologist — a general one without specialization — to try pills. She is afraid, believes her difficulties come from not having rested for several years, and fears pills will burn her out completely [msg #696]. The author's answer:
- First understand which pills, for what purpose, and for which symptoms [msg #696].
- There are no pills for autism; for ADHD it is always worth trying, because it can make life much easier [msg #696].
- If the pills are antidepressants and you have neither depression nor anxiety, there is no indication for them [msg #696].
- A psychologist cannot make decisions about medication. If the psychologist is effectively sending you to a psychiatrist, that is never superfluous — and a visit to a psychiatrist does not mean you will necessarily be prescribed drugs [msg #696].
- Burnout is quite possible, and then antidepressants clearly won't help — but first you need to figure out what is actually happening to you [msg #696].

### ADHD treatment problems in Russia (forwarded from NO STIGMA)
The forwarded post names two key current problems [msg #208]:
1. **Insufficient qualification and outdated knowledge of doctors:** many doctors take in new knowledge about ADHD pharmacotherapy poorly and often diagnose other disorders — bipolar disorder type II and borderline personality disorder — instead of ADHD. This leads to overdiagnosis and wrong treatment, which worsens patients' condition [msg #208].
2. **No available medication:** effective ADHD drugs such as bupropion and methylphenidate are not permitted for sale in Russia. As a result many people suffer, lose jobs and relationships, and sometimes even die by suicide because of untreated ADHD. Instead of solving this, the post says, doctors focus on creating new courses and renaming the disorder to profit from a supposed "rethinking" of the diagnosis — "Кринж" ("Cringe") [msg #208].

The post's position: psychiatry should focus on diagnosis and prescribing, while psychotherapists do therapy; a skew toward psychotherapy at the expense of pharmacotherapy and correct diagnosis makes things worse. Its three conclusions [msg #208]:
- Doctors should keep their knowledge up to date and diagnose more accurately [msg #208].
- Effective ADHD drugs should be legalized [msg #208].
- Each specialist should do their own job: psychiatrists — medication and diagnosis; psychotherapists — psychotherapy [msg #208].
It claims these measures would substantially improve the quality of life of people with ADHD [msg #208].

### Q&A on autism/ADHD diagnosis and support (Part 2 of a Q&A)
The author's answers to subscriber questions [msg #58]:
- **Medication for adult ADHD:** yes — atomoxetine preparations and stimulants (stimulants are allowed only in some countries) [msg #58].
- **Working with a psychologist when you have ASD/ADHD:** the author states a "достаточно жесткое мнение" ("fairly tough opinion"): (a) psychologists must be good at diagnosis; (b) they must understand ASD/ADHD, because that lets therapy be adapted to your traits. So it is best to look for a CBT psychologist who works with neurodivergence [msg #58].
- **Differentiating complex PTSD + skill deficits from ADHD:** a big, complex question needing careful differential diagnosis, especially when they co-occur. Unfortunately the probability of trauma is high in neurodivergent people, and even higher in women [msg #58].
- **Should all clients be screened for ASD and ADHD, even socially adapted ones? Is medication needed in adulthood?** For ADHD — no; for ASD it is best done preventively, because masking can be very deceptive about how socially adapted someone is. Medication is needed by people with ADHD [msg #58].
- **Can an autistic person be disorganized, or is that ADHD?** Both. Autistic people do like rules and organization, but with executive dysfunction, or if they have no rule about order, there can be disorganization; it can also be an ADHD manifestation [msg #58].
- **Literature for a 21-year-old newly diagnosed with ASD:** *Полное руководство по синдрому Аспергера* (The Complete Guide to Asperger's Syndrome), which the author calls the most adequate on the Russian-language market so far [msg #58].
- **How to read books with ADHD:** "Вопрос - боль)" ("The question is a pain"). Little by little — plan it and read 1–2 pages at a time without forcing yourself; and when it's interesting, you can read in hyperfocus [msg #58].
- **Is it primarily a social problem (e.g., women face very high demands and are criticized even when they mention their diagnosis)?** There is a social part, but it is unlikely to be the main one, since difficulties show up in families and in people without high demands; the modern social context often aggravates the problem [msg #58].
- **Differential diagnosis of ADHD + ASD vs bipolar disorder:** you must distinguish depressive and manic phases from periods of hyperactivity / special interests and from exhaustion in neurodivergent people [msg #58].
- **Is difficulty starting or finishing tasks a reason to suspect a diagnosis?** You can always get checked — it won't hurt — but it is only one manifestation and may be unrelated to ASD or ADHD; depending on the cause there are different methods of self-help with procrastination [msg #58].
- **Age of diagnosis:** ASD can be diagnosed from 18 months (1.5 years), ADHD from 3 years [msg #58].

### The author's own experience: the right antidepressant
Asked whether she feels satisfied with her life and how she copes with her neurodivergence, the author answers honestly [msg #854]:
- All her life she has felt that just living requires constant, unrealistic effort. She finished medical school under an unreal level of stress, with no understanding of her condition at the time [msg #854].
- Her first psychologist, whom she saw in her 6th year, helped her a lot, especially with anxiety, but could not understand this and told her not to dramatize [msg #854].
- In the 10 years since, her life has improved greatly and she has learned to feel satisfaction with some aspects of it. "Сильно облегчил жизнь подходящий антидепрессант и отсутствие регулярных депрессий" ("A suitable antidepressant and the absence of regular depressions made life much easier") [msg #854].
- The next important step was understanding her neurodivergence, accepting her traits and adapting to some of them [msg #854].
- Life is still hard: work is hard at times, working the way she likes and is used to has been very hard lately, food annoys her, and socializing outside work is extremely difficult. Would she trade her life for another? "Не уверена)" ("Not sure") [msg #854].
Lesson carried by the story: the right medication and understanding one's neurodivergence improved life substantially but did not remove the difficulty; and a helper who doesn't understand neurodivergence can still help with some things (anxiety) while dismissing the core struggle [msg #854].

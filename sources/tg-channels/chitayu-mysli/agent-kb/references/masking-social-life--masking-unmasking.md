# Masking, compensation and unmasking

## What this covers

How neurodivergent people (ADHD, autism spectrum — РАС) learn masking, skills and adaptation while growing up, why that adaptation costs a great deal of energy and breaks down (decompensation) with age, load and illness, and a published model of what motivates masking, what forms it takes and what it costs. It also covers how the childhood instruction to endure (терпи) becomes a pillar of masking that hides one's own sensory profile, and what unmasking looks like in concrete, everyday terms. The author's position is that masking is rather bad than good and lowers quality of life, so reducing it wherever it is currently possible should be actively reinforced [msg #96, #144, #145, #277, #777].

## Core ideas

- **Adaptation is expensive.** Growing up, neurodivergent people learn masking, skills and adaptation, but this takes an enormous amount of energy, especially if they ignore their traits and do not rest enough; see *Decompensation mechanism* [msg #96].
- **Symptoms "worsening" usually means compensation is failing.** When all neurodivergent manifestations intensify, look for what changed in load or health; age 30+ makes compensation harder if one neglects special needs [msg #96].
- **Masking is more harmful than helpful.** It has identifiable motives, forms and consequences (energy depletion, anxiety, loss of self, others' stereotypes); see *Model of masking* [msg #144, #145].
- **"Endure it" is a pillar of masking.** Children with sensory sensitivity who were taught to tolerate discomfort grow into adults who no longer notice what overloads them [msg #277].
- **Less masking, less fatigue.** In a safe, familiar setting where masking can be "turned down to the minimum", even a large gathering can be not tiring but warm [msg #777].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Decompensation mechanism (why symptoms seem to appear or worsen later)
Purpose: answers a question patients often bring to the author — "Почему раньше я так не ощущал симптомы СДВГ/РАС?" (Why didn't I feel ADHD/ASD symptoms like this before?). Typical versions: "at university I was distracted, but not this much, and now I can't concentrate at all", or "my sleep used to be rock-solid, and now I can't stop my hyperactivity if I've worked to the limit" [msg #96].
1. **Learning to compensate.** As they grow up, neurodivergent people learn masking, skills and general adaptation [msg #96].
2. **The cost.** This requires a huge amount of energy, especially if one does not take one's traits into account and does not give oneself enough rest [msg #96].
3. **Youth hides the cost.** At a young age most people have more strength and bodily resources, so adaptation comes much easier [msg #96].
4. **Capacity falls.** With age, and with growing load, more complex social situations and physical illnesses, the organism's ability to compensate decreases [msg #96].
5. **Decompensation.** As a result, all symptoms become sharper; the author calls this decompensation (декомпенсация) [msg #96].
6. **What to do.** If you feel all your neurodivergent manifestations have intensified, look for what changed in your load or your health. And age 30+ — "как бы мы не шутили" (however much we joke about it) — is when, if you pay little attention to your special needs, the organism finds it hard to compensate well [msg #96].

Author's own example: against a background of heavy load, including mental load, not only her ADHD but all her autistic symptoms — which she normally notices much less — flared up: she could hardly be outside without noise-cancelling headphones, was enraged by overhead light at home, and was exhausted by any dog's yapping [msg #96].

### Model of masking: motivation → forms → consequences
Source: a diagram the author translated into Russian and presented at a conference talk on how masking forms, which models exist and why masking is rather bad than good; credited to "Putting on My Best Normal: Social Camouflaging in Adults with Autism Spectrum Conditions", August 2017, Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders 47(4). The author calls it "прекрасная модель маскинга и его последствий" (a wonderful model of masking and its consequences) that visually shows the causes leading to masking, masking itself and its consequences [msg #144, #145, img #145]. The diagram gives only labels; the explanations below stay at the level of those labels.

**A. Motivation for masking (Мотивация для маскинга)** — two clusters feeding into masking [img #145]:
1. **Assimilation (Ассимиляция)**, fed by:
   - *Действующий член общества* — being a functioning member of society;
   - *Вопросы безопасности* — safety concerns;
   - *Достаточная норма* — being "normal enough".
2. **Being in society and being accepted (Быть в обществе и быть принятым)**, fed by:
   - *Принятие, отсутствие отвержения* — acceptance, absence of rejection;
   - *Снижение стресса* — stress reduction.

**B. What masking is (Что такое маскинг?)** — two forms branching from masking [img #145]:
3. **Compensation (Компенсация)**:
   - *Правильные виды выражений* — using the "correct" kinds of expressions;
   - *Планирование каждого разговора* — planning every conversation.
4. **Camouflage (Маскировка)**:
   - *Избегание быть слишком аутичным* (label shown with the last two words in guillemets) — avoiding being too autistic;
   - *Играть соответствующую роль* — playing the appropriate role.

**C. Consequences (Последствия)** — three outcomes [img #145]:
5. **Other people's stereotypes (Стереотипы у других людей)**: *Ты не можешь быть аутистом* (you can't be autistic) and *Слишком хорошо скрываются трудности* (difficulties are hidden too well).
6. **I'm falling apart (Я разваливаюсь на части; split across lines in the diagram)**: *Истощение энергии* (energy depletion) and *Тревога сделать что-то неправильно* (anxiety about doing something wrong).
7. **Not my true self (Я не мое истинное я)**: *Кто же я?* (who am I, then?), *Обман людей* (deceiving people), and *Предательство себя* (self-betrayal).

Author's takeaway from preparing the talk: she re-thought how much masking can lower quality of life and concluded it is very important to reinforce (подкреплять) the reduction of masking wherever it can be removed at the present moment [msg #144].

### Learning your sensory profile after "endure it" upbringing
Purpose: for adults (especially autistic) who were trained in childhood to tolerate sensory discomfort and now live in diffuse, unexplained discomfort and fatigue [msg #277].
1. **Understand the origin.** Children with sensory sensitivity — especially those who grew up in the scarcity of the 1990s — were often taught to "endure" and tolerate discomfort. The author grants that the skill of tolerating discomfort is extremely important ("а то мы превращаемся в снежинок" — otherwise we turn into snowflakes), but says it does not work with sensory sensitivity. Such children, most often autistic, learn not to show that they dislike something or are uncomfortable — until a meltdown or shutdown [msg #277].
2. **Recognize the adult result.** Later, when nobody says "ешь что дали" (eat what you're given) or pulls a scratchy sweater onto you anymore, these adults no longer even think about what brings discomfort and overload into their lives — often because life as a whole is one big discomfort, plus a constant tiredness "for some reason" [msg #277].
3. **Study yourself gradually.** One needs to learn, little by little, to explore oneself and one's sensory profile [msg #277].
4. **Permit accommodations.** Examples given: refuse food that is unpleasant in texture, choose comfortable clothing, wear sunglasses even indoors [msg #277].
5. **Expect discoveries.** Once sensations are no longer suppressed, specific sources become visible; before, "все сливается в один общий" (everything merges into one general "painful-uncomfortable" stream of stimuli) [msg #277].
6. **Know the diagnosis.** This is why it matters for people with level 1 support needs, and even those in the broader phenotype, to understand and know their diagnosis: then you start to understand what to do with yourself — and to study your sensory profile [msg #277].

Author's example: after realizing she is on the spectrum, she very cautiously began allowing herself such life hacks. She found that walking in winter is more pleasant in sunglasses, and that towels irritate her, so she switched to a waffle bathrobe. She now knows that sun getting into her eye is physically painful and that loud sounds which don't bother others genuinely hurt her ears. Were these there before? Yes — but when you endure and don't pay attention, you don't know it [msg #277].

## Detailed insights

### What unmasking looks like in practice
- At the conference where she spoke about masking, the author's "unmasking for today" was sitting in noise-cancelling headphones because another speaker was presenting very loudly [msg #144].
- On a trip to Sochi for her sister's birthday — 10 people and 2 infants — she was surprised to feel almost no fatigue and instead warmth and a sense of home comfort, which is very unusual for her in large groups. Her tentative conclusion ("Пока я пришла к выводу" — so far I've concluded) is that this resulted from reduced masking and reduced anxiety about being in company and having to adjust to others [msg #777].
- Concretely she: left when she needed to; stayed silent at the table if she didn't want to talk; spent the whole drive to Abkhazia in the car in headphones. Her sister later called her and her sister's husband (who is "the same") "asocial", but lovingly and without hostility [msg #777].
- Lesson: in a setting where you can turn your masking down to the minimum, there is much less fatigue. Her caveat: the whole group was people she knew. She also half-jokingly adds that the golden autumn scenery may have helped — she was constantly looking at the beauty around and not noticing people [msg #777].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The author affirms that tolerating discomfort is an important skill in general; her claim is only that it does not work for sensory sensitivity [msg #277].
- The Sochi conclusion is explicitly provisional ("Пока") and confounded: the company was all familiar, and the scenery may have drawn her attention away from people [msg #777].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: the #145 model is a diagram of labels only; the source does not explain each node (e.g. what "safety concerns" concretely involve) beyond the labels [msg #145].
- (!) UNCLEAR: the post's banner image only shows the festival name (#ЛюдиКакЛюди, autism-awareness week); the talk's content beyond the model is not given [msg #144].
- Term overlap: "компенсация" in #96 means the organism's ability to cover for traits; in the #145 model it is a specific form of masking [msg #96, #145].

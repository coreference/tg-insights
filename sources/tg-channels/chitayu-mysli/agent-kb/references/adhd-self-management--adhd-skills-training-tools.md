# ADHD skills training, coaching and organization tools

## What this covers

This topic covers the practical help formats for adult ADHD the author works with: the group CBT skills training (Young-Bramham protocol) she runs with Maria Novikova, how it is organized, what the pre-group interview is for, and why there are no combined ASD+ADHD groups; ADHD coaching as a separate format; everyday organization tools (capture lists, paper "genius ideas" sheet, a planner and the Singularity app); her own route back to reading; and her repeated message that ADHD skills fade and must be relearned. According to the author, generic time-management methods fail people with ADHD because they are used piecemeal and not adapted to neurodivergence, so the point of training is to build skills into a personal system and to keep returning to them [msg #840, #675].

## Core ideas

- **Capture everything outside your head.** With ADHD, ideas come "on the go" and are then forgotten; a phone to-do list that is always with you plus a paper sheet for ideas during work reduce forgetting (see *Two-tool capture system*) [msg #220].
- **There is no universal planning system.** What matters is finding what fits you; the one firm rule is that calendar and to-do list are always with you [msg #252].
- **Skills training builds a system, not a collection of tricks.** Methods fail when used separately and unadapted; the training teaches how to assemble skills into one individual system, plus peer exchange of life hacks [msg #840].
- **The training format itself teaches time sense.** No recordings are made, because a person with ADHD given a recording will not watch it; live attendance is part of the method [msg #570].
- **ADHD skills are not installed for life.** They depend on context, fall away after illness, moves or emotional swings, and must be relearned — even by the author, who teaches them constantly [msg #675, #694].
- **Groups need homogeneous deficits.** ADHD is homogeneous enough for a single protocol; ASD is not, and ASD+ADHD even less so, so no combined groups exist [msg #803].
- **Skills without changing the environment rarely help.** If the environment exhausts you, knowing skills alone is unlikely to be enough [msg #700].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Two-tool capture system
Purpose: fewer "forgettings" and a way to organize life with ADHD. The author introduces it by describing the typical ADHD cycle around writing posts: you come up with a great topic on the go, then naturally forget it, then remember it but are too lazy to write, then start suffering and postpone even more — "стандартная СДВГшная жизнь" (*standard ADHD life*). She notes that she intended to write about something else entirely — itself an illustration [msg #220].

1. **A notebook or to-do list where you write everything.** Ideally, in current conditions, it should be electronic and convenient, so that when you are walking down the street and suddenly come up with a post idea or remember something important, you can quickly open the phone and immediately write it into your to-do app — or dictate it by voice [msg #220].
2. **"Листок гениальных идей" (sheet of genius ideas).** Put a single sheet of paper or a tear-off notepad next to your workplace. When your "incredibly genius ideas" come to mind while working, write them there *without getting distracted by them* and go back to work. When you finish work, look at the sheet, cross out the absolute nonsense, and move the genuinely good ideas and things you will do into the to-do list. Then return to step 1 and plan when exactly you will do that item [msg #220].

Why it works (author's logic): the paper sheet lets you park an idea without breaking focus, and the later sorting step separates impulses from real tasks before they enter the plan [msg #220].

### Five ways back to reading with ADHD
Context: the author and friends discussed what to do about compulsive phone scrolling ("залипанием в телефон"), which "не даёт ни отдыха, ни чувства прожитой жизни" (*gives neither rest nor a sense of a life lived*). Reading is one of her ways to cope, but "#СДВГ и чтение это так слоооожнооо" (*ADHD and reading is sooo haaard*): although books have been her special interest since childhood, in recent years she could not read at all, "прям до слёз" (*to the point of tears*). Five things helped her return to reading this year instead of scrolling [msg #289]:

1. **A small e-reader.** She loves paper books, but besides threatening to push her out of her home, they are hard and heavy to carry. A small e-reader goes anywhere, even on walks with the dogs. Pitfall: reading on a phone or tablet is "провальная история" (*a failed story*) — there are a million options to get distracted by everything else on the device [msg #289].
2. **Start with something light.** She came back to reading through fantasy. Her inner critic protested ("мы в 1 классе прочитали хоббита… какое ромфентэзи в 30 😱" — *we read The Hobbit in first grade… what's with romantasy at 30*), but she reminds herself what it is for: (a) to *start* reading — which is also a skill of decomposition and reinforcement, so anything that grips you works; (b) the goal is not to scroll but to rest, and serious books often do NOT suit rest [msg #289].
3. **Audiobooks** — see *Audiobook ladder* below [msg #289].
4. **Schedule.** Allocate time in your schedule specifically for reading. It does not have to be one, two or three hours — 10 minutes, 15, half an hour. The key is to stop if hyperfocus begins [msg #289].
5. **Praise yourself** [msg #289].

### Audiobook ladder
A three-rung rule from the reading post [msg #289]:
1. "если не читается - слушай" (*if it doesn't read — listen*).
2. "если не слушается - делай с этим что-то впараллель" (*if it doesn't listen — do something in parallel with it*).
3. "если прям нужно вникнуть - читай и слушай параллельно одну и ту же книгу" (*if you really need to get into it — read and listen to the same book in parallel*).

### Structure of the 8-session ADHD skills training (Young-Bramham protocol)
The CBT skills group the author runs with Maria Novikova follows the Young-Bramham protocol [msg #758, #840]. According to the author, all current ADHD skills-training protocols look alike: 8 meetings, once a week, 2 hours each [msg #570, #599]. Content by session, as she describes it [msg #570]:
1. **Sessions 1–3: organization and planning, and working with time blindness.**
2. **Sessions 4–6: executive dysfunction** — working with emotions, inhibition skills, self-motivation and self-reinforcement.
3. **Sessions 7–8: organizing space and relapse prevention.**

Each session covers a new group of skills [msg #570].

No recordings: the author never records meetings. Reason one: it contradicts the setting of ADHD groups — "дайте человеку с СДВГ запись чего-либо и он не придет и не посмотрит" (*give a person with ADHD a recording of anything and they won't come and won't watch it*); the task of her and all ADHD trainers is to teach a sense of time and the assimilation of skills. Reason two: confidentiality [msg #570].

### Format of a single 2-hour skills-group session
Answering a reader who asked how a session runs (lecture, questions, stretch breaks), the author lists the components [msg #599, #570]:
1. **First hour — homework review.** Who succeeded at what, what did not work and why; consolidating the week's skills. Part of the discussion happens in small groups where participants themselves discuss and share successes — this helps them immediately start using skills independently [msg #599, #570].
2. **10-minute break** [msg #599].
3. **Second hour — new skills.** Mostly a lecture part, but in some sessions there are exercises to practice the skills [msg #599, #570].
4. **Then practice at home** until the next week [msg #599].
5. **Movement allowed.** Participants are always allowed to walk around and move during the training [msg #599].

### Three tasks of the pre-group interview
Source: cards forwarded from the NeuroDive channel. The word "собеседование" (interview) makes many people anxious, because it is associated with job hiring or project selection ("Я должен себя проявить, чтобы меня взяли, как на работу?" — *Must I prove myself to be taken, like for a job?*). The post sets out to explain what the interview is, why it is needed and why not to fear it [msg #758, img #758].

Definition: the interview before forming a group is a dialogue in which the trainers get to know potential participants: "Мы хотим узнать вас, выслушать жалобы и подсказать, насколько наша работа может быть вам полезна" (*We want to get to know you, hear your complaints and advise how useful our work can be for you*) [img #759].

1. **Learn needs and goals.** The trainers want to make sure the group will suit you and help solve your problems. You share your complaints, and they tell you whether the training will be useful specifically for you — so as not to waste money [img #760].
2. **Screen out contraindications.** There are reasons why a person will not be able to do the skills-training tasks needed for effective therapy, for example severe depression, the active phase of an addiction, psychosis. In that case the recommendation is first to relieve the acute state and then return to the group [img #761].
3. **Assess motivation.** The trainers need to understand how ready you are for the training, again to avoid unnecessary financial and time costs. So at the interview they explain the format of the sessions and how the process will run, and answer all questions [img #762].

Closing points: the interview "ЭТО НЕ ПОСТАНОВКА ДИАГНОЗА" (*is not diagnosis*) — it is a conversation to ensure comfortable and effective interaction for every group member. After the dialogue you can be enrolled if your difficulties match the training topic — "Даже если у вас нет подтверждённого диагноза СДВГ" (*even if you have no confirmed ADHD diagnosis*) [img #763].

### Three properties of ADHD skills
From the post "Навыки при СДВГ не вбиваются навсегда — и это нормально" (*Skills with ADHD are not hammered in forever — and that's normal*). Clients often say: "Я прошёл(а) тренинг, было лучше, но теперь всё опять развалилось. Наверное, со мной что-то не так" (*I did the training, it was better, but now everything has fallen apart again. Something must be wrong with me*), or that if skills "fall off", maybe they don't work at all. The author's "little secret": skills with ADHD (and in neurodivergent people generally) are not consolidated forever, even if they helped perfectly before [msg #675]. Skills:
1. **"живут не в голове, а в связке с контекстом"** (*live not in the head but bound to context*) — when the context changes, the skill may not fire [msg #675].
2. **"требуют переобучения после сбоев"** (*require relearning after disruptions*) [msg #675].
3. **"не закрепляются на “всю жизнь”"** (*are not fixed "for life"*), especially if you are ill, moving house or caught in emotional swings [msg #675].

What to do: it does not mean skills are useless — it means "вы живой человек с живой нервной системой, которая не всегда может удерживать структуру" (*you are a living person with a living nervous system that cannot always hold structure*). If everything that worked has "fallen off", it is not your fault and not a "breakdown", but a natural consequence of changes. Don't be ashamed or angry — just start again, and not with a big task but with a small act of self-care. Maintaining skills is a separate skill. For people with ADHD skills are "не задача, не этап, это путь" (*not a task, not a stage, but a path*) [msg #675].

## Detailed insights

### The author's own skill relapses
- After surgery, during two weeks of recovery, the author dropped out of all her habits: food, water, cleaning, self-care. Not because she "stopped wanting to live mindfully", but because the body switched to survival and the brain to "эконом-режим" (*economy mode*). Each time this happens she has to remind herself that maintaining skills is a separate skill — even though she knows the skills by heart, "хоть ночью разбуди" (*wake me at night and I'll recite them*) [msg #675].
- Responding to a reader's feeling that with ADHD and ASD you have to keep *learning* all your life — you understood, read and memorized everything, then after a while it is as if erased and you relearn — the author "comforts" readers with her own record: about 8 ADHD trainings per year for about 5 years, many ADHD clients in therapy earlier, a written book, constant articles/posts/podcasts on the topic, and doing the skills herself for about 6 years. "И ВСЕ РАВНО ОНИ ВЫПАДАЮТ И К НИМ ПОСТОЯННО НАДО ЗАНОВО ВОЗВРАЩАТЬСЯ" (*AND STILL THEY DROP OUT AND YOU CONSTANTLY HAVE TO COME BACK TO THEM*). Motto: "У нас нет цели - только путь" (*We have no goal — only the path*) [msg #694].

### Why generic methods fail and what training adds
Many people with ADHD, by adulthood, have tried many time-management variants and other common aids, especially from business culture, are quickly disappointed, and conclude "я все попробовал, но ничего не работает" (*I tried everything, but nothing works*). The author's explanation: it is not that nothing works, but that these methods are (1) usually applied piecemeal and (2) not adapted to neurodivergence, so no common framework and structure for using helpful skills forms. In the skills training they teach not only separate skills but how to build them into a single system unique to each person with ADHD, and participants constantly exchange life hacks, which many later name as extremely useful [msg #840].

### ADHD coaching
- **What it is:** the author calls coaching for people with ADHD one of the excellent and proven ways of helping. The format differs from psychotherapy and from skills training: the coach helps step by step to build the life of a person with ADHD, often acting in parallel as a buddy ("бадди"). Besides techniques and skills, you can sit down with the coach and plan your week, set priorities. The coach is in principle on call and constantly checks what you did and didn't do — "работает кнутом и пряником" (*works with stick and carrot*) [msg #559].
- **When it fits:** when a person at some point in life cannot cope with daily life and organization on their own [msg #559].
- **Why it is scarce:** it requires specific skills and deep immersion in ADHD and is a heavy load on the specialist. So ADHD coaching is in high demand worldwide, yet such specialists are very few. In the Russian-speaking space a search returns many people, but it is very hard to verify whether they really understand ADHD, have coaching education *and* specifically ADHD-coaching education, which has many particular nuances [msg #559].
- **Training is costly:** according to the author, essentially only one institute (ADDCA) teaches ADHD coaching; she and Maria Novikova have long looked at its programs, but the cost is 8 thousand dollars per level and two levels are needed. Few can afford it, so certified ADHD coaches are also expensive — "такая грустная история с коучингом СДВГ" (*such a sad story with ADHD coaching*) [msg #559].

### Why there are no ASD+ADHD skills groups
- A reader asked whether separate support groups for ASD+ADHD would appear, noting that groups and even tests exist separately for ASD and for ADHD. The author: there are no AuDHD trainings and it is not certain there ever will be. ADHD is more homogeneous, so a single protocol can be made. With ASD it is harder: even at the same support level it is extremely difficult to assemble a group with similar deficits. The ASD+ADHD combination would most likely not be very effective as a group. She leaves it open: "посмотрим, что как сложится дальше" (*we'll see how things develop*) [msg #803].
- To a mother with AuDHD struggling with sensory overload while caring for a 3-year-old (asking for help already used 100% and exhausted; several days a week together 24 hours, with meltdowns and shutdowns), the author answered that there are no protocols as such for adult ASD skills training, but she and Maria Novikova recently made an ASD self-help training for "Справиться Проще". Her caveat: if you do not change the environment that exhausts you, mere knowledge of skills is unlikely to help [msg #700].

### Planning tools
- The author shows the to-do planner she is trying (half-jokingly: "посмотрит справлюсь ли я с этим to do планером" — *let's see whether I can manage this to-do planner*). Its pages, per the photo, have sections: "ПЛАНЫ НА НЕДЕЛЮ" (*plans for the week*: important meetings and tasks for which you can specify an exact time), "ДОЛГОСРОЧНЫЕ ЦЕЛИ" (*long-term goals*: goals requiring a longer time, which the planner suggests breaking into subtasks, making them much easier to achieve) and "ТОП-З ЦЕЛИ НЕДЕЛИ" [sic, OCR] (*top-3 goals of the week*) [msg #252, img #252].
- General rule: "нет идеального и универсального планирования, тут важно найти то, что подходит именно тебе. Главное, чтобы календарь и тудушник всегда были с собой!" (*there is no ideal and universal planning; what matters is to find what suits you. The main thing is that calendar and to-do list are always with you!*) [msg #252].

## Tools and resources mentioned

- **Singularity App** — task app for phone and computer. The author strongly recommends it (unpaid, she jokes); it suited many of her clients very well because it has reminders and makes prioritization easy right away [msg #252].
- **Paper to-do planner** with weekly plans, long-term goals broken into subtasks, and weekly top-3 goals — the author is trying it; no verdict yet [msg #252, img #252].
- **Small e-reader** — recommended over phone/tablet for reading, because it is portable and lacks distractions [msg #289].
- **Audiobooks** — used per the *Audiobook ladder* [msg #289].
- **ADDCA (addca.com)** — per the author, the only institute training ADHD coaches; about $8,000 per level, two levels needed [msg #559].
- **Young-Bramham protocol skills training** — 8-session CBT group for adults with ADHD run by the author (Anastasia Afanasyeva) and Maria Novikova, with a mandatory pre-group interview [msg #758, #840].
- **ASD self-help training for "Справиться Проще"** — made by the author and Maria Novikova; suggested for adults with ASD since no formal ASD skills protocols exist [msg #700].

- The claim that "all" current ADHD skills protocols share the 8×2-hour format is the author's generalization [msg #599].
- (!) UNCLEAR: #570 says the first hour is "разбору домашнего задания и усвоению навыков за неделю" and the second "новые навыки"; #599 adds a 10-minute break and small-group discussion — consistent, but #570 does not mention the break.
- The impossibility of ASD+ADHD groups is hedged ("не факт, что когда-то будут", "скорее всего") — an expectation, not a final verdict [msg #803].

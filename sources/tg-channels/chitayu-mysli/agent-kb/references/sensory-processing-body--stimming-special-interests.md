# Stimming, special interests and repetitive behaviours

## What this covers

Three posts by the channel author on autistic repetitive behaviour and focus: what stimming is and what it is for (answering a subscriber question about whether masturbation can be a stim), how to decide whether hair pulling or skin picking is OCD or autistic stimming by looking at the behaviour's function, and what distinguishes a special interest from an ordinary hobby and from a hyperfixation. According to the author, the distinctions matter practically: stimming is a regulation tool that is costly to suppress, and for neurodivergent people a special interest is "не просто хобби, а ресурс" (not just a hobby but a resource) [msg #624, #626, #704].

## Core ideas

- **Stimming is defined by repetition, not by form.** Any repeated movement or action can be a stim — muttering sounds, rocking, fiddling with objects, even masturbating. See *Three functions of stimming* [msg #624].
- **Using stimming is usually cheaper than suppressing it.** Stimming can be controlled, but only at a high energy cost, so if it breaks no social norms and helps with stress, the author holds it is easier to use than to suppress [msg #624].
- **Hair pulling and skin picking are classified by function.** The same visible behaviour can be OCD or autistic stimming; what decides it is what the behaviour does for the person — see *Function test* [msg #626].
- **A special interest is deep, sustained and regulating**, distinct from a hobby (less emotionally charged, part of everyday routine) and from a hyperfixation (a flash, intense but unstable) [msg #704].
- **A special interest is a resource** for neurodivergent people — for recovery, meaning, connection, career and stability [msg #704].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Three functions of stimming
Purpose: to understand why a person stims and so whether the stim is serving them. The author defines stimming as "повторяющееся поведение, которое выражается в определенных движениях или действиях" (repetitive behaviour expressed in particular movements or actions) and concludes "поведение может быть любым" (the behaviour can be anything). Her examples: making sounds under one's breath ("буркать под нос какие-то звуки"), rocking, fiddling/sorting through something ("перебирать что-то"), and even masturbating — which answers the subscriber's question "Может ли быть стиммингом мастурбация?" (Can masturbation be stimming?) in the affirmative [msg #624].

Stimming has several functions:
1. **Relaxation and lowering discomfort** — "расслабление и снижение уровня дискомфорта (особенно при стрессе и напряжении)" (relaxation and reducing the level of discomfort, especially under stress and tension) [msg #624].
2. **Help with concentration** — "помощь с концентрацией внимания" (help with focusing attention) [msg #624].
3. **Getting pleasure** — "получение удовольствия" (obtaining pleasure) [msg #624].

How it feels and what to do with it: from the inside stimming is felt "как навязчивая потребность что-то сделать" (as an obsessive/insistent need to do something). It can be controlled, but this takes "очень много энергии" (a great deal of energy). Hence the author's rule: if a stim does not violate social norms (her example caveat: "не будем мастурбировать на публике" — we won't masturbate in public) and helps cope with stress, it is easier to use it than to try to suppress it [msg #624].

### Function test - OCD vs stimming for hair pulling and skin picking
Purpose: answering the question "Трихотилломанию можно рассматривать как форму стимминга при РАС ?" (Can trichotillomania be considered a form of stimming in ASD?). The author says behaviour involving pulling out hair, picking at skin and similar must be investigated carefully, because it can be either a form of OCD or stimming in ASD. The method is to establish the **function of the behaviour** ("выясняем функцию поведения"):

1. **Anxiety-only, cyclical pattern → most likely OCD.** If the behaviour happens at the peak of anxiety ("на высоте тревоги"), lowers it in the short term, and then repeats cyclically time after time, it is most likely OCD. The author hedges that it can still look like stimming, so the assessment is long and laborious ("долго и муторно обследуем" — we examine at length and tediously) [msg #626].
2. **Broader functions → most likely stimming.** If the behaviour has not only the function of relieving anxiety but also relaxation, getting pleasure and improving concentration (i.e. the functions listed in *Three functions of stimming*), it is most likely stimming [msg #626].

Caveat on the classification itself: with excoriation (skin-picking) disorder and trichotillomania "всё очень пока не очевидно" (everything is still very unclear); some researchers and scientists propose classing them as addictions, since many people experience pleasant sensations and pleasure from the behaviour [msg #626].

### Six markers of a special interest
Definition: a special interest is "не просто интерес" (not just an interest) but "интенсивный, глубокий и устойчивый фокус, который занимает большое место в жизни человека" (an intense, deep and sustained focus that takes up a large place in a person's life). It need not be lifelong, but within its period of life it is something that truly absorbs you ("прямо поглощает вас"). It can be about anything, from broad to narrow; the author's examples: dynasties of China, a specific music album, types of tea, disease classification systems, a particular character [msg #704].

How to tell it is specifically a special interest:
1. **Calm and belonging, not just joy** — the topic brings not merely happiness but a sense of peace, "«я на своём месте»" ("I am in my place") [msg #704].
2. **Depth and system** — the interest is often deeper, and organised more systematically, than other people's; one wants not just to know but to really understand/work it out ("хочется не просто знать, а разбираться") [msg #704].
3. **Need to share and organise** — a need arises to share, to talk about it, to put the knowledge in order [msg #704].
4. **Intertwined with identity** — it can become part of who the person is [msg #704].
5. **Regulation** — it often works as a way of regulating oneself: it calms, centres, stabilises [msg #704].
6. **No practical goal needed** — it does not always have a "practical" purpose; the interest exists for its own sake [msg #704].

### Special interest vs hobby vs hyperfixation
- **Ordinary hobby:** more often built into everyday life and does not always carry a strong emotional charge. A special interest is "чуть ближе к жизни" (a bit closer to life): it is not just "liked" but woven into thinking, speech and lifestyle [msg #704].
- **Hyperfixation:** often a flash — sudden, powerful, with sharpened attention, but unstable. A special interest is long-lasting and rhythmic, sometimes with breaks but with a return to it; it is about depth more than peak intensity. The author notes that often, especially in people with ASD/ADHD, a hyperfixation arises on the basis of a special interest, lasts some time and then gets replaced [msg #704].

### Five ways a special interest works as a resource
Why distinguish it at all: because for neurodivergent people a special interest is not just a hobby but a resource. It can:
1. help a person recover/restore their energy ("помогать восстанавливаться") [msg #704];
2. give a sense of meaning [msg #704];
3. be a way of communicating with others [msg #704];
4. turn into a profession or a mission [msg #704];
5. simply be "«тем, что держит»" ("the thing that holds you together / keeps you going") [msg #704].

The post was prompted by subscribers sharing their special interests the previous day, which raised the question of how hobby and hyperfixation differ from a special interest [msg #704].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The OCD-vs-stimming test gives probabilities ("скорее всего" — most likely), not a definitive rule; the author concedes OCD can still resemble stimming and requires lengthy assessment [msg #626].
- (!) UNCLEAR: the classification of trichotillomania and excoriation disorder is described as not yet settled, with some researchers proposing to treat them as addictions; the source names no specific studies [msg #626].
- The "use rather than suppress" rule is conditional: only when the stim does not break social norms and helps with stress [msg #624].
- (!) UNCLEAR: the source writes "РАССДВГ" as one word; it most plausibly means ASD/ADHD (РАС + СДВГ), but this is not stated [msg #704].

# How adult ADHD and autism are diagnosed

## What this covers

This topic gathers what the channel author, psychiatrist Anastasia Afanasyeva, teaches about how ADHD (СДВГ) and autism spectrum disorder (РАС) are diagnosed in adults (and briefly in children): what the diagnosis actually consists of (behavioural observation, detailed anamnesis, sometimes longer observation, tests as a bonus), why lab tests and imaging (EEG, MRI, genetic panels) cannot detect them, which ICD codes make an adult diagnosis official in Russia, why Russian state psychiatry often fails adults, how to pick a psychiatrist and talk to them, how AuDHD and masking hide criteria, and what diagnosis abroad is like. In her view, diagnosis matters because without it one cannot understand what is happening, build a competent treatment plan, or avoid ineffective or harmful therapy — and avoiding "labels" perpetuates stigma [msg #123, #783].

## Core ideas

- **Adult ADHD and autism are officially diagnosable in Russia.** ICD-10 contains both in the rubric of disorders that begin in childhood and persist throughout life; ICD-11 removes any age binding for autism. See *ICD codes* below [msg #124, #869].
- **Diagnosis is clinical, not instrumental.** It rests on observation, detailed anamnesis, sometimes longer observation and tests as a bonus; EEG only reliably shows epilepsy and sleep phases, MRI only structural lesions, and genetic panels give nothing usable [msg #598, #605, #606, #612].
- **Not every psychiatrist is competent.** Many are uninformed about adult ASD/ADHD (especially support level 1, masking women, broader phenotype); choose a specialist who works with neurodivergence [msg #603, #697, #746].
- **Russian state psychiatry has structural reasons for missing adult autism** — historical inertia, lack of training, bureaucratic barriers and difficulty with "mild" forms [msg #869, #59].
- **Diagnosis is not "labelling" but understanding**; neurodivergence should be checked early, because therapy that works for neurotypicals can be ineffective or worsening for neurodivergent people [msg #123, #783].
- **Comorbidity complicates the picture.** ASD and ADHD often co-occur and the combination (РАСДВГ) can mask individual criteria; diagnosis must be slow and detailed [msg #365, #612].
- **Not everyone who suspects ASD has it**, and neurodivergent people's complaints are not always caused by their neurodivergence — the diagnostic process checks this [msg #545].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Four components of diagnosing ADHD/ASD by international standards
Purpose: what a diagnosis must be based on. Afanasyeva states that "По всем международным стандартам, исследованиям и всем этим занудным вещам" (by all international standards, research and all these boring things) the following are needed [msg #605]:
1. **Observation of behaviour during the meeting** — what the clinician sees in the session itself [msg #605].
2. **Detailed collection of anamnesis** — a thorough life history [msg #605].
3. **Sometimes observation over a longer period**, especially if there are also comorbidities that need to be separated out [msg #605].
4. **Test results "as a bonus"** — supplementary, not decisive [msg #605].
She gives this in reply to someone advised to do a brain MRI: there are no analyses that indicate ADHD; MRI is "по сути 3D картинка мозга" (essentially a 3D picture of the brain) and shows nothing except cysts, tumours, cortical thinning and similar, so "на МРТ не тратьте деньги, лучше потратьте на что-то более приятное" (don't spend money on an MRI, better spend it on something more pleasant) [msg #605].

### Three data sources for ASD diagnosis (child or adult)
Answering how she diagnoses ASD in adults (a reader had seen English-language discussions about multi-day assessments, parent interviews and "explain to an alien how to brush your teeth"), Afanasyeva says that whether the patient is a child or an adult, they rely on three sources of data [msg #612]:
1. **Observation during the conversation** — how the person communicates, how facial expressions show, how far facial expression matches what the person is saying [msg #612].
2. **Detailed anamnesis** — sometimes one meeting is enough; sometimes she sends the person away to observe their state and gather information before continuing [msg #612].
3. **Tests as a bonus** — not always needed, but they can give good ground for discussion, especially when questions remain after the first meeting [msg #612].
Because ASD rarely occurs alone, when other disorders are suspected and the client requests it, she diagnoses "всего остального человека" (the whole rest of the person) too. At live psychiatric appointments she tries to quickly ask about all possible symptoms in the first meeting, though this is not always possible [msg #612].

### Diagnosing ASD and ADHD in parallel along the life line
How a doctor determines that a person has both ADHD and autism: because ADHD and ASD very often occur together, when she has suspicions and enough time she begins asking about ADHD symptoms in parallel with ASD. Sometimes, while going "по линии жизни" (along the life line, i.e. chronologically through the person's life), she asks about symptoms of both at once. The reason: the picture in РАСДВГ differs somewhat from pure ADHD or pure ASD, so it needs to be assessed as a combination [msg #612].

### Recognising AuDHD (РАСДВГ) — how the combination distorts ASD criteria
Purpose: avoiding missed ASD in people with ADHD. Given the high comorbidity of ASD and ADHD, the combination is not rare, and it especially often makes it hard to recognise ASD in someone with ADHD [msg #365]. Afanasyeva describes three criteria that get distorted:
1. **Order and systematisation.** People with ASD love order, systematisation and fixed sequences; with ADHD added, the person loves all of this but lives in chaos. The person suffers from that, and it also strongly misleads specialists, who dismiss the criterion as not met [msg #365].
2. **Novelty.** People with ASD dislike the new and unpredictable; people with ADHD are thrilled by novelty. In РАСДВГ people often like trying new things, but only in areas where they feel more confident and safe — e.g. trying new food only in a particular context (only if they cooked it themselves, or only in a certain familiar café) [msg #365].
3. **Criterion A, communication — the most misleading in her observation.** Where people with ASD tend to have a fairly limited social circle, people with РАСДВГ often have a large one and may enjoy new acquaintances; but "дьявол кроется в деталях" (the devil is in the details): difficulty getting close and maintaining closeness, understanding the other person in relationships, acquaintances that are often rather superficial, rapid fatigue from socialising [msg #365].
Rule: do not hurry; question and examine carefully exactly where things initially seem obvious. ASD looks different in everyone, and РАСДВГ can combine completely individually — for some it gives relief and partial compensation of their ASD and ADHD, for others it makes the symptoms heavier [msg #365].

### Criterion B rigid rules — what they look like in masked adults
Afanasyeva calls criterion B (rigid rules) one of the hardest criteria when diagnosing ASD in adults with support level 1, especially in women who mask [msg #288]. Mechanism: people build their routine so well, making many tiny rules, that they don't notice the rules are rigid — "Ну правильно же так, в чём проблема" (Well, that's the right way, what's the problem). It is very hard to recall these in the moment of an appointment, particularly if you don't live with people who notice these "oddities" [msg #288]. Examples she lists:
- dishes stored in a particular order (she herself is jarred if a big plate is put on a small one, or a glass is not in its place);
- getting dressed in a particular order;
- greeting every person you see;
- always reading to the end whatever you started;
- arranging objects this way and no other;
- drinking tea only from one mug and coffee only from another [msg #288].
There can be countless such small rules whose violation causes discomfort, without the person thinking about them. Key caveat: rigid means too strict — the rule holds regardless of context. Example: you are visiting someone and cannot drink coffee from the same cup you drank tea from the day before [msg #288]. Her own example: on a trip she noticed that cash must ALWAYS lie in ascending order of denomination, and if not she must rearrange it at once; and, to the point about rigidity, she recalled it had always been so, even as a child when she took money from her parents' wallets and played with it — showing that a rule's lifelong persistence is part of what makes it relevant [msg #288].

### Four reasons adult autism is "invisible" in Russian state psychiatry
Afanasyeva describes (as of 2026) the situation in Russian state medical institutions as still difficult, despite some formal changes in recent years [msg #869]:
1. **Historical inertia and the "Soviet school".** For a long time domestic psychiatry held that autism is exclusively a childhood diagnosis; at 18 it was thought either to "pass" or turn into something else. As a result, until the mid-2010s, on transfer from the children's to the adult clinic, an ASD diagnosis was almost always changed to schizophrenia or schizotypal disorder. Old-school psychiatrists sincerely believe "аутистов-взрослых не бывает" (there are no adult autistics) and classify features of social interaction as symptoms of mental illness [msg #869]. An illustration from 2023: at a conference a colleague of hers was told by an acquaintance working at a large state centre: "ну не знаю конечно, а вы что из тех, кто верит во взрослое СДВГ? У нас на кафедре конечно на это не так смотрят, и либо вялотекущую ставят после 18 лет, либо просто диагноз снимают" (well, I don't know, are you one of those who believe in adult ADHD? At our department they don't see it that way — after 18 they either diagnose sluggish [schizophrenia] or simply remove the diagnosis). Afanasyeva notes that amid the popularisation of neurodivergence she had even become shy of telling psychologists in lectures that somewhere people with ASD still get schizophrenia after 18, presenting it rather as history — "А тут вон оно как" (and here's how it is) [msg #59].
2. **Lack of specialised education.** Training for "adult" psychiatry for decades did not include in-depth study of ASD, nor ADHD. Training happened only in child psychiatry and only for support levels 2–3. So PND doctors are trained to identify acute psychoses, depression or dementia but often don't know what autism looks like in an adult, especially one with high intelligence who has learned to "mask". ASD symptoms are often mistaken for OCD, social anxiety or personality disorders [msg #869]. (She also says no one is taught about ADHD in residency — "нам даже в детском отделении не особо рассказывали" (even in the children's department they didn't tell us much) [msg #124].)
3. **Bureaucratic and legal barriers.** Although the Ministry of Health issued a letter back in 2017 on the inadmissibility of unjustified change of an "autism" diagnosis at 18, in practice the system resists and points 1–2 continue. There are still no standards of care or adequate adopted clinical guidelines. For children with ASD there are established routes (special education, tutors), but for adults there are practically no systems of "supported living" or employment in state structures. Disability for autism is extremely hard for adults to obtain: MSE experts often require a concomitant intellectual impairment to confirm status — and again all of this is only for support levels 2–3 [msg #869].
4. **Difficulty diagnosing "mild" forms.** Adults who come to the PND themselves for a diagnosis (often people with conditional Asperger syndrome) find they are "слишком нормальные" (too normal) for state psychiatry. If a person can hold a dialogue and finished school, the PND doctor may consider complaints of sensory overload or communication difficulties "причудами" (quirks) or "дурным характером" (bad character) [msg #869].

### Three benefits of early diagnosis in children
A parent sees ADHD and autistic traits in a 6-year-old and asks at what stage to see a psychiatrist. Afanasyeva says research shows that the earlier a child receives neurodivergence-related diagnoses, the [msg #842]:
1. less shame and non-acceptance of their own features will form;
2. easier it is to introduce the needed non-medication help and develop behavioural strategies;
3. lower the probability of developing co-occurring disorders in future, such as depression and anxiety disorders.
Her advice: don't postpone; show the child to a child psychiatrist [msg #842].

### Three reasons ADHD belongs to psychiatry, not neurology
A reader asks why psychiatrists rather than neurologists diagnose neurodivergence if it concerns nervous-system development. Afanasyeva calls it a reasonable question: neurology and psychiatry overlap a lot, disputes arise over who handles which disorder, so areas of responsibility are divided by the symptoms being worked with. Example: epilepsy is basically handled by neurologists, but secondary mental states within epilepsy itself by psychiatrists [msg #844]. ADHD is handled by psychiatrists because:
1. we can only minimally influence its biological basis;
2. the main diagnostic process must take the form of psychiatric anamnesis collection, since analyses show nothing;
3. diagnosis requires careful differential diagnosis specifically with other psychiatric disorders [msg #844].

### What to do when criteria seem not to be met but traits interfere with life
Case: a reader sees very many ASD (possibly РАСДВГ) traits but recalls no childhood behaviours that the diagnosis requires; the psychiatrist doesn't see it and diagnoses recurrent depression (several episodes, always amid burnout at study/work). Afanasyeva's recommendation [msg #851]:
1. **Return to the question after mood stabilises** — not during an affective episode.
2. **Get one or two second opinions.**
3. **Remember that how the anamnesis is collected matters.** We often feel we remember little, but the way questions are asked is crucial; sometimes information is collected "по крупицам" (bit by bit) by recalling how school lessons went, what one played (the source ends this list with "и т.д."), until the needed information is gathered.
So she recommends coming back and trying to figure it out after all [msg #851].

## Detailed insights

### ICD codes: adult ADHD and autism are official diagnoses in Russia
Afanasyeva constantly meets the claim that one cannot get an ADHD diagnosis in Russia, "особенно раз МКБ-11 не приняли" (especially since ICD-11 wasn't adopted). She regrets ICD-11 not being adopted "до слёз" (to tears), but says this doesn't stop clinicians knowing it and using it outside of coding [msg #124]. ICD-10 does have a code for ADHD: **F 90.0 "Нарушение активности и внимания"** (Disturbance of activity and attention). It just doesn't look like "ADHD" and sits in the "children's" rubric, which confuses and scares everyone, doctors included. It is in that rubric because the rubric contains disorders that begin in childhood and persist throughout life; the same applies to autism, and there are often small-print footnotes and ICD annexes saying so. Hence, "вот вам и диагноз #СДВГ по МКБ-10. Официально" (there you have an ADHD diagnosis under ICD-10. Officially) [msg #124].
For autism in private clinics: first, in ICD-11 autism has no age binding at all — just a single rubric; second, even in ICD-10 the rubric with the forms of autism belongs to disorders BEGINNING in childhood and PERSISTING throughout life, so clinicians have every right to diagnose adults. What matters is meeting the criteria and onset in childhood [msg #869].

### What laboratory and instrumental tests can and cannot show
- **EEG.** Although EEG is often prescribed for both ADHD and ASD, current research says EEG can reliably show only two processes: (1) epilepsy; (2) sleep phases and their disturbances (which is why EEG is part of polysomnography). So it cannot detect autism, in adults or otherwise — "ничего мы там не увидим" (we won't see anything there) [msg #598].
- **MRI** — see *Four components* above: only structural findings (cysts, tumours, cortical thinning) [msg #605].
- **Genetic tests.** Following a discussion about genes and Genotek, where she said one should expect nothing sensible from them beyond entertainment, she saw at the Gemotest lab an advertised panel. The photographed price list showed: panel "Hereditary epilepsies and seizure conditions" — NGS of 560 genes, 30 days, 34,900 ₽ (code GNP319); panel "Developmental delay and autism" — 286 genes linked with delayed psycho-speech development, intellectual disability and autism, claimed to help build a personal treatment and support plan for children, 30 days, 34,900 ₽ (code GNP321); panel "Cerebral palsy (motor development disorders)" — NGS of 945 genes, 30 days, 34,900 ₽ (GNP322); "Large neurological panel" — NGS of over 2,000 genes for hereditary nervous-system diseases, 30 days, 44,900 ₽ (GNP318) [img #606]. Her verdict: it's "грустно" (sad); the test won't give much, and at such a price; moreover it treats autism exclusively together with developmental delay and intellectual disability. She reiterates that there is currently no evidence that such tests — or tests for antidepressant response and similar — give genuinely usable information [msg #606].

### Why diagnose at all: against "don't put labels"
Afanasyeva often hears from psychologists, students and colleagues: why "label" the state at all, just help the client. She finds this approach understandable in psychology — until we are in the field of clinical disorders [msg #123]. Her supervision group's metaphor: "why treat the leg if the person limps — just help them not to limp". Her counter: diagnosis exists precisely to understand WHY the person limps, HOW LONG they have limped, and whether, because of the limp, they keep injuring the leg — falling into potholes and coming to the doctor with injuries beyond the limp itself [msg #123]. "Упрямый врач внутри меня" (the stubborn doctor inside me) holds that fear of diagnosing prevents understanding what's happening and building a competent treatment plan, and above all continues stigmatisation in mental health. Analogy: imagine a doctor told a coughing patient "don't put a label on it", just take bromhexine and do breathing exercises. In psychiatry/psychology too one must understand what works and why — to explain it to the client, write a case conceptualisation and separate which symptom comes from where [msg #123].
In 2025 she adds a further argument formulated in supervision: diagnosis of neurodivergence should be not only timely but often anticipatory, even before specific complaints (she expects accusations of overdiagnosis). When neurodivergence is not looked at first, or diagnosis is postponed while treating other conditions, the work — especially psychotherapy — is often ineffective or even worsens the state, since what works well for a neurotypical person may work poorly for a neurodivergent one. Personal example: in her first therapy, still at university, they long struggled with certain problems that would not resolve, and she despaired; once she understood what they were linked to, controlling them became much easier. Hence her rule: diagnosis of neurodivergence should come, if not first, then at least right after acute affective symptoms decrease and at the slightest hints of neurodivergence [msg #783].

### Finding a competent psychiatrist and how to present your suspicion
- To find out whether you have ASD you must see a psychiatrist "100%", but not any psychiatrist: many are uninformed about adult ASD, especially at support level 1 and/or near the broader phenotype. She points to her lists of specialists (tag #специалисты): one crowd-sourced list where doctors added themselves, and one of graduates of her and Maria Novikova's Neurodive course whom they trained themselves [msg #603].
- On "where in Moscow do they diagnose ADHD best": she doesn't know of a "best" place; in fact you just need a psychiatrist who understands adult ADHD — herself or any psychiatrist specialising in it, e.g. from her specialist table [msg #697].
- Whether to say you suspect РАСДВГ when booking or at the visit, or not say it and just describe your features: as with any doctor, you may say at the start that you suspect a condition, or just list your complaints. But since not all psychiatrists know adult forms of neurodivergence well, choose a specialist who works with this [msg #746].
- A patient registered at a PND with bipolar disorder (БАР), who suspects ADHD and is ashamed to say so because doctors roll their eyes and treat patients as hypochondriacs: "Если врач не верит во взрослое СДВГ боюсь вам ничего не поможет" (If the doctor doesn't believe in adult ADHD, I'm afraid nothing will help you). But sharing your self-observations with doctors is normal — "никто не знает вас так хорошо, как вы" (no one knows you as well as you do). If the PND doctor reacts badly, go to another doctor who works with ADHD and can perform differential diagnosis with bipolar disorder [msg #57].

### Suspected but absent: diagnosis checks alternatives
A frequent question: do people come for ASD diagnosis and turn out not to have it? Yes. At an appointment doctors look at the state and what the person reports and quickly run through what it could be linked to; since neurodivergence often resembles a hundred other conditions, diagnosis checks this. Sometimes it isn't ASD or ADHD at all; sometimes there is neurodivergence but the complaints are not caused by it [msg #545].

### Late-appearing symptoms and single signs (Q&A from DocPsyFest)
- If two thirds of symptoms appeared in adolescence and the rest after 30: most likely after 30 compensatory abilities and the body's resources decreased, so what the body used to cope with and compensate began to show [msg #57].
- Women twirling hair on a finger while talking, reading or thinking — is it ADHD or ASD? Not necessarily: disorders are not diagnosed by one sign, though it can serve as stimming [msg #57].
- Do features of ADHD in women also apply to men? Overall ADHD manifests similarly; some signs simply look different and less noticeable in women [msg #57].
- Methods to calm and focus the brain and follow a plan: a skills training for people with ADHD contains practically everything; she also hoped her book with practical advice would come out by spring [msg #57].
- Tension-type headaches (ГБН) are a real "pain" of neurodivergent people; main advice: rest more and practise muscle relaxation, especially head massage [msg #57].
- Fitting into society without suppressing yourself: find a community that accepts you as you are — search via your special interests; there are now self-support groups for neurodivergent people [msg #57].
- Asked about the pronounced positives of women with ADHD and ASD, she answers jokingly: "Жизнь - боль. Положительные стороны - мы женщины и мы классные" (Life is pain. The positives — we're women and we're cool) [msg #57].

### The СДВГ vs РДВГ naming debate
Some online voices (she links a psychologist's post) assert that СДВГ is a mistranslation and should be called РАССТРОЙСТВО (disorder) rather than СИНДРОМ (syndrome), so the abbreviation РДВГ may now appear online. After discussing with Maria Novikova whether to change it in their course, Afanasyeva — explicitly as her personal opinion, acknowledging others may differ — finds it irritating and suspects hype on a now "fashionable" diagnosis. Her arguments: (1) medicine beyond psychiatry has many "historical" names and eponyms/syndrome names that don't hinder treatment; (2) words matter, but first we should talk about normal diagnosis of ADHD and other neurodivergence, competent psychotherapy, needed medications with doses, and accounting for the female phenotype and cycle — and the official translation in ICD-11 remains СДВГ. She calls it "чистой воды демагогия" (pure demagogy), while reflecting that her autistic rigidity and attachment to the familiar term may play a role — her irritation comes from the idea that everything should be consistent and by the rules, and there was no discussion or centralisation. She says she wants to offend no one and invites opinions [msg #205].

### Diagnosis abroad and confirming a Russian diagnosis
- Afanasyeva used to wear "rose-tinted glasses" about Europe being much better and easier for these diagnoses than the CIS; after several years of close work with patients from various countries it turned out less rosy. First, everything depends on the specific country or state. Second, on the field where you study or work: studying seems easier, since there is real help — study-process support, tutors, buddies. Work differs: many fear and do not confirm their ASD in Europe because it can really harm their career. So research in advance your specific field and country before moving. Access to more ADHD medications is an undoubted plus [msg #870].
- Confirming a diagnosis obtained in Russia/CIS is fairly simple, depending on the country and availability of a psychiatrist (especially one who works with neurodivergence — same as in Russia). If you have a detailed report in the right format from a Russian doctor, stating problems, diagnosis and recommendations, in some countries it has recently become possible even via a family doctor who simply prescribes the medication per the recommendations. A couple of years ago that was hard to imagine; she supposes the increased patient flow in Europe simplified the process. Previously, in some countries one waited 4–6 years for access to an ADHD psychiatrist. Summary: foreign doctors in most cases readily accept Russian reports [msg #870].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- Afanasyeva's positions are her clinical opinions; she hedges the РДВГ critique as personal and possibly coloured by her own rigidity [msg #205], and anticipates accusations of overdiagnosis for her "anticipatory diagnosis" stance [msg #783].
- (!) CONTRADICTION (between the system and practice): a 2017 Ministry of Health letter forbids unjustified changes of autism diagnoses at 18, yet she reports the practice and the belief "no adult autistics / no adult ADHD" persist in state centres [msg #869, #59].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: the DIVA-5 questionnaire file and the stream recording are not transcribed (the audio has no text detected), so their content is not available [msg #184].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: the specialist lists under #специалисты are referenced but their contents are not in the source [msg #603, #697].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: she says what to do about suffering from chaos in РАСДВГ will be discussed separately; not covered here [msg #365].
- The experience abroad varies by country/state and field; her statements are generalisations from her patients [msg #870].

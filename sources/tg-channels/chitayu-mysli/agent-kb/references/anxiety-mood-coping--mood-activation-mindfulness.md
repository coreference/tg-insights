# Behavioral activation, movement and mindfulness for mood

## What this covers

This topic gathers the author's posts on non-drug, everyday ways to support mood: behavioral activation and walks (particularly for people with ADHD and other mental-health conditions), a personal anti-slump plan for November, the three "conditions for neuroplasticity" she presented at a talk (sleep, movement, safety), mindfulness practised in very short, enjoyable formats suited to ADHD, and research on how much of our happiness is inherited [msg #41, #166, #239, #358, #787]. The author, a psychotherapist working in CBT and ACT, presents these as things she recommends to clients and also struggles to do herself; her recurring message is that small, regular activity helps even when motivation is absent, and that people's achievable happiness differs, so comparison is pointless [msg #166, #41].

## Core ideas

- **Move even when you don't want to.** Behavioral activation and physical activity, walks above all, are "extremely important" for people with ADHD and for people with mental-health conditions in general; the author's own bad-day walk in the rain lifted her mood. Fully explained under *Behavioral activation through walks* [msg #166].
- **Any regular activity counts.** Sport, even just stretching, a walk, or sitting on a bench by the house all improve quality of life when done regularly [msg #166].
- **Seasonal slumps call for a concrete plan.** For November, the author lists vitamin D testing and supplementation, a plan of pleasant activities and returning to sport [msg #358].
- **Neuroplasticity needs sleep, movement and safety.** Sleep integrates experience, movement drives neuron growth (BDNF), and low stress means high learning capacity [msg #787].
- **Mindfulness for ADHD starts super short.** It is not about long meditations or retreats; it is about finding an enjoyable activity that keeps you in the present moment, for the author, photography [msg #239].
- **Happiness is partly heritable.** Studies she cites estimate wellbeing and life satisfaction as 30–40% heritable, with a genetic basis for one's general happiness level, so different people can reach different levels of happiness and comparing yourself with others makes no sense [msg #41].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Three-step plan for the November slump
Purpose: a self-help plan the author wrote for herself against the seasonal low she experiences every November in Moscow, and which she recommends to readers with the same problem ("Чего кстати и вам советую, если у кого-то такие же проблемы" — "Which, by the way, I advise you too, if anyone has the same problems") [msg #358].

Context: she describes November as a month when all she can do is sleep and work ("и то, потому что куда от этого денешься" — "and only because there's no getting away from it"). Posts don't get written, her book doesn't get written, she doesn't watch series or even read books, and "Только депрессия подглядывает из-за двери" ("Only depression peeks out from behind the door"). She notes this happens even though she generally likes "conditionally bad weather"; she calls November in Moscow "какой-то портал в ад" ("some kind of portal to hell") [msg #358].

The steps, in her order:
1. **Get vitamin D tested and start taking it again** ("Сходить сдать витамин D и снова начать его пить"). The word "again" shows this is something she has done before. She first gets the blood test and then resumes supplementation; the post gives no dose [msg #358].
2. **Draw up a plan of "pleasant" activities** ("Составить план "приятной" активности"). This is the behavioral-activation tool: deliberately scheduling enjoyable activities instead of waiting for the mood to produce them. The post does not list example activities [msg #358].
3. **Return to sport more actively** ("Активнее вернуться к спорту"). This links to the author's broader point that regular movement improves mood (see *Behavioral activation through walks*) [msg #358].

Honest caveat from the author: "Осталось как это начать делать 😵‍💫" ("All that's left is to start actually doing it"). Making the plan and acting on it are separate, and starting is the hard part [msg #358].

### Conditions for neuroplasticity (sleep, movement, safety)
Purpose: what the brain needs for neurons to form new connections and for new habits to form, and how to take this into account in psychotherapy. The author presented it at a talk at Mosfilm (the slide bears the event's wellness slam logo) [msg #787, img #787].

In the post she sums it up as: she had to explain "как важны сон, еда и движение, чтобы нейроны создавали новые связи и у нас формировались новые привычки" ("how important sleep, food and movement are for neurons to create new connections and for us to form new habits") [msg #787]. The slide photographed in the post shows these numbered items:

4. **Sleep: integration of experience** ("Сон - интеграция опыта"). Sleep is where what was experienced and learned gets integrated [img #787].
5. **Movement: growth of neurons (BDNF)** ("Движение - рост нейронов", followed on the slide by "(BDNF)"). Physical movement is linked to neuron growth through BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) [img #787].
6. **Safety: low stress = high learnability** ("Безопасность - низкий стресс = высокая обучаемость"). Feeling safe keeps stress low, and low stress is the state in which the brain learns best [img #787].

### Short, activity-anchored mindfulness for ADHD
Purpose: building mindfulness skills in neurodivergent people, for whom long formal meditation is a poor entry point [msg #239].

1. **Understand why mindfulness comes first.** The author defines mindfulness as "группа навыков" ("a group of skills") that neurodivergent people especially need. Neurotypical people also benefit, but when "ты плохо чувствуешь свое тело/эмоции и вообще понимаешь себя, а ещё и куча разных коморбидностей" ("you poorly sense your body/emotions and understand yourself in general, plus a heap of different comorbidities"), mindfulness skills are often one of the first things therapy starts with [msg #239].
2. **Drop the myth of long meditation.** A common misconception is that mindfulness means meditation, ideally long sessions of an hour or two, or even going away on a retreat. Her answer: "Спойлер - нет" ("Spoiler: no"). People with ADHD in particular should start with something "супер короткого" ("super short") [msg #239].
3. **Expect it to be hard, and train it constantly.** The author says these skills never come easily to her either, and they need to be practised all the time [msg #239].
4. **Anchor presence in an activity you enjoy.** "Важно найти что-то, что вам нравится как занятие, и что будет помогать вам задерживаться в текущем моменте" ("It's important to find something you like doing that will help you linger in the present moment") [msg #239].
   - Her example is photography, which she found in early adolescence when she got her first film camera. Photography lets her return to the "here and now" and stretch out the moment. While you search for a beautiful frame and think about it, "мир преображается, пока ты смотришь на него через призму красоты" ("the world is transformed while you look at it through the prism of beauty") [msg #239].
   - She mentions that she "hyperfixated" on photography so strongly that she worked as a photographer for several years. She presents this as an ADHD-style intense interest and does not tell the story here [msg #239].

## Detailed insights

### Behavioral activation through walks (ADHD and mental-health conditions)
The author's case: a month before the post, on the day after the Crocus attack, she was in an awful mood and the weather was dreadful, with everything melting unattractively and drizzle falling. Even so, she put her dog Shirik in his vest and took him to the park for a walk with a friend, and her mood "очень даже улучшилось" ("improved quite a lot"). That was in the rain, in "ugly" weather (she adds, "хотя как погода может быть некрасивой?" — "though how can weather be ugly?"), and in a bad state. She found this note when finishing an old entry in her journal [msg #166].

The lesson she draws: behavioral activation, and specifically physical activity in the form of walks, is "крайне важна" ("extremely important") for people with ADHD and for people with mental-health conditions in general. Done regularly, any sport (even just stretching), a walk, or simply sitting on a bench near the house improves quality of life. Her conclusion: "даже, если не хочется, надо двигаться" ("even if you don't feel like it, you need to move") [msg #166].

**Dogs as a built-in activation device.** The author says dogs help her a lot. Rain, hail or snow, you go out of the house for at least 10 minutes whether you want to or not. That gets you fresh air, you notice something beautiful in nature, and your blood gets moving. The obligation to walk the dog removes the need for motivation [msg #166].

**Journaling, honestly.** She keeps a journal for mindfulness and reflection skills and usually recommends this to others, yet admits that however hard she tries, she cannot fill it in regularly. In this case she found an entry started a month earlier and finished it. The post shows that even an irregular journal can surface useful evidence, here that a walk improved her mood [msg #166].

### Heritability of happiness and comparison
The author first came across the idea that our happiness level may be "programmed" a few years earlier, while studying CBT and ACT intensively. She was very upset and needed "некоторое радикальное принятие" ("some radical acceptance") of the fact that she will never experience as much joy as some lucky people (joking that she will remain "унылой какашкой", a "glum little poop"). She now looks at this fact with acceptance, as she does at the general unfairness of the world. She notes that happiness and positive psychology are one of the large-scale projects of modern psychotherapy [msg #41].

Research she cites:
- Wellbeing and life satisfaction, which are closely linked to happiness, are 30–40% heritable (Røysamb et al., 2018). The rest depends on our lifestyle and our environment. She quotes the same paper: "«генетические факторы, по-видимому, играют важную роль в большинстве характеристик человека»" ("genetic factors apparently play an important role in most human characteristics"), p. 2 [msg #41].
- Not all genetic studies use twins. Some compare the genome, meaning all the genetic information a person needs to grow and develop, across unrelated people and groups (Larsen et al., 2017) [msg #41].
- A 2022 study comparing individual genomes found a genetic basis for the "общего уровня счастья" ("general level of happiness"), as opposed to momentary changes caused by temporary circumstances. This basis also affects brain structures that stay unchanged throughout life (Ward et al., 2022) [msg #41].

Her conclusion: different people can reach different levels of happiness, so "ни в коем случае нельзя сравнивать свое "счастье" с другими" ("under no circumstances should you compare your 'happiness' with others'"). Everyone's is different, and she adds that for some it is glum [msg #41].

# Differential diagnosis: ADHD and autism vs bipolar, depression, BPD and OCPD

## What this covers

This topic gathers the author's clinical rules for separating neurodevelopmental conditions (ADHD, autism) from conditions that can look similar: bipolar II disorder with hypomania, depression, dysthymia, borderline personality disorder (BPD) and obsessive-compulsive personality disorder (OCPD). It also covers the author's position that the activist "neurodivergent umbrella" should not absorb acquired, treatable disorders such as OCD and bipolar disorder, plus what is known about the adult outcomes of childhood ODD and DMDD. According to the source this matters because misdiagnosis is common — many adults with previously undiagnosed ADHD receive an erroneous bipolar diagnosis [msg #11] — and because labelling a treatable disorder as a "neurodivergence" can lead to normalising it instead of treating it [msg #75].

## Core ideas

- **ADHD energy cycles mimic bipolar II.** ADHD produces "pseudo-bipolar" cycles of frantic over-engagement followed by exhaustion and even depressive episodes; the main discriminator is whether mood itself is persistently elevated, plus age of onset [msg #11]. Full method: *ADHD vs bipolar II differentiation*.
- **What drives a mood swing tells you the diagnosis.** Bipolar mood shifts are biological, persistent and context-insensitive; ADHD swings follow interest/novelty and above all energy; BPD swings follow interactions with other people [msg #371]. See *Mood-swing triggers*.
- **Feeling good is not hypomania.** People with dysthymia — common in neurodivergence, CPTSD and personality disorders — may not know what normal mood is and mistake recovery for hypomania; hypomania has specific duration, mood and sleep markers [msg #345].
- **Neurodivergence = neurodevelopmental, lifelong.** Neurodivergences belong to the neurodevelopmental rubric because they form mainly in utero and persist for life; OCD and bipolar disorder are predispositions that may or may not manifest and can be cured or put into long remission, so they should be treated, not normalised [msg #75, img #75].
- **Impulsivity and overcontrol can coexist.** ADHD (impulsivity) and OCPD (overcontrol) co-occur, often because OCPD traits develop secondarily as a coping strategy against lifelong criticism (forwarded post) [msg #311].
- **Suspected autism changes the depression work-up.** If autism is suspected, it is worth confirming, mainly for self-understanding and psychotherapy; and depression must be differentiated from autistic burnout, the key difference being the absence of persistently low mood for most of the day over more than 14 days [msg #604, #617].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### ADHD vs bipolar II differentiation (mood change + age of onset)
**Purpose.** Separating ADHD "pseudo-bipolar cycles" from bipolar II disorder (hypomania alternating with depressive episodes). The author calls this a real problem for differential diagnosis, made worse when a person has both conditions — which, she says, nobody is protected against — and the reason many adults with undiagnosed ADHD are wrongly diagnosed with bipolar disorder [msg #11].

**How the pseudo-bipolar cycle looks (the author's own example).** She describes returning from holiday with plenty of energy, and her ADHD saying: "давай возьмем еще парочку проектов" (*let's take on a couple more projects*), "надо срочно увидеться со ВСЕМИ" (*I urgently need to see EVERYONE*), "а еще надо сходить воооон на ту выставку и записаться на тот курс" (*and I also need to go to that exhibition over there and sign up for that course*). From the outside one sees a person rushing back and forth, grabbing "100500" tasks, getting distracted and leaving some tasks unfinished. Then the body's energy runs out and the person lies down and stays down — a week, two — and sometimes slides into "нормальный такой депрессивный эпизод" (*a proper depressive episode*). This sequence closely resembles bipolar II: hypomania → depressive episode → hypomania again [msg #11]. The attached meme captures the ADHD energy-phase mindset: "perhaps THE adhd mood: I will have seven first priorities." [img #11]

**Discriminating steps.**
1. **Check for a change in mood itself — the main anchor.** The author's "главная наша опора" (*our main anchor*) is the change in mood: no amount of hyperactivity gives a persistently elevated mood of the kind seen in hypomanic phases. In ADHD the person is busy, scattered and over-committed, but not stably euphoric. She adds that a mood change is likewise present in the low phases of bipolar disorder (!) UNCLEAR: the sentence "так как в фазах гипомании, а так же есть и при фазах сниженного настроения" is grammatically garbled; it appears to mean that bipolar disorder shows genuine mood change in both hypomanic and low phases [msg #11].
2. **Check the age of onset.** ADHD symptoms most often appear in early childhood, from ages 3–5, whereas bipolar phases are found in adolescence and young adulthood. A lifelong pattern from early childhood points to ADHD; a picture that begins in the teens/youth points to bipolar disorder [msg #11].
3. **If both are present** — the author says this is "совсем другая история" (*a completely different story*) to be covered later. (!) INCOMPLETE: the source does not explain how to diagnose or handle combined ADHD + bipolar disorder [msg #11].

### Mood-swing triggers: bipolar vs ADHD vs BPD
**Purpose.** Discussed at a training on executive dysfunction: how mood changes and emotional dysregulation differ across bipolar disorder, ADHD and BPD [msg #371].
1. **Bipolar disorder vs ADHD — the "relatively simple" split.** In bipolar disorder mood changes are biologically grounded and persistent: even elevated mood lasts several days and responds little to changes in context. This does not happen in ADHD, "даже если вы словили гиперфиксацию" (*even if you've caught a hyperfixation*) — an intense ADHD absorption does not produce days-long, context-independent mood elevation [msg #371].
2. **BPD vs ADHD — the harder split.** Both show emotional dysregulation and can be hard to tell apart. The author's "небольшой лайфхак" (*little life hack*): look at what the fast mood shift is tied to.
   - **ADHD:** rapid mood shifts are most often tied either to interest/novelty or — mostly — to energy. "Вот у вас есть силы - и всё классно и зашибись. Устали - привет почти депрессия." (*You have energy — and everything is great and awesome. You're tired — hello, near-depression.*)
   - **BPD:** what matters is interaction with other people — who looked at me how, how someone treated me [msg #371].

### Normal mood vs hypomania criteria (dysthymia vs bipolar)
**Purpose.** For clients with stably lowered mood not reaching depression (dysthymia) who, when therapy starts working and their mood evens out and they begin to feel joy, get frightened and decide it is hypomania. The author says it is important to separate this from genuine bipolar hypomania [msg #345].

**Why it happens.** Dysthymia can be a frequent companion of neurodivergences, complex PTSD and personality disorders, so the person may simply not know what level of mood corresponds to the norm [msg #345].

**What is normal (the author's examples):** jumping in a puddle and enjoying it; laughing and running with your dog when you are over 30 and a serious person at work; looking at a sunset and feeling existential delight — "нормально!" (*normal!*) [msg #345].

**What is not normal (hypomania markers), all together:**
1. Lasting **more than 4 days**;
2. Rushing about, not finishing tasks and switching between them;
3. Feeling euphoria **or irritability** for the **greater part of the day**;
4. Sleeping only **3–4 hours** and feeling fine about it.

Everything outside this is "вполне нормальное состояние человека. Даже с СДВГ 🙂, даже с РАС и со всем остальным" (*a perfectly normal human state. Even with ADHD, even with ASD and everything else*) [msg #345].

### Neurodevelopmental vs acquired disorders (what belongs under the neurodivergent umbrella)
**The problem.** Online and among mental-health activists (психоактивисты) there is a lot of information about what belongs to the neurodivergence family; the author says some of it makes you scratch your head — e.g. claims that personality disorders are a neurodivergence, or OCD, "because the person thinks differently." She grants that it is great that destigmatisation is working, "но... НО!" (*but... BUT!*): scientists, doctors and other specialists try to understand causes and mechanisms of onset and development, which is why classifications and research exist [msg #75].

**The author's classification logic.**
1. Psychiatry currently divides, strictly speaking, into "приобретенные" (*acquired*) disorders and "врождённые и сохраняющиеся всю жизнь" (*congenital and lifelong*) conditions [msg #75].
2. Neurodivergences sit in the rubric "нарушения психического развития" (*neurodevelopmental disorders*) precisely because of their neuro-ontogenetic mechanism: they form mainly during intrauterine development and disappear "только вместе с гибелью особи" (*only together with the death of the individual*) [msg #75].
3. Other disorders involve a predisposition that may "fire" at some point in life — or may not. Moreover, some of them can be cured completely or brought into long remission; this applies to OCD and bipolar disorder [msg #75].
4. **Conclusion:** "не смешивать теплое с мягким" (*don't mix apples and oranges*, lit. "warm with soft") and treat what needs treating rather than normalising, for example, OCD thinking [msg #75].

### Impulsivity vs overcontrol spectrum (ADHD + OCPD comorbidity)
*Forwarded from the channel Маша does therapy.* [msg #311]
- **The model.** Mental-health problems can be placed on an impulsivity–overcontrol spectrum: in some conditions the core is a lack of ability to control impulses; in others the core is excessive control [msg #311].
- **Can opposite poles co-occur? Yes.** ADHD lies on the impulsivity side and OCPD on the overcontrol side, yet ADHD–OCPD comorbidity is encountered. A study the post cites (linked on guilfordjournals.com) looked at links between ADHD and personality disorders and found OCPD among the four most frequent combinations. The author of the forwarded post says she sometimes meets this combination in clinical practice [msg #311].
- **Mechanism (secondary development).** Clients with ADHD typically face criticism from early childhood for their difficulties completing tasks, and are forced to develop coping strategies in the form of very rigid demands and standards for themselves. With great effort they do everything "правильно" (*correctly*) and control themselves heavily to avoid constant criticism — and so many OCPD traits may develop secondarily [msg #311].

### Autistic burnout vs depression differentiation
- **When to get assessed for autism.** A reader in depression treatment was persuaded to take several autism tests, scored high, and was told people with autism have specific features in depression treatment. The author's view: if autism is suspected it is worth checking, especially with high test scores. It will have little effect on pharmacotherapy, but a very strong effect on understanding one's own features and on psychotherapeutic interventions [msg #604].
- **Why it matters for depression.** When autism is present or suspected, depression must be differentially diagnosed against autistic burnout — so it is better to clarify open questions [msg #604].
- **What autistic burnout is.** A condition not yet in disease classifications, but a definite symptom picture observed in autistic people. The author says that over the last 5 years about 40 studies on it have appeared (she checked PubMed the day before writing) [msg #617].
- **The key differentiator.** "Самое главное отличие от депрессии - это отсутствие стойко сниженного настроения большую часть дня в течение более 14 дней." (*The main difference from depression is the absence of persistently lowered mood for most of the day over more than 14 days.*) [msg #617]

## Detailed insights

### Adult outcomes of ODD and DMDD
Asked what ODD (oppositional defiant disorder) and DMDD (disruptive mood dysregulation disorder) potentially turn into in adulthood, the author says there is little research. From what exists:
- **ODD** is currently thought possibly to pass into personality traits of the same oppositional kind, or to take shape within some personality disorder — for example closer to antisocial [msg #749].
- **DMDD** gives an increased risk of developing depression and anxiety disorders in adulthood, but does not guarantee it 100%. "Пока это всё, что нам известно" (*That's all we know so far*) [msg #749].

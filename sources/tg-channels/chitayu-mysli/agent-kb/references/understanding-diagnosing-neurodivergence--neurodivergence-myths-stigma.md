# Self-diagnosis, myths and stigma around neurodivergence

## What this covers

How the author (a psychiatrist/psychotherapist working with autistic and ADHD adults) thinks about self-diagnosis from social media, the rapid rise in autism and ADHD diagnoses and the slogan that "everyone is neurodivergent", the clash between the medical and the social model, the myths and stereotypes she keeps hearing, whether a helper must share the condition, misinformation about ADHD from doctors of other specialties, and the 2025 Russian rule that bars people with an F84 diagnosis from driving. According to the source this matters because stigma — "especially among helping specialists" — is still frighteningly high, and because distorted images and blurred boundaries can devalue the real difficulties of neurodivergent people and keep others from the help they need [msg #709, #375, #546].

## Core ideas

- **Self-diagnosis is acceptable as a stop-gap, not as an endpoint.** Doing skills when you suffer and cannot reach a specialist is better than doing nothing, but social media create a loop of distorted self-recognition; use verified information and still go to specialists (see *The author's position on self-diagnosis*) [msg #375].
- **We will not all become neurodivergent.** The share of people with ASD and ADHD in the population is probably constant; diagnoses grow because we have become better at recognizing them (see *Two reasons why it feels like everyone is neurodivergent*) [msg #546].
- **The social model without the medical one blurs boundaries.** Only the social approach "без медицинского никуда не годится" (is no good without the medical one) because it can devalue real difficulties [msg #546].
- **A specialist does not need the condition to help well, and having it guarantees nothing.** What matters is expertise and continuous training [msg #379].
- **Autism is a spectrum, and most autistic people are invisible.** ASD does not equal speech and intellectual impairment [msg #146].
- **Stigma is embedded in law and professional practice.** The F84 driving ban has no medical basis and reflects traditional stigmatization of psychiatric diagnoses [msg #728].
- **Real advocacy defends all disorders**, not only the "liked" ones [msg #38].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### The social-media self-diagnosis loop
Purpose: explains why a study showing TikTok misleads about ADHD matters, even though the author is not against self-diagnosis as such. The author calls it a "патологический круг" (pathological circle) and notes it is in general normal when everyone starts encountering something new [msg #375].
1. **One person shares.** Someone on social media starts sharing their knowledge and symptoms — which the author considers good in itself [msg #375].
2. **Many recognize themselves.** Viewers see themselves in the description but do not go to a specialist [msg #375].
3. **They become broadcasters.** These people start carrying their own "knowledge" to the masses. The problem is here: it is not certain that this second person has ADHD or any other disorder [msg #375].
4. **The next people build on the distortion.** Each subsequent wave relies on a possibly distorted perception, so what we see online does not always correspond to the truth [msg #375].
Consequences named by the author: (a) it can devalue the real difficulties of people with ADHD; (b) it can prevent those who suspected ADHD but actually have a different disorder from getting the help they need [msg #375].
The trigger was a forwarded post by Liza (Psycho_pharmacologist) about a study — reported on psyandneuro.ru — showing that TikTok misleads about ADHD; she framed it as evidence "что по соц сетям не надо ставить себе диагноз и доказывать его врачам" (that you should not diagnose yourself via social media and then prove it to doctors), noting that strictly the study is about TikTok being misleading, and that she planned a separate post on the problems of self-diagnosis [msg #374]. ### The author's position on self-diagnosis
1. Self-diagnosis is not bad when you are suffering and there is no possibility to see a specialist: "Лучше делать навыки, чем их не делать" (better to do the skills than not), and in principle better to do anything that improves your life [msg #375].
2. Psychoeducation is "топ" (top, the best thing) [msg #375].
3. Self-diagnosis can be fine as a temporary solution [msg #375].
4. But: use verified information and still try to turn to specialists in the field [msg #375].

### Two things to know before choosing a neurodivergent specialist
Context: someone voiced the opinion that a neurotypical person cannot be a good specialist in neurodivergence. The author agrees that many neurotypical people do not understand the inner experience of people with ASD and ADHD, which delays timely help, and that many people — especially in psychiatry and psychotherapy — look for specialists who are neurodivergent too. She calls this "a not bad strategy", with two caveats [msg #379]:
1. **A specialist does not have to have a problem to treat it well.** Just as a pediatrician need not have children. Example: her colleague Maria Novikova, with whom she runs many neurodivergence projects, has neither ASD nor ADHD, yet her sensitivity to symptoms and clients is rare [msg #379].
2. **A specialist with ADHD and/or ASD has the same problems with attention and communication**, so the "double empathy" story can apply to them too. This can make correct diagnosis harder or create limits and problems in psychotherapy. She cites a qualitative study (BJPsych Open) on autistic psychiatrists' experiences of recognising themselves and others as autistic [msg #379].
Conclusion: nothing guarantees that a specialist will be sensitive and attentive to your autistic (or other) symptoms; assuming so is closer to a cognitive error, as in the pediatrician example. The key is that the specialist knows their field and keeps raising their qualification. Doctors are human, not robots, and make mistakes — "Даже ИИ с этим не справляется" (even AI can't manage that) [msg #379].

### Two reasons why it feels like everyone is neurodivergent
The author's answer — stressed as "мое личное мнение и не более того" (my personal opinion and nothing more) — to the claim that soon everyone will be neurodivergent [msg #546].
1. **Real growth in diagnosed, self-accepting people.** The number of diagnosed people who understand and accept their neurodivergence has risen sharply and will keep rising. Four factors:
   - improved diagnostics;
   - an expanded pool of people being diagnosed (for example adults, and especially women);
   - lower stigma toward psychiatry worldwide;
   - greater availability of medical care.
   Against this background it is logical that numbers grow and the feeling appears that "вокруг одни нейроотличные" (there are only neurodivergent people around) [msg #546].
2. **A position of tolerance in society** — the medical and social approaches collide here (see *Medical vs social model*). She says she was very surprised that a British professor said such a thing at all [msg #546].
Verdict: no, we will not all become neurodivergent — as many people with ASD and ADHD as there were, so many there will most likely be in the population; we just know better how to recognize them. There is no point in calling everything neurodivergence; she still disagrees with including everything indiscriminately, such as personality disorders and OCD [msg #546].

### Medical vs social model of neurodivergence
- **Medical approach:** stricter, with concrete criteria [msg #546].
- **Social approach:** the brain simply works differently in different conditions, and we should accept that we are different — normal, but different [msg #546].
- **Author's ("вредный", contrarian) view:** only the social approach without the medical one is useless, because it really blurs boundaries and may "обесценивать" (devalue) the real difficulties of neurodivergent people [msg #546].

### Six myths about neurodivergence the author hears most
The author says that when the wave of psychoeducation about neurodivergence began there were many more stereotypes, but some beliefs persist, resurface regularly and hurt "будто по живому" (as if cutting into living flesh) [msg #709]:
1. «У людей с РАС не должно быть эмпатии и эмоций» (people with ASD must not have empathy and emotions) [msg #709].
2. «У всех бывают трудности с вниманием / общением / усталостью — это просто жизнь» (everyone has difficulties with attention / communication / fatigue — that's just life) [msg #709].
3. «Мы тогда тут ВСЕ НЕЙРООТЛИЧНЫЕ» (then we're ALL neurodivergent here) [msg #709].
4. «Если у тебя есть друзья, то ты точно не аутичный» (if you have friends, you're definitely not autistic) [msg #709].
5. «При СДВГ не может быть успешной карьеры» (with ADHD you can't have a successful career) [msg #709].
6. Her "personal favourite": «Если тебе тяжело — значит, ты неправильно живёшь» (if it's hard for you, you're living wrong) [msg #709].
In an update after reading comments, she added the myth that someone is "too" warm, smart or organized to be neurodivergent: nobody thinks about what this "too" costs a person [msg #709]. She hopes such statements will become rarer, but the level of stigma, especially among helping specialists, is still frighteningly high [msg #709].

### Analysis of the F84 driving ban (Russia, from 1 September 2025)
Forwarded from the channel Чистые Когниции: an interview with psychiatrist Anastasia Afanasyeva about the updated official list of diseases that bar driving. The list now includes ICD-10 section F84 «Общие расстройства психологического развития» (pervasive developmental disorders), which covers autism and Asperger syndrome — diagnoses that ICD-11 groups as autism spectrum disorders. Obtaining or renewing a licence becomes questionable for people with these diagnoses [msg #728].
1. **What it means in practice.** A licence requires a certificate from a medical board and from the ПНД (psychiatric dispensary); if the medical record contains an autism diagnosis, the certificate will not be issued. As a rule there are no other checks while driving: the traffic police (ГИБДД) do not check diagnoses themselves and act only on the medical certificate, so the current formal "filter" is the dispensary psychiatrist. Theoretically, concealing a diagnosis could lead to the licence being cancelled if discovered [msg #728].
2. **Is there a medical basis.** Studies show that adolescents and adults with ASD get licences less often, but when they drive their accident rate is not higher than that of neurotypical peers. There are specific difficulties — complex turns, reacting to non-verbal signals of pedestrians and other drivers — but they do not lead to a systematic increase in accidents. Hence there are no medical grounds for a total ban; the decision rather reflects the traditional stigmatization of psychiatric diagnoses in Russian law, where they often serve as grounds for restrictions despite current scientific data [msg #728].
3. **Possible consequences.** (a) Discrimination: the ban creates the impression that all people with ASD are dangerous drivers, which is untrue. (b) More secrecy: people will hide the diagnosis or not seek help when they suspect it, for fear of losing their licence — worsening access to qualified help. (c) Legal collisions with licences issued before the ban; how this will work is still unclear [msg #728].
4. **Bottom line.** The restrictions will hardly affect every person with ASD; there is no reason to panic — it is a bureaucratic change, not mass roadside checks. But the very appearance of such laws shows how important it is to talk about stigma and its consequences [msg #728].
Follow-up from the author in the discussion [msg #730]:
- Adults are diagnosed with ASD very poorly in Russia, so the probability that you will get this diagnosis at the dispensary or the licence exam is extremely low — "и это плюс" (and that's a plus) [msg #730].
- On the other hand, with the universal rollout of ЕГИСЗ, where all data sit, it is hard to predict how dispensaries will react [msg #730].
- On the wording about "chronic" conditions: everything relating to neurodevelopmental disorders are states we have ALL our life, so under that wording they fall into it automatically [msg #730].

### Four objections to anti-ADHD posts by a pediatrician
Readers asked about pediatrician-blogger Fyodor Katasonov's (федиатрия) recent posts on ADHD. The author stresses this is "свое ценное мнение (и только мое)" (my valuable opinion, and only mine) [msg #843]:
1. **Stay in your specialty.** Pediatricians and somatic doctors should not meddle in psychiatry or other specialties unless they have been trained in them and worked in them for some time [msg #843].
2. **Tired of "great male minds".** In medicine and psychology many have their own unique, "only correct" view of a problem; ADHD in particular "никому жить не дает" (gives no one peace). On reading, half of the speculation is unsupported or far-fetched. In his article, half argues that scientific research is falsified, yet at the end he cites articles himself — only those that fit his opinion [msg #843].
3. **Manipulation by generalization.** On the claim «Официальная медицина, иногда заблуждаясь, а часто намеренно, годами и десятилетиями врет своим пользователям» (official medicine, sometimes mistakenly and often deliberately, has lied to its users for years and decades): it is "kind of true and kind of not" — this is "манипуляция обобщением". Yes, there are paid studies and studies with poor samples, as in any field of science; yes, our knowledge is often limited. But that does not lead to "НАМ ВСЕМ ВРУТ ФАРМКОМПАНИИ" (pharma companies lie to us all). The remedy: learn to read research (taught at university), follow results of large studies, and again, don't meddle in every field [msg #843].
4. **No clear point, and treatment is not only drugs.** It was hard to grasp the article's meaning or what ADHD is supposed to be in it (her spoiler: "явление" — a phenomenon, unclear what it includes, especially given the concept of neurodivergence), apart from the message that it need not be treated. She reminds that treatment is not only medication but also non-drug, which "за заговорами фармкомпаний видимо забылось" (was apparently forgotten behind the pharma conspiracies) [msg #843].

## Detailed insights

### Autism is a spectrum and mostly invisible
On World Autism Awareness Day (2 April) the author writes that each of us should keep showing again and again how much autism is a spectrum: ASD does not equal a child or adult with speech and intellectual impairments, it is not necessarily a person banging against a wall, and "нас очень и очень много" (there are very, very many of us). Every week she sees wonderful people of whom, unless they choose to disclose, you would never say they have ASD: smart, educated, stylish, adult, young, actors, scientists, housewives, psychologists, doctors, programmers, physicists — "Перечислять можно бесконечно" (the list is endless). In her view people with neurodivergence are part of this world and a worthy and equal part [msg #146].

### Mental-health advocacy must cover all disorders
A forwarded statement (author litol.rat.bpd, via Нейропанк): you cannot be a mental-health advocate if you stigmatize disorders; you cannot choose whom to support and whom to demonize. Advocates must defend all disorders, not only those they like while demonizing those they hate; if you still stigmatize them, you are not a real advocate [msg #38].

### The UK statistic behind "everyone will be neurodivergent"
While writing a book chapter on why bare statistics should not be taken at face value, the author quoted a British article: a 2021 study found a 787% rise in autism diagnoses in the UK between 1998 and 2018. Its author, Professor Ginny Russell of the University of Exeter, said diagnostic boundaries may widen further: «Я действительно думаю, что это будет продолжаться до тех пор, пока, возможно, всех не отнесут к категории нейроразнообразных» (I really think this will continue until perhaps everyone is classed as neurodiverse) [msg #533]. The author's response is the framework *Two reasons why it feels like everyone is neurodivergent* [msg #546]. (!)

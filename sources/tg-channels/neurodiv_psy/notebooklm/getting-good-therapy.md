## Making therapy work and spotting therapist red flags

### What this covers

This topic holds two practical checklists for people in therapy or looking for it. The first is a card set forwarded from the channel Чистые Когниции (text by Антон Вотрин and Алексей Демин). It lists eight small actions a client can take to make work with a psychologist more effective. The second is a single card of seven "red flags" that may mean a psychologist or psychotherapist does not suit you. The channel posted that card under the heading of what to watch for during consultations [#96, #161].

The source explains why this matters with one main idea: the client is not a passive recipient of therapy. They are an active participant who can help both themselves and the process. The red-flags list gives clients criteria for noticing when the problem is the specialist rather than them [#96, #161].

### Core ideas

- You are an active participant in your therapy. The card set opens with this claim. If you are already in therapy, it is important to remember that you take an active part in the process, and it is within your power to help yourself and your therapy. The tips are presented as small but useful actions that raise the effectiveness of work with a psychologist. The full set is under *Help your psychologist* below [#96].
- Some signals mean a specialist does not suit you. The card names seven behaviours or features of a psychologist or psychotherapist that may indicate they are not a fit. They cover boundaries, qualifications, safety, reliability, focus on the client, respect for the client's culture, identity and diagnoses, and openness to feedback. The full list is under *Red flags of a psychologist* below [#161].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Help your psychologist (Помоги своему психологу) — 8 client tips
Purpose: a checklist for people who are already in therapy. It lists small actions that improve how well the work with a psychologist goes. The cards were published by Чистые Когниции under the label "СОВЕТЫ ЧК" ("ЧК tips"), with text by Антон Вотрин and Алексей Демин and design by Марина Белоусова. Card 1 is the cover, and the tips are on cards 2–9. Each card carries a single instruction. The cards give no reasons or examples beyond the instruction, so the explanations below stay at the depth of the source [#96, img #96].

1. Ask questions. "Задавайте вопросы своему психологу, если что-то непонятно" (Ask your psychologist questions if something is unclear). If something in the process, a technique or the psychologist's words is unclear to you, ask. Do not stay silent about it. This tip comes from card 2 [#97].
2. Slow down and study yourself. Do not rush to get new experience. Learn to slow down, and study your own thoughts, emotions and behaviour. The card's wording is "Не торопитесь в получении нового опыта, учитесь замедляться" (Don't hurry in gaining new experience, learn to slow down). The point is to observe your inner reactions and actions closely rather than push quickly for change. This tip comes from card 3 [#98].
3. Be curious about yourself. "Старайтесь быть любопытным к себе и своей жизни" (Try to be curious about yourself and your life). The card recommends an attitude of interest towards yourself and your life. This tip comes from card 4 [#99].
4. Talk openly about difficult things. Where possible, speak openly with your therapist about difficult things. The card adds the hedge "По возможности" (Where possible), so openness is encouraged but not demanded at any cost. This tip comes from card 5 [#100].
5. Notice and record what you are not ready to share. Notice the experiences you are not ready to share yet. Don't share them for now, but be sure to write them down or record them in some form. The card's words are "Пока не делитесь, но обязательно фиксируйте его" (Don't share it for now, but be sure to record it). This tip makes room for not disclosing everything at once while still keeping track of this material. It balances tip 4 (see *Caveats*). This tip comes from card 6 [#101].
6. Set goals and keep commitments. Actively set goals for changing your life, talk about them in therapy, and stick to the commitments you have made. The card treats goal-setting as the client's own active job, not only the psychologist's. This tip comes from card 7 [#102].
7. Work on obstacles and discuss both setbacks and wins. Work on the obstacles that get in the way of change. Discuss with your psychologist "как промахи, так и победы" (both slip-ups and victories). The lesson is to bring your failures and setbacks into therapy as well as your successes. This tip comes from card 8 [#103].
8. Build a working relationship with your psychologist. Build a working relationship (рабочие отношения) with your psychologist, and explore the difficulties that come up while you are building it. The card puts «строительства» ("construction") in quotation marks. The relationship itself is treated as something to build, and the difficulties in it as material to explore. This tip comes from card 9 [#104].

#### Red flags of a psychologist (Красные флажки психолога) — 7 signs a specialist does not suit you
Purpose: a list of what may indicate "что психолог или психотерапевт вам не подходит" (that a psychologist or psychotherapist does not suit you). The channel shared it as a list of things that are important to watch for during consultations with a psychologist or psychotherapist. The card credits the idea to @wangechi_the_therapist and the Russian translation to @ia_psy. The card does not say what to do once you see a red flag. It only frames them as signs of a poor fit [#161, img #161].

1. The specialist tries to relate to you as more than a psychologist. The card's examples are that they invite you to their home ("зовет вас в гости"), ask you for a favour ("просит об услуге") or flirt ("флиртует"). In other words, the specialist moves outside the professional role [#161].
2. No relevant professional education. The card defines relevant education as higher education in psychology, medical education, or long retraining courses ("длительные курсы переподготовки"). A specialist with none of these is a red flag [#161].
3. They judge and criticise you, and you feel unsafe. Condemnation, criticism and a feeling of not being safe with the specialist are signs that they do not suit you [#161].
4. They constantly reschedule and cancel sessions. The card's word is "Постоянно" (constantly), so the flag is a repeated pattern of moving and cancelling meetings [#161].
5. They keep talking about themselves in sessions. The specialist constantly talks about themselves and steers topics towards themselves ("переводит темы на себя"). Sessions stop being about the client [#161].
6. They speak judgmentally about your culture, identity or diagnoses. "С осуждением высказывается о вашей культуре, идентичности, диагнозах" (Speaks with condemnation about your culture, identity, diagnoses). A specialist who condemns who you are, where you come from or your diagnoses is not a fit [#161].
7. They are not willing to hear feedback. "Не готов_а выслушивать обратную связь" (Is not ready to listen to feedback). The underscore in "готов_а" is a gender-inclusive spelling for "ready" (masculine/feminine), and the card spells "Специалист_ка" the same way [#161].

### Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- Both checklists are cards with one line per item. The source gives no reasoning, evidence or examples beyond what is written above, apart from the examples on red flag 1 and the definition of relevant education on red flag 2 [#96, #161].
- Tips 4 and 5 of *Help your psychologist* pull in slightly different directions: talk openly about difficult things, yet you may keep some experience unshared for now. The source reconciles them only through the hedge "По возможности" (where possible) and the instruction to record what you are not sharing. It does not say when or how to bring that recorded material into therapy. (!) INCOMPLETE [#100, #101].
- The red-flags card says these signs "может говорить" (may indicate) a poor fit. It presents them as signals, not as proof. It does not say whether one flag is enough to change specialists, or what steps to take [#161].
- Both items are third-party content the channel shared. The tips were forwarded from Чистые Когниции, and the red-flags card is a translation of an idea by @wangechi_the_therapist. The channel added only the framing line about what to watch for during consultations [#96, #161].

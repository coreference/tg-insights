## Self-compassion, comparison, shame and emotional literacy

### What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's self-support material on four related subjects: coping with chronic comparison of oneself with others (a five-card carousel), a card listing eight things one should not be ashamed of, a three-step self-compassion technique offered as an alternative to New Year "summing up" for neurodivergent people with holiday blues, and a forwarded Russian translation of an emotion wheel based on Brené Brown's book *Atlas of the Heart* for naming feelings. According to the source, these matter because the internet has made the circle of comparison global and comparison easily turns toxic, because neurodivergent people more often carry shame for "not meeting expectations", and because accurately naming emotions ("emotional literacy") is a skill that "changes our life for the better" [#39, #356, #253].

### Core ideas

- Comparison is natural but becomes toxic at internet scale. Comparing is how we form identity, but media made the whole world our comparison circle; working with it is a long-term practice supported by self-care and self-compassion (full detail in *Five ways to help yourself…*) [#39].
- Social media shows only highlights. Every path has ups and downs, but people post only bright moments, so comparing your full life with others' feeds is distorted [#40].
- The inner critic does not motivate. Constant insults and comparisons "never help anyone"; distancing from the critic (naming it) and treating yourself like a friend are the antidotes [#43, #44].
- Some things are not shameful: emotions, prioritising yourself, asking for help, refusing people, rest, boundaries, changing plans, moving slower than others [#61].
- Holiday blues hit neurodivergent people harder, and summing up the year is optional; self-compassion is offered instead, and it does not mean giving up [#356, #357, #358].
- Naming emotions is a skill. Emotional literacy — accurately recognising and naming emotions — helps you notice your states and understand yourself; the emotion wheel exists to train it [#253].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Five ways to help yourself if you constantly compare yourself with others
Purpose / when to use. For people who spend a lot of time online and keep feeling that they look "not good enough", earn less than "some former classmate", and that their life looks less interesting than others' lives [#39].

Background reasoning. The channel says comparison is "важный и естественный процесс формирования нашей идентичности" (an important and natural process of forming our identity): by comparing ourselves with others we understand who we are, what we are good at and what not so much. Before media, people could only compare themselves with those they knew during their lives; now "наш круг сравнения - это весь мир" (our circle of comparison is the whole world), and in such conditions comparison easily becomes toxic. Working with comparison is described as "длительная практика" (a long-term practice): even after a long time the unpleasant voice of the inner critic will sometimes sound in your head, and the success diary will need new entries again and again — but care and a compassionate attitude toward yourself can help maintain stable self-esteem [#39].

1. Clear your space ("Расчистите пространство"). You can always unfollow people you constantly compare yourself with — even if they are your acquaintances. Why: any path consists of ups and downs, but people tend to upload only bright moments to social media and few talk about their failures, so the feed is a skewed sample. How: if you notice that a particular social network constantly triggers negative emotions in you, try limiting the time you spend in it [#40, img #40].
2. Remember your own path and achievements. Remind yourself that not everyone has the same starting conditions — the card names health, finances and family. Also, we often find what we would like to do not at 17 but much later. Remember the path that *you* specifically have walked. How: keep an achievement diary (дневник достижений) — it does not matter whether you succeeded at work or managed to brush your teeth when you had no energy: if you are proud of something, write it down. Why: these pleasant reminders help strengthen self-esteem [#41, img #41].
3. Remind yourself of your values. We can be upset by thoughts of an acquaintance who moved to another country and earns a lot. Try to think whether you actually want such a life. Perhaps you do not want to leave your family and friends and go abroad for a long time; or you would rather pursue a hobby than work 12 hours a day. Keep reminding yourself what matters specifically to you, even if it does not fit the standard idea of a successful person's life [#42, img #42].
4. Give your inner critic a name. We often hear the unpleasant voice of the inner critic in our head, and it is hard to argue with because it is our own voice — and maybe, we think, it helps with motivation? The card answers: no, constant insults and comparisons never help anyone. How: give the critic a name and invent an image for it. Imagine you hear the same words not from yourself but from a grumpy neighbour, "Николая Петровича" (Nikolai Petrovich). Then ask: would you believe these words if a barely-known person said them to you? The mechanism is distancing — hearing the criticism as coming from an outsider makes it easier to question [#43, img #43].
5. Become a friend to yourself ("Станьте себе другом"). We may consider ourselves good, understanding friends, yet we are often terrible friends to ourselves. How would you comfort a friend in a hard situation? Perhaps sit with them and talk about their feelings, try to remind them what a wonderful person they are. But toward ourselves we are rude and cruel: "Ничего ты не можешь, все зарабатывают больше тебя" (You can't do anything, everyone earns more than you), we tell ourselves — something you probably would not say to a friend who is already upset. Instead of being harsh, sympathise with yourself and offer words of support [#44, img #44].

#### Things you should not be ashamed of (8-item card)
A card titled "Вещи, за которые тебе не должно быть стыдно" (Things you should not be ashamed of), posted under the #поддержка (support) tag. The card gives the items as labels with icons, without further explanation; all eight are [#61, img #61]:
1. Emotions (Эмоции).
2. Being your own main priority (Быть своим главным приоритетом).
3. Asking for help (Просить о помощи).
4. Refusing / saying no to people (Отказывать людям).
5. Rest (Отдых).
6. Setting boundaries in relationships (Устанавливать границы в отношениях).
7. Changing plans (Менять планы).
8. Moving not as fast as others (Двигаться не так быстро, как другие).

#### Self-compassion technique for holiday blues (3 steps)
Purpose / when to use. At the end of a year when, as the post puts it, the results are "пустота, усталость и ощущение, что ты никуда не двигаешься" (emptiness, tiredness and a feeling that you're not moving anywhere). The channel's instruction: "Не подводите итоги, если не хочется, вместо этого попробуйте технику на самосострадание" (Don't sum up the year if you don't want to; instead try the self-compassion technique). The post also states: "Это нормально, если год был отстойный" (It's normal if the year sucked), and that it is fine to have neither holiday mood nor any desire to work on yourself in 2026 [#356, img #356].

Definition and context given by the channel. Holiday blues (праздничная хандра) is a feeling of melancholy during the holidays that may be linked to: financial pressure from endless gifts; loneliness; reflection on one's life; and unrealistic expectations of oneself and of the holidays. It is described as especially relevant for neurodivergent people, because New Year shopping and gatherings with relatives can be very overloading, and summing up the year is yet another collision with one's own limitations. The channel lists that neurodivergent people more often [#356]:
- live in a mode of chronic overload;
- spend more energy on basic things;
- experience more shame for "несоответствие ожиданиям" (not meeting expectations);
- burn out not only from work but also from constant adaptation.

The steps [#357, img #357]:
1. Check in and name the feeling. Stop for a second and ask yourself: "Как я сейчас?" (How am I right now?). Try to find a couple of words for your feelings — the card's examples are tiredness, emptiness, anger, disappointment, apathy. (Naming feelings is the skill the emotion wheel below trains.)
2. Imagine another neurodivergent person in your place. Picture another neurodivergent person — someone close to you or a character — who had the same year and is in the same state. Would you scold them and demand more? Almost certainly not. The card concludes that you can treat yourself no worse (the original reads "не жуже" [sic], a typo for "не хуже").
3. Ask what could support you even a little. Ask yourself: "А что могло бы меня хоть немножко поддержать?" (What could support me even a little?). Examples on the card: reducing the number of obligations; escaping from the gathering with relatives; diving into a special interest or hyperfixation over the holidays; or simply doing nothing.

What self-compassion is not. The closing card states that self-compassion does not mean "опустить руки и смириться" (giving up and resigning yourself): "Просто важно не добивать себя, когда вам и так плохо." (It is simply important not to finish yourself off when you already feel bad.) The card ends with a humorous illustration: a list of lines "2022 будет моим годом" (2022 will be my year) repeated for 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 (the 2023 line appears struck through in the transcription), ironically depicting the yearly repetition of the same New Year promise [#358, img #358].

#### Emotion wheel based on Brené Brown's *Atlas of the Heart*
What it is and why. A post forwarded from the channel Можно помедленнее, а? (Stasya Polushkina) presents a Russian translation of an emotion wheel based on Brené Brown's book *Atlas of the Heart*. It contains 87 emotions grouped by "Местам, куда мы идем, когда…" (places we go when…). The wheel was created "чтобы чуть расширить взгляд на то, что мы чувствуем" (to widen the view of what we feel a little) and to understand the diversity of emotional states and the possibility of managing them. Rationale given: language plays an important role in our lives — it helps us seek meaning, heal, learn and get to know ourselves; "Слова — это ключи к нашим эмоциям" (words are the keys to our emotions), letting us share them with others and understand ourselves better. "Наша способность точно распознавать и называть эмоции — это эмоциональная грамотность" (our ability to accurately recognise and name emotions is emotional literacy) — a skill that changes life for the better. The main goal of the wheel is "учиться называть эмоции" (to learn to name emotions), which helps notice one's emotional states and understand oneself better [#253].

How to use it. Find the situation you are in among the inner "places we go when…" sectors, then look through the emotions in that sector's outer ring to find the most precise word for what you feel. The author also found a Notion template of this wheel (in English) with which you can study your day and observe it through the prisms of feelings or moments [#253].

Full content of the wheel (centre label Место куда мы идем, когда... — *the place we go when…*; transcribed from the image, English glosses by the archivist) [#253]:
1. Сравниваем (we compare): сравнение (comparison), восхищение (admiration), почтение (reverence), зависть (envy), ревность (jealousy), обида (resentment), злорадство (schadenfreude), радость (joy — at another's good fortune, by position in this sector).
2. Все не то, чем кажется (things are not what they seem): сарказм (sarcasm), ирония (irony), парадокс (paradox), когнитивный диссонанс (cognitive dissonance), ностальгия (nostalgia), сладкая горечь (bittersweetness), забава (amusement).
3. Мы не справляемся (we are not coping / falling short): стыд (shame), унижение (humiliation), вина (guilt), перфекционизм (perfectionism), самосострадание (self-compassion), смущение (embarrassment).
4. Ищем близости (we seek closeness / connection): принадлежность (belonging), чувство нахождения на своем месте (feeling of being in one's place / fitting in), связь (connection), оторванность (disconnection), беззащитность (defencelessness / insecurity), невидимость (invisibility), одиночество (loneliness).
5. Высокая самооценка (high self-esteem): гордость (pride), высокомерие (arrogance / hubris), унижение (literally "humiliation"; see caveats).
6. Жизнь хороша (life is good): счастье (happiness), радость (joy), спокойствие (calm), удовольствие (pleasure / contentment), благодарность (gratitude), ожидание радости (anticipation of joy), облегчение (relief), спокойствие (calm, listed a second time).
7. Мы с другими (we are with others): сострадание (compassion), жалость (pity), эмпатия (empathy), симпатия (sympathy), границы (boundaries), относительное страдание (comparative suffering).
8. Наше сердце открыто (our heart is open): любовь (love), отсутствие любви (lovelessness), позиция защиты (defensiveness), предательство (betrayal), вера в себя (self-trust), сердце разбито (heartbreak), доверие (trust), затопление (flooding), боль (hurt / pain).
9. С нами поступают незаслуженно (we are treated unfairly / feel wronged): гнев (anger), презрение (contempt), отвращение (disgust), бесчеловечность (dehumanisation), ненависть (hatred), самоуверенность (self-righteousness / self-assurance).
10. Все идет не по плану (things don't go to plan): фрустрация (frustration), отказ (refusal / rejection), обескураженность (discouragement), сожаление (regret), ожидания (expectations), разочарование (disappointment), скука (boredom).
11. Встречаемся с невероятным (we encounter the incredible): удивление (surprise), интерес (interest), любопытство (curiosity), смущение (here likely confusion; see caveats), трепет (awe).
12. Нам больно (we are hurting): горе (grief), грусть (sadness), отчаяние (despair), безнадежность (hopelessness), страдания (anguish / suffering).
13. Мы в чем-то не уверены или подавлены чем-то (we are uncertain about something or overwhelmed by something): стресс (stress), подавленность (overwhelm), тревога (anxiety), беспокойство (worry), избегание (avoidance), возбуждение (excitement), ужас (dread / terror), страх (fear), уязвимость (vulnerability).

### Detailed insights

#### Why comparison hurts and how the tools fit together
The five comparison practices work on different levels: reducing exposure to the triggering input (unfollowing, limiting time in a network), correcting the distorted reference point (different starting conditions, late discovery of one's calling, highlight-only feeds), anchoring in one's own values instead of the "standard idea of a successful person's life", and changing the internal voice (naming the critic, speaking to yourself as to a friend). The channel frames the whole as ongoing maintenance rather than a cure: the critic will keep sounding sometimes and the success diary must keep being refilled [#39, #40, #41, #42, #43, #44].

#### The friend perspective recurs across posts
The same move — "how would I treat someone else in my situation?" — appears both in the comparison carousel (comfort yourself as you would comfort an upset friend) and in step 2 of the holiday-blues technique (would you scold another neurodivergent person who had the same year?). In both, the point is that the harshness we direct at ourselves is something we would not direct at others, and that we can extend the same treatment to ourselves [#44, #357].

#### Self-compassion appears inside the emotion wheel
On the Brené Brown–based wheel, self-compassion (самосострадание) is placed in the sector "Мы не справляемся" (we are not coping), together with shame, humiliation, guilt, perfectionism and embarrassment — i.e., among the places we go when we fall short [#253].

### Tools and resources mentioned

- Achievement / success diary (дневник достижений / дневник успехов) — a running list of anything you are proud of, from work success to brushing your teeth when you had no energy; recommended by the channel to strengthen self-esteem; needs ongoing new entries [#39, #41].
- Brené Brown, *Atlas of the Heart* («Атлас сердца») — the book on which the emotion wheel is based [#253].
- Russian emotion wheel (translation by Мария Варич, adaptation by Настя Полушкина, original by thecolorfultherapist) — 87 emotions grouped by "places we go when…", for learning to name emotions; full content transcribed above [#253].
- Notion template of the emotion wheel (premiumtemplates.notion.site; in English) — found by the forwarding author; lets you study your day and observe it through the prisms of feelings or moments. The author says she loves charts and tools that help simplify life and look at it from different angles [#253].

### Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- (!) INCOMPLETE: the "things you should not be ashamed of" card gives only labels with icons; the source does not explain any of the eight items [#61].
- (!) UNCLEAR: the post says the wheel contains 87 emotions; counting the labels visible on the wheel image gives 86 (спокойствие appears twice in "life is good"), so either one label is missing from the graphic or the count differs [#253, img #253].
- (!) UNCLEAR: some translated labels look ambiguous. "Унижение" (humiliation) appears both under "we are not coping" and under "high self-esteem" next to pride and arrogance; "смущение" (embarrassment/confusion) appears both under "we are not coping" and under "we encounter the incredible". The source does not clarify whether these are intended as different emotions in the two sectors [#253].
- The emotion-wheel post is forwarded from another channel and reflects that author's (Stasya Polushkina's) views, e.g. her claim that emotional literacy changes life for the better [#253].
- The claims about neurodivergent people (chronic overload, more energy spent on basics, more shame, burnout from constant adaptation) are stated by the channel without cited data [#356].

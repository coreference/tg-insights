# Neurodivergent burnout and chronic fatigue

## What this covers

This topic gathers three card series posted in the channel: a forwarded explainer from the channel Nerdivergent on burnout in neurodivergent people (where it comes from, how to recognise it, what to do), a translated series by @autisticrose on why many autistic people are tired all the time, and the channel author's (@ia_psy) own account of constant fatigue as an autistic person with ADHD and GAD (generalised anxiety disorder), with lists of what does and does not help her. The source treats burnout as something neurodivergent people often meet simply by living as students, office workers or parents in environments not adapted to them, and treats constant lack of energy as a central, painful symptom — the author calls it "самый грустный симптом для меня" (*the saddest symptom for me*) [msg #108, #234, #293].

## Core ideas

- **Burnout is built into neurodivergent life in social contexts.** Anyone can burn out at any time for many reasons, but for neurodivergent people burnout is often an inseparable part of existing in social settings, because spaces are unadapted and "unusual" traits are not accepted [msg #108].
- **Masking is the most frequent cause; overload is the second; missing resources and accommodations add to it** (see *Causes of neurodivergent burnout*) [msg #109].
- **Burnout is recognised by three indicators:** chronic fatigue not explained by other conditions, lowered tolerance to stimuli, loss of executive function [msg #110].
- **Recovery starts with root causes** (sensory load, masking, boundaries) and is supported by self-care, routine and neurodiversity-informed therapy [msg #111, #112].
- **Autistic tiredness has several distinct sources:** chronic illness, different sensory processing, less REM sleep, and the cost of being around people who do not understand you [msg #235, #236, #237, #238].
- **Techniques work only when practised consistently.** Physical activity helps when regular and gentle; a one-off intense effort when already exhausted backfires [msg #293].
- **Individual variation.** Both the Nerdivergent series and the author stress that not everyone's burnout or fatigue looks like what is described [msg #107, #293].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Causes of neurodivergent burnout
Purpose: to explain where burnout comes from in neurodivergent people (card "ОТКУДА ЭТО ВООБЩЕ?" — *Where does this even come from?*), from the Nerdivergent series [msg #109].

1. **Masking (most frequent factor).** Masking is defined as "сокрытие своих проявлений, чтобы соответствовать ожиданиям" (*hiding one's manifestations in order to meet the expectations*) of society, i.e. of some abstract "normality". It is named "Самыми частым фактором выгорания" (*the most frequent factor of burnout*) [msg #109].
2. **Overload (second cause).** Because a neurodivergent person must hide their natural behaviour *at the same time* as sustaining executive functions — concentration, productivity, punctuality, following plans — they get overloaded quickly, and this overload can itself be a second cause of burnout. The mechanism is the double load: suppressing oneself plus performing demanding executive tasks [msg #109].
3. **Lack of resources and accommodations.** The absence or shortage of necessary resources and accommodations can also contribute to the development of burnout [msg #109].

Context from the preceding card: the background condition is that many spaces are not adapted, and "unusual" manifestations are rejected both by society and by neurodivergent people themselves; this harms those who are forced to suppress those manifestations and the needs connected with them. The stress tied to one's place (school, office, home as a parent) and/or surroundings is what pushes people into burnout [msg #108].

### Three indicators of neurodivergent burnout
Purpose: to help a person tell whether they are in neurodivergent burnout (card titled "How to tell?"; OCR reads the heading as "KAK пОНЯТЬ?") [msg #110].

1. **Chronic fatigue not linked to other syndromes and conditions.** Signs you may notice: sleep disturbances, loss of strength and energy, headaches, apathy, and other similar signs. The key qualifier is that the fatigue is not explained by another condition [msg #110].
2. **Reduced tolerance to stimuli.** You may get overloaded more often and faster; it may become harder to be in social situations, to talk, and similar [msg #110].
3. **Loss of executive function.** It becomes harder to concentrate, memory worsens, and everyday tasks such as cleaning or work become harder to do [msg #110].

### Burnout recovery, part 1: working with root causes
Purpose: what to do first if you suspect burnout (card "ЧТО ДЕЛАТЬ?" — *What to do?*). The series advises working "в первую очередь … с первоисточниками" — first of all with the original sources of the burnout [msg #111].

1. **Reduce sensory load.** Where possible, avoid overloading places, wear more comfortable clothing, and use sensory protection: earplugs, headphones, dark glasses [msg #111].
2. **"Take off the mask" ("Снять маску").** Try more often to behave in the way most natural for you [msg #111].
3. **Set boundaries and remind people of them.** The card acknowledges that saying "no" can be genuinely hard, but stresses that it is important for recovery [msg #111].

### Burnout recovery, part 2: self-care and therapy
Purpose: additional supports (card "ЧТО ЕЩЁ ДЕЛАТЬ?" — *What else to do?*); self-care "в самых разных её проявлениях" (*in its most varied forms*) can help with burnout [msg #112].

1. **Do comfortable things.** Set aside time for your special interests and hyperfixations, favourite films and series, and eat comfort food [msg #112].
2. **Maintain routine.** "However banal it sounds", going to bed on time, eating several times a day and keeping up hygiene as far as possible really can help you cope [msg #112].
3. **Therapy — with a condition.** Therapy can also help with burnout, *provided* the therapist knows about your neurodivergence and about methods of working with it [msg #112].

### Four reasons autistic people are often tired (@autisticrose, translated by @ia_psy)
Purpose: to answer "Почему многие аутичные люди все время уставшие?" (*Why are many autistic people tired all the time?*) [msg #234].

1. **Chronic illnesses ("Хронические заболевания").** Many autistic people have chronic illnesses, many of which can cause fatigue; constantly living with pain and other symptoms can be very exhausting [msg #235].
2. **Sensory information ("Сенсорная информация").** Autistic brains process information differently from non-autistic brains, and therefore, the card says, "we" get tired faster [msg #236].
3. **Non-restorative sleep (Russian: невосстанавливающий сон).** Autistic people spend less time in REM sleep than non-autistic people — 15% compared with 25% (no study is named on the card) [msg #237].
4. **Other people and communication ("Другие люди и общение").** "Неважно, маскируемся мы или нет," (*It doesn't matter whether we mask or not,*) being around people who do not understand us is very tiring [msg #238].

### Personal fatigue map (@ia_psy, "Работаю из кровати: нейроотличия и усталость")
Purpose: the author's own structured account of constant tiredness ("Working from bed: neurodivergence and fatigue"), posted after her talk at the conference "Нейроотличники". She explicitly frames it as what happens *specifically to her* as an autistic person with ADHD and GAD; readers need not have the same problems. It is organised in four parts that can serve as a template for mapping one's own fatigue [msg #293].

**A. The problem (what it looks like)** [msg #294]:
1. She is a low-energy person, both physically and morally (emotionally).
2. She has no desire to do anything, even pleasant things.
3. She tends to spend all her free time lying down.
4. She gets worn out quickly when she leaves the house.
5. Sometimes she wakes up with brain fog ("туманом в голове").
6. She almost never has spontaneous bursts of energy.
7. "Я даже работаю из кровати." (*I even work from bed.*)

**B. Why it happens (her causes)** [msg #295]:
1. Sleep problems.
2. Constant overload — from internal and external stimuli.
3. Body pain.
4. Anxiety.
5. Social situations and their unpredictability.
6. Low physical activity.
7. Difficulty planning work and rest.
8. Delayed information processing ("Отложенная обработка информации").
9. Masking.

**C. What does not help her** [msg #296]:
1. Coffee.
2. Short, intense physical activity.
3. Doing breathing techniques once (a single, one-off session).
4. Loud sounds and bright light.
5. Pretending she has no problems and loading herself heavily.

**D. What helps her** [msg #297]:
1. Knowing what tires her most.
2. Control over how much she works and rests — keeping a balance.
3. Rest without obligations ("Отдых без обязательств!").
4. Antidepressants and therapy.
5. Attention to her body and emotional state.
6. Relieving pain ("Купировать боли").
7. Not spending much time in places where she is sensorily uncomfortable.
8. Crawling out outside and moving more — "(в долгосрочной перспективе)" (*in the long term*).
9. Unmasking where possible and safe.
10. Accepting her limitations.

## Detailed insights

### Why physical activity both helps and does not help
The author explains the apparent contradiction between "low physical activity" being a cause of her fatigue and "short intense physical activity" being on her does-not-help list. According to research (she does not name the studies), physical activity improves mental health if it is done *consistently* — even if it is just walking. She also feels better when she does undemanding physical activity *several times a week*. But if she is tired and, after a week of sitting at home, tries to perk herself up with a *single intense workout*, she simply collapses ("вырублюсь") afterwards. Her conclusion, delivered ironically: "Техники работают, если делать их стабильно (кто бы мог подумать)" (*Techniques work if you do them consistently (who would have thought)*). The same logic explains why a one-off breathing exercise is on the does-not-help list and why moving more is qualified as helpful "in the long term" [msg #293, #296, #297].

### Burnout is common, but its presentation varies
The Nerdivergent series opens by framing spring as a time to notice one's burnout "and do something about it (if you want, of course)", and closes with a caveat: of course not everyone's burnout shows up exactly as described, but the authors hope the advice can help [msg #107].

### Masking as a thread through both burnout and fatigue
Masking appears in every series: as the most frequent cause of burnout [msg #109], with unmasking as a first-line remedy [msg #111]; in @autisticrose's cards as not the whole story — even without masking, being among people who do not understand you is exhausting [msg #238]; and in the author's personal map as one of nine causes, with unmasking listed as helpful only "там, где возможно и безопасно" (*where possible and safe*) [msg #295, #297].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The author's fatigue lists describe her own experience as an autistic person with ADHD and GAD; she states others need not have the same problems [msg #293]. Likewise Nerdivergent notes burnout does not show up the same way in everyone [msg #107].
- (!) INCOMPLETE: the REM-sleep figure (15% vs 25%) is given on the card without a named study or population [msg #237]; the claim that consistent physical activity improves mental health is attributed to unnamed "research" [msg #293].
- The first indicator of burnout requires that the chronic fatigue is *not* linked to other syndromes and conditions [msg #110], while @autisticrose's cards and the author's list name chronic illness and pain as causes of autistic tiredness [msg #235, #295]; the sources do not discuss how to tell burnout-related fatigue apart from illness-related fatigue. (!) UNCLEAR how the two framings relate.
- Therapy is recommended only conditionally — the therapist must be informed about the person's neurodivergence and how to work with it [msg #112].

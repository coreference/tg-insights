# The neurodiversity paradigm, terminology and myths about autism

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's foundational vocabulary post ("Про нейроразнообразие и нейроотличия" — "About neurodiversity and neurodivergence") with its summary card set "Нейроразнообразие: основные термины" ("Neurodiversity: basic terms") [msg #12] [img #13], plus three later reposts: a myth-busting card about what autistic people can be and do [img #38], a reposted rebuke of functioning comparisons between autistic/ADHD people [msg #195], and a post on autism awareness vs acceptance with a study summary on neurodiversity training for hospital staff [msg #304]. The author explains why the new words are needed at all: the words "нейроразнообразие", "нейроотличный" and "нейротипичный" are increasingly heard, mostly when discussing autism and ADHD, and the post sets out what they mean and why "new words to talk about familiar conditions" are used [msg #12]. According to the source, the stakes are the quality of life of neurodivergent people: much of their suffering stems from discrimination and non-inclusive environments, and how people talk about them signals respect or its absence [msg #12] [img #19].

## Core ideas

- **Brains vary like eye and hair colour.** Neurodiversity is the natural variety in how brains are built and work; the idea of a single "normal" or "healthy" brain type is treated as a stereotype that does not benefit society [msg #12] [img #14].
- **Not every neurodivergence is a disease.** In the neurodiversity paradigm only conditions that interfere with life and cause suffering, and that can be separated from the person (e.g. epilepsy), are diseases; autism and ADHD shape the whole personality, cannot be separated or cured, and are accepted and adapted to instead (see *Neurodiversity paradigm criterion*) [msg #12].
- **Much of the difficulty is social.** Many autistic, ADHD and dyslexic people are hindered not by their condition but by discrimination and the lack of an inclusive environment [msg #12].
- **Support is still needed.** The paradigm does not deny difficulties or the need for medical/psychological help; inclusion work and clinical help serve the same goal (see *Critics of the movement and the author's answer*) [msg #12].
- **Words signal respect.** Using "neurodivergent" does not deny diagnoses; using the words neurodivergent people ask for shows you don't see them as mere "deviations from the norm" [img #19].
- **Stereotypes and comparisons invalidate.** Autism is compatible with many traits people assume it excludes [img #38], and one autistic/ADHD person's capability is no yardstick for others [msg #195].
- **Acceptance goes beyond awareness.** Awareness is knowing autistic people exist; acceptance is respecting, accommodating and valuing them, and training can shift professionals toward it [msg #304].

## Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

### Neurodiversity paradigm criterion: which neurodivergences are diseases
Purpose: to distinguish, within the paradigm, between neurodivergences that should be treated as illnesses and those that are ways of being. The source gives two categories, each with an example and reasoning [msg #12].
1. **Neurodivergences that are diseases.** Only those forms of neurodivergence "которые мешают людям жить" (which prevent people from living) and cause them suffering are considered diseases. Example: epilepsy can be called a disease because people with epilepsy suffer from seizures that worsen their quality of life. Such diseases can easily be separated from the person's personality: "Если избавить человека от эпилепсии, то личность его фундаментально не поменяется" (If you rid a person of epilepsy, their personality will not fundamentally change) [msg #12].
2. **Neurodivergences that are not diseases.** Other neurodivergences affect all spheres of a person's life. Autism and ADHD influence how a person perceives the world, thinks and communicates, what interests them, what comes easily and what does not. They are "невозможно отделить от личности человека" (impossible to separate from the person's personality) in a way that leaves the person still themselves. In the paradigm such conditions are not considered illnesses and also cannot be cured; the person and those around them can only accept the neurodivergence and adapt to it [msg #12].
Corollary given by the author: many autistic people, and people with ADHD or dyslexia, are troubled "не их состояние" (not by their condition) but by discrimination and the absence of an inclusive environment; suffering can result from barriers imposed by social norms, leading to social isolation and inequality [msg #12].

### Classification of neurodivergences by origin
The source divides neurodivergences into three kinds [msg #12] [img #17]:
1. **Innate (врожденные)** — e.g. autism, ADHD, dyslexia (and others, as the source itself says).
2. **Acquired (приобретенные)** — e.g. brain injuries, conditions caused by long-term alcohol use (and others, as the source itself says).
3. **A combination of innate and acquired factors.**

### Conditions commonly counted as neurodivergences
The author notes that classifications differ from one another, but they often include the following nine groups [msg #12] [img #18]:
1. Autism spectrum disorders (РАС, ASD).
2. Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (СДВГ, ADHD).
3. Down syndrome.
4. Specific learning disorders — dyslexia, dysgraphia, dyscalculia.
5. Dyspraxia.
6. Intellectual developmental disorders.
7. Tourette syndrome.
8. Sensory processing disorders.
9. Mental disorders such as bipolar disorder (БАР), obsessive-compulsive disorder (ОКР) and others.

### "You can be autistic and..." (myth-busting list)
A card by @autieselfcare, translated by the channel and posted under the hashtag #поддержка (support), titled "ВЫ МОЖЕТЕ БЫТЬ АУТИЧНЫМИ И..." (You can be autistic and...). Its purpose is to counter stereotypes that would make someone "not autistic enough": each item is a trait or experience that people often assume excludes autism. The full list of 22 items (left column, then right column, as on the card) [img #38]:
1. be in romantic relationships;
2. have a job;
3. have a social life;
4. look people in the eyes;
5. use sarcasm often;
6. be empathetic;
7. express emotions;
8. communicate well;
9. not like trains;
10. understand emotions;
11. enjoy intimate moments;
12. have a sense of humour;
13. not be a white cis man;
14. not have sensory sensitivities/features;
15. have trouble with maths/sciences;
16. not have a special interest ("не иметь специнтереса");
17. look attractive;
18. make phone calls;
19. take care of your hygiene;
20. like fiction;
21. have imagination;
22. have well-developed speech.
The implicit lesson of the card: none of these — relationships, employment, eye contact, empathy, emotional expression and understanding, humour, imagination, good speech, being a woman or non-white or trans, lacking sensory issues or a special interest, struggling with STEM — rules out being autistic [img #38].

### Awareness vs acceptance
Forwarded from Стася Полушкина's channel on 2 April, Autism Awareness Day, with all of April described as Autism Acceptance Month [msg #304].
1. **Awareness** — Kaylynn Partlow: "Осведомлённость об аутизме — это когда ты знаешь, кто я..." (Autism awareness is when you know who I am...). Building on Patrick Casale, the post's author frames awareness as knowing that autistic people exist [msg #304].
2. **Acceptance** — Partlow: "Принятие аутизма — это когда ты рад видеть меня" (Autism acceptance is when you are glad to see me). Acceptance is "нечто гораздо большее" (something much more); it says: "Аутистичные люди заслуживают уважения, адаптации и того, чтобы их ценили такими, какие они есть" (Autistic people deserve respect, accommodation and to be valued as they are). Concretely it means: neurodivergence-affirming stance, listening to autistic voices, supporting self-advocacy, making the world inclusive and breaking stigma — a shift "от «исправления» к адаптации" (from "fixing" to adaptation) and respect for different ways of thinking and communicating [msg #304].
The author considers it important to keep talking about what happens in the neurodivergent community and to advocate for a world where autistic people can live fully, authentically and safely [msg #304].

## Detailed insights

### Definitions
- **Neurodiversity (нейроразнообразие)** is the diversity of brain structure and functioning in different people. The author's analogy: just as people differ in eye and hair colour, brain functioning can differ too [msg #12] [img #14].
- **The neurodiversity movement** is built on this idea of variability. Its supporters advocate civil rights, equality, respect and full social inclusion for neurodivergent people. The notion that there is one "normal" or "healthy" type of brain or neurocognitive functioning is regarded as a stereotype that does not benefit society [msg #12].
- **Neurodivergent (нейроотличный) / neurodivergence (нейроотличие).** If a person's brain works substantially differently from the norm accepted in society, the person is called neurodivergent and the state of their brain can be called a neurodivergence [msg #12] [img #15].
- **Neurotypical (нейротипичный).** People whose brain functioning relatively corresponds to social standards of «нормальности» ("normality"); accordingly they have no mental disorders or other neurodivergences [msg #12] [img #16].

### Critics of the movement and the author's answer
The author states that the neurodiversity movement has "немало противников" (quite a few opponents). One objection: treating autism or ADHD as natural variations of brain functioning implies that neurodivergent people do not need support. The author's reply is explicit: "*Поддержка нужна*" (Support is needed). Neurodivergent people acknowledge that they have difficulties, but many of these difficulties are tied to discrimination, social expectations and the environment [msg #12].
The author's position on what to do: it is important to fight for greater awareness of neurodivergence, reduced stigma, inclusive environments and accommodations. Until society achieves this, it is "особенно важно" (especially important) for neurodivergent people to receive medical and/or psychological help. In the end the fight for inclusion and acceptance and medical/psychological help serve "одной и той же цели" (one and the same goal) — improving neurodivergent people's quality of life [msg #12].

### Why the terminology matters
The final card of the terms set argues: using the term "нейроотличный" (neurodivergent) does not mean denying diagnoses and the difficulties associated with them. By using the words neurodivergent people ask you to use, you show that you respect them and do not regard them simply as «отклонения от нормы» ("deviations from the norm") that need to be fitted to society's standards [img #19].

### Functioning comparisons invalidate others
A post reposted from the channel "Нейропанк" (tags #нейропанк_аутизм, #нейропанк_напоминания — autism, reminders), translating a social-media post by purpleflamebOi, rebukes the line «У меня аутизм и СДВГ, и я все равно все ещё могу делать это, отмазки кончились!!» ("I have autism and ADHD and I can still do this, no more excuses!!"; English original: "I have autism and ADHD yet I can still do this, you have no excuse !!"). The answer: "Заткнись. Не все функционируют так, как ты." (Shut up. Not everyone functions the way you do.) Not everyone can get employed the way you can; not everyone can go through life the way you do; "Не все такие, как ты." (Not everyone is like you.) The lesson: one neurodivergent person's level of functioning, employment or daily coping cannot be used as a standard to dismiss another's difficulties as "excuses" [msg #195].

## Evidence and numbers

**Study on autistic-doctor-led neurodiversity training in an Australian hospital.** The forwarded post says a recent study in Australia showed how neurodiversity training helps doctors better understand and accept autistic people; the author translated the study's summary card, which is based on work by Bernard S. et al. published in *Neurodiversity* (2025) and titled "What happens when autistic doctors teach hospital teams about neurodivergence?" [msg #304] [img #304]:
- *Why it was done:* negative attitudes (prejudices) toward autistic people can hinder quality medical care. Neurodiversity training could help, but a hospital in Western Australia was not doing it. To fix this, neurodiverse doctors and researchers designed a project and delivered training to teams working in the hospital [img #304].
- *How it was done:* the study leads met with hospital teams and learned what they thought about the training; feedback showed they wanted to learn more about experience of working in the hospital. Three teams of doctors and nurses went through the training. The training was delivered by autistic, neurodiverse doctors who were leaders of hospital teams (the OCR renders the word as "артистичные" — an OCR misreading; the card itself reads "аутистичные", autistic) [img #304].
- *Results:* 9 out of 10 said the training was useful; 8 out of 10 felt more confident supporting autistic people; 9 out of 10 became more accepting of autistic people; neurodiversity (training) helped overcome prejudices; the hospital teams wanted to learn more about supporting neurodiverse people; they changed their attitudes to better understand neurodiverse people [img #304].
- *Authors' claim:* to their knowledge, this study is the first in an Australian hospital to demonstrate that neurodiversity training led by clinician doctors leads to greater confidence and acceptance of autistic people [img #304].
(!) INCOMPLETE: the card gives no sample size, no comparison group and no description of the training's content or duration [img #304].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The author hedges that classifications of neurodivergence "отличаются друг от друга" (differ from one another); the nine-item list is what they "often" include, not a fixed canon [msg #12].
- The disease/non-disease distinction is presented as the position of the neurodiversity paradigm, and the source itself records that the movement has many opponents; the objection discussed is that the paradigm implies no support is needed, which the author rejects [msg #12].
- Definitional tension (as stated in the source): neurotypical people are defined as having no "mental disorders" or other neurodivergences, and mental disorders such as bipolar disorder and OCD are listed among neurodivergences, while elsewhere the paradigm says only some neurodivergences are diseases [msg #12]. (!) UNCLEAR: the source does not say whether bipolar disorder or OCD fall on the "disease" or "not a disease" side of the paradigm's criterion.
- OCR notes: the list card's OCR reads "дистрафия", a misreading of дисграфия (dysgraphia) as in the text post [img #18] [msg #12]; the study card's "артистичные" is a misreading of "аутистичные" (verified against the image) [img #304].
- (!) UNCLEAR: the closing line of #304 says the author advocates a world where "аутентичные люди" (authentic people) can live fully, authentically and safely — probably a slip for "аутистичные" (autistic), but the source text reads "аутентичные" [sic] [msg #304].
- The study results are self-reported attitude changes from three hospital teams in one hospital, as summarised on a translated card; no further methodology is given [img #304].

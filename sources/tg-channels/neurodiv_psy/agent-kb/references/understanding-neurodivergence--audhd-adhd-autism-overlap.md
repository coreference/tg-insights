# AuDHD: how ADHD and autism overlap and conflict

## What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's material on the co-occurrence of ADHD (СДВГ) and autism: comorbidity statistics in children and adults, shared genetic factors, the traits the two conditions share versus those specific to each, the opposing needs that make people with both (AuDHD, АуСДВГ) feel like a walking contradiction, the distinction between special interests, hyperfixations and hyperfocus, why AuDHD is usually more impairing than either condition alone, and why rest is especially hard with AuDHD. The author writes from personal experience — she says she is both autistic and has ADHD — and stresses that the two diagnoses are "in some ways very similar and in some ways completely opposite", which is exactly what makes life at their intersection difficult [msg #26, #70].

## Core ideas

- **ADHD and autism frequently co-occur.** One and the same person can be both autistic and have ADHD (the author gives herself as an example); the conditions share many traits but are opposite in others [msg #26].
- **The co-occurrence is a new research area.** Until 2013 the two were considered mutually exclusive diagnoses ("if you have ADHD you cannot have autism, and vice versa"); recent research shows they occur together very often [msg #70].
- **Shared traits, different causes.** Many difficulties (executive function, social cognition, interoception, emotional regulation) appear in both, sometimes for different underlying reasons [msg #26, #67].
- **Opposing needs create internal conflict.** Autistic needs (routine, the familiar, a constant special interest, plans kept) collide with ADHD tendencies (disorganization, craving novelty, switching interests, lateness) [msg #68, #70].
- **AuDHD is usually harder than either alone**, and ADHD symptoms are usually addressed first [msg #69, #70].
- **Three different "interest/focus" phenomena** — special interest (autism, lifelong), hyperfixation (ADHD, months), hyperfocus (ADHD, hours) — are often confused and should be distinguished [msg #33, #37].
- **Rest is hard with AuDHD** because one part needs stimulation and fun while the other needs calm and quiet [msg #361].

### Eight overlapping traits of ADHD and autism
A text list (author: neurodivergent_insights, posted by the channel) explaining in more depth what ADHD and autism share [msg #26]:
1. **Executive-function difficulties** — problems organizing tasks, with object permanence, memory, concentration, decision-making and attention regulation [msg #26].
2. **Social cognition** — both groups have difficulties, but for different reasons. In people with ADHD the difficulties may stem from inattention and impulsivity; in autistic people, from trouble intuitively perceiving social cues, body language and understanding subtext [msg #26].
3. **Interoception problems** — interoception is the ability to sense the signals your body sends (hunger, thirst, the need to go to the toilet, emotions). It matters for emotional regulation: a person with interoceptive difficulties finds it harder to understand and respond flexibly to their internal states and emotions [msg #26].
4. **Emotional-regulation difficulties** — due to various factors (brain functioning, interoception, rigidity and sensory particularities), regulating emotions may be harder [msg #26].
5. **Increased risk of OCD** — given as 30–50% [msg #26].
6. **Alcohol dependence** — 5–10 times more common in people with ADHD, and 2 times more common in autistic people [msg #26].
7. **Increased risk of self-harm and suicidal behaviour** [msg #26].
8. **A higher share of queer people** among autistic people and people with ADHD [msg #26].

A shorter version of the shared-traits list appears on the AuDHD cards and in the AuDHD post: difficulties with executive functioning (attention, organization/self-organization, decision-making and other functions); communication particularities, "хоть и разного рода" (*though of different kinds*); emotional-regulation problems; difficulties understanding the body's internal signals (hunger, thirst, when it's time to go to the toilet); sensory particularities; altered perception of time; hyperfocus; stimming; and other traits [img #67, msg #70]. Note that this version adds **altered time perception** and **hyperfocus**, which are not in the eight-item list [img #67].

### AuDHD as a "walking contradiction": four opposing pairs
The card states that people with AuDHD sometimes feel "как ходячее противоречие" (*like a walking contradiction*), because autistic and ADHD traits pull in opposite directions. Each pair: autism side ↔ ADHD side [img #68]:
1. **Striving for routine and order** ↔ **difficulties with organization and planning.** The person needs order but struggles to create it [img #68, msg #70].
2. **Wish to stick to familiar things** ↔ **craving for new experiences.** The post words it as the ADHD "жажда спонтанности и новых впечатлений" (*thirst for spontaneity and new impressions*) versus the autistic drive to keep to what is already familiar [img #68, msg #70].
3. **Need for a constant special interest** ↔ **switching interests to stay engaged** [img #68].
4. **Distress when others break plans (e.g. being late)** ↔ **difficulty arriving on time oneself** [img #68].

The author adds that these differences "могут сильно затруднять существование человека с ауСДВГ" (*can make the existence of a person with AuDHD very difficult*) [msg #70].

### Special interest vs hyperfixation vs hyperfocus
Purpose: the three terms come up constantly in discussions of ADHD and autism, and the last two even sound alike; the series (title card "Специнтересы, гиперфиксации или гиперфокус?" — *Special interests, hyperfixations or hyperfocus?*) explains how to tell them apart [img #33, msg #37].

1. **Special (особый/специальный) interest — autism, long-term.**
   - What: a strong, long-lasting interest (often lifelong) that autistic people are very focused on [img #34, msg #37].
   - Function: helps autistic people understand the world around them and regulate their emotions; can help perceive the world and better understand emotions and relationships [img #34, msg #37].
   - Significance: incredibly important to autistic people and often felt as part of their identity; usually a big source of joy [msg #37].
   - Social side: sometimes it can be the only topic an autistic person is able to talk about, especially if they are not very good at masking [msg #37].
   - Life impact: often stays for life and can influence the choice of university, job and similar [msg #37].
   - Shape over time: focused, narrow and long-term; never disappears completely but is not necessarily equally intense in different periods of life [msg #37].
2. **Hyperfixation — ADHD, days to months.**
   - What: an intense, all-consuming interest lasting from a few days to a few months (sometimes people speak of several years) [img #35, msg #37]. (The card says "до нескольких месяцев (или лет)" — up to several months or years.)
   - Objects: shows, film franchises, books, ideas, food and even people [msg #37].
   - Mechanism: during a hyperfixation you absorb information at enormous speed and the brain gets the stimulation it was lacking — the card frames it as helping the brain cope with understimulation (недостимуляция). When the novelty wears off, interest gradually fades [img #35, msg #37].
   - Who: usually associated with ADHD but can occur in other neurodivergent people [msg #37].
3. **Hyperfocus — ADHD, hours.**
   - What: a short (up to a few hours) feeling/state of deep concentration on something, during which it is almost impossible to tear yourself away, and you neglect all other needs including basic ones — food, sleep and work [img #36, msg #37].
   - Object: you can hyperfocus on your hyperfixation, but also on other things, for example cleaning or reading a story [msg #37].
   - Who: usually linked to ADHD but can occur in other neurodivergent people [msg #37].

Key distinguishing axis: duration (lifelong → months → hours) and nature (an identity-level interest vs a novelty-driven interest vs a state of concentration). Hyperfocus is a state that can attach to a hyperfixation or to anything else, whereas special interests and hyperfixations are interests [msg #37].

## Detailed insights

### What "AuDHD" means and the history of the diagnosis
АуСДВГ (AuDHD) is the term used to denote autism + ADHD together, because these neurodivergences often occur together and share many traits [img #65, msg #70]. The author explains that research on autism together with ADHD is a very new field: until 2013 they were considered two mutually exclusive diagnoses — if you had ADHD you could not have autism, and vice versa. The latest research shows that autism and ADHD very often occur together [msg #70]. She writes as "человек, который сам живет с ауСДВГ" (*a person who herself lives with AuDHD*) and knows first-hand what living at the intersection of these neurodivergences is like [msg #70].

### Rare "cancelling out" vs the usual heavier burden
In rare cases autism and ADHD symptoms seem to cancel each other out: a person with AuDHD may have less visible social difficulties thanks to (ADHD) sociability, or a weaker drive toward routine because of disorganization and spontaneity [msg #70]. But most often people with AuDHD have a harder time than people with only autism or only ADHD, and experience more serious functional impairment [img #69, msg #70]. In such cases the usual advice is to work first on reducing ADHD symptoms — for example by trying medication, individual psychotherapy or skills training [img #69, msg #70].

### Why rest is hard with AuDHD
A post forwarded from the channel Доказательная Жабля describes the author's weekend spent hyperfocusing on rest for neurodivergent people, trying to understand why rest is so hard with AuDHD [msg #361]. Her description of the mechanism:
- It is as if two different people live inside — "одному нужна стимуляция и веселье, а другому — покой и тишина" (*one needs stimulation and fun, the other calm and quiet*) [msg #361].
- When you try to do nothing, the brain immediately starts throwing up ideas [msg #361].
- When you try to do something, you fall into thoughts unrelated to the task [msg #361].
She says she studied several studies and built a tool where you mark your current brain state and receive tips on what to do and why (see *Tools and resources mentioned*). (!) ## Evidence and numbers

- 22–83% of autistic children meet diagnostic criteria for ADHD (estimates; Sokolova et al., 2017, as cited by the author) [msg #26, img #66, msg #70].
- 30–65% of children with ADHD have pronounced / clinically significant levels of autistic traits (Sokolova et al., 2017) [msg #26, img #66, msg #70].
- In adults, the co-occurrence rate is 20–37%; the author notes there is still little research on adults and these are estimates from some studies [img #66, msg #70].
- ADHD and autism share about 50–72% of contributing genetic factors [img #66, msg #70].
- OCD risk is increased (30–50%) in ADHD and autism [msg #26].
- Alcohol dependence: 5–10 times more common with ADHD; 2 times more common in autistic people [msg #26].
- Before 2013 the two diagnoses were considered mutually exclusive [msg #70].

## Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The co-occurrence ranges are very wide (22–83%, 30–65%) and described as estimates; adult data are scarce ("Исследований про взрослых людей пока мало" — *there is little research on adults so far*) [msg #26, msg #70].
- (!) UNCLEAR: the 30–50% OCD figure and the alcohol-dependence ratios are given without a source or a definition of the base population [msg #26].
- (!) CONTRADICTION (minor): "impulse control problems" are placed in the shared zone of the Venn diagram, while "impulsivity" sits in the ADHD-only zone [img #25]. Hyperfixation duration is "up to several months (or years)" on the card vs "from a few days to a few months (sometimes people speak of several years)" in the text [img #35, msg #37].
- The "cancelling out" effect is described as rare; the more common pattern is heavier impairment [msg #70].
- Hyperfixation and hyperfocus are "usually" ADHD-associated, not exclusive to it [msg #37].

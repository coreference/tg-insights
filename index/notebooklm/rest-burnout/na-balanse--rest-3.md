## Burnout: prevention and exit

### What this covers

This topic gathers everything the channel taught about burnout: what it is made of (four kinds of energy, a finite resource), how to measure how close you are (a 16-question test), the red flags and their counter-moves, why passion for work is a common entry route, how a "get sick to rest" fantasy is a symptom, and how Katya and guest experts exited burnout and prevent it now. According to the source it matters because, as with any "illness", preventing burnout is easier than getting out of it — Katya needed to quit her job and spend more than two years restoring her health and psyche [#127].

### Core ideas

- Energy is a finite resource, not a bottomless well. One's state and energy must be replenished regularly so they do not run out [#127].
- There are four kinds of energy, not one — physical, emotional, intellectual and spiritual — and all of them have to be watched; physical is the base (see *Four kinds of energy*) [#127].
- Burnout = prolonged deprivation of all kinds of energy; the antidote = regaining the ability to feel. If I understand what I want, what is happening to me and why, I will take care of myself (see *Recipe vs antidote*) [#127].
- Prevention is easier than exit; exit is only "through". Once burnout has happened, the only way is through it, in small, patient steps; if the test says you are scorching, start now and seek a specialist if you can't [#127].
- Passion is a frequent entry route. Burnout often starts with a crazy love for one's work; what looks like "high engagement" is often already a signal of lost balance, and unmanaged enthusiasm turns work into a form of addiction (Olga Sorina) [#451].
- Rest fantasies are symptoms. "It would be nice to get sick so I can rest" — and even a romantic thought about a vacation — signals exhaustion that likely started much earlier [#236].
- Boundaries, self-knowledge and therapy are the recurring levers in both guest stories and Katya's own experience [#207, #377].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Four kinds of energy
Purpose: a model for what "energy" consists of, used to diagnose where you are depleted. Katya and Masha Tsegelnik presented it at the stream "how not to burn out" (the stream announcement promised to discuss what energy consists of and how to replenish it, to share personal experience, and how to take care of yourself so burnout doesn't happen) [#125, #127].
1. Physical energy — sleep, nutrition, movement. It is the *base*: without normal sleep, food and movement it will be hard to restore the other spheres, "because on a chemical level we won't be able to" [#127].
2. Emotional energy — irritation/anxiety levels, time with loved ones, enjoyable activities, noticing one's own merits [#127].
3. Intellectual energy — focus, strategic vs "firefighting" work, time for reflection, planning and creativity, not working evenings/weekends [#127].
4. Spiritual energy — spending time and energy on what you do best, love most and consider truly important; deciding from your own goals rather than demands; positively influencing the world [#127].
Katya adds a direction-of-travel framing: through our actions, habits and deeds we are always moving somewhere — either toward premature death or toward flourishing: «Решать вам.» ("It's up to you.") [#127].

#### Burnout proximity test ("Тест: Насколько близко выгорание?")
Purpose: check your "level of scorching". Made by the authors of "На балансе", based on the Harvard Business Review article by Tony Schwartz and Catherine McCarthy. How to use: answer 16 yes/no statements, count the "yes" answers, look at the result, then see which sphere sags the most and start with it [#127].

Physical energy [#127]:
1. I regularly don't sleep 7–8 hours and often wake up tired.
2. I often skip breakfast or make do with whatever is at hand.
3. There is little movement in my life.
4. I rarely take breaks from work and often eat lunch at the computer.

Emotional energy [#127]:
5. I often feel irritation and anxiety («Я часто чувствую раздражение и тревогу»).
6. I don't have enough time for family and loved ones, and when I'm with them I'm often distracted.
7. I have no time for activities that bring me pleasure.
8. I rarely notice my achievements and feel pride and gratitude.

Intellectual energy [#127]:
9. It's hard for me to concentrate on one thing; I'm easily distracted.
10. Most of the day I "put out fires" instead of doing something strategically important.
11. I don't devote enough time to reflection, strategic planning and creativity.
12. I work evenings/weekends and often check email on vacation.

Spiritual energy [#127]:
13. I don't devote enough time to what I do best and what I love most.
14. I don't spend most of my time and energy on what I consider truly important.
15. My decisions are driven more by demands than by understanding my own goals.
16. I make no effort to positively influence the world and the people around me.

Scoring — number of "yes" answers [#127]:
- 0–3 — you are in excellent condition.
- 4–6 — you are still resourced, but there is already something to think about.
- 7–10 — you clearly have a resource deficit; burnout is close.
- 11–16 — "Oops, it seems you are already in crisis."

What to do with the result: if you took the test and realised you are scorching, don't wait — start right now. Katya says she has no doubt you know what to do; if you don't know, can't, doubt or don't believe in yourself, go to a specialist: «Не затягивайте, умоляю.» ("Don't put it off, I beg you.") [#127].

#### Recipe for burnout vs antidote to burnout
The core theses from the stream with Masha Tsegelnik [#127]:
- Recipe: arrange for yourself, for a prolonged time, deprivation across all kinds of energy. Katya adds: «я точно знаю, я так делала» ("I know for sure, I did it that way").
- Antidote: learn to feel, return to yourself the capacity to sense (чувствование), find time for reflection. The mechanism: «Если я понимаю, что я хочу, что со мной происходит и почему это так, я буду заботиться о себе.» ("If I understand what I want, what is happening to me and why, I will take care of myself.")
- Pauses as the delivery mechanism: life and the calendar need pauses — unfilled moments with free attention; these are exactly the opportunity to feel.
- If burnout has already happened: the only way is "through" (Katya uses the English word, "as we say here in the US") — in small steps, patiently.
- Main advice: believe that your life can be different, even if you are currently turned 180 degrees away from routines and practices of healthy energy, and imagine that life.

#### Red flags of burnout and what to do instead (Mila Plyushch)
Guest author Mila Plyushch (CMO at Yandex, author of the blog "Невыгоревшие") lists the red flags she failed to notice for too long before burning out, each paired with what she does now [#207]:
1. Red flag: thinking only about work, at any hour of day and night. It still happens, but when it drags on for months or years — «привет, уголек» ("hello, ember"). What she does: techniques like in meditation — when she catches herself chewing "work mental gum" after hours, she calmly stops it without judgment, telling herself the thought can wait for working hours: «наш блестящий ум никуда не денется» ("our brilliant mind won't go anywhere") and will produce an equally good solution as the one it generated "in the toilet at 3:36 a.m." [#207].
2. Red flag: constant overwork. It usually creeps in gradually: working hours drift a bit into the evening; "I'll do an important presentation on the weekend"; "I'll answer a couple of urgent messages on vacation" — and that becomes the norm. What she does: sets boundaries — both for herself and for other people. Declining an after-hours meeting (especially with the boss) used to seem wild; now she has the skill: «Извини, в 20 не могу - давай назначим в рабочее время, пожалуйста» ("Sorry, I can't at 8 pm — let's schedule it during working hours, please"). She still catches herself to avoid explaining reasons ("I need to pick up my son"), because a valid reason can even be wanting to lie on the couch and stare at the ceiling: «Нерабочее время нам на то и дано, чтобы не работать.» ("Non-working time is given to us precisely so as not to work.") [#207].
3. Red flag: tolerating what she didn't like — accepting toxic self-talk and a team culture of relationships and communication alien to her as a given; crying in the toilet after meetings and scolding herself for being so sensitive. What she does: gets to know herself — what categorically doesn't suit her versus what she can change her attitude to — and takes back responsibility for choices: she consciously chooses whether to stay or leave. This is far calmer than living in the state of «оно само со мной так происходит, а я просто страдаю тут бедная» ("it just happens to me on its own, and I'm just poor me suffering here") [#207].
4. Red flag: ignoring her needs — going to work sick, not noticing she had needed the toilet for three meetings in a row or had skipped two meals; work was more important than everything, even the need for rest. What she does: studies herself and now knows when she needs rest (or lunch, or the toilet). At the point of full burnout only a 4-month sabbatical (prolonged rest without work) helped, but when you monitor yourself regularly, recovery is easier [#207].

Mila's key caveat: many of these counter-moves would not have been possible without regular psychotherapy, which helped her find inner supports, feel out her boundaries and learn to defend them — she "won't tire of recommending" it [#207].

#### Balancing enthusiasm and burnout (Olga Sorina)
Context: Katya says burnout often begins with crazy love for work and that this was likely her case — when she joined advertising after reading Beigbeder, she was so gripped by excitement that she wanted to work all the time, answer emails until 1 a.m., skip lunches, weekends and vacations: «Так я начала брать силы в долг сама у себя.» ("That's how I started borrowing energy from myself on credit.") [#451].

Olga Sorina's mechanism: the paradox is that what looks like "high engagement" is often already a signal of losing balance; unmanaged enthusiasm makes work a form of addiction (she says WHO is seriously considering workaholism on a par with other addictions). You do something useful, get recognition, feel meaning — but gradually stop breathing, eating, resting, living outside work. This happens because work has everything needed: a goal, a result, feedback — a clear scheme for getting joy and satisfaction. Life outside work lacks such clear confirmations of being needed and such vivid feedback; we simply live with other people who have their own wants, needs and demands of us. In life you must invent for yourself why you do something, where to find joy, and how to feel "good enough" without external evaluation and without always being useful to others [#451].

What helps keep the balance [#451]:
1. Track the moment engagement becomes unmanageable. If it is hard to tear yourself away, that is not a sign of passion but a warning bell. Trying to stop triggers rationalisations: "if I finish now, tomorrow will be easier", "people will look at me strangely if I drop it unfinished", "if I keep indulging myself and resting a lot, things will stall", "you're not that tired, work is more important". All are ways of convincing yourself to continue, built on fragile grounds and ignoring long-term consequences. Remedies: ask loved ones to help you finish on time, or schedule enjoyable fixed-time activities right after work — theatre, meeting friends, a workout.
2. Create interest outside work. Not necessarily a hobby — anything that also engages and gives a sense of aliveness, perhaps relationships with friends and loved ones. Ask yourself where your joy is and whether you feel satisfaction and interest in your life as a whole. The questions are hard, but without answers you cannot rebuild the work situation in the long term.
3. Learn to be "useless". Sometimes it is important to "be" rather than "do"; allowing yourself uselessness is a form of self-care. Many treat themselves as a function, so when they aren't performing a role they immediately feel wasted time, loneliness, meaninglessness. Try "useless" time doing what you like even without much sense in it — at least 5–10 minutes a day.

Her definition: «Баланс — когда мы вовлечены, но не растворяемся в работе.» ("Balance is when we are engaged but don't dissolve in work.") Balance is not rigidly allotted time; we can never build a static system. Those prone to over-engagement must constantly watch that they don't drown in work emotionally and physically; the boundaries between work and personal life will always shift one way or the other, and the task is to stay attentive to them — «Быть включенными в работу, но при этом живыми» ("To be switched on at work, yet alive") [#451].

#### Katya's energy experiments to avoid burning out (March 2025)
Context: after her energy diary showed her energy that year was "so-so", Katya took steps to feel better — "and it seems I even do" [#299]:
1. Changing sleep habits — successfully. Her latest realisation: her sleep problems are largely in her head, so she is now rebuilding her beliefs about sleep, not only changing external habits [#299].
2. Getting up 30 minutes earlier to plan the day and do strategic tasks before meetings start. She noticed planning gives her energy and makes her feel more conscious and fulfilled. It was hard for about 2 weeks during the switch; now "super" [#299].
3. Planning less. She no longer tries to squeeze anything between meetings. She does less, but doesn't lose energy on switching or the feeling "I can't get anything done" [#299].
4. 3–5 minutes of sensory deprivation before/after client meetings. She feels it reboots her. Additionally she goes to a sensory-deprivation capsule twice a month for an hour-long floating session [#299].
5. Putting gadgets away when there are many meetings (especially back to back). On low energy she badly wants to grab the phone or turn on a podcast — which exhausts her even more. By willpower she takes herself for a walk without headphones and phone; around minute 10 it becomes very good, and by the end she feels boundless gratitude to herself and wants to live, even if before it seemed life was no picnic [#299].
6. Testing the "basic rest formula" (1 day a week, 3 days a month, a week each quarter). Finding: "1 day a week" doesn't suit her — she needs 2 full days off; "3 days a month" is her option (tested in February, will do monthly); she has scheduled "a week per quarter" and wrote the post from her first such vacation [#299].
7. Monday as an [almost] meeting-free day. Without a day set aside for creativity, strategy and admin, none of it happens given her number of calls and the (lack of) energy after them. A couple of 30-minute meetings remain; she will not add new ones [#299].
8. Not reading comments, let alone replying. You can drain a bucket of energy there. She also started an "Instagram-check diary" — now she hardly wants to go there, only reaching for it at very low energy [#299].
9. Not seeking, watching or reading new content until she has read the old. Consuming new content eats energy [#299].
10. Sunday as a day without plans. Previously she allowed herself to start the morning with a series, stay in bed two hours or stare at the ceiling only once she had driven herself to the point she couldn't get up. Now she does it every Sunday: nothing planned for the first half of the day, "go with the flow". «От планов и решений тоже нужно отдыхать.» ("You also need rest from plans and decisions.") [#299].
11. Testing new day "repertoires". Her habitual routine of "afternoon magic" (a couple of hours of rest after lunch, then working 15:00–18:00 with fresh energy) proved not ideal — rest often killed her desire to continue. New variant: work the same 2–3 hours right after lunch and put all rest afterwards. It turns out productive and she isn't more tired; she will keep observing [#299].

Main conclusion: she "touched her boundaries in terms of workload" and realised she does not want to live with that many meetings [#299].

#### Two questions for exiting a meaning crisis
Katya's account of what got her out of the burnout and existential crisis of 2018 and helped her "reinvent herself" — turning habits around 180°, changing profession and country [#377]:
- A meaning crisis needs two things: (1) noticing what is going on in your head, and (2) understanding where it came from and why it is not the truth — otherwise how do you allow yourself to change it. A third: concrete steps toward transformation. Therapy helps with the third; contemplative practices and philosophy with the first two. This combination pulled her out.
- At her "point of no return", what was important yesterday abruptly lost weight while new meanings hadn't yet appeared. Two questions started pulling her out: «Что у меня в голове прямо сейчас?» ("What's in my head right now?") — the domain of mindfulness practices; and «Откуда берутся мои мысли?» ("Where do my thoughts come from?") — the domain of philosophy. Together they helped rebuild her meaning apparatus so as to «снова хотеть жить свою жизнь, а не жизнь по инструкции» ("want to live my own life again, not a life by the instructions").
- Lenses (an idea from the book she is reading, «Перевал в середине пути» — "The Middle Passage"): heredity, sex, culture and family environment are lenses through which we look; in the first part of life (some people until the end) we live in a reality formed by these lenses rather than from our own essence. If meaning is lost, the first step is to acknowledge the limitations of these lenses, since a crisis is often about a divergence between true nature and instilled culture.
- Transformation is not an event but a process — in her case a way of existing. She still needs to understand how she thinks, what limits her, where she sets her own frames: «Без этого развитие не едет.» ("Without this, development doesn't move.")
- What developing "philosophical intellect" gives, per Katya: the ability to see your own premises, tolerate uncertainty, untangle the mess of «надо/принято/ожидают» ("must/customary/expected") so as to choose your own living «хочу/важно/верю» ("want/important/believe") — an upgrade in depth without which surface changes may not stick. She also states that this type of growth always precedes financial growth.

### Detailed insights

#### "Get sick to rest" as a symptom
Katya regularly hears clients say: «Иногда я думаю, что было бы классно заболеть, чтобы немножко отдохнуть» ("Sometimes I think it would be great to get sick so I could rest a little"). She naively thought she was no longer in that league — then on the 6th day of bed rest realised that a few days before falling ill she had been thinking exactly about rest, not in that wording but "definitely 'it'" [#236].

The sequence she reconstructs: early the previous week she fantasised about Christmas holidays and a vacation; she already felt overheated, and (crediting herself — she notices this much earlier than before) decided to "ease off the horses" and make Thursday and Friday short days. She did so with variable success: in the freed-up time she did not really rest but «набивала себя новым опытом и впечатлениями» ("stuffed myself with new experiences and impressions"). Now she has been "resting" for almost a week — though, she notes, rest would go better in a healthy state [#236].

Lessons she draws [#236]:
- The romantic thought about a vacation was a symptom — «Я задолбалась» ("I'm fed up / worn out"). Symptoms most likely appeared much earlier, and the thought may have lived in her head long before it became intrusive enough to notice.
- Filling freed time with new experiences is not rest.
- Doing reasonably well at work/life balance is not the same as work/rest balance, at which she still does poorly.
- Her response: make "rest research" one of her themes for 2025 — learn to hear herself and the symptoms in time, understand what kind of rest is needed and when, and what restores and fills her.

#### Katya's burnout history
- Katya's past burnout required her to «уволиться с работы и больше двух лет восстанавливать здоровье и психику» ("quit my job and spend more than two years restoring my health and psyche"); she literally couldn't do or even want anything. Her point to readers: you can simply not let it get that far by starting to change your life little by little right now [#127]. In the stream announcement she describes it as having to "pump herself back" for two years [#125].
- She dates the burnout and crisis to 2018, and the exit involved changing habits, profession and country [#377].
- Its origin, in her telling, was passion for advertising work after reading Beigbeder (see *Balancing enthusiasm and burnout*) [#451].
- She herself, as a burned-out person, took a course with career guide Olga Polishchuk ("and you see what became of me") [#255].

#### Causes and exits beyond quitting (Olga Polishchuk)
Katya recommends Olga Polishchuk — career guide and her close friend, who since 2021 has helped more than 2000 people find themselves and a new direction and has much experience with burned-out people. The framing of her work: understanding why burnout happens and what to do about it besides quitting and resting for half a year (Katya notes hers took two years), and moving out of survival mode so you can start thinking about meaningful work. Three themes her workshop "От чего я выгорел? Работа, роль, среда" ("What did I burn out from? Work, role, environment") covers: frequent and non-obvious causes of professional burnout; changing job and career during burnout; burnout prevention. (!) ### Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- The test is the channel's own adaptation of an HBR article, not a validated clinical instrument; the source gives no validation data [#127].
- The WHO statement about workaholism is Olga Sorina's claim; no source is given [#451].
- Duration of recovery varies across stories: Katya needed more than two years (and quitting her job); Mila needed a 4-month sabbatical; Olga Polishchuk's framing mentions "half a year" of rest as the typical assumption [#127, #207, #255].
- Katya's experiments are personal n=1 observations still in progress ("I'll keep observing"); "1 day a week" of the basic rest formula did not fit her [#299].
- Several recommendations (Olga Polishchuk's workshop, Humanity course, Olga Sorina's group) came with commercial promotion; in #377 Katya is a paying participant of the course she recommends [#255, #377, #451].

## «Отдохнувшие»: the rest laboratory

### What this covers

This topic gathers everything the channel says about «Отдохнувшие» ("The Rested Ones"): Katya Strelnikova's personal essay that defined what being rested means to her, the closed peer group of seven women she ran for about ten months in 2025, what that group taught its members (in Katya's retrospective and in three member testimonials), the design and promised outcomes of the open six-week laboratory launched in January 2026, and two client case studies (Anya, Sasha) that show the rest methods applied in one-to-one coaching. [#280, #450, #465, #491, #495, #598, #628]

According to the source, this matters because rest is not a reward or a luxury but a skill that has to be planned, observed and built into real life; people who skip it run on "the last breath", notice fatigue only when they are already lying down sick, and cannot sustain any other change (work, food, weight) without it. [#280, #495, #598, #628]

### Core ideas

- Rest is an activity that must be planned. Katya defines rest as a process and activity aimed at restoring body, energy and resource, which happens only when it is competently planned. Full detail in *Rested-me essay*. [#280]
- Rest is a skill, not a luxury or reward. The laboratory's first promised outcome is the understanding that rest is "не роскошь и не награда, а навык, который можно развивать и встроить в жизнь" (not a luxury and not a reward but a skill that can be developed and built into life). Members (Radmila, Karina) independently report the same realisation. [#495, #466, #468]
- Observation + people + regularity keep you on track. Katya built the group because these three supports help her keep pace in other areas; weekly written check-ins made recurring drains impossible to "unsee". See *Observation, people, regularity* and *Weekly check-in diary*. [#450]
- Experiments beat goals for rest. Goals for rest fail when one's skill level is near zero because life and habits intervene; a hypothesis → test → change loop works better. See *Experimental approach*. [#450]
- Energy is cyclical, for everyone. The group's most valuable lesson for Katya was seeing energy cycles in others, which made slumps acceptable: they are unavoidable, harsher if you squeeze yourself, and happen to everyone. [#450, #467]
- Rest more, tire less, work better. The Anya case concludes: "больше отдыхаешь, меньше устаешь, больше эффективность" (you rest more, you tire less, efficiency is higher). Anya now does more in 5–6 hours than previously in 10. [#598]
- The main result is a new contact with oneself; habits follow. Katya's view is that the key outcome of rest work is not new habits but a new contact with oneself, after which habits appear on their own. [#598]
- Work with the state, not the symptom. Sasha came about food and weight and found that without rest there is no energy and without energy there is no change. [#628]

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Rested-me essay — «Отдохнувшая. Я.» (11 markers of the rested state)
Purpose: an essay on rest was one of the tasks in the first month of the closed club; Katya published hers and invited readers to say what resonates and what she forgot. Each paragraph opens with the refrain "Отдохнувшая. Я." (Rested. Me.) and describes one condition under which she is rested. It works as a self-description template: a reader can write their own list of when they are rested. She notes (in a screenshot of her note) that she wrote it right after waking up on her third consecutive day off — "настолько преисполнилась к третьему выходному" (was that filled up by the third day off). [#280, img #281]

1. Inspiration and desire. Rested is when there is inspiration and energy to realise what was planned, time without hurry, and when she really "ХОООЧЕТСЯ" (waaants) to do things. [#280]
2. Enough sleep. When she has slept enough — gave herself as much sleep as needed, wakes early without an alarm, falls asleep with pleasure (closing her eyes and thinking "господи, какой же кайф просто вот так лежать с закрытыми глазами" — God, what bliss just to lie like this with closed eyes) and wakes with pleasure, anticipating the day. The contrast is the mode "как бы дотянуть до вечера / выходных / отпуска" (how to hold on until evening / the weekend / vacation). She admits that sometimes from the very morning she feels she needs rest — "what can you expect from such a day?" [#280]
3. Desire to live and attention to small things. When she feels the desire to live, does every simplest action with attention and pleasure, notices reflections of light on walls and birdsong, and has time and wish simply to look out the window. [#280]
4. Energy not spilled. When she did not spill energy on fuss, checking social media, squabbles and useless conflicts, and did not dry herself out with meetings and calls ("колами"). [#280]
5. Breathers. When she gave herself pauses instead of jumping "как угорелая" (like a madwoman) between tasks, apps and browser tabs. [#280]
6. One thing at a time. When she did one thing at a time and was not distracted by thoughts of what else needs doing while doing what is in front of her. [#280]
7. Being unavailable. When she is unavailable and herself chooses the time and place for interactions with, and influences from, the outside world. [#280]
8. No inner self-eating. When she did not "eat herself" inside, noticed and responded to internal conflicts and confusion, and practised mindfulness and self-observation. [#280]
9. Care and planned rest. When she treated her body, energy and resource with care, and understood that rest is a process and an activity aimed at restoring them, which happens only when competently planned — emphasised in the essay as "Когда. Грамотно. Планировала." (When. I. Competently. Planned. [rest]). [#280]
10. Attending to state — and acting on it. Not only constantly asking herself "Как я сейчас?" (How am I now?), but listening to and hearing the answer, reacting to what she heard, and having a repertoire of actions for doing so. The lesson: check-ins without a response repertoire are not enough. [#280]
11. Interest and compassion. When she treated herself and life with interest and compassion, and valued life. [#280]

Closing: "Отдохнувшая ли я? Пока не до конца, но я туда иду." (Am I rested? Not fully yet, but I'm heading there.) Context: Katya named 2025 her "годом нетоксичного развития" (year of non-toxic development), which she says is turning out to be more a "year of getting rid of illusions". It has two foci — development (how to grow her practice and income more systematically; for this she works with a coach, plans more clearly and keeps reports) and recovery (because in the growth process it is easy to spill what is always in short supply; for this she found like-minded women and created the closed club «Отдохнувшие» for those for whom it matters to keep focus on rest). [#280]

#### Observation, people, regularity — three supports for keeping pace
Katya's origin story: a year before November 2025 she realised she wanted to help people rest, but did NOT launch an open group, because she had no strength herself ("надо самой отдохнуть, Катя" — you need to rest yourself, Katya) and did not feel like an expert in rest — "Скорее, двоечница" (more of a failing student). It seemed she first had to learn to be rested herself. She then recalled what helps her keep pace in other spheres: "Наблюдение. Люди. Регулярность." (Observation. People. Regularity.) [#450]

1. Observation — systematically recording one's energy (implemented as the weekly check-in diary below). [#450]
2. People — a closed group "только для своих" (only for one's own people): like-minded women ready to devote a year to researching energy and resource. There turned out to be seven: Katya, Olya, Anya, Natasha, Radmila, Yana and Karina. [#450]
3. Regularity — fixed rhythm: monthly meetings plus weekly check-ins, which created social commitment. [#450]

#### Weekly energy check-in diary plus monthly group meeting
How the closed group operated, per Katya: [#450]
1. Set rest goals for the year together at the start. [#450]
2. Meet once a month to discuss what worked and what did not, and to plan the next month. [#450]
3. Weekly check-in. Katya promised the group a weekly check-in; once promised, it felt "неприлично сливаться" (indecent to bail), so the diary "stuck" — for the first time she managed to keep a diary for 10.5 months. [#450]
4. Why it works: if you record your energy every week, you begin to notice. When week after week you write down the same thing that wears you out, you can no longer "развидеть" (unsee) it — you have to change it. At some point the other members also felt the effect and started checking in. [#450]
5. Bring something new to report. It is pleasant to write something new in the check-in and monthly plan, so Katya regularly tried new things — this fed the experimental approach. [#450]

#### Experimental approach — hypothesis → test → change
"Цели ничто. Эксперименты все." (Goals are nothing. Experiments are everything.) Katya found fairly quickly that goals are not the best choice for rest, especially when one's level of working on it is still at zero: life inevitably happens, habits take over, and roughly everything goes wrong. So she switched on her favourite approach (and, she says, her clients' favourite) — the experimental one: "Гопотеза → проверка → изменение." [sic] (Hypothesis → test → change.) [#450]
1. Hypothesis — pick something to try for rest/energy. [#450]
2. Test — try it during the month and record in check-ins. [#450]
3. Change — keep, adjust or drop, based on what was observed, and plan the next experiment at the monthly meeting. [#450]

#### Laboratory outcomes — "Что изменится?" (What will change?)
The open laboratory card lists what a participant is meant to build and experience over six weeks. [#495]
1. Reframe rest — understand that rest is not a luxury or reward but a skill that can be developed and built into life. [#495]
2. List of fatigue signs — compile your own list of signs of tiredness so you can stop before "полного обесточивания" (total power-out). [#495]
3. Rest formula — assemble your own "формулу отдыха": how to support yourself on a regular basis. [#495]
4. Express first-aid kit — "экспресс-аптечку на случай «пожара»": concrete ways to recover when there is no strength at all. [#495]
5. Early signals — start noticing the first signals of fatigue and manage to stop not at the moment when it is already "всё" (all over) but a bit earlier. [#495]
Effects promised: (01) mood improves, inspiration grows, clarity and lightness are felt; (02) energy appears — not the explosive kind from coffee, sugar and cheap dopamine, but calm and stable; (03) a sense of inner support forms: you know when to stop and how to support yourself. [#495]

The lab is framed as "6 недель наблюдений, открытий и мягких перемен" (6 weeks of observations, discoveries and gentle changes) for those who want to stop coping, proving and fighting, and instead learn to rest and start living. Format described: 6 weeks, 5 meetings, a group chat with Katya's feedback, more than 10 practices, and theory based on research and her own experience; participants leave with their own recovery formula and "аптечку" (first-aid kit), and materials stay with them. After the active six weeks she proposed a slow format of one meeting a month for the rest of the year if enough people wanted it. There was also a tariff with deeper individual work, positioned as a test-drive of one-to-one coaching. [#465, msg #465, #491]

#### Client rest-coaching protocol — the Anya case
Purpose: Katya's description (shared with the client's consent) of 4.5 months of one-to-one work with Anya, whose goal was "Впервые в жизни хочу научиться отдыхать" (For the first time in my life I want to learn to rest). [#598]

Starting point. Anya: entrepreneur, fourth year in emigration, business in Moscow, works from home with no boundary between work and life; believes she must be reachable for Moscow at 7 a.m. London time; endless phone and work chats; notices fatigue only when already lying down (previously this happened with pneumonia, now "just like that"). Her beliefs: "Надо преодолевать" (You have to overcome), "Отдых — только когда сделала все дела" (Rest only when all tasks are done), "Много отдыхать стыдно" (Resting a lot is shameful), "Чем больше усилий, тем больше достигаешь" (The more effort, the more you achieve), "Круто надо делать всегда" (You must always do things brilliantly) — plus an image of the entrepreneur broadcast by media and family: red-eyed, always at maximum. [#598]

Steps (as listed by Katya):
1. Start with beliefs. Name them and replace them — "Пока их не назовешь и не заменишь, они продолжают управлять" (Until you name and replace them, they keep running things). [#598]
2. Collect fatigue signs. Anya's: sleeping badly, skipping sport, body aches, a feeling that something is stuck inside. Fatigue has concrete symptoms that can be learned to read earlier than the body puts you down with pneumonia. [#598]
3. Build safety cushions — the minimum that holds even in hard periods, "не роскошь, а база" (not a luxury but the base). Anya's: reading before sleep, 9+ hours of sleep, walks with the dog. [#598]
4. Write principles of the rested state — specifically Anya's version, not universal internet advice but what works for her: work 5–6 hours split into before lunch and after; lunch with a walk; sleep as much as she wants; pleasure — every day something from "хочу" (want) rather than "надо" (must): a book, a walk for herself, a relaxed film, actions out of curiosity. [#598]
5. Work with subpersonalities. They found "Аня на максималках" (Anya at max) — always fully battle-ready, cannot stop, thinks "what will others say?", turns even rest into something correct, intensive and over-stuffed — and "Аню отдохнувшую" (rested Anya) — engaged, says "no" when something does not match her interests, allows herself simply to go out with the dog. The aim was NOT to kill one and grow the other: "Anya at max" helped build the business and cope with emigration. They learned to notice when she switches on too intensely and out of place. [#598]
6. Count real working hours, then ask how many she wants. It turned out Anya's success and the business's success are not measured in hours at all — "a simple thought that is hard to accept when the whole coordinate system is built around the amount of effort". [#598]
7. Other work — Katya adds "И много чего еще…" (and much more). (!) INCOMPLETE: the remaining steps are not described. [#598]

Results. Anya works 5–6 hours a day and does more than she used to in a strained 10; sets clear hours for contact with Moscow; notices fatigue before she "crashes"; knows her safety cushions and does not bargain with them even in busy periods; is creating her own image of an entrepreneur; says "no" when something does not match her interests; switched from phone to a book in the evening; started sleeping in a separate bedroom (sleep improved for both her and her husband); took her first vacation without work; allowed herself rest without a plan or usefulness. Katya's reading: the main result is a new contact with oneself; habits appear by themselves after it. [#598]

#### Personal rest research plan (Anya's goal structure)
An image in the Anya post shows how her goal was formulated as a research project: [#598]
- Goal: conduct a study of rest and, for the first time in life, learn to rest — in order to feel inspired, filled and happy more often and for longer.
- Research: (1) figure out what rest is for me — how, when and how much I need to rest; (2) understand which beliefs, habits and convictions hinder and which help; (3) see what fills/rests me and what takes energy; (4) find new ways of support and resource; (5) determine which activities can absorb me as much as work does; (6) "и др." (an open-ended continuation in the original).
- Then: work out concrete steps based on the research; then implement the changes and steps. [#598]

#### Seven shifts from health coaching — the Sasha case
A client's forwarded post about six months with Katya. She came with a request about food and habits: through 2025 she had very little energy, much anxiety, weight was rising and health was sending signals. She thought it was a task with a technical solution; it was not. [#628, img #628]
1. Changed the starting point. For several months they discussed that without rest there is no energy and without energy there are no changes; she had been trying to start from a very low base. As soon as she really rested, the process went: energy appeared and weight began to fall with the same efforts. [#628]
2. Started noticing what she already does. Walking, cycling, dog walks, yoga "не считалось" (didn't count) because it was not the gym or weekly hikes. That bar never lowers however much she does, and it is precisely this bar that eats her energy faster than any load. She began to notice "small steps". [#628]
3. Stopped motivating herself with anxiety. Anxiety as a "морковка сзади" (carrot from behind) moves you but burns you. She began to watch this and sees she has become steadier; her Oura ring helps here. [#628]
4. Saw roles beyond work. For a couple of years she lived only for work; now she notices other roles — mother, wife, friend, student. She enrolled in the second level of Gestalt training, did a German bootcamp, returned to mentoring: she wanted to live and to have something besides work and the anxiety around it. [#628]
5. Started living during business trips so that not all routines collapse: watching what she eats, trying to get enough sleep; soon she expects to pack trainers again. [#628]
6. Allowed herself to accept help. Trainer, doctor, coach, psychotherapist — helping specialists strengthen her. She "hacked" German medicine: did all the check-ups she had postponed, changed her main doctor, got proper recommendations and referrals. At physical training she set boundaries with the supervisor at once, so that they guide and do not shame; she trains with a friend, which supports her. [#628]
7. Noticing self-care again. Massage, a walk without headphones, vitamins, sleeping on time are not trifles. Knowing she tends to see only what is unfinished, it is worth deliberately noticing what works. Being able to talk about self-care turned out to be linked to being able to talk about her successes in general — which she does badly, especially at work. [#628]

Her main conclusions: work had to be done not with the symptom but with the state it grows from; follow desire rather than invent what is "правильно" (correct); if you know you tend to scold yourself, praise yourself deliberately — "Это не самолюбование, это гигиена" (This is not vanity, it is hygiene). "Я шла разбираться с едой. А разобралась с тем, как я с собой живу." (I went to sort out food and sorted out how I live with myself.) Her next steps: (1) add one fitness activity a week; (2) think about movement on business trips and see what works; (3) notice more often the good that already exists and find a format for it besides regular massage. She adds that with Katya she manages to look "вне рамок" (outside the frames). [#628]

### Detailed insights

#### What the closed group gave Katya (10 months)
- Acceptance of slumps through seeing others' cycles. "Если будешь слишком быстро гнать — слишком быстро приедешь." (If you drive too fast you'll arrive too fast.) Slumps are, first, unavoidable — and very cruel if you squeeze yourself — and second, they happen to absolutely everyone. A favourite group ritual: laughing at one's illusion "ну я-то особенная и всемогущая" (well, I'm special and all-powerful), then jointly admitting: no, not special (maybe just a little). [#450]
- Permission to be without energy. Especially when work requires shining with energy in sessions and blogs, one sometimes wants to whine, be vulnerable, without inspiration or strength, and still feel loved, supported and considered the best. It is priceless to hear "Катя, может, тебе нужен отпуск?" (Katya, maybe you need a vacation?) and reluctantly agree — because she had long thought so herself but did not want to notice it and allow it. [#450]
- Mirroring. Katya "will never stop singing odes" to group work, calling it a treasure trove of insights: you listen to someone else and suddenly realise "у меня же точно также" (it's exactly the same with me); it is as if someone highlighted your shadow; you can no longer unsee the problem and cannot not start solving it. [#450]
- Community. In emigration Katya lacked community and like-minded people; the group became an outlet, and the women became friends who now discuss life plans, money and family as well as energy. [#450]
- Theory alongside practice. Besides practical insights she studied research and articles and had read 4 books on rest, and does not plan to stop. (!) INCOMPLETE: the books are not named in these messages. [#450]

#### What members say the group gave them
- Radmila Khakova (writer, blogger, podcast host): the group gives support of like-minded women and returns focus to recovery; it is "легальное время на рефлексию" (legal time for reflection) and a place to speak openly about difficulties and ask for support. Most important is exchange of experience — what worked, what didn't, what works for others; the women sometimes generate ideas specifically for her, creating fertile ground for a new relationship with rest. She saw that people can get tired in any circumstances, and that rest does not arise by itself — it must be fitted to real life; "Это навык" (It's a skill). For her it is about small steps suited to her situation and about building recovery when time is fragmented ("время рваное"). The group showed that everyone is alive with different lives and that this is normal, and became about life, support, resilience and ways of being gentler to oneself. [#466, #493]
- Olya Bastron (ICF coach): the group gave colossal support in her year of moving, when she lost her familiar close circle; it was a space to bring both whining and joy. At the start of the year she did not want to admit her energy level had changed. Her journey had two phases: first she explored where energy leaks and tried various techniques; in the second came acceptance — she saw cyclicity, understood that the base — "сон, стресс, отдых, питание, природа" (sleep, stress, rest, nutrition, nature) — really works, and rebuilt her life around the amount of strength she has now. She accepted she is "не special" and cannot ignore the body's rhythms and needs; became more tolerant and gentle with herself; rest turned from rare snatches into a daily regime. Without the group she would have kept grinding "по старой карте развития" (by the old map of development) and been harsher to herself; in the group one can be "не такой" (not right) this week, out of resource, and not count it as failure. Her inner voice now sounds softer. [#467, #492]
- Karina Lvova (entrepreneur, business consultant): the group helped her move from theory to regular practice; when rest and fatigue regularly enter one's field of attention, there is less chance of drifting far down the familiar crooked path. Examples of groupmates — real women with children, jobs, piles of work — motivated and supported her. She began to plan rest both before and after loads, strengthen routines in busy and stressful periods, and set up actions for regular rest during the week. She used to think rest was a luxury; after observing, she found it is a necessity for her to live and work well. Unexpectedly the group about rest became a group about life: a space of support, of timely questions she would not have asked herself, and of frank conversations with real experience. She loves the video circles members send each other — from floating, sound healing, from rest under a palm tree — which motivate her to stop and schedule rest for herself. [#468, #494]

#### Who the laboratory is aimed at — signs you need to learn to rest
Invitation list ("Приходите, если устали быть усталыми" — Come if you're tired of being tired): [#465, #491]
1. You have lost energy and do not know how to gather it back. [#465, #491]
2. You need strength for life and new achievements. [#465, #491]
3. You want to learn to breathe out. [#465, #491]
4. You don't want to rest with guilt, to have to earn rest, or to get sick in order to rest. [#465, #491]
5. You feel it is important to develop the "skill" of rest and make it a regular habit. [#465, #491]

"For you if" list: [#465, #491]
1. You live on autopilot and have long not felt inspiration. [#465, #491]
2. You do everything *right* but are constantly tired. [#465, #491]
3. You want to exhale but cannot allow yourself to. [#465, #491]
4. You are tired of being tired and are looking for a way to fill up. [#465, #491]
5. You want to regain clarity, energy and lightness. [#465, #491]

Symptom list of chronic under-rest (from the last-call post): [#480]
1. Chronic fatigue, even after the weekend. [#480]
2. Wanting to get sick just to rest a little. [#480]
3. Waking up already tired even after eight hours of sleep. [#480]
4. Sleeping badly, falling ill often, snapping over trifles — and "не знаете почему" (not knowing why). [#480]
5. Inability to concentrate, constant distraction. [#480]
6. Seeming to rest but feeling even worse afterwards. [#480]
7. Not knowing how to rest without guilt. [#480]
The stated aim: find what really fills you — to hear yourself, to do what you have long wanted, to want, "И ЧТОБЫ НА ВСЕ ЭТО БЫЛИ СИЛЫ" (and to have the strength for all of it). [#480]

Katya's credentials as stated: a year of researching rest on herself and almost 4 years helping busy, goal-driven women rest as a coach; she declared 2026 a year of rest and of «Отдохнувшие». [#465, #491]

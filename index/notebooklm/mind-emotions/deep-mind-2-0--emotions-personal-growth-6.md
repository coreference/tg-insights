## Masculinity, gender and leadership

### What this covers

This topic collects the channel author's posts on men's psychology and gender: the Hollis-based model of male fear and compensation, the critique of hegemonic ("real man") masculinity and its poor fit with the digital economy and modern leadership, the observation that men use supportive practices far less than women, the critique of gender holidays and the "survival mindset", the author's reflections on mature male figures and wholeness, and a 2026 essay on "new masculinity" supported by dating and relationship data. It also covers the parallel argument that leadership is turning toward feminine qualities, integration of diversity and empathy. According to the source this matters because unhealed compensatory masculinity costs men (rage, loneliness, sadness, suicide, failed relationships, falling market value as leaders) and limits society's capacity for creativity and development [#103, #146, #389, #1716].

### Core ideas

- Much of men's lives is run by unacknowledged fear, compensated by power. Following James Hollis, the author holds that the fear of not matching the image of a man and the fear of physical/psychological trial are men's hidden life drivers, and that the striving for status and power is their compensation (full model in *Hollis' model* below) [#103].
- Traditional masculine "strength" is often a reaction to fear of being insufficient, not inner stability; under the lens of science, psychotherapy and attention to power structures, most of what is traditionally considered masculine falls apart [#1716].
- Hegemonic masculinity adapts poorly to the new economy and to modern leadership, which expects flexibility, empathy and communication skills [#389].
- Leadership is turning toward feminine qualities and toward integrating diversity, because patriarchy is not effective in an interconnected world [#146, #51].
- Healing starts with a man admitting fear and vulnerability, first of all to himself; wholeness means being strong thanks to vulnerability, not despite it [#103, #1471].
- Men use external support and practices much less than women, which the author sees as making women more adaptive under stress [#174].
- Attractiveness and relationships are shifting from structural role to personal qualities, so masculinity must move from the function of power to authentic personhood [#1716].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Hollis' model of male fear and compensation
Purpose: explains what drives men, why they compensate, what it costs and how they can heal. The author wrote it after waking up "in a terrible state" with sharpened fears "о которых большинство мужчин не в силах публично признаться" (which most men are unable to admit publicly), deciding to protect himself from decay "через контакт с мудростью старейшин" (through contact with the wisdom of elders) by reading the book on men's path by his favourite Jungian analyst James Hollis. He frames it as a thought about men's reality whose awareness is necessary for "оздоровления культуры" (the healing of culture). He also states that the ability to solve important questions is, for a man, a question of his self-realization and his "состоятельности" (solvency / being a man of substance) [#103].

1. Fear governs a significant part of men's lives. There are two fundamental male fears:
   - *Fear of not matching the image of a man* ("не соответствовать образу мужчины"). The defence against it is rivalry and the attitude that a man of substance is a results-producing man ("состоятельный мужчина - это результативный мужчина").
   - *Fear of a physical or psychological trial* ("страх физического или психологического испытания"). It shows itself as doubts about one's ability to protect oneself and one's family [#103].
2. Men fear admitting these fears so much that the fears condition them maximally. Under the influence of culture, the fears become men's main, unconscious life drivers. "Для нас страшнее не пройти «испытание» и не состояться, чем заболеть и умереть." (For us it is scarier to fail the "trial" and not to make something of ourselves than to fall ill and die.) Any form of powerlessness turns out to be worse than leaving life [#103].
3. Fear that cannot be shared leads to compensation. Driven by fear and unable to share it (he would be shamed), a man comes to compensation: a man who grabs at showy masculinity, fashion trends and expensive things is, to some degree, compensating a sense of his own small significance. The most common form of compensation is power. "За демонстрацией власти скрывается комплекс, за комплексом - страх." (Behind a display of power hides a complex, behind the complex — fear.) "Самые опасные животные в природе - это напуганные животные." (The most dangerous animals in nature are frightened animals.) The complex of striving for status and power is, in the author's words, the main driving force in men's lives: it makes men act and in the end traumatizes them. Giving rein to rage, some men wound others and then feel regret, shame and sadness, growing distant from each other [#103].
4. Unawareness of fears and of culturally imposed role ideas about how strong a man must be is costly. The world of men is largely a world of rage, loneliness and sadness. Men commit suicide 4 times more often than women (figure as stated by the author). Driven by complexes and fears, men compete instead of partnering, co-building and co-creating [#103].
5. Unhealed men will not be able to lead modern organizations. The author predicts that men not healed of these complexes "не смогут быть лидерами в современных формах организаций" (will not be able to be leaders in modern forms of organizations) and that their market value will fall rapidly in the new decade. Mechanism: compensatory pseudo-masculinity locks a man inside his own complexes and completely deprives him of the ability to listen without bias, to see, and to positively influence others [#103].
6. Healing requires that a man stop being ashamed of his fear (numbered as a second "5." in the source). This is very difficult: admitting fear means risking no longer feeling like a man and waiting to be shamed by others. A vicious circle forms: fear deepens loneliness and leads to new fears. The author's prescription has two levels:
   - *Collective*: create collective free spaces where men gather for development — build self-awareness, conduct modern forms of initiation, find elders and share their experience of life.
   - *Individual* (per Hollis, final changes happen individually): "Мужчины должны найти силы и признаться в первую очередь самим себе в слабости, уязвимости и страхах. Это позволит им действительно стать мужчинами и обрести силу." (Men must find the strength to admit, first of all to themselves, their weakness, vulnerability and fears. This will allow them to truly become men and gain strength.) [#103]

#### Five reasons for the turn toward feminine qualities in leadership
Purpose: the author's explanation (written for 8 March, explicitly "не попытка задобрить женщин в праздник" — not an attempt to placate women on the holiday) of why masculine leadership models, dominant for many years, are giving way. He grants that under masculine models humanity made great progress, but also saw "ужасающими однобокостями, перегибами и проблемами" (horrifying one-sidedness, excesses and problems). He believes the turn toward more femininity and balance has already happened in many places: men express their own femininity or "female qualities" more actively and healthily, and women leaders occupy more and more leading positions. The accumulated reasons [#146]:
1. Psychotherapists have shown the machismo complex as a person's striving to compensate their own vulnerability [#146].
2. We have seen how psychic trauma arises, how it is passed from generation to generation "на генетическом уровне" (at the genetic level, as the author states), and have begun to understand its key role in producing the aggression and violence characteristic of hierarchies; we have learned to heal it and reduce violence in the world [#146].
3. We have realized the colossal role of emotional and bodily intelligence, which the dominant male culture denied [#146].
4. Society is maturing and becoming more sensitive to violence and direct displays of power; young people refuse to work with traditional leaders [#146].
5. Flexible forms of management have established themselves in the organizational world and proved more effective than conventional, originally army-derived hierarchies [#146].

Conclusion drawn by the author: patriarchy is not effective in an interconnected world and is under pressure from all sides. He expects growing demand for feminine qualities in leadership: more socio- and world-centricity, flexibility, the ability to listen, empathy, compassion and love. These qualities provide integration — connecting people into one in their diversity — and "только через интеграцию разнообразия можно обрести устойчтивость [sic] оргнизаций [sic] и сообществ в современном мире" (only through integrating diversity can organizations and communities gain sustainability in the modern world) [#146].

#### Five hidden meanings of traditional masculine traits
Purpose: from the 2026 "Новая мужественность" (New masculinity) essay; a deconstruction of what traditional male traits turn out to be "под лупой науки, психотерапии и внимательности к структурам власти" (under the magnifying glass of science, psychotherapy and attention to power structures). The author says most of what is traditionally masculine falls apart under this lens — it "не просто плохо пахнет насилием, но и очевидно хрупко в основании" (not only smells badly of violence but is obviously fragile at its foundation) [#1716]:
1. Demonstrative hypersexuality looks like a way to prove one's normality [#1716].
2. Homophobia turns out to be fear of one's own vulnerability, and also simply a concrete rejected sexual desire [#1716].
3. Toughness is a consequence of trauma that distances one from one's own feelings, or a cover for anxiety [#1716].
4. Emotional distance is a form of self-defence [#1716].
5. Constant competition is an attempt to hold on to a shaky identity [#1716].

The author's verdict: "Как мужчина скажу — во всем этом нет никакой мужественности." (As a man I will say — there is no masculinity in any of this.) With a hedge ("Конечно, не всегда" — of course, not always), he states that too often traditional male "strength" is not inner stability but a reaction to the all-pervading fear of being insufficient, and asks: if you remove this fear, plus power and the need to conform, what remains? He frames examining the data below as getting at least close to "шагу номер 0 — осознать свое положение" (step number 0 — becoming aware of one's position) [#1716].

#### Survival mindset vs creative mindset (critique of gender holidays)
Purpose: the author's explanation of why Soviet gender holidays (23 February and 8 March) persist and why he rejects them [#395].
- Mechanism — cultural ritualization: Soviet gender holidays are an example of cultural ritualization, when an action starts being performed automatically while its meaning gradually washes out. "Основной смысл в повторении этих праздников сейчас — делать то, что делали всегда." (The main meaning in repeating these holidays now is to do what has always been done.) This creates a feeling that everything is stable and life is under control, and unites people around hegemonic masculinity or habitual models of gender in a patriarchal society [#395].
- Harm: packing men and women into someone's strange expectations and assigning them specific role models causes much pain and suffering and greatly limits the whole society's capacity for creativity and development [#395].
- Survival mindset (Russian: выживанческого майндсета, genitive in source): the author suggests he may not grasp the holidays' meaning because he and all following generations no longer have a survival mindset — a set of attitudes formed by unprocessed traumas and violence, which sees the world as an unsafe place surrounded by enemies. State propaganda relies on it and tries to strengthen it, including through gender holidays and other ritualization of culture that is no longer adaptive. Young people also want safety, but safety together with the striving for basic physiological comfort is not the main driver they act from [#395].
- Four theses the author offers instead:
  1. Instead of crossing themselves before diving into an ice hole, men had better think about how they harass their women and how much that makes them "real" men [#395].
  2. The most dangerous enemies sit inside the country, not outside it [#395].
  3. In the modern connected world one must learn to create and negotiate, not dig in and defend [#395].
  4. "Ничего хорошего из страха создать никогда не получится. Для созидания нужны смелость, открытость и любовь." (Nothing good can ever be created out of fear. Creation needs courage, openness and love.) [#395]
- The author's proposal: instead of 23 February and 8 March, celebrate a day of the diversity of genders and of love [#395].

#### Shift from power function to authentic personhood in relationships
Purpose: the author's answer to "if masculinity can no longer rest on a hierarchy of dominance, what remains?" [#1716].
1. Notice the mismatch. The data (see *Evidence and numbers*) signal a mismatch between the habitual form of male self-presentation and what is perceived as attractive once systemic pressure decreases. That self-presentation is often built on an identity traditionally supported by the system of power and prescribed gender roles — marriage, economic hierarchy, cultural norm. Today men increasingly must prove their worth "без *возможности принудить к выбору*" (without the possibility to compel the choice), even subtly. The author stresses this is "не приговор конкретным людям" (not a verdict on specific people) [#1716].
2. Recognize what status does and does not do. Money and power increase visibility and widen access to partnership — "было бы глупо это отрицать" (it would be stupid to deny this) — and a watch still attracts girls' attention; but by themselves they do not create stability, do not guarantee relationship quality and are not what women want most [#1716].
3. Move from structural role to personal qualities. The data together point to a possible change in criteria of male attractiveness — from structural role to personal qualities [#1716].
4. Become an authentic person who builds genuine relationships. Perhaps the answer lies in moving from the function of power to the ability to be an authentic person and create genuine relationships — not through suppression and superiority, but through the ability to take responsibility for one's expressions and to really see one's partner. In traditional cultures people rarely could see each other this way, because they met first of all as roles [#1716].
5. Treat the crisis as an opportunity. The current crisis can change this millennia-old norm, and the author wants only to accelerate the transition: it does not merely "threaten" male identity but creates the basis for relationships that can give freedom, love, joy and support immeasurably greater than before [#1716].

### Detailed insights

#### Hegemonic masculinity and the new economy
The author asks whether hegemonic masculinity prevents adaptation to the new economy and answers that habitual models of masculinity, or of "real" men, show lower adaptivity in the new digital world [#389].

He quotes sociologist and political strategist Mark Penn: behaviours and habits suited to men in more dangerous eras are not very useful in the information era. Readiness for risky acts, pride (self-love) and a tendency to switch attention helped men in all times, from the Stone Age to the end of World War II; today they rather hinder. "Преследование дикого кабана и изучение статей в Википедии требуют совершенно разных навыков." (Chasing a wild boar and studying Wikipedia articles require completely different skills.) Society expects men to be providers, and expects efficiency, practicality and reliability from them rather than sociability; but with social media, culture became oriented toward communication, which makes some men feel like superfluous people. Penn's forecast: if the microtrend of men's poor adaptivity grows, women will continue to move into leading positions in society [#389].

Marianne Legato, a specialist in gender aspects of medicine, reaches a similar conclusion: despite the many advantages of their position in the past, today "classical" men are in great trouble and are more vulnerable than ever before to biological and social threats [#389].

The author adds his own observation: in modern leadership and effective flexible teams this trend is also evident; hegemonic masculinity is quickly read as a toxic phenomenon there, all the more so because leaders today are expected to have more flexibility, empathy and skills that "real" men rarely possess [#389].

#### Women politicians and the desacralization of politics (Finland example)
The author quotes a Forbes piece on how women came to power in Finland: over the last 10 years women politicians rejected the "male" model closed on work and efficiency. It turned out they have personal lives, children, psychological traumas, dreams and failures, they may even look awkward (paparazzi once caught Li Andersson drunk), but this does not stop them being politicians. To some extent women desacralized politics by refusing to make the usual sacrifices to it, and instead brought their life experience into it. They all grew up in the 1990s — years of economic crisis and loss of social guideposts, when those in power maniacally believed in an "efficient economy", mainly in cutting social spending [#51].

According to the quoted text, the new wave of women politicians is characterized by thinking based on "целостности восприятия и эмпатии" (wholeness of perception and empathy). This resonates with voters, who are ready to vote for their female peers, support them and link their personal future with them, because it is easy to entrust one's future to a person who is like you and understands what worries you [#51].

#### Diversity as real strength
The author argues that differences between people can no longer be denied: science has shown how strongly our chronotypes, nervous systems, ways of processing information and types of thinking differ. In this biological reality, any attempt to make people "march in formation" is doomed to instability and conflict [#146].

For well-being, he writes, it is not enough to strive to squeeze the whole world oil market; one must learn to create an integrated culture — a culture of entrepreneurship in which our differences serve as the basis for innovation and creativity. That is real strength, and it is gained when diversity is seen not as a threat to stability and to our domination but as the most valuable wealth. Accepting difference, we encourage our own growth and heal from one-sided compensatory pictures of the world: "Перестаем бояться и начинаем любить." (We stop fearing and start loving.) The post's headline thesis: "Мы разные и это сильно." (We are different, and that is strong.) [#146]

#### How men cope (or don't) with stress
In April 2020 (isolation, uncertainty, loss of assets, increased police presence), the author asked men how they cope. He had led a mindfulness session at an "embodiment circle" — an open international initiative of weekly events with supportive practices — where there were almost 50 women and he was the only man [#174].

His observation: women far more actively turn to external support and to keeping themselves sane through constructive tools — bodily, psychotherapeutic and meditative practices. He describes his own "possibly extreme" view: for about five years he has not understood city life without regular work on his psychic resource through mindfulness practice and psychotherapy — "Зачем плохо жить, если можно хорошо?" (Why live badly if you can live well?) — except perhaps when there are truly no resources for it [#174].

He cites polls putting anxiety in Russian society at 68%; in this situation women look more adaptive and, he predicts, "Через месяц будут лучше перформить" (in a month they will perform better) — "Это просто смарт" (it is simply smart). He asks whether men don't need such support because they are "strong and will endure everything", and lists the likely male substitutes: sport; alcohol, carbohydrates and workaholism. His warning: if you distract yourself from emotions and uncertainty by working 15 hours a day, what will happen when you burn out? He explicitly says he is not moralizing but genuinely curious [#174].

#### Mature male figures and wholeness (personal reflection with a lesson)
On a trip to the Caucasus mountains, the author recalls his grandfather, head of a regional Soviet organization who electrified remote mountain areas by building power lines, earning the titles of Honoured Builder and Honorary Power Engineer of the Union. Always sporty, he died early, at 59, of a heart attack right in his office. The lesson the author draws: perhaps his grandfather left an important and very sad reminder "относиться к жизни проще, меньше работать и вообще следить за собой" (to take life more simply, work less and generally take care of oneself) — a lesson addressed to most men. The author says he has learned part of it and is still learning the rest, especially "умение не повторять судьбу внешне сильных, но внутри истощённых фигур" (the ability not to repeat the fate of outwardly strong but inwardly exhausted figures) [#1471].

He longs for mature male figures in society — "Тех, кто соединяет силу с уважением жизни" (those who combine strength with respect for life) and who have values. He distinguishes this from "лжи о традиционных ценностях" (lies about traditional values) by people who are the first to trample them — not keeping their word, lying, cheating, abusing the weak; none of this has anything to do with masculinity. Three years earlier, discussing the typical "male initiations" of the author's childhood and youth, Holger said something like "это катастрофа, а не инициации" (this is a catastrophe, not initiations) [#1471].

The author is not a fan of rigid roles or gender stereotyping ("a man must be such, a woman such"). Such thinking is part of the reason people find it hard to become whole: weakness has to be hidden behind control and suppression. "Когда мы внутри цельны, мы признаём разные части себя и становимся сильными не вопреки уязвимости, а благодаря ей." (When we are whole inside, we acknowledge different parts of ourselves and become strong not despite vulnerability but thanks to it.) This produces a man who can write poems and sing, show tenderness, cry when crying is needed, and maintain the dignity of being present in his own life — taking responsibility for his personality, choices and actions [#1471].

He notes that his own initiations were varied — otherwise he would not hold these ideas — and that he did once experience "мужские теплые руки, нежность и любовь" (warm male hands, tenderness and love) that stay with him forever. His conclusion: what matters is not so much searching for examples of masculinity or femininity outside, but becoming a bearer of wholeness oneself and creating a space where it becomes possible [#1471].

#### Symbols of gender today
The "New masculinity" essay opens with a scene: two female friends admired that instead of the now-usual digital watch the author wears a mechanical watch ("котлы, как батя" — slang: a watch, like dad's). This led him to think about through which symbols we perceive gender differences today and what it means to be a man in the modern world; he later notes that the watch still attracts girls' attention, but such status markers do not create stability by themselves [#1716].

### Evidence and numbers

- Male suicide: men commit suicide 4 times more often than women — figure stated by the author without a source [#103].
- Anxiety in Russia (spring 2020): according to polls cited by the author, the level of anxiety in Russian society rose to 68% [#174].
- OkCupid dating platform analysis: showed a stable asymmetry of perception — under free choice women rate men significantly more strictly than men rate women; 30% of men evoke in women not a neutral but a distinctly negative reaction [#1716].
- Long-term partner preferences (David Buss, "Preferences in Human Mate Selection", as linked by the author): for long-term relationships women value reliability, kindness/goodwill and emotional stability more highly than status symbols. The author stresses this is "не мой вывод… а данные" (not my conclusion… but data) [#1716].
- Predictors of relationship quality (an APA PsycNet study the author cites): status symbols are not among the predictors of long-term relationship quality at all [#1716].
- Pew Research: 63% of men under 30 in the US are not in a relationship; among women of the same age the figure is half as high. The author interprets this as the gap between classical masculinity and what is actually needed affecting men's ability to build relationships [#1716].

### Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- (!) UNCLEAR: in #103 the numbering contains two items labelled "5." (leadership/market value and healing); reproduced here as steps 5 and 6 [#103].
- The claim that trauma is passed between generations "на генетическом уровне" (at the genetic level) and the 4x male suicide figure are stated by the author without citing sources [#146, #103].
- The predictions (unhealed men's market value "will fall rapidly in the new decade"; women "will perform better in a month") are the author's forecasts, not measured results [#103, #174].
- The author hedges his own views: he calls himself "возможно обладатель экстремальных взглядов" (possibly a holder of extreme views) on regular psychological self-work, says he is "не морализирую" (not moralizing), and in #395 allows that he may simply not grasp the holidays' meaning because his generation lacks the survival mindset [#174, #395].
- In #1716 the author hedges with "Конечно, не всегда" (of course, not always) and admits money and power do increase visibility and access to partners; he frames the women's-preference findings as data, not his own conclusion [#1716].
- The Finland and Penn/Legato passages are quotations of third parties, not the author's own research [#51, #389].

## Community, belonging and loneliness

### What this covers

This topic gathers the author's (deep mind channel) views on why social isolation is dangerous, why small communities built on shared values and honesty are the most "alive" and effective structure of society, and how to tell such communities apart from status-driven "alpha" or narcissistic groups that look like spaces of development but trap people. It also covers a personal practice of generosity between peers (the "Silicon Valley question") and why the author prefers the company of people who have done real inner work. According to the source, this matters for health (isolation affects mortality more than smoking), for employees' ability to work, for personal growth and maturity, and — after 2022 — for the emotional survival and future identity of emigrants from Russia, Ukraine and Belarus [#192, #537, #613, #1291].

### Core ideas

- Isolation kills; solitude does not. Social isolation affects mortality more strongly than smoking, but what harms is the subjective feeling of being cut off, not living alone (see *Isolation vs solitude*) [#192].
- Shared values create trust. Social health is improved through micro-communities with expressed values, because values filter people and raise trust inside the group [#192].
- Small communities (10–100 people) are the living core of society, because they contain knowing each other, trust and real mutual help; totalitarian societies, by contrast, seek to atomize people [#613].
- Status communities block growth. Success and business communities run on the evolutionary "alpha" pattern and force members to keep masks of success, which rules out the genuine experiences that growth needs [#537].
- Narcissistic communities are a trap disguised as development: their informal priority is inflating ego and status; they can give a feeling of superiority and access to resources, but not real freedom [#1291].
- The antidote is honesty and openness — and egalitarian groups where power is distributed and people matter for authenticity rather than achievements [#537, #1291].
- Living from abundance: proactively offering support ("How can I support you?") builds strong connections and new projects [#123].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### The Silicon Valley question — "How can I support you?"
Origin / when to use. The author had lunch with his friend Mike from San Francisco, who researches consciousness at Stanford (the influence of meditation, trauma and psychedelics on the brain and human activity). They had not seen each other for a year and a half; the author briefly described what deep mind does — working through leaders' outdated ideas about business, helping establish communication and teaching teams to manage their state. Mike listened attentively, said he wanted to ask a "Silicon Valley question", and after a pause asked: «Как я могу тебя поддержать?» ("How can I support you?") [#123].

Effect on the author. He felt confused and then a warm emotional response. He realized he had become somewhat stuck in a "Russian or Moscow mentality": a sale only happens if you are actively selling; you have to ask others to help you and actively do the "work" yourself even at a friendly meeting. The question broke that frame [#123].

Why ask it — Mike's two reasons (the author asked him why he had asked):
1. "Это приятно" (It is pleasant). Mike feels meaning in such acts. The two of them share the same values, so by helping the author he also helps himself realize those values [#123].
2. "Это развивает" (It develops). "Please use my network and knowledge." By opening access to one's resources and frequently asking such questions, people create new projects and strong connections [#123].

The author's takeaway. «Классно жить из ощущения достатка и быть проактивно открытым человеком.» ("It's great to live from a sense of abundance and be a proactively open person.") [#123]

Scaling idea. The author reflected on what strong changes could be achieved by gathering 10–20 people with values of creation (созидание) and helping them ask each other their Silicon Valley question in an atmosphere of supportive dialogues and practices — an idea for a roughly 5-day support retreat [#123].

#### Isolation vs solitude
- Isolation is not the same as being alone. «Изоляция не равна уединению.» ("Isolation is not equal to solitude.") Many people live alone and meet a small number of friends, yet are quite healthy and content [#192].
- The deciding factor is subjective: «Разницу определяет субъективное чувство отрезанности от других.» ("The difference is determined by the subjective feeling of being cut off from others.") [#192]
- Workplace relevance. From the standpoint of employees' state and ability to work normally, under conditions of physical separation (the post is from May 2020, during lockdown), it is important to pay attention to this feeling and question [#192].
- Remedy. Globally, social health can be improved precisely through micro-communities with expressed values: values filter people and increase trust within the group [#192].
- The author ran a poll to very roughly measure the level of social isolation in a narrow sample of people interested in development, mindfulness and new trends in management; he notes that sociological surveys for society as a whole exist without him. #### Small communities as the structure of society
Context. Written in March 2022: around 300+ thousand Russians, 3+ million Ukrainians and some number of Belarusians are "as if spat out into open space" from their own countries. The author calls the unprecedented outflow of the most qualified, talented professionals from Russia the second most significant tragedy of the situation after the fighting itself — a large personal and collective tragedy that requires a period of grieving and depression [#613].

Argument, step by step:
1. Think about tomorrow. At some point the evil will end, and we will be left with the results of the decisions and actions taken now; so the question is where to apply effort to create a happy life for future generations [#613].
2. More effective systems win. From management theory and business history, more effective systems ultimately defeat less effective ones. Combined with Martin Luther King's line «Those who love peace must learn to organize as effectively as those who love war», the author concludes that the future depends not on the ability to assimilate and dissolve into Armenian, Turkish, British or American environments, but on the ability to find in oneself genuine leadership, meanings and ways of organizing that far surpass the "low discourse" that has captured modern Russia [#613].
3. Totalitarianism atomizes. Citing Hannah Arendt's claim that a totalitarian society strives to atomize people, the author sees small communities — from 10 to 100 people — as playing a key role in the struggle for self-awareness and as spaces where leadership and meaning are created [#613].
4. Why small communities work: three ingredients — they have already "taken off" as the most significant and living structure of society because they contain *знание* (*knowing* each other), *доверие* (trust) and *реальная взаимопомощь* (real mutual help) [#613].
5. New identity will come from them. Not now, but eventually people will grieve their losses and find strength to build a new identity. It will come from the experience of supporting each other and the connections in small communities — "precisely there, and not in families or some offices" — that an understanding of oneself as an important group of people on this planet will arise [#613].
6. Most effective way of organizing. For a de-Putinized, decommunized and de-Stalinized Russia these connections and meanings will be very important, because they are created in the most alive places, out of kindness, intelligence and care for each other. «Кажется, это и есть самый эффективный способ организации.» ("It seems this is the most effective way of organizing.") The call: create, join and support communities [#613].

The author's own case. In November 2021 deep mind launched two communities on themes key to them — conscious human development (mindfulness) and the crypto world — first gathering about 60 people in each, then aiming at around 100 each. In the first weeks after February 2022, the author says that for him personally and for many of "us" communities played "almost the most important role" in adapting to the new reality: there people discussed a lot, went through things together, argued and conflicted, but still helped each other a great deal [#537, #613].

What solidarity looked like in practice. Out of solidarity the communities publicly opened or jointly prepared materials: a practice of emotional regulation (EFT); a list of useful resources for mental stability ("let's keep our heads in place"); how not to harm yourself with mindfulness practices; why "they" cannot be talked out of it, or how totalitarian sects work; supports in dark times — a meeting with a Buddhist nun; and, from the crypto community, a widely shared guide on how to move rubles into crypto. Community members, under deep mind supervision, launched a group of supportive practices in Yerevan for emigrants and organized a small local deep mind community on Koh Phangan for coordination and help in Thailand, with Russians, Ukrainians and Belarusians in it. #### Alpha-pattern communities vs communities of honest growth
Diagnosis. «Сообщества успеха и бизнеса плоские и душные.» ("Success and business communities are flat and stuffy.") They are built on the automatic evolutionary pattern of *альфовость* (alphaness): the strongest in the pack gets more resources and attention. In most communities, even young ones, it has stayed the same — the norm is to show that everything is fine with us in order to get even more from others [#537].

Why growth is impossible there (mechanism):
1. Growth means deepening the understanding of oneself and of other people [#537].
2. To deepen, one must consciously meet the emotions, thoughts and sensations present in one's experience and integrate them into self-knowledge [#537].
3. In a community this looks like meeting together with real pain, problems, fears and anxieties — and, of course, jointly experiencing joy, pleasure, connectedness and love [#537].
4. Such genuine experiences are extremely rare in places where we must keep masks of success, or be anxiously and painfully on guard in case someone suspects we don't measure up [#537].

Two traps. Worse still for growth: getting used to the rules of such communities, people over-play them and start sincerely believing in their own chosenness and superiority. The author says this inevitably leads to one of two traps for development: (a) dependence on power and external approval, or (b) boredom and the need to keep entertaining oneself [#537].

Antidote. The author believes that in 2021, with today's level of technology and the spread of psychology and mindfulness, it is possible to go beyond old models of social life and build an environment of real development and support. «Противоядие от автоматических схем и проекций я вижу в честности и открытости.» ("I see the antidote to automatic schemes and projections in honesty and openness.") This is the main thing he wants to create in both of his communities [#537].

#### Narcissistic community — definition, functions, warning signs, alternative
Background. The author argues that capitalism gave people an unprecedented degree of freedom but had side effects. One of the main problems of modern culture is a distorted interpretation of individualism: instead of real self-realization it often turns into narcissism — a "neurotic need" to build oneself and one's status through external attributes of success, which include not only houses but also subtle things like a network or reputation [#1291].

Definition. In group dynamics this produces *narcissistic communities*, which look like spaces of development but are in fact a trap that hinders real movement toward the birth of one's adult personality. A narcissistic community is a group whose main informal priority — possibly not obvious at first — is inflating ego and social status rather than the real development of participants. The author says he has long preferred to leave such circles or watch them from a distance [#1291].

Disguise. Such groups are often formally built around constructive themes — entrepreneurship, personal growth, spiritual practices — but these themes are from the start, or become over time, just a screen for solving quite different tasks [#1291].

The three hidden functions:
1. Strengthening hierarchy (Укреплять иерархию). Resources, attention and influence flow upward, and the leaders' status becomes ever more significant. Mere membership creates a feeling of significance, especially before those who are not in the group [#1291].
2. Creating exclusivity (Создавать эксклюзивность). Access is limited by money, connections and other external markers of success, even if they are falsely phrased as inner qualities of a person [#1291].
3. Maintaining functional relationships (Поддерживать функциональность отношений). Instead of real support and authentic contact, people use each other as a resource [#1291].

The four warning signs ("маячки"):
1. Constant competition (Постоянная конкуренция). Members compare themselves to each other; relationships are built on demonstrating success [#1291].
2. Rigid hierarchy (Жесткая иерархия). Power is concentrated in a small circle and is "felt in the air". Conversations have low transparency; it is hard to constructively discuss power in the group or the principles of its existence. Someone determines levels of access to events or information [#1291].
3. Illusion of chosenness (Иллюзия избранности). "We are the best, the rest are losers." What matters is not the process of development but the chance to belong to the elite [#1291].
4. Toxic retention and communication mechanisms (Токсичные механизмы удержания и коммуникации внутри). Members seem to have to constantly prove their worth, including through participation in expensive rituals — programs, courses, membership fees [#1291].

Bottom line. «Такие сообщества могут давать чувство превосходства и обеспечивать доступ к ресурсам, но не ведут к подлинной свободе» ("Such communities can give a feeling of superiority and provide access to resources, but they do not lead to real freedom") [#1291].

Psychoanalytic grounding (as cited by the author):
- Otto Kernberg, one of the main researchers of narcissism, described *pathological narcissism* — on which such groups are built — as the inability to create authentic relationships based on vulnerability. In narcissistic groups, instead of seeking a genuine identity, a person increasingly hangs in a world of external attributes, in a three-part pattern: «я должен вписываться / чувствую собственную значимость от того, что я вписался / свысока отношусь к тем, кто в подобные группы не вписался» ("I must fit in / I feel my own significance from having fitted in / I look down on those who did not fit into such groups") [#1291].
- Heinz Kohut claims a healthy personality is built on self-worth, not external recognition. Narcissistic communities, in the author's view, constantly heat up dependence on external evaluations, replacing inner work with an endless race for status. That worldview has little in common with the realistic and complex picture of the personality we actually are. It seems to give a person a sense of strength, but simultaneously places them in a hierarchy of constant competition and insecurity through exclusivity. Worse, such an environment pulls attention away from the values and ways of being with others that actually help people become mature personalities — "this is harmful to health" [#1291].

Healthy alternative. There are always healthy alternatives: groups built on equality rather than hierarchy. Power is distributed, real values move away from statuses, and participants matter not for achievements but for authenticity [#1291].

#### Interaction as a "dance of ranges" — why the author prefers people who have done inner work
(Forwarded from the author's personal channel.) Other things being equal, the author finds it more interesting to meet people experienced in inner work, though he tries to stay open to all kinds of people, including marginalized groups (he mentions wanting to talk with a homeless man who was crying in San Francisco, but not feeling confident enough yet in the new city, where it may also be dangerous). In terms of comfort and joy in communication he observes a pattern: it is more interesting with those who are going through some coherent path of inner work [#1120].

Two explanations he rejects (as the main reason):
1. *A shared field for talking* — it is always easier to interact with someone who shares your interests and experience [#1120].
2. *A wish to discuss inner-work experiences* — ayahuasca, therapies, retreats, coaching. In practice he finds few people interesting to discuss therapy or retreats with, although many try to talk to him precisely about this since he writes about it and runs a business on it. Only a few people work on development seriously and systematically, and they no longer chatter about it; the rest usually greatly exaggerate the significance of their second vipassana for others — though he sincerely rejoices at any achievement and stage of the path [#1120].
He grants that both have some sense in them, but they are not the real reason [#1120].

The real reason. With such people it usually becomes possible to move a bit faster into a conversation «по-чеснаку» ("for real, honestly"), in which you don't have to build anything of yourself — partly because the other person doesn't switch on strong "evaluation radars" and isn't actively trying to sell an image of themselves [#1120].

Why people evaluate and filter. The author understands these evaluations and efforts: the importance of survival/prosperity, which many, out of ancestral historical habit, pursue through constant filtering of people — sorting "ours vs. others", creating groups, cliques and gangs, which "nobody has legislatively abolished yet" [#1120].

Choosing to be oneself. Increasingly, he wants to play this pattern through himself less and simply be himself. His concrete examples: at a meeting of successful founders he says he is having a hard month, if that's true; he wears to an evening what matches his mood, not the occasion; he doesn't eat when he doesn't want to while everyone around is having dinner. In short he behaves "a bit like a marginal" who is easy to misunderstand [#1120].

Mechanism — the dance of ranges. With people who have gone through their own path and dropped some personal clamps, becoming more honest inside with themselves, there is immediately more lightness, authenticity and freedom. However free he himself is (he adds that he has much room to grow), «любое взаимодействие это все-таки танец диапазонов» ("any interaction is still a dance of ranges"): if the other person is actively playing roles rather than living themselves in the moment, it pulls him down into roles and masks too [#1120].

Conclusion. These preferences are about neither safety nor attachment to experiences and inner work. «Опыты — всего лишь опыты и не представляют особой ценности, если человек не растет и не меняется.» ("Experiences are just experiences and have no special value if a person does not grow and change.") Rather, they reflect a love of freedom and honesty, "above which there is no pleasure in this world" — where two "marginals" can meet and discover they are not marginals at all [#1120].

## Willpower, self-control and habits: what the evidence says

### What this covers

One long post by the deep mind 2.0 author traces the 20-year life of the "willpower is a muscle" idea (ego depletion): the original 1998 experiment, the snowball of supporting studies and pop applications (identical founder breakfasts and T-shirts, decision fatigue, glucose, the Israeli judges), and the collapse after correction for publication bias and a large preregistered replication. It then covers the post-collapse picture — a possible threshold effect, Inzlicht's motivation/attention model, the failed "belief in depletion" idea — and the author's practical conclusion: high self-control comes from habits and fewer temptations, and real gains lie in intention, conative intelligence and attention training. According to the source this matters because people scold themselves for "weak will" possibly for nothing, and because careers and trainings were built for two decades around an effect that most likely does not exist [#1902].

### Core ideas

- Willpower is not a muscle. The theory that self-control is a depletable resource was treated as established fact for about 20 years and then did not pass verification: bias-corrected meta-analysis and a 36-lab replication both found an effect statistically indistinguishable from zero [#1902].
- Science is a process of refinement, not a set of reinforced-concrete facts. Smart, conscientious people built careers and trainings on an effect that "most likely" does not exist; the author adds "we are still in the sandbox" [#1902].
- High self-control is mostly about not needing willpower. People with high self-control face choices less often, because they have fewer temptations on their path and more habits in the right places [#1902].
- Do not fall in love with explanations because they are scientific or beautiful — including this one. The author explicitly extends the skepticism to his own post [#1902].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Rise and fall of ego depletion (timeline of evidence)
Purpose: a worked case study of how an attractive scientific theory spreads and then fails, used by the author to calibrate trust in "scientific" self-help [#1902].

1. 1998 — the original experiment. Roy Baumeister seated hungry students in front of a plate of cookies and a bowl of radishes. One group was allowed the cookies, the other only the radishes; afterwards everyone got a puzzle that had no solution. Those who had chewed radishes (i.e., had to resist the cookies) gave up twice as fast — 8 minutes versus 19. The conclusion "suggested itself": self-control is a resource and it gets spent. The theory was named ego depletion (истощение эго) [#1902].
2. The muscle metaphor. It came later, together with the promise that will can be used up or "pumped" by regular exercises. The metaphor perfectly explained evening breakdowns, a collapsed diet, and the inability to be a good parent right after negotiations — which is part of why it was so appealing [#1902].
3. The snowball. Hundreds of confirming experiments; a meta-analysis with a solid effect; side branches in all directions: (a) will "feeds on" blood sugar; (b) decision fatigue — the source of the identical breakfasts and T-shirts of founders; (c) the Israeli judges, whose share of approved parole (УДО) fell from 65% at the start of a session to almost zero before lunch; (d) Baumeister's bestseller. The theory moved from labs into trainings and entrepreneurial practice [#1902].
4. 2014 — first signs. The same meta-analysis was recalculated with a correction for publication bias. The author explains the mechanism: journals print experiments that worked, while those that showed nothing stay out; the size of this skew can be estimated, and after correcting for it the willpower effect turned out indistinguishable from zero [#1902].
5. 2021 — the large replication. The original experiment was repeated at scale: 36 laboratories, 3,500 people. The protocol was preregistered: everyone committed to publish the result whatever it was and not to tweak the analysis after the fact. Result: again statistically indistinguishable from zero [#1902].
6. The side branches fall too. The glucose version was not confirmed. The judges, it turned out, did not hear cases in random order — prisoners without lawyers came at the end of sessions, which explains the drop without any depletion [#1902].
7. 2025 — a partial finding. Another study did find the effect, but only with an exhausting task. The author's reading: depletion may have a threshold, but possibly it is not about willpower at all [#1902].

#### Inzlicht's motivation-and-attention shift model
Michael Inzlicht proposed a different model of what happens after long effort: nothing "runs out"; instead motivation and attention shift — the brain gets tired of "надо" ("have to") and is pulled toward "хочется" ("want to"). In the author's words this is a need, not an empty tank of will. It is offered as the alternative explanation for the threshold effect seen in the 2025 study [#1902].

#### Alternatives to willpower (what the author says actually helps)
1. Fewer temptations and more habits. Per a newer line of research the author finds convincing, people with high self-control rely on willpower less; they stand before a choice less often because there are fewer temptations on their way and more habits in the needed places [#1902].
2. Science plus radical empiricism. The author suggests relying not only on science but on science together with the radical empiricism used in contemplative traditions (first-person testing) [#1902].
3. Work with intention and conative intelligence. According to the author, contemplative traditions long ago found that the real profit lies here [#1902].
4. Attention training. The author says what is lacking is stability of attention sufficient to move toward a goal without distractions, so attention should be trained [#1902].
5. Environment design — works, but modestly. Environment design also works, but more modestly than commonly thought; the author backs "скромнее" with a link to the Retraction Watch report on JAMA journals retracting six papers by food-marketing researcher Brian Wansink [#1902].

### Detailed insights

#### The founders' identical breakfast as a downstream myth
The post opens with the popular practice of entrepreneurs eating the same thing for breakfast to avoid wasting willpower on trivia and save it for important decisions. The author traces this habit (and identical T-shirts) to the decision-fatigue branch of ego depletion, and closes: you may decide what to eat for breakfast and wear different T-shirts [#1902].

#### The "it's all suggestion" turn (Dweck)
The author addresses trainees who explain any change of state after a practice as mere suggestion (самовнушение). The story had a "beautiful twist" for them: Carol Dweck found that willpower depletes mainly in those who believe it depletes — "checkmate". The author calls this tempting but untrue: a later check did not confirm the idea. The lesson is the same as the whole post: an elegant explanation is not thereby a true one [#1902].

#### Self-blame for "weak will"
Given the evidence, the author says that if you scold yourself for weak willpower, you may be doing so in vain — the deficit is better addressed through habits, fewer temptations and attention than through effort [#1902].

### Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- (!) UNCLEAR: the key sentence on high self-control is garbled in the source — "люди с высоким самоконтролем реже вообще меньше на нее опираются" (people with high self-control less often at all rely less on it); the intended meaning appears to be that they rely on willpower less, but the exact wording is broken [#1902].
- The author hedges the central claim: the effect "скорее всего" (most likely) does not exist, and the 2025 study did find an effect under exhausting tasks — so a threshold form of depletion remains open, with Inzlicht's model offered as one possible explanation, not a settled one [#1902].
- The claims about contemplative traditions (intention, conative intelligence, radical empiricism) are the author's opinion and are not backed by studies in the post; the author also applies his skepticism to his own text [#1902].
- The post contains typos ("мастшабе", "не подвердилась", "в воли" [sic]) that do not affect meaning [#1902].

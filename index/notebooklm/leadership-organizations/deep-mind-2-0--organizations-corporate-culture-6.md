## Founder lessons from building a meaning-driven company

### What this covers

This file collects what the founder of Deep mind (the author, Max) taught from his own experience of building a company devoted to mindfulness, well-being and humane organizational culture between 2019 and 2023: why and how Deep mind was born, his "error of the year" post-mortem on a five-founder partnership, the partnership and management model that replaced it, two-part year reviews with concrete numbers, the 2022 restructuring after the war began, how a community formed around the business, why projects about human development fail, the concept of generative entrepreneurship, his views on mentoring, founder overload, fear of novelty and leadership, and the "healing" culture that emerged in the team. [#52, #134, #257, #349, #350, #720, #728, #817, #821, #855, #859]

According to the author, sharing such lessons openly is itself a business practice: a policy of openness builds the habit of being honest and careful with words and therefore increases the resilience of his business. [#134]

This part collects the founder's (Max Rodin's) reflective posts about deep mind, the first company on the post-Soviet space built around corporate mindfulness: what the hiring market reveals about hunger for healthy workplaces, the 2024 crisis in which he publicly considered closing the company, his article on the founder's fear of failure, a structured post-mortem of three failed projects, a year review of the company's "firsts" (including inclusivity), and the 2025 update on how the crisis was resolved. According to the source, these lessons matter because meaning-driven, non-capitalist-purpose projects that survive in a capitalist environment without donations are rare, and classical business logic (scale, delegate, hire ahead of demand) can destroy them [#932, #1148, #1404].

### Core ideas

- Early-stage companies need survival management, not collective leaderless governance. A young company is closer to crisis management than to business; either an authoritarian style or a very highly integrated team works — inviting many experienced founders without a clear leader produced chaos instead of synergy (see *Five-founder partnership post-mortem*). [#134]
- Hierarchies are outdated as the main form of organization. They cause negative selection, suppress creativity and human relationships; new organizational forms require negotiating, influencing and sensing other people rather than commanding. This conviction is the origin of Deep mind. [#257]
- Focus and client selection drive growth. Refusing clients who must be persuaded of the value of caring for employees, and focusing on advanced, flexible companies, is credited for 2020 growth; lack of a single scalable product (MVP) and overload remained the main failures. [#349, #350]
- For knowledge-intensive projects, content is the most ecological route to corporate sales, more than a sales department. [#720]
- Human-development projects fail for specific reasons: founder naivety about development, lack of expertise, poor work with experts and the wrong (non-helping) motivation. [#821]
- Generative entrepreneurship is the label the author found for Deep mind: business should solve current problems and also create health, resilience and resources for the future, cultivating community rather than extracting. [#817, #859]
- Culture as lived values, not formal documents. Deep mind has only three formulated values and no KPIs, yet team members speak of "healing"; the author attributes this to the systematic embodiment of values and mindfulness skills over three years. [#859]
- Leading something new means constantly absorbing others' projections, anxieties and anger; most of the work is methodical evolution against inertia rather than revolution. [#855]

- Healthy, meaningful spaces are a competitive asset, not a soft extra. Candidates from big corporations say they would work even for free for meaning and an ecological environment; deep mind's healthy community produced investment, hires and a waiting list [#932].
- "Skin in the game" and no-alternative commitment drive long-term results, but fatigue and loss of faith are dangerous to the enterprise; systemic fatigue comes from imbalance between effort and reward [#1148].
- In a meaning-driven company founder dependence is the essence of the process at this stage, mainly product dependence, because few people have the interdisciplinary expertise and can withstand the cultural conflict the product tries to resolve [#1148, #1404].
- Fear of failure narrows focus to "sell at any cost", pulling attention from the client and from the mission; it must be noticed, accepted and partly used, partly released [#1230].
- Failures should be shared and analysed openly in a structured way (idea, results, reasons, lessons) [#1258].
- The investment-driven pivot to focus only on B2B, hire ahead of demand and over-delegate was the main error; cutting everything and returning to core value produced the most stable revenue in six years [#1404].
- deep mind cannot be reduced to a traditional business: it is about authenticity and love, which cannot be sold but can be found and passed on [#1404].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Five-founder partnership post-mortem ("error of the year")
Purpose: the author's public analysis of his main founder mistake of 2019, written when the five-person partnership format ended (February 2020) after a long, morally difficult process that began in November and which he says led to great personal growth for each partner. [#134]

1. The error. He invited into the company not executors but founders — many of them, each with established experience of running their own business. [#134]
2. What he already knew. An early-stage company must first of all survive: manage finances tightly, decide fast and watch client needs extremely closely. This resembles crisis management more than business, and an authoritarian style is adequate for this period. The alternative is a flexible team with a very high degree of integration: a shared vision, shared values and very good procedures for fast decision-making. [#134]
3. What they did instead. Such integration is hard to achieve at once, yet they intuitively chose a model of collective management without a manifest leader. [#134]
4. His mistaken assumptions. (a) Since people came for his vision, he assumed they also agreed on the ways to achieve it. (b) Thinking in terms of a business rather than self-employment, he expected experienced people to throw themselves at the tasks and "сделают пятилетку за три года" (fulfil the five-year plan in three years). [#134]
5. What actually happened. In a company without a clear product, the road to the goal looks different in different heads. "Вместо эффекта синергии мы реализовали эффект хаоса" (Instead of a synergy effect we realized a chaos effect), and they suffered from painful attempts to pull each other in their own habitual directions. [#134]
6. Outcome. Current projects were closed successfully and part of the partnerships ended. He feels great liberation and a return of speed and flexibility, plus some sadness that they were not conscious enough about the question of power, which, approached correctly, could have bound a strong team together for larger results. He thanks the partners and says he learned much from the conflicts and differences. [#134]

Lesson stated in a separate post: with more frequent contact with experienced mentors he would have discussed in detail which partner brings what into the project and how results are evaluated (see *Mentoring as a two-way road*). [#52]

#### Manager-executor model with meshwork and satellite partnerships
Purpose: the model the author committed to after the failed partnership, "до создания самоорганизации" (until self-organization is created). [#134]

1. Management model: manager-executor ("руководитель-исполнитель") within a culture of direct communication, adult-adult relationships, an evolutionary purpose — to make business more humane — and values defined by the founder. [#134]
2. Partnerships by meshwork and satellites: a network of potential partners and narrow specialists for projects is already formed and constantly expanded. [#134]
3. No equity in the core company: he gives nobody a share in Deep mind; instead, for each task he creates a separate joint project. [#134]
4. Explicit contribution contracts: partners define very clearly what resource they invest and how they understand the success of their contribution. [#134]
5. Narrow core team, project-based specialists: there is a small core team, but most specialists are selected per project. [#134]
6. Why: according to the author, all this provides maximum efficiency and adaptability of the product to the client. [#134]

#### Essentialism filter for projects
In 2019 Deep mind did many projects of very different nature; the novelty of each allowed strong growth but also caused fatigue from lack of focus. For 2020 the author practices essentialism: he refuses everything that does not evoke the feeling "точно да" (definitely yes) and does not fit three formats: (1) mindfulness courses, (2) group formats for leaders, (3) manual, long, expensive work with organizations. [#134]

The opportunities he said would inspire him for late 2020–2021 (showing what "definitely yes" meant for him):
1. A consulting project on corporate culture and well-being in the USA, Europe or Asia.
2. A consulting project on culture and efficiency for the state apparatus in Russia.
3. Leading, alone or with one of Deep mind's trainers, regular mindfulness classes in a beautiful place in Moscow.
4. Helping implement a full-cycle cultural transformation project for a commercial company in Russia.
5. Community-based volunteers to help organize open projects on mindfulness and leadership. [#134]

(!) CONTRADICTION: item 2 (consulting for the Russian state apparatus) conflicts with the later stance that Deep mind fully stopped selling to state companies and bureaucratic organizations (see *Client selection*). The later post presents this as a learned change. [#134, #350]

#### Year review in two parts (2020)
Purpose: an annual "integration of experience" done after all projects close — first listing what was wanted but not done, then what was wanted and done — and rebalancing goals for the next year. The author stresses he does not experience the first list as self-criticism or something to worry about: he simply looks at what he wanted and could not do. He considered 2020 the first truly full year of successful work as a startup rather than an idea. [#349]

Part 1 — wanted but did not do:
1. Did not find the MVP. They launched 3 new products in addition to 2 existing ones, but it was still unclear what would become the key direction or form of working with people. [#349]
2. Could not focus on that one product and start scaling it. This follows from point 1, and it is very important for creating a sustainable business rather than a simple service company. [#349]
3. Spoke publicly and worked with media too little. Because of the focus on products and clients, they missed broad marketing and could not tell well about their socially important work; few people learned about them, which he finds painful. [#349]
4. Lost contact with the mission (but "Хорошо хоть не похерили ценности" — *at least we didn't screw up the values*). Overloaded with projects in the second half of the year, all attention went to quality of client work and they forgot the big tasks that make it worth overcoming difficulties: to revolutionarily improve organizations by shifting focus to the human in the company; to increase the well-being, satisfaction and happiness of clients; to create a culture of healthy and meaningful life. [#349]
5. The founder could not substantially unload himself from small tasks. Despite a personal assistant for the second year and a noticeably developed delegation skill, he remains in permanent overload. This affects contact with the mission and the ability to see perspective and understand strategy, so the business moves slower than it could. [#349]
6. Did not build a truly working advisory board. Its importance was clear a year earlier; they do consult other entrepreneurs, but he wants it to be a reproducible, regular, quality process rather than random chaos. [#349]
7. Did not sort out fundraising. Maybe they do not need it (though it seems they do), but they did not clearly understand it and could not approach the question properly. [#349]

Part 2 — wanted and did:
1. Rebuilt the product line in the crisis and survived. February–March were frightening and uncertain; in April they pivoted and created a fast, simple course on working with stress through mindfulness practices and modern science, *Less stress*, which became their main basic product and was much needed that year. They also launched 2 more products: in-person retreats and a 3-month leaders' program. [#350]
2. Became partners of several leading companies in employee development and advanced corporate culture, judged by projects extended into 2021. They concentrated on the most advanced Russian companies developing flexible non-hierarchical structures and trying to work globally, not only locally. [#350]
3. Started working with two Silicon Valley startups; receiving transfers from Silicon Valley Bank was very pleasant and he wants to develop this direction more in 2021. [#350]
4. Fully stopped investing time in clients who must be persuaded of the value of real care and employee development — which he names as the cause of points 2–3 (see *Client selection*). [#350]
5. Created a truly integrated, flat and living core team. Largely thanks to working with the right clients who understand the value of their tools, but also thanks to a tedious, long elaboration of team values at the start of the year and a lot of intense work together. They began applying in practice the principles of a living self-organizing team, built "даже не на доверии, а на любви друг к другу" (not even on trust but on love for each other). Integration naturally raised product quality. [#350]
6. Entered the 11% of companies that grew turnover in 2020 (see *Evidence and numbers*). [#350]

The author's interpretation: the year's successes reflect attunement with objective processes. Those who grew were those who sense change and adapt rather than cling to the old. Digitalization, remote work and the need for adaptive flexible teams did not appear because of COVID but were accelerated by it; this will accelerate further, as will demand for tools that help teams rebuild for life in the information economy. [#350]

#### Deep mind's field of theories and practices
The author lists the field that became the fundamental basis of all Deep mind products, adding that the list is not even complete: [#257]
1. Ken Wilber's Integral theory — a meta-model for the development of people and organizations, from which many of the theories below flowed.
2. Otto Scharmer's Theory U — a model for transforming organizations.
3. Embodiment approach — development through bodily awareness and practices.
4. Mindfulness and neuroscience — development through attentiveness and the creation of new neural connections.
5. Neo-psychoanalysis and modern psychotherapy.
6. Psychology of adult development — Torbert, Cook-Greuter, Beck and Cowan.
7. Non-directive coaching.
8. The living-organization paradigm — Kegan, Laloux, Holacracy, Sociocracy. [#257]

These drove him since 2009, when he attended his first retreat and read Wilber's "A Brief History of Everything" ("Краткую теорию всего"); he certified in many of the approaches and continues to. He believes these tools brought him to qualitative change himself, which made him realize their potential. Deep mind's approach: apply theories and practices from psychology and development to problems companies could not solve at the level of product or business processes. [#257]

#### Four failure patterns of human-development projects
Context: Deep mind receives requests for advising or partnership from projects on human development or team culture at least several times a month; over 4 years the author watched the rise and fall of many such projects almost from the inside. The market looks sweet — needs are obvious, good solutions are rare, and building them seems easy — so new teams build test apps, record courses on their own meditation method, or mix psychotherapy exercises with AI. Most end in nothing, even with team sizes and investments Deep mind never dreamed of. The patterns, which the author says he personally senses and which are specific to this market: [#821]

1. Beginner's cognitive error: believing development is simple. Having consumed a lot of psychological experience, many think they have found the truth. His illustrative story: five years earlier he asked his colleague and embodiment teacher Mark Walsh (UK) for the world's top meditation-instructor training programs; Mark said something like "найди превосходного учителя и будь при нем 10 лет. Нельзя обучиться медитации на курсах" (find an excellent teacher and stay with them for 10 years. You cannot learn meditation in courses). [#821]
2. The founder lacks expertise. Most founders of psychology-based projects do not understand the essence of what they work with. A person or a group is a very complex living system, no easier to understand than economics, politics or finance, and knowledge in this field is in a renaissance and changing fast. Founders transfer marketing or consulting experience to the product without realizing they took on the far harder task of working with the human psyche; they do not feel the psychological process underlying the product and therefore cannot build a working process. [#821]
3. Founders build poor interaction with experts. Smart founders know that without education they know little about psychology and bring in experts. But because the founder does not live in expert communities and does not sense them, he either cannot tell a good expert from a bad one or tries to remake the expert into an entrepreneur instead of building a trusting partnership. [#821]
4. A helping project launched without helping motivation. The wrong motivation — to make something big rather than valuable, or to make money ("бапки") and an exit — is bad for any project, though some run on it for a while. For projects about people it is doubly bad because it destroys the value-creation chain or the logic of the process at the very start. [#821]

Caveat from the author: info-business works without any of this and is often more successful than real projects; but in his view this model is not a sustainable way of doing business, and its satisfaction cannot compare with the meaning that arises in projects with real value. [#821]

#### Generative entrepreneurship
Purpose: the label the author finally found for Deep mind's activity, which always felt cramped within "impact" or "social" entrepreneurship. [#817]

Definition. Generative Entrepreneurship is an approach focused on creating long-term value and sustainable social change by developing new innovative business models that work with more perspectives and dimensions than traditional extractive business. It goes far beyond positive impact and includes rethinking some commonly accepted principles for a collective transformation capable of affecting future generations. Instead of squeezing resources out of the world around it, it focuses on cultivating community. [#817]

Core idea. "бизнес должен решать не только текущие проблемы, но и создавать здоровье, устойчивость и любые другие блага и ресурсы для будущего" (business must solve not only current problems but also create health, resilience and any other goods and resources for the future). This not only improves shared reality but also increases the resilience and adaptability of the business itself in an era of constant change, because it is built on the deepest value and accounts for a broad range of perspectives. [#817, #859]

Why now. Generative projects existed before, since some people grew into ecosystem thinking long ago, though not many. Now they can be singled out as a new wave because public consciousness is growing and systems vision appears more often; combined with technological progress, the author expects (with "вероятно" — *probably*) exponential growth of such businesses and of the desire to work in them in coming years, which will make extractive business even less sustainable. [#817]

Differences:
- From impact entrepreneurship: generative entrepreneurship aims at systemic change for all participants of the ecosystem, not only the disadvantaged. [#817]
- From ordinary business: benefit goes to the whole ecosystem, not only to owners. This creates the primacy of value over short-term profit, which breaks a whole paradigm of supposedly unshakable business logic. [#817]

Methods (generative entrepreneurs extend business practice with methods that take maximum perspectives into account):
1. Co-creative planning from Theory U — a process of creating business models with active participation of all stakeholders in the broad sense, from clients to citizens and nature; it helps create innovations meeting the needs of the whole ecosystem.
2. Sustainable financing — financial management that takes into account social and environmental indicators, not only financial results.
3. Open innovation.
4. Systems approach.
5. Integration of values. [#817]
(!) INCOMPLETE: methods 3–5 are only named in the source, not explained.

Examples:
- The Empowerment Plan — makes special down coats that convert into sleeping bags for homeless people; the idea is to create jobs for homeless people and reduce homelessness in America. [#817]
- Echale a tu casa — a Mexican company helping people build homes from ecologically clean materials; it provides access to training and financing so families can build their own homes, and creates jobs for local residents. [#817]

Potential. Given the failure of many global institutions and state policies to solve a range of social problems, the author suggests GE may become a new model of collective change for challenges previously outside business attention: sustainable development, justice, poverty, mental health and healing of collective trauma. [#817]

Later the author connects this to team culture: once people are free of permanent fear they make creative decisions and launch incredible initiatives — which he calls the main essence of generative entrepreneurship; many mistakenly see it as an idea for romantics, missing its huge potential. [#859]

#### Mentoring as a two-way road
Context: the author, whose company itself sells courses, notes an emerging allergy to courses from all sides, yet values getting very practical knowledge from others quickly. Taking a friend's course on relationship management, he failed nearly all assignments (studying in December is hard) but still got a large benefit, and decided to work seriously on mentoring in 2020. [#52]

1. Make current mentors conscious. He already had several "unconscious" mentors — very experienced people he meets several times a year to share successes and news: an ex-head at the level of the Russian Government, a leader of the embodiment community, an ex-head of communications of AIZhK, spiritual teachers, trainers and facilitators from Brazil, France and Norway. He does not always understand why they help, but the help is much needed. [#52]
2. Identify the gaps. He saw he especially needed business mentors and mentors in publicity and CEO effectiveness; later also in business, partner relationships and life under overload. He expects to find "действительно взрослых людей с высокими ценностями" (truly grown-up people with high values). [#52]
3. Count the cost of not having them. More contact with people who have gone all the way in building a business and changing culture would have avoided many of his year's mistakes: he would have discussed in detail which partner brings what and how results are evaluated; instead of spending half a year looking for someone to speak with him and write articles, he would have written them himself; he would have refused half of incoming tasks; he would have been less nervous; and he would have expressed anger even faster and with greater transparency. [#52]
4. Find new mentors for specific needs. His new mentor was the head of RBC Publishing House; he felt pleasant goosebumps hearing "yes". Making business more humane requires painstaking media work that is hard for him even after a year leading communications for a large international grain supplier. [#52]
5. Mentor others too. To build the process effectively one must also mentor someone; then one understands the joy of it, since "менторство - это точно двухсторонняя дорога. Мы растем вместе" (mentoring is definitely a two-way road. We grow together). He planned to mentor 2 people in authorship of their own lives — searching for meanings, values and authenticity, and above all the courage to fight for them — with a commitment of at least 3 months, at least an hour a day of various practices, without skips or excuses. [#52]

#### Community emerging around a business
1. Do not invent a community for fashion or money. Deep mind's community arose naturally a couple of years after start, through active steps toward its mission of creating a more meaningful and healthy society. [#728]
2. Participants want continued contact. After the first programs in 2019 (run to influence company atmosphere and share tools from psychology and mindfulness), participants wanted to stay in touch with the team, or with the values of ecological growth, safety, openness and sensitivity which the team tries — "не всегда успешно" (not always successfully) — to maintain and transmit. [#728]
3. Why environment matters. In purposeful desired change (often called personal transformation, a word the author dislikes), surroundings become either a brake or a support; for someone tired of the old life model and seeking genuine development, such support is hard to overestimate. [#728]
4. Overload signals the moment to change form. Requests for support became so numerous that the founder felt overload and imbalance violating the value of doing everything from love, including love for himself. He did not want to ignore them, feeling responsibility for people's searches: how to find a good psychotherapist; where to go on retreat; what will truly advance my development; why do I feel bad; how to become an ecological leader; where to learn to build a humane team. [#728]
5. Grow from a hub into a space. The team decided to grow from a center that distributes such requests from its own expertise into a center creating a space free of bullshit methods and violence, where people help each other and get more benefit. In October 2021 they began developing the community as a product so that it would be sustainable for everyone. [#728]
6. What holds it together. Probably interest in a fuller, deeper life and requests about development tools, warmth and openness in relationships. Members chat, meet in person, run events with professionals and create projects together, but when asked why they stay they most often describe a simple, not fully rational feeling: "Я просто знаю, что мне надо здесь быть в это время" (I just know I need to be here at this time). This is much less about useful contacts, education or content — and this feeling changes the whole space and distinguishes it. [#728]

#### Working with the fear of failure
Purpose: a method from the author's article for The Top Voices (an American media outlet for entrepreneurs, titled on the cover image "A FOUNDER'S GUIDE TO MANAGING FAILURE", by Max Rodin, Founder and CEO, Deep Mind, October 31, 2024) for transforming the founder's fear of failure into a resource [#1230].

Context / why it is needed: when starting a business we face harsh statistics — the author states that 95% of businesses do not survive the five-year mark. However strongly a leader believes in success, fear of failure always lives in them; the higher the stakes, the stronger this conscious or unconscious fear. Research the author cites names fear of failure as one of the main barriers to entrepreneurship: it hinders seeing opportunities, restrains risk-taking and growth, and isolates founders from colleagues and partners, depriving them of support and lowering the chance of success. It can knock a person down physically — a founder friend of the author's ended up in hospital when an ambitious project's investor money ran out [#1230].

The mechanism of harm: successful projects depend on many variables (the economy, the founder's ability to build a team and take right strategic steps), but ultimately a project will not succeed without the ability to feel what value and benefit it gives the user and to actually create it. Fear of failure focuses the entrepreneur on the need to sell at any cost, drawing attention away from the client, the client's needs, and the founder's own drive or mission — which is the only thing that lets one overcome the hard challenges of the entrepreneurial path. This can strip the business of its essence and lead the founder to exhaustion [#1230].

Steps:
1. Become aware of and acknowledge the fear. Skillful work "безусловно начинается с его осознания и признания" (certainly begins with its awareness and acknowledgment). If something is so frightening that we cannot look at it and push it to the back of consciousness, we lose all ability to manage it [#1230].
2. Check your experience for this fear and normalize the possibility of failure. In one's beliefs, meditations and therapeutic work, one should test experience for the presence of this fear and build a worldview in which the probability of failure is something normal one can interact with, rather than a scenario to be avoided with all one's strength [#1230].
3. Hold the fear in awareness and feel it in the body. With the capacity to keep fear in the field of consciousness and even to sense it bodily, transformation of the emotion into a resource can begin [#1230].
4. Recognize fear's constructive contribution and thank it. The author says research shows not only harm but benefit: fear prompts entrepreneurs to plan better, prepare for difficulties and analyse risks. By acknowledging this and thanking this manifestation of one's emotional sphere for its care, one balances its influence [#1230].
5. Select: keep the useful part, release the rest. Choose the part of the fear that is useful and constructive, and refuse the other part that slows creative thinking and necessary risks. The author frames awareness of and gratitude toward fear as "практикой благородных воинов" (a practice of noble warriors), which teaches avoiding fear's negative influence on the project and leaning on it where useful [#1230].

Author's own example: after going full-time into deep mind, his fear never ended; he believed in success and sometimes worked more than he could, but each stage of success brought joy together with the knowledge that it guaranteed nothing. Fear partly motivated him to work, partly prevented seeing the full picture. After five years of deep inner work he realized it had substantially knocked his focus off: his failures in working with fear led him toward a product he did not really want to do. Only by truly contacting the fear and living through an understanding of the reality of the project's death could he let it go and move into products that really matter. He still feels gratitude toward fear because it always tried to help — "просто его возможности и методы весьма ограничены" (it's just that its capabilities and methods are rather limited) [#1230].

#### Failure post-mortem template (Idea, Results, Reasons, Lessons)
Purpose: the structure the author uses to share deep mind's "fakaps" (failures) after 5.5 years of experimenting with formats, audiences and products in mindfulness and helping people. He believes that "в наше нарциссическое время" (in our narcissistic time) it is useful to discuss failures, not only successes. Each project is described as Idea → Results (or current status) → Reasons for closure / limited success → Lessons [#1258].

Case 1 — deep mind Community
- Idea: create a permanent supportive environment for people interested in development practices and methods.
- Results: over 2 years, with an annual fee of 50–60 thousand rubles, about 120 people joined; more than 60 meetings with leading experts were held (11 of them made publicly available), plus many sessions and mini-programs. Many participants still express gratitude for the processes in the community.
- Reasons for closure: (a) no sustainable business model was found without the founder's constant personal involvement; (b) the project demanded huge resources, distracting from other important directions without giving deep mind overall stability; (c) the team lacked experience in creating governance processes and building self-organization — the only form in which the community could exist; (d) people with different values appeared, along with conflict situations the team could not resolve effectively.
- Lesson: from the very start think through a sustainable development model for the community and rely on successful experience in creating governance mechanisms and shaping culture [#1258].

Case 2 — Emotional Balance (Эмоциональный баланс)
- Idea: create an optimal process for restoring the nervous system through a specially composed set of physiological practices.
- Results: the product solves its task, helps not only to cope with stress symptoms but teaches managing difficult emotional states; a study after the first launch showed impressive results (!) INCOMPLETE: the study's figures are not given.
- Status: still sold regularly via deep mind's channels, but could not be scaled and therefore developed.
- Reasons for limited success: loss of focus — the company focused all this time on the B2B direction, which showed better results; difficulty explaining the product's value to a broad audience; no effective scaling channels found.
- Lesson: understand the limited personal resources of founders and the importance of focus; a great product by itself, without understanding how to bring it to the audience, is unlikely to take off [#1258].

Case 3 — Cultural adaptation program for emigrants
- Idea: create a deep, scientifically grounded process for integrating into a new culture, based on mindfulness skills.
- Results: despite involving a specialist with an academic degree who teaches at a European university, and a month of intensive product work, the program could not be launched successfully.
- Reasons: (a) poor launch timing — July, when people are less inclined to learn; (b) possibly overestimating people's readiness for deep integration into the local culture; (c) mismatch between the request identified in research and the audience's real need.
- Lesson: choose launch timing carefully and check the audience's real readiness for the proposed solution [#1258].

Closing point: each "failed" project still gave valuable experience and benefited many people; the team continues learning from mistakes [#1258].

#### Recovery from the steroid-team crisis (return to core meaning)
Purpose: how the author got deep mind out of the 2024 crisis (4 months of completely lost revenue) [#1404].

The error (cascade): after raising an investment round, the team decided it could no longer afford lack of focus and should do only the corporate direction, which historically brought the most revenue. The wish to "roll up sleeves and take the market by storm" led to a cascade of wrong actions: a "стероидная команда" (steroid team) appeared — people hired ahead of demand to improve processes and increase sales. The author over-believed in delegation and external expertise, and "немного забыл про смысл того, что делаю" (somewhat forgot the meaning of what I do), drying up morally because he cannot only chase metrics [#1404].

Steps of the recovery as described:
1. Start untangling with yourself. The crisis was not separate from the founder himself; reality "приказала отпустить любые фиксированные идеи и сдаться самым сильным страхам" (ordered me to let go of any fixed ideas and surrender to the strongest fears) [#1404].
2. Allow more play; stop forcing. He stopped "натягивать сову на глобус" (*pulling the owl onto the globe*, i.e. forcing reality to fit a plan): no longer trying at all costs to hit the revenue plan, no longer building B2B sales as a separate function, no longer pouring money into team and marketing for growth [#1404].
3. Accept the playing-coach role. He understood dm is a unique, very personal process born around a personal mission and built around his own expertise; a business of a completely new format where he may always have to be a playing coach — and that is fine because through the essence of these processes he grows and feels highest realization [#1404].
4. Take functions back and cut costs. He took back many tasks he had for years tried to delegate "по обычной бизнес-логике" (by ordinary business logic); the team was reduced and spending of investment stopped entirely for now [#1404].
5. Refocus on core value in live products. Focus shifted away from B2B, processes and scaling toward simple concentration on the main value he wants to create and its implementation in live products [#1404].

Result: the situation changed "моментально" (instantly); from October to September they achieved the most stable workload and revenue in all 6 years, and deep mind became highly diversified by products. The author became more satisfied and calm because he stopped forcing himself to do what he does not want; he has never been so inspired and so busy at once, and now has two assistants [#1404].

### Detailed insights

#### Origin of Deep mind: a career crisis and a critique of hierarchy
Deep mind was born of the author's personal career crisis in 2017, when he was head of communications of a large company (OZK), unhappy despite a good salary, big tasks and former deputy ministers as bosses. He allows that he may simply not have been made for hierarchies — or that "иерархии способны удовлетворить вдолгую все меньшее количество людей" (hierarchies are able to satisfy fewer and fewer people in the long run). [#257]

His generalization: large corporate machines produce negative personnel selection. Only two types take root: narcissistic people who want to rule and prove their worth, or simple executors glad to follow orders and not bother as long as they are left alone. "В иерархиях нет ничего плохого, но они слишком часто насилуют творчество, гибкость и человеческие отношения" (There is nothing wrong with hierarchies, but they too often violate creativity, flexibility and human relationships). They are simply outdated as the main form of organization. [#257]

Mechanism: society is moving into the information age; whole generations grow up connected to the internet; many changes demand working faster, more flexibly, more effectively than hierarchies allow. Facing this, people seek new organizational forms in which one cannot lead by command alone: "мы должны договариваться, влиять, чувствовать других людей" (we must negotiate, influence, sense other people). [#257]

Path: the author left the corporate world, began lecturing on modern companies and working personally with people on mindfulness practices. Experimenting with what he truly liked, he felt a deep resonance with helping the world move to more humane forms of business — living organizations, conscious capitalism, teal companies. By then he had 10 years of expertise in human-development practices, which he believes lay a correct, deep foundation for the next leap in the organizational world. [#257]

#### What Deep mind does — the "unclear product" problem
People often told the author that Deep mind does something obviously great and his talks resonate, yet they do not understand what exactly the company does. For an entrepreneur this is an alarm: the product is poorly articulated. He agrees they are "on the way" — but so is the world's organizational culture; they are adepts of a new emerging paradigm which can perhaps be explained in two words but not understood without complex shifts in worldview. [#228]

Well-being and mental-health products and startups are clearly growing; Deep mind somewhat involuntarily plays in this field, talking to companies about stress, burnout and mindfulness, but its idea was always broader. The big transition organizations are being forced to make is a qualitative change of business approaches and environment — learning to really work differently: rebuild traditional grade and ladder systems, find meaning for soulless corporate machines, learn how to be alive at work, handle one's own states and emotions and those of other people, how to engage, inspire and build deep relationships. "Это как переезд в другую страну, но только для компаний" (It's like moving to another country, but for companies). Adding mindfulness trainings to a corporate university or launching well-being programs are possible first steps but a drop in the ocean of the work needed. [#228]

The author's answer: Deep mind helps people and organizations make this complex, painful and necessary transition faster and less painfully; for them it is a transition to a more meaningful and healthy society. People today want meaning and health, and an organization that does not catch up with this change will have problems. Changing requires not just products about stress and burnout but a strategic partnership with specialists in inner work and the human being — with experiments, mistakes, unpleasant truth, layoffs and pain. [#228]

#### Client selection: stop educating laggards
Deep mind moved away from trying to sell consulting or education to state companies, bureaucratic organizations and generally any company whose leaders think stress is for the weak or that employees should be maximally economized on and squeezed. Such views contradict their values, modern science of humans and management, and global market trends. "Заниматься бесплатным образованием отстающих нам не хочется" (We do not want to provide free education to laggards). The author credits this refusal for their partnerships with leading companies and Silicon Valley startups, and the right clients also helped build the integrated core team. [#350]

#### 2022 restructuring after the war began
After almost a year without public updates because of the war, the author described how priorities changed by business line. [#720]

- Content. For intellectual, science-intensive projects, content creation — which has little to do with traditional marketing — becomes one of the key processes. From March 2022 they wrote and spoke much more because their expert topics (trauma and psychological state) were again top priority after COVID; in July they held a first conference on new leadership to gather human-development topics into one panel and show more advanced leaders. But content began to take too much time needed by products, so from September they moved to a *less is more* principle: fewer posts, articles and talks, created from greater inner resonance. [#720]
- Consulting and training in companies. In spring they talked to 30 leading Russian companies and approached global startups, but this laborious work led to practically no new contracts. In the crisis, only those already familiar with Deep mind supported employees through its programs; others found it hard enough without starting experiments. The lesson confirmed again: their most ecological path into corporate work lies through content — broadcasting their own values and tools — not through a built-out sales department. For the second time they fully abandoned proactive promotion in this market, focusing on responding to requests and on key current clients. Based on research into large IT companies, they cut the business line to one program: an 8-week program on people management from the perspective of psychology, physiology and soft skills for teams in a hybrid office, aiming to help teams add scientific understanding of human psychology and build skills of the future such as self-regulation, adult empathic communication and team integration. [#720]
- B2C. The asceticism in B2B is linked to focusing (meant to help with long-standing overload) and to an unexpectedly good B2C track. In July they launched *Emotional balance*, a program for individuals on self-healing psychological trauma and reducing anxiety. Users from the first two test groups, who sought tools to reduce stress and gain stability in stormy times, rated results 9–10 points. "Ценность, стоимость и масштабируемость собралась в едином продукте" (Value, cost and scalability came together in a single product). It can grow into a full system for healing trauma and restoring the nervous system, which most people have problems with; the author claims it already greatly increases the efficiency and speed of psychotherapy and in some cases could replace it. They had not yet learned to communicate this value to many people; about a year of work remained to launch a full-scale platform. He supposed that in spring the product could raise investment and go to American accelerators: while many entrepreneurs look at Latin America and Asia, the USA and developed countries still have far more people who understand the importance of psychological work and of healing developmental trauma. [#720]
- Communities. The crypto community failed because its launch coincided with the war and recession; in the crypto winter they stopped actively supporting it and removed it from all materials. [#720]

#### Founder overload, fear and the meaning of the work
The author repeatedly names his permanent overload — despite a personal assistant and delegation — as harming his contact with the mission and strategic vision [#349]; it also triggered the community's transformation [#728] and the B2B focusing [#720].

He quotes Qiwi founder Sergey Solonin, whom he deeply respects and finds ideologically close, on the same experience of a blurred, overloaded time: "Последние 6 месяцев опять прошли как в сумасшествии… Вроде, кучу дел сделал, а ощущение — бабах! — и все прошло, все пронеслось. И опять я ничего не помню. Нечего вспомнить." (The last 6 months again passed as in madness… It seems I did a pile of things, but the feeling is — bang! — and it all passed, it all rushed by. And again I remember nothing. Nothing to remember.) Other Solonin lines shared: it would be great if everyone woke up in the morning and understood what can be changed at their own level; people will leave because it is hard to create in a prison; there will be no Silicon Valley in Skolkovo or Innopolis if guards with machine guns stand there and everyone marches in formation. [#112]

Sources of meaning: the author feels great joy and energy when he notices which processes he is part of through Deep mind — for example a team discussion of trends shaping the future of work: the AI transformation that frees up the need for humans in whole fields, and a new form of freelancing where the most talented leave employment and effectively work for several companies with a different degree of freedom and complexity. The most important value for him is creating the future — the next step of humanity or peak of culture that breaks through in places "словно дерево через бетон" (like a tree through concrete) and then blossoms with a wide crown, changing the landscape. Even without a big role, he feels its presence and a layer of meaning he could not find in a dozen other organizations. Deep mind's concrete part is bringing new soft skills into society — important not in themselves but to support a culture of care, attentiveness and ethics that is growing, with creaking, in organizations and in the minds of individual leaders. [#855]

Leaders and evolution: over 4 years he has worked with incredible leaders — from an international startup founder to a team lead with 5 people — almost always brave people who sense the flow of life and are ready to risk new things; they create the future where their zone of care lies. Yet most of his work lies not in revolutions, to which he is temperamentally inclined, but in methodical, ecological evolution: careful work with inertia, fear of the new and unwillingness to change even when the status quo is hated and hinders flourishing. [#855]

Absorbing projections: he suspected but did not know how constant and voluminous the work of removing others' projections, anxieties and anger from himself would be — any proclamation of novelty attracts them like a strong magnet. Like the hero of the film "14 Peaks", he finds inspiration in opening toward these fears and difficulties, and through this tries to understand the deep meaning of real leadership: embodying authentic values and doing uncomfortable, often dangerous but right things; leadership truly changes something rather than drifting with the habitual current and creates possibilities that seem impossible. He wants to support those used to fearing the new, who may have forgotten they are leaders themselves. [#855]

#### Adult relationships in teams and founders' role (the Meta case)
Commenting on a public scandal about the sale of the mental-health project Meta (founders Ivan Zamesin / Ruslan Zaidullin), the author explicitly says he does not know the relationships and agreements in the project and does not judge "в чужом разводе" (in someone else's divorce). His two points: [#443]
1. It is sad when an entrepreneur's reputation is easily ruined publicly under the pretext of caring about the hiring market and trust. In a country with zero unicorns, the role of the entrepreneur still seems harder than that of a startup employee and should be cherished first. [#443]
2. The care for the team lacks the team itself: before loud statements to thousands, why not invite the team members and hear their position — what they think, how they agreed, why they did not ask for options, where the papers are, do they even need this money? If one treats the team as adults, then however they lived, agreed and decided — so be it. Adults do not need an outsider to raise a problem; they do it themselves as subjects of their own lives. Otherwise businesses will continue to be dragged by founders rather than built by teams, no unicorns will appear, and startups in new markets will keep being sold for the price of the founder's salary. [#443]
He considers the sale a successful case and a signal of the development of mental health in Russia, where it is very needed, even if the project was bought cheaply — again a matter for the adults involved. [#443]

#### Hunger for healthy, meaningful workplaces
During active hiring deep mind reviewed 82 applications for 2 vacancies and held many interviews. A recurring voice from candidates: "Я очень устал_а от нездоровой среды и мечтаю оказаться в команде, как ваша. Готов_а хоть за бесплатно трудится, лишь бы со смыслом и в экологичной среде." (I am very tired of an unhealthy environment and dream of being in a team like yours. Ready to work even for free, as long as it has meaning and an ecological environment.) These are not new graduates: they apply for roles with options, many have strong qualifications, and deep mind is "literally taking them" from big corporations where a healthy environment cannot be found [#932].

The author's interpretation: precisely because of this problem, leading world companies put employee well-being first in their 5-year strategies (he refers to a global well-being survey). He believes corporations can shift culture toward meaning and health, partly by acquiring and integrating the experience of small teams like deep mind that prioritize each member's state and have psychological knowledge. The situation is a symptom of a serious problem in society and the workplace, and a huge opportunity: in a society atomized by the Soviet legacy and autocracy, in workplaces with unpleasant hierarchy or aggressive achievement-orientation ("агрессивным достигаторством"), people are not happy. In the West too there is a lag (he cites McKinsey surveys) in companies' understanding of employees' mental-health needs, though it is being corrected fast. Workplaces have little contact, openness and joy, which creates a strong hunger for a "simply" normal environment with meaning, direction and support [#932].

Forming such spaces is, in the author's words, deep mind's core skill, talent and product. In modern culture they seem intangible and almost unimportant, but they yield results — the evidence he lists:
- 65% of investment was raised from deep mind's own community, which they try to build as a healthy, meaningful space;
- 1 of 3 new key team positions was filled from the community almost instantly, saving much hiring time;
- in the deep mind team, except special cases, nobody works even 8 hours a day, nobody hears criticism directed at them, and people aim to stay for years;
- Masterheart, a space of co-presence and support for leaders without functional networking, always has a full group and a waiting list [#932].

His call: "Cделайте ваши пространства осмысленными и экологичными, и люди будут вас обожать!" (Make your spaces meaningful and ecological, and people will adore you!). Corporations face large systemic work; until they start it seriously, the best people will leak into any projects with meaning and ecology, even for less money [#932].

#### The 2024 near-closure crisis
Commitment as foundation: the author has repeatedly seen that long-term results come to those who really "надо" (needed) it and were willing to sacrifice much; difficulties can break us if we are not ready to put our skin on the line. Five years earlier he decided never again to work as an employee in large organizations, though he could — at 28 he already headed a function in a state company with strong support. But "чтобы от жизни не тошнило, в ежедневном деле должен быть значимый смысл" (so that life doesn't make you sick, daily work must have significant meaning), and it was absent there amid unpleasant circumstances. So he built dm "без права на неудачу" (without the right to fail), sacrificing everything; this lack of alternatives became the basis of all successes, and even the idea of changing that paradigm now feels like a big crisis [#1148].

The crisis: 2024 was a particularly hard period with a sense of lost perspective. By his own framework, fatigue and loss of faith are extremely dangerous for an enterprise, so he studies and works with them. One clear cause of systemic fatigue: imbalance of effort and reward. In the very year they significantly expanded the team and invested heavily in the corporate direction, response there fell rather than grew. Many reasons relate to the redistribution of the IT-company market after the war began: 3 large clients went into long processes of changing management teams, slowing or cancelling contracts; a fourth, Qiwi, was practically shut down by the regulator [#1148].

Founder dependence: much effort is required while restoring personal resources gets harder. deep mind is strongly tied to its founder; he wished from the start not to create such a system, but founder dependence turned out to be the essence of the process at this stage — less marketing dependence than product dependence [#1148].

Why: the market has very few people with enough expertise to create interdisciplinary products while withstanding the intense pressure of the cultural conflict the product tries to resolve. Standing against the beliefs and habits of the whole system, this conflict starts to pass physically through your body and mind: when making products that change the usual order, the border between the old and new runs literally through you. A facilitator, leader or methodologist who senses the dead end of the current management system and wants to make it more human will try to bring into it what the system tried for years to eradicate, and will meet the full force of the collective pattern. That is what real leadership looks like, but it involves strong neglect and devaluation. Though everyone truly needs change, habit beats everything, and real transformations begin only in harsh crisis; in their context needed growth is postponed due to the "ретравматизации тоталитаризма" (retraumatization of totalitarianism), which he believes will only deepen the crisis [#1148].

This is a hard challenge for all lightworkers. Nevertheless the decision is to continue, without alternative: besides difficulties there is responsibility, a great team, several strong hypotheses, and potential for big projects when everyone wakes up in September. Going against the usual system of hiding weaknesses, he chose to share his ("Если не я, то кто?" — *If not me, then who?*), asking whether vulnerabilities can become fuel for common growth and help the lightworker community understand the shared context and unite more skilfully [#1148].

#### How meaning-driven entrepreneurship transforms the founder
Full-time entrepreneurship multiplied by work with deep meanings, over 5 years, made the author grow strongly and expanded his self-conception. His CEO role — "не очень любимой, но необходимой на данном этапе" (not very beloved but necessary at this stage) — is full of operations and routine (writing to someone, syncing with the team, agreeing a project with a trainer), yet meaning always stands behind it. Even if he wanted to lose that meaning, it is now hard, because it is embedded in the essence of the products and all communication; meaning permeates his activity by default. This is the result of many hard decisions toward a life for meaning, and also a great privilege, since not everyone can make such decisions [#1218].

Identity transformation: the 5 years made him "much bigger" than the career and corporate experience he strongly identified with. His career and fairly unique experience of being in power became just the past, a step on the path: "Перестало быть Я, хотя и осталось моим." (It stopped being Me, though it remained mine.) Identity softened and shifted toward humanity in general and not limiting oneself with prepackaged ideas about life. It became much easier to see himself as a trainer rather than an entrepreneur, an employee rather than a founder, a real emigrant, or a monk if he wanted — as long as it matches his inner aspirations and needs — and he describes this as an amazing liberation [#1218].

Social-circle transformation: although the corporate world can look evil and unfeeling, it contains a huge number of feeling, kind people; sometimes they mimic the general culture, but dig deeper and you find them everywhere. Almost every day he talks with people at the epicenters of functional or harsh culture who keep their openness to everything human and often try to change their surroundings in that direction. Through his choice to live with meaning he found investors who support the idea of the project, new friendships (Dasha Zolotukhina), a renewed long friendship (Lena Volkovskaya), and colleagues such as a woman who, after 15 years at McKinsey in Chicago, decided to help leaders open their hearts. The lesson he draws: "нас больше и мы сильнее, чем кажемся" (there are more of us and we are stronger than it seems). His new task is to make this vision accessible to most people — those who want to move toward humanity, development and kindness but fear being strange or do not believe change is possible — including through Resonance, a space for connecting on the level of values and meanings [#1218].

#### Year review 2024: firsts and inclusivity
Milestones the author lists: in 2019 deep mind became the first company in the post-Soviet space to build a business around corporate mindfulness, and in 2023 the first to raise significant money for such a business. From the start he wanted to build a company, not run trainings himself or build a personal brand, which was the only level at which the market worked then. Six months after the start he invited co-founders — 5 founders in total — and that configuration lasted only briefly "пока неизбежно не разосрались" (until we inevitably fell out). At that time they were among the first 50–100 fully counter-cultural companies in the world, which are now countless [#1263].

In 2020 they ran the first full-fledged Russian-language retreat on Theory U, integrating with due qualification 4 key directions of inner work in one process — not merely talking about Theory U at trainings or adding yoga to a meditation retreat (!) INCOMPLETE: the 4 directions are not named. It ran several times in B2C and for Qiwi's top team during Covid. In 2024 they created the first window of access to internationally qualified psychedelic therapy in Russian [#1263].

The author's greatest joy: the first full transformational program in the post-Soviet space focused on Theory U methodology with a large team (10 people), and the first full implementation of inclusivity on their market. Five participants took part who without special attention from organizers and community could not have gotten in, including a visually impaired participant. Motivation and discomfort: his time in the US influenced the wish to make the process more accessible — the strong attention to inclusivity at Naropa Institute, where Dasha studied, was contagious. Worldview claim (author's opinion): if people tried less to destroy each other in pursuit of individual happiness, there would already be enough resources for a prosperous, harmonious society. The idea of individualistic happiness detached from others — and the derived harmful idea that one can create something while harming or even killing others — is, in his view, the same delusion and narrowness as the once-widespread belief that the Earth is flat. He hopes that shared challenges and faster-than-planned AI development will catalyse growth in the quality of consciousness on the planet [#1263].

#### What deep mind is (2025 view)
After another year, the author concludes deep mind cannot be reduced to a traditional business — it never was and never will be, though it has products, a team and can earn money. The world lacks sustainable projects that, existing in a capitalist environment and not on donations, serve non-capitalist meanings; there are very few chances to build them and even fewer that a classical entrepreneur will. Filling this gap means doing not what the system expects of you but what is right. Hence deep mind is "про подлинность и любовь. Их нельзя продавать, но можно попробовать найти в себе и передать вокруг" (about authenticity and love. They cannot be sold, but one can try to find them in oneself and pass them around). He wants to dedicate his life to this and to engage others, hoping that together they find a sustainable model — a task bigger than any business [#1404].

Support as a resource: after publishing the possible-closure post he received much support; he credits readers' care, Dasha's unconditional support, and investors driven "в первую очередь смысл, а во вторую деньги" (first by meaning, second by money) for helping him continue, and stresses noticing shared strength and helping each other [#1404].

### Caveats, disagreements and open questions

- All results and diagnoses are the founder's self-reports and personal views ("ощущаю я" — *as I sense them*), not independent data. [#350, #821]
- (!) CONTRADICTION: in 2020 the author sought consulting for the Russian state apparatus [#134], but later says Deep mind stopped selling to state and bureaucratic organizations [#350].
- The team describes its values as maintained "не всегда успешно" (not always successfully). [#728]
- The author acknowledges info-business often succeeds more than real human-development projects; his argument against it is sustainability and meaning, not market results. [#821]
- He admits he lacks direct evidence that healing culture leads to flourishing. [#859]
- On the Meta case he explicitly disclaims knowledge of the internal agreements. [#443]
- (!) INCOMPLETE: #257 announces a continuation on products and work with companies, and #720 ends with a continuation marker; those parts are not in this input. [#257, #720]

- (!) CONTRADICTION / tension: in 2024 the author attributes the no-alternative, sacrifice-everything stance to all successes [#1148], while in 2025 he says the recovery came from letting go of fixed ideas, playing more and stopping forcing himself [#1404]; the source does not reconcile these explicitly.
- Founder dependence: in 2024 he wished to avoid it but called it the essence of the process at this stage [#1148]; the 2025 conclusion is that he may always need to be a playing coach and that this is fine [#1404]. The community failed partly because it needed constant founder involvement [#1258].
- The focus lesson cuts both ways: Emotional Balance suffered from lack of focus because B2B got priority [#1258], while the exclusive B2B focus after fundraising was later called one of the main errors [#1404].
- Statistics (95% five-year failure, research on fear) are cited by the author without study details in this text [#1230].
- The author is the founder selling deep mind's corporate programs and community; claims about deep mind's culture (nobody works 8 hours, no criticism) are self-reported [#932].
- The emigration program's failure reasons are partly hedged ("Возможно, переоценили" — *possibly overestimated*) [#1258].

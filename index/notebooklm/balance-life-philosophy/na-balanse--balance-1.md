## What is balance (and is it real)

### What this covers

This file collects everything the channel "На балансе" ("On balance") said about its own title question: what balance is, whether it exists, and what alternatives to the classic "everything in equal measure" idea look like. It spans Katya Strelnikova's personal positions (lagom vs contrasts, the 10.5:1.5 rhythm, freedom, inner speed limit, psychological equilibrium), forwarded critiques ("fuck the balance" / allostasis, the opposite-extreme method, dosage) and borrowed models (Tim Ferriss's planned balance, The Curricula's definition). According to the source, the question matters because there is "no single recipe of happiness" and a rigid ideal of balance can itself become a trap; what counts is one's own good wellbeing [#54, #340].

This part adds one long personal-experience post (October 2026) that deepens the first of the six components of psychological equilibrium already described in this topic — conative balance ("knowing what you really want", originally from Lobsang's 2018 retreat [#566]). Here Katya explains what conation is, names the skill of "conative intelligence", walks through the three types of conative imbalance as taught in the course Cultivating Emotional Balance (Tibet House NY), maps two of them onto her own 20 years of chasing thinness and efficiency, and gives the four questions that, by her account, preceded her life turning "180 degrees". It matters, according to the source, because desires are the compass that decides where our time, energy and attention go; misaligned desires kept her unhappy for years despite "achieving a lot" [#720].

Relation to the existing file. It *extends* the conative-balance item of *Six components of psychological equilibrium* [#566]: that item gave a one-line definition and three everyday examples of its absence; this post supplies the terminology (conation, conative intelligence), a three-way typology of imbalance and a self-audit method. It also *echoes* *Freedom as conscious necessity* [#352]: Katya again revisits her old idea that freedom meant indulging every impulse, now framed as conative hyperactivity [#720].

### Core ideas

- There is no single correct attitude to balance. Katya's own answer is enough-ness (lagom), calm and "air in the schedule", but she stresses it is personal; see *Lagom vs contrasts* [#54].
- Balance cannot be found, only planned (Tim Ferriss): different life areas must be scheduled in advance, ideally by giving whole days to one type of task [#93].
- Balance as a skill set: harmony between work, relationships, health and growth, built through five trainable skills [#109].
- Switching beats any single state (forwarded critique): a healthy nervous system and a healthy living system are defined by the ability to move between tension and relaxation, openness and closedness — stability through change (allostasis) [#153].
- Real balance comes from being able to swing to the opposite extreme, i.e. integrating one's shadow, not from averaging familiar habits [#171].
- Balance is dosage: happy people honestly define their own dose of everything and re-check it against themselves [#306].
- Balance can be a long rhythm, not a daily routine: periodic switching between life spheres, with priorities chosen per period [#340].
- Freedom is conscious necessity: healthy choices are not "restrictions" but the most direct form of freedom [#352].
- Slowing down to one's inner speed limit; exhaustion is an indicator, not a cause [#500].
- Safety and stability must be built inside, since no outer place is safe [#549, #563].
- Psychological equilibrium — a stable trait with six components — is presented as the "ultimate" inner form of balance and is developed gradually through practice [#566, #567].

- Balance in desires shapes how happy we feel. Katya says this course helped her understand how balance in what we want affects the sense of happiness [#720].
- Desires are a compass. Conation (wanting, striving, intending) sets the direction of time, energy and attention; choosing desires wisely is a skill called conative intelligence [#720].
- Three ways wanting goes out of balance: too many desires (hyperactivity), clear desires pointing the wrong way (dysfunction), and not being able to want (deficit) [#720].
- Goals and actions must be checked against what you call happiness. Katya's change began with honest questions about what happiness is for her and whether her desires and daily actions lead there; she discovered "thin ≠ happy" and "efficient ≠ content with myself" [#720].
- Results as a consequence, not a target. Once she aimed at health and happiness, a body shape she likes and steady work output came anyway — as a side effect, not a goal for which she breaks herself [#720].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Lagom vs contrasts (Katya's position on balance)
Katya says she formulated this position unexpectedly, after an animation claimed "I don't need balance, I need contrast" and after an interview in which Alena said that with balance you will not achieve success in any direction. She offers it "for contrast with our heroines" [#54].
1. Question the success premise. "__Мысль о том, что можно «не добиться успеха» рождается в системе, где успех — обязательный критерий полноценной и счастливой жизни.__" (The thought that you might "not achieve success" is born in a system where success is a mandatory criterion of a full and happy life.) She does not share that concept; about 7 years earlier a friend joked she should write a book "Hi, I'm Katya. I eat porridge and I have a red hat"; striving for success already felt unnatural to her then, though she kept doing it by inertia. She says she is ok with possibly not "achieving success" in the traditional sense; she does not believe one can be happy only by "constantly achieving something", nor that "without goals you are not a person" [#54].
2. Do things because you like them, in measure. She goes to the gym not to lose weight, get a "dream body" or lower body-fat percentage but because she simply likes it; she shoots stories and reels not to become a top blogger but because she likes it; she likes running, working with clients, reading, and sleeping (she says every night as she lies down "Господь, как же хорошо" — *Lord, how good this is*). The pleasure is that all of this is present "В МЕРУ" (IN MEASURE) [#54].
3. Lagom as the model. She admires Sweden as the home of «lagom», explained as "достаточность, в самый раз, умеренность, оптимальность" (sufficiency, just right, moderation, optimality) — "не про воздержание и дефицит, а про целесообразность" (not about abstinence and deficit, but about expediency) [#54].
4. Contrasts as a trap. "«Контрасты», напротив, — мое персональное колесо Сансары." ("Contrasts", on the contrary, are my personal wheel of Samsara.) She had to dig herself out of cycles of "work yourself out in order to rest to the full", "travel so much you don't want to go anywhere", "party so hard you lose any desire to talk to anyone". That life did not make her happy. In terms of vertical development theory, she once moved from "achiever" to "individualist" and relaxed a lot [#54].
5. Her definition of happiness: calm, stability, "air in the schedule", free time, the opportunity for reflection, and the proverbial balance [#54].
6. Caveat built in: it is normal if this does not suit someone; "__нет единого рецепта счастья. Нет одного правильного отношения к балансу__" (there is no single recipe for happiness. There is no one correct attitude to balance). There is only you and your (good) wellbeing — with goals and excesses or without them [#54].

#### Tim Ferriss's planned balance
Katya retells, in first-person voice "as if Tim found an hour to chat with me on Zoom", a video in which Ferriss explains how he advances in business, podcasting, book-writing and the spiritual and creative parts of life [#93].
1. Manage attention effectively. Everyone has 24 hours a day and 365 days a year and several spheres that each try to absorb us entirely [#93].
2. Balance can't be found but can be planned. If you want to cover different aspects, it must be planned in advance [#93].
3. Dedicate separate days to separate types of tasks. Switching between tasks of different categories costs a lot of energy. He does not try to cover the artistic, productive and spiritual in one day. Example: on Mondays he only does certain activities — recording videos, team calls, admin work; podcasts he usually records on Fridays [#93].
4. "If it isn't in the calendar, it doesn't exist." Putting things in the calendar is the only way to integrate the different aspects into life. If he needs to publish one blog post a week, writing time must be blocked, usually before noon or before lunch, on a day with similar tasks [#93].
5. Quarterly content week. Once a quarter he has a week in which he writes, records and prepares a pile of material to publish over the next three months [#93].
6. Weekly creativity slot. At least one morning a week he starts with a 90-minute drawing lesson, before any work or distracting activity; an artist-teacher comes to him and they work with charcoal, pencil and paints [#93].
7. Quarterly spiritual retreat. For the spiritual aspect and self-reflection, once every three months he plans one to two weeks fully or partly offline in nature. He may write there but not read. The time is blocked and paid in advance — having already paid for accommodation, participation or an expert's time helps him not to back out [#93].

#### Five skills to build for balance (The Curricula definition)
Katya asked the AI guide site The Curricula about "balance in life" and summarised it in her interpretation. Definition: "Баланс — поддержание гармонии и равновесия между работой, отношениями, здоровьем и личностным ростом." (Balance is maintaining harmony and equilibrium between work, relationships, health and personal growth.) Achieving it requires prioritising, setting boundaries, carving out time for oneself, acknowledging the importance of both work and rest, and making sure no single aspect dominates. Katya says the list unexpectedly described the whole range of the channel's interests and is convenient to take to a health coach and a psychotherapist [#109].
1. Healthy habits — establishing behaviour that keeps you in a resourced state [#109].
2. Self-awareness — learning to reflect and to distinguish emotions, thoughts and values, and to manage your behaviour [#109].
3. Mindfulness — learning to concentrate on the present and to hold attention [#109].
4. Self-regulation — developing the ability to maintain mental health and wellbeing [#109].
5. Time management and goal-setting — understanding how to set goals, plan and prioritise [#109].
She closes by asking readers what else should be added to the list (no additions are given) [#109].

#### Forget balance: the five principles of allostasis (adaptivity)
A forwarded post (tag #мацкевич, from channel Шмит16), written as a reaction to an earlier post about openness, argues that switching between states is more important and practical for the health of a living system than any state itself. Headline thesis: "*fuck the balance - доказано научно*" (fuck the balance — scientifically proven) [#153].
- Nervous-system basis. The author says that after rereading many books and papers on the nervous system, the key idea is: the nervous system is healthy when it can switch quickly and easily between activation and deactivation — not when there is no stress, not when one is always in balance or safety, not when one is always at rest. Terms for this: Neuroflexibility / Autonomic Flexibility [#153].
- Loss of plasticity. In the scientific literature on depressive states and post-traumatic disorders, these are often described as a loss of plasticity — "я потерял способность переключаться в зависимости от потребностей среды или моейго тела. я залип" (I lost the ability to switch depending on the needs of the environment or my body. I got stuck) [#153].
- Allostasis vs homeostasis. From living-systems theory: allostasis is the ability to achieve stability through change. Homeostasis implies maintaining a constant internal state; allostasis describes dynamic adaptation. Living systems — a human organism, an ecosystem, a social structure — achieve stability not through fixed balance but through constant adjustment to change [#153].
The five principles:
1. Departure from a static understanding of balance — instead of striving for a fixed state, speak of dynamic adaptation [#153].
2. Switching as the key skill — the ability to move quickly between states (e.g. tension and relaxation) matters more than maintaining one state [#153].
3. Stability through change — a system becomes more resilient through its ability to change, not by resisting change [#153].
4. Adaptivity as health — health is defined by the ability to respond effectively to challenges and return to a functional state [#153].
5. Training plasticity — the emphasis is on developing the skill of switching between states, not on constant calm or openness [#153].
- Same idea at a psychological level. In Heather Plett's book the author found that being open all the time is as maladaptive for you and others as being dissociated or closed. Metaphor for psychological boundaries: a cell membrane that regulates what goes in and out; this selective permeability ensures the cell's survival and health [#153].
- Lesson taken and applied. Instead of idealising a state of openness or calm, train switching. "Напрягаться - супер, чувствовать небезопасность - офигенно, закрываться для кого-то и открываться для другого. Все это кайф, если получается между состояниями переключаться." (Tensing up is great, feeling unsafe is awesome, closing to one person and opening to another. All of it is a joy if you manage to switch between states.) What can block switching is precisely the demonisation of stress, unsafety or closedness. Then let go and trust that the nervous system will itself figure out how and when to relax or open [#153].
- The author admits how often he has lived under the narrative that one must be in some ideal state — open, balanced, calm [#153].

#### Balance through the opposite extreme (shadow work)
A forwarded post by Dumik, introduced as a "contradictory thought": people think that to come into balance you should look toward the balance point; in fact you must look at the point opposite to where you are now — at the other extreme. Image: a tightrope walker who sees the rope in the centre but can only catch balance to one side [#171].
The six examples:
1. If you are only about meanings and values — go and "make some dough" [#171].
2. If you are only about money — go to a meditation retreat or serve in a temple without pay for a month [#171].
3. If you are the smartest in the room — become a loser who looks up from below and says "stupid things" [#171].
4. If you are only about "helping people" — raise your price 5 times, go sell aggressively and live as an egoist for a while [#171].
5. If everyone can always rely on you — screw up and complain to everyone that "I'm scared and don't know how" [#171].
6. If you are used to getting things done at others' expense — find something important and do "everything yourself" [#171].
Mechanism: to stay in balance you need to be able to "swerve" to each side. "Если вы видите от 0 до 100, то ваша середина будет 50. А вот если вы умеете в -100, то тогда легко приземлитесь в ноль." (If you see from 0 to 100, your middle will be 50. But if you can do -100, you will easily land at zero.) This is about the shadow: seeing what you do not allow yourself, owning it and making it gold — then you find the real balance, not the average between your habitual positions [#171].

#### Personal dosage
A forwarded post (Salatshop, shared by Marina Sidorich): people who live truly happily and fully almost always manage to honestly define their own dose of everything — coffee, wine, sun, sugar, strong hugs; how much shopping and travel is enough, how much tasty food, reels, time with friends or celebrity news they need [#306].
- It is a special skill: determining the dosage that suits you, starting from your needs and lifestyle, seeing the big picture and staying attentive to yourself and your changes [#306].
- Examples of re-dosing: maybe it is time to reduce coffee — or the opposite, time to relax and eat a croissant for breakfast; maybe cancel the fifth workout of the week that depletes rather than helps; maybe instead of a trip with friends choose a relaxed weekend in silence and solitude, which right now will give much more resource [#306].
- Rule: orient not on other people's portions and dosages but check in with yourself every time — "ориентироваться не на чужие порции и дозировки, а каждый раз сверяться с собой" [#306].

#### Personal rhythm and period-based priorities (the 10.5:1.5 model)
Katya: a year and a half of talking about balance means she no longer sees it as something rigid and immovable. She now smiles inwardly at clients who want to "keep all their routines even while travelling": the pull toward stability and control is understandable but, in her experience, not very reproducible. More realistic is a strategy of periodic switching between spheres — which most of the interviewees of the "На балансе" rubric (Maryana, Alena in the very first interview) also state [#340].
1. Find your own rhythm. Prompted by Anya Fedorova: 1:1:1:1:1 can be a rhythm, and so can 10.5:1.5:10.5:1.5. Having lived long enough, we can see longer pauses where they arise and where they are needed [#340].
2. Accept that something must go. When she travels to Russia she knows for sure: "От чего-то. Придется. Отказаться." (Something. Will have to. Be given up.) She cannot simultaneously coach clients, study, run blogs, invent projects, train intensively, spend lots of time alone, socialise and see doctors [#340].
3. Choose priorities in advance and don't scold yourself. She picks priorities "on the shore" and does not blame herself when a non-priority sphere drifts off. In Moscow her priority is always loved ones and "maintenance" (техобслуживание, i.e. health check-ups/upkeep); everything else is as it comes — few posts, no new product, no big sales, even skipped workouts, because now that is not what matters [#340].
4. Her ratio: 10.5:1.5 hermit life : socialisation. She loves her quiet, measured life in the forest and does not seek new friends there because it would encroach on a lifestyle she enjoys; once a year she comes to Moscow and gets a "vaccination of communication" — 1.5 months of socialising for the year ahead. "Дома я купаюсь в лесу, в Москве я купаюсь в людях." (At home I bathe in the forest, in Moscow I bathe in people.) [#340].
5. Let other parts of yourself out. The trip is a chance to walk her "party part" — the one for all-nighters and parties. Since she does not adjust her schedule from the US time zone, in Russia she turns for a month from a radical lark into an owl: bars, festivals, seeing dawn before sleep rather than after waking [#340].
Conclusion: "баланс — он может быть таким разным. Главное, чтобы был своим." (balance can be so different. The main thing is that it is your own.) [#340].

#### Freedom as conscious necessity
An essay Katya wrote for the coaching channel "Со всеми все окей", on what freedom is and how it affects our state and lifestyle [#352].
- The inversion she sees. Working with lifestyle change, she notices modern culture has turned things upside down: name any basic "healthy" action — eat a normal salad, walk after dinner, go to bed before midnight — and the label "working on yourself" pops up, as if our natural mode were lying around, scrolling and washing down Raffaello with soda, and anything beyond "plastic happiness" is a feat [#352].
- Marketing's promise. For years marketing has said: pleasure is Bounty, gratitude is Merci, celebration is Coca-Cola with chips — but never promised that energy, health and a long happy list of blood tests come with it. "Нам навязали ощущение, что можно жить этим «идеальным» сладким/солёным/экранным образом и оставаться бодрыми, здоровыми" (We were sold the feeling that you can live this "ideal" sweet/salty/screen way and stay energetic and healthy). When body, brain and blood tests say "sorry, no", we feel restricted and indignant that we can't eat a bag of sweets, scroll all night and wake fresh [#352].
- Definition. Freedom is not anarchy ("I do what I want when I want") nor a chaotic set of actions in a fit of random mood. "Настоящая свобода — это осознанная необходимость" (True freedom is conscious necessity), as Spinoza said and Marx repeated: seeing the options, understanding the consequences and, knowing them, choosing [#352].
- Resentment is pointless. Being offended at having to choose between sofa and walk is like being offended that you have to breathe to live: the laws of physiology did not sign a convenience contract with us. Sweets give a minute of pleasure; health and energy you must earn yourself [#352].
The four-part method of free choice:
1. I know what I want — e.g. health, a clear head, a healthy back [#352].
2. I know the actions that lead there and those that don't — salad vs Snickers, sleep vs one more episode [#352].
3. I understand the consequences of each [#352].
4. I choose freely and take responsibility for the consequences [#352].
- Caveat: sometimes everyone reaches for a sweet or picks phone-scrolling over yoga, and sometimes that even helps rather than harms; but in the long run you have to "pay for the ride on this train" (with health, mood, energy, attention). The deal is transparent [#352].
- Examples. Morning at the fridge: option A, croissant and coffee — quick and tasty, but joy only until 11:00 when sugar crashes and she ends up radically hungry with low energy; option B, an omelette with greens — more effort, but the brain works and the body doesn't ask to lie down a couple of hours later. She usually picks B. Work day: either sit glued to computer and calls, feel like a squeezed lemon after lunch and struggle to fall asleep from over-arousal, or plan quiet pauses between calls (massage glasses, a walk, looking at greenery) and end the day still alive. She freely decides what matters more: tastiness and dopamine from ticking off extra tasks, or energy and long-term wellbeing [#352].
- Freedom shows in small things: set an alarm for 10 pm and actually switch off the screen; leave a Telegram chat with forty silly notifications; drink a glass of water instead of soda. These are not restrictions but the most direct form of freedom: "я решаю, какое будущее я для себя хочу." (I decide what future I want for myself.) [#352].

#### Three ways to support yourself in summer
Tips Katya asked from coaches Ira Oganesyan and Polina Perusheva. Context: one wants to live summer "the right way" — rest, see friends, recharge for a year and not miss career goals — but often it becomes an attempt to combine the incompatible: on Zoom under a fan you think about quitting, on vacation about not losing shape, at meetups about not failing a work project. This is not necessary if you [#353]:
1. Don't accelerate where you don't want to go. Managing everything is not the same as living. Sometimes an exhale is also forward movement. If everything "falls out of your hands", it may not be weakness but having taken on too much [#353].
2. Give yourself a "seasonal window". As nature has seasons, so does life. Maybe this summer is about feeling more rather than building strategies: set aside time without plans and without the task of "levelling up", and find the "want" that lights you up [#353].
3. Make "rest" and "develop" friends. Development is not always about effort; sometimes the new arrives while you lie in a hammock doing nothing [#353].

#### Inner speed limit (Tsoknyi Rinpoche)
Katya wrote this at year's end, when everything accelerates, after reading Tsoknyi Rinpoche's "Open Heart, Open Mind". She had grown sick of catching herself thinking "need to go faster" [#500]:
- Faster working.
- Faster selling.
- Faster being "successful".
- Faster being "financially independent".
Concept. Everyone has an inner speed limit; the question is not laziness or ambition but what speed one's own psyche, body and heart can sustain while staying in contact with reality. "Проблемы начинаются там, где мы этот лимит регулярно превышаем." (Problems begin where we regularly exceed this limit.) [#500].
What accumulates inside when living above the limit (while outside everything may look fine — coping, growing, achieving) [#500]:
- chronic tension;
- exhaustion that weekends don't cure;
- loss of sleep and of the ability to rest;
- inability to simply daydream;
- irritation at loved ones and a wish that "nobody touch me";
- broken social contacts because "there's no time".
Three recognisable scenarios from the book [#500]:
1. A person always hurrying not because they truly lack time but because of the thought "if I slow down, I'll be overtaken"; they push themselves, then the team; mistakes and tension grow.
2. Constant "I don't have time for this now" — for lunch, friends, rest; everything "after the project / report / hard period", but the hard period never ends and life narrows to work and anxiety.
3. Trying to get done even what need not be done, because otherwise one feels guilty — even when nobody demands it and it can be submitted later; inner pressure of faster, better, more, or "I'm not good enough".
In all these stories people broke not because they were weak but because for too long they did not notice how they drove themselves, not hearing body, fatigue and fear; speeding gradually destroys health, relationships and the joy of life [#500].
Key reframes [#500]:
- "истощение — это не причина, а индикатор." (exhaustion is not a cause but an indicator) — a signal that we have long been going faster than we can bear. The habit of speeding narrows thinking, impoverishes feelings, makes us confuse value with tension, and creates fear that "there's no other way". "Но иначе можно." (But another way is possible.)
- Slowing down is not "drop everything and move to the forest" but attention to yourself, your rhythms, bodily and emotional signals; the ability to notice where you speed up out of fear of being worse, late, losing or not measuring up, and where that acceleration no longer helps but destroys.
- The honest question: "*я сейчас живу — или пытаюсь догонять и успевать?*" (am I living now — or trying to catch up and keep up?)
- Katya considers the skill of noticing inner speeding one of the key ones for an adult, stable and truly successful life; without it neither health, long-term success, warm relationships nor contentment is possible.
- Watch this limit like road speed — not out of fear of punishment but out of respect for yourself and the path. "Иногда самый зрелый шаг — не ускориться. А вовремя притормозить." (Sometimes the most mature step is not to speed up but to brake in time.)
- About the book: one of the few that really influenced her that year; it did not inspire a sprint or give "one more way to become better" but made her slow down and look at what speed she lives at and whether she wants to live like that.

#### Inner safe place and small acts of loyalty to oneself
From Katya's account of being on holiday in Dubai when the US–Iran strikes began [#549], and its follow-up [#563].
- The realisation. She had long avoided war, choosing "calm" places. Woken by an air-raid alert at night, her heart pounded for three hours; by 3 am she reached an inner agreement that the bed was safe enough. The scariest part was not the sounds but the realisation that vulnerability is not somewhere far away in the news — it is here and always was; there is no "safe" place on the planet and never was, "Но как было чудесно об этом не знать" (But how wonderful it was not to know) [#549].
- Conclusion: build safety inside. "единственное, что имеет смысл создавать — безопасное место внутри" (the only thing that makes sense to create is a safe place inside) — not a bunker, not an illusion of control, but inner supports, rituals and silence one can return to even when everything outside collapses. Also, of course, earning money — because it is very painful to read chat messages like "our family is being evicted from the hotel, we have no money" [#549].
- Small acts of loyalty to oneself. With a friend: "rockets are rockets, but we went to the gym and did a workout"; they cooked breakfast; face massage was not cancelled either — "Маленькие акты верности себе, когда мир ведет себя невменяемо" (Small acts of loyalty to yourself when the world behaves insanely). Bonus: in such moments it's impossible to read the news [#549].
- Impermanence practice. She had been meditating on impermanence and death for four months; life said "enough theory" — death, hers and loved ones', can arrive like a rocket, unexpectedly, without time to prepare; a good reason to live the best life now and develop wisdom [#549].
- Krishnamurti quotes she closes with (on division and conflict): defending oneself is being aggressive; "Пока мы делим себя на группы, нации, различные религии и идеологии, всегда будет тот, кто нападает, и тот, кто защищается" (As long as we divide ourselves into groups, nations, religions and ideologies, there will always be one who attacks and one who defends); "там, где существует разделение, существует конфликт" (where there is division, there is conflict); "Я сознаю, что я - это мир, а мир - это я" (I realise that I am the world and the world is me); also that we lost reality when, professing God's love, we began to condone killing our fellows and to justify mass killings in the name of peace and freedom [#549].
- Choosing calm over hysteria (follow-up). She had come to Dubai with a hidden wish to truly exhale and find stability and safety. Paradoxically, the unpredictable, anxious experience gave her calm and stability. From the start she felt that panicking or calmly surrendering to the Universe was her choice: after two cancelled flights she decided not to storm the airport or check chats every 10 minutes, but to buy a ticket for a later date, extend her apartment and live her best life a week longer. She lost a long-awaited Schengen visa appointment (visa centres don't reschedule even for war, plague and closed skies). She could have panicked, looked for culprits, spent energy on what cannot be changed; instead she chose to live as life goes — "Не бороться с реальностью, а принять ее. Go with the flow в чистом виде." (Not to fight reality but to accept it. Go with the flow in its pure form.) — something new for her [#563].
- Supports discovered: herself and her routines, and loved ones; especially her husband, who told her not to go to the airport, to stay in the sun, rest and not fuss — and even on departure day not to worry if she wasn't boarded [#563].
- Money anxiety released. An astrologer once told her she'd never have money problems but would always worry about money. Seeing people storming the airport and sleeping in corridors, the fear of "dying under a fence" left her — the anxiety went, the wish to earn stayed [#563].
- Contrast clarifies preferences. After three months in Moscow and then Dubai, she confirmed she doesn't want to live in Moscow; from the US (a place that doesn't suit her) Moscow had seemed the best city on earth [#563].
- Retreat insights (Sergey Kupriyanov's retreat, which she could attend because the war kept others away): "у меня все есть. И единственный человек, который системно портит мне жизнь, это я сама." (I have everything. And the only person who systematically spoils my life is me.) Taking responsibility and giving yourself permission to leave yourself alone is not even relief — it is freedom, and energy appears from somewhere. Second insight: she can contact "something bigger" (Universe, God, the higher, flow) and create conditions for it, but only in a positive key; by carefully shielding herself from experiences she labels negative, she blocks full contact with herself and reality — "Оберегая себя от темного, я обрезаю и доступ к свету." (Protecting myself from the dark, I cut off access to the light.) This is what she will work on. She wants to keep the feeling that "life lives me" but does not yet know how [#563].

#### Six components of psychological equilibrium
Source: Katya re-listening to a 2018 retreat by her teacher Lobsang. Definition: psychological equilibrium is not a fleeting emotion or mood but a stable characteristic arising in a balanced mind — not "I feel good now" but "я в порядке, no matter what" (I'm okay, no matter what). Research she cites says people with high equilibrium cope better with stress, recover faster from difficulties and are generally happier — not because they have fewer problems but because they relate to them differently [#566].
1. Conative balance — knowing what you really want. The ability to tell which desires lead to wellbeing and which give instant relief but leave emptiness long-term. Absence: buying another course out of fear of falling behind rather than interest; agreeing to a meeting you're tired of in advance; eating from boredom or anxiety rather than hunger [#566].
2. Ethical balance — living in accord with yourself. How far actions match values; acting against what we believe literally drains energy. Absence: saying family is the main thing but being on the phone every evening; claiming self-care matters but continuing to choose work; believing in honesty but regularly saying "all good" when it isn't [#566].
3. Attentional balance — you run your mind, not the reverse. The ability to hold focus relaxedly and without effort. Meditation trains exactly this: not getting rid of thoughts but noticing where attention went and bringing it back, without judgement. Absence: five minutes into work you drift to a week-old conversation or scroll reels; while someone talks you formulate your reply instead of grasping meaning; admiring a sunset while replaying the evening plan [#566].
4. Cognitive balance — seeing reality, not your projections. Perceiving the world without overlaying fears, expectations and old stories. Absence: partner doesn't reply for two hours and we "know" they're angry and all is bad; the boss didn't look our way so we'll surely be fired; we're told "not bad" and hear "not good enough" [#566].
5. Emotional balance — don't suppress, don't drown. Not always being calm, but avoiding extremes — neither hypersensitivity where any trifle knocks you off course, nor apathy where nothing touches you. Absence: every remark is a blow, every uncertainty a catastrophe; or the opposite — no crying, no joy, no feeling [#566].
6. Spiritual balance — knowing who you are. Not "I'm an introvert and like coffee" but understanding your nature, your place and how you relate to the rest of reality. The highest joy, eudaimonia, arises here — not from pleasures or achievements but from the sense of living in accord with something real. Absence: living by inertia without asking why; doing things because "that's how it's done"; achieving and feeling empty — success without meaning [#566].
Katya calls the set "ultimate": developing these six can make one truly happy. None can be bought, downloaded or obtained over a weekend, but all can be developed through gradual practice [#566].
How to develop it (follow-up). It is a practice — gradual, preferably with support (Katya says she herself came to independent practice only after several years) and in an environment where you can discuss it and ask a competent person. The goal is eudaimonic happiness, built not on pleasures and achievements but on meaning, clarity, gratitude and stability. Her evidence: she took Lobsang and Anya Bichevskaya's happiness course right after moving to Miami while in a depressive episode and still became happier — "даже по замерам, которые я делала до и после, не только по субъективным ощущениям" (even by measurements I took before and after, not only by subjective feeling); she also took their philosophical-intelligence lab and still practises what she found there [#567].

#### Three conative imbalances (hyperactivity, dysfunction, deficit)

Purpose / context. Presented in the course Cultivating Emotional Balance (Tibet House NY), which Katya is taking with her teacher Lobsang. The course examines the criteria of psychological equilibrium and began with conative balance. Definitions given first [#720]:
- Conation («Конация») is "способность желать, стремиться, намереваться" (the ability to desire, strive, intend) — all our "want", "must", "going to". In essence a compass: desires set the direction in which we will spend time, strength and attention [#720].
- Conative intelligence: "Умение мудро выбирать желания, отсеивать те, что ведут не туда, и укреплять те, что ведут к благополучию, называют конативной разумностью." (The skill of wisely choosing desires, filtering out those that lead the wrong way and strengthening those that lead to wellbeing, is called conative intelligence.) [#720].
The lecture analysed in detail what causes conative imbalances; Katya says she finally understood what in her had been arranged so that she felt unhappy for so many years, and invites readers to recognise themselves [#720].

1. Conative hyperactivity — too many desires.
   - *What it is:* an excess of desires, often impulsive [#720].
   - *Impulsive form (Katya's case):* she once believed freedom meant eating anything, smoking, drinking and denying herself nothing — "Хочу торт, хочу еще бокал, хочу еще одну вечеринку" (I want cake, I want another glass, I want one more party). Every desire was fulfilled at the first call, yet she did not feel free — or happy [#720].
   - *All-at-once form:* simultaneously wanting everything — lose weight, start running, accept herself, get enough sleep — "Все одновременно, все срочно, все важно" (Everything at once, everything urgent, everything important). The outcome: no real progress in anything, and accumulating evidence for "со мной что-то не так" (something is wrong with me) [#720].
   - *Never-satisfied form:* when something did work she did not notice it and was never satisfied. Started sleeping a steady eight hours? "Зато двигаюсь недостаточно" (But I don't move enough). Introduced one habit and was immediately upset it wasn't three [#720].
   - *Mechanism / why it fails:* if there is never any satisfaction, why continue? The result is relapse, self-pity and "старые добрые паттерны" (the good old patterns) [#720].

2. Conative dysfunction — clear desires that lead the wrong way.
   - *What it is:* desires are understandable and clear, but they lead in the wrong direction [#720].
   - *Katya's case:* she was sure that once she lost weight and achieved things she would become happy, and chased this for years even though body and psyche clearly signalled "stop, wrong way". Result: 20 years of an eating disorder (РПП) and burnout, after which she was out of breath for another year and a half and could not work at all for three years [#720].
   - *Two kinds of mismatch:* her goals did not match what she actually wanted, and they did not "land" on reality either (she notes she had recently written about why she will never be thin) [#720].
   - *Goal–behaviour gap:* the "funniest" part — even when she told herself she wanted to be healthy, her actions did not lead to health: skipping a meal, staying up late, postponing exercise until Monday — and she did not even notice it. "Цель жила в одной комнате, поведение в соседней, а двери между ними не было." (The goal lived in one room, behaviour in the next, and there was no door between them.) [#720].

3. Conative deficit — when wanting does not work.
   - *What it is:* the inability to want. Katya says she did not have this one but often sees it in her coaching work [#720].
   - *How it shows up:* she asks clients "А что бы вы хотели изменить в своем образе жизни?" (What would you like to change in your lifestyle?) and gets silence — even though people around them could easily throw out twenty ideas: go to bed earlier, move more, eat vegetables, work with emotions [#720].
   - *Distinguish from depression:* in depression the absence of desires is one of the symptoms, and that is the domain of psychiatrists and psychotherapists. Conative deficit is different: the person is generally okay but has long stopped believing anything can change, or is so exhausted that no resource remains for wanting itself [#720].
   - *Consequence:* "Желалка сломалась, и мы застреваем в рутинах, которые нам не служат." (The wanter broke, and we get stuck in routines that don't serve us.) [#720].

#### Four questions for auditing desires, goals and actions

Purpose / when to use. Katya's answer to "what changed before my life changed" (she has said before that her life turned 180 degrees). It is a check of the link between what you want, why, and where it actually leads; she now runs her desires and actions through regular "revision" against clearly drawn goals [#720].
1. What does being happy mean for me? — define your own target, not an assumed one [#720].
2. How do I feel in such moments? — ground the definition in the actual felt experience of happiness [#720].
3. Does the desire that has just arisen really lead to what I call happiness? — test each new desire against the definition at the moment it appears [#720].
4. Does what I do every day lead me to happiness? — test daily behaviour, closing the goal–behaviour gap described under conative dysfunction [#720].

Why it works (author's account). When she answered these honestly and thoughtfully, "все встало на место" (everything fell into place). She found that thin does not equal happy — "а я худела до 48 кг — я точно знаю" (and I lost weight down to 48 kg — I know for sure) — and efficient does not equal content with oneself. For years she had acted as if one automatically gives the other and never once checked that logic [#720].

Result. Her goals are now clearly drawn — to be healthy and happy — and desires and actions regularly undergo revision, "Чтобы больше не возникало вопроса «А как мы здесь оказались?»" (So that the question "How did we end up here?" never comes up again). In the end she did get into a shape she likes and produces regular quality and quantity in client work and the blog — "Но как следствие, а не как цель, ради которой я хочу себя ломать." (But as a consequence, not as a goal for which I want to break myself.) [#720].

Pitfall / caveat. P.S.: "Это не призыв ничего не хотеть и ни к чему не стремиться." (This is not a call to want nothing and strive for nothing.) It is an invitation to check the chain: what I want, why I want it, and where it actually leads me. She closes by asking readers whether they have regular practices of reconciling desires, goals and actions and whether they recognise one of the three distortions [#720].

### Detailed insights

#### Different positions on the channel itself
The channel holds conflicting views side by side, deliberately. Katya presents her lagom position "для контраста с нашими героинями" (for contrast with our heroines), against guests who argue for contrast or say balance prevents success [#54]; she then forwards a post titled "Забыть о балансе или искусство адаптивности" (Forget balance, or the art of adaptivity) [#153] and one arguing that balance comes from extremes [#171]. #### Balance and success
Two positions on whether balance hampers success: guest Alena (as Katya reports) said that with balance you won't succeed in any direction; Katya responds that this fear only arises in a system where success is mandatory [#54]. In the speed-limit post, by contrast, Katya says noticing inner speeding is necessary for "truly successful" life and long-term success [#500].

#### From "thin and efficient" to "healthy and happy"
For almost 20 years Katya had two main desires: to be thin and to be efficient. For them she dieted, "tuned" herself in every photo and built a marketing career as if her life depended on it — and, she notes wryly, constantly felt unhappy. Now she wants something else, to be healthy and happy, and "(страшно сказать) получается" (*(scary to say) it's working*). The post title frames this reversal as the achievement: "Я многого добилась: я больше не хочу худеть и быть эффективной" (I have achieved a lot: I no longer want to lose weight and be efficient) [#720].

#### Freedom revisited
The hyperactivity story updates Katya's earlier freedom essay (*Freedom as conscious necessity* [#352]): the belief that freedom is indulging every impulse produced instant fulfilment of each desire but no felt freedom or happiness — here explained as an excess of impulsive desires rather than as a question of choice and consequences [#720].

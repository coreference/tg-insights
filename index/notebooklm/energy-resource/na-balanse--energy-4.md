## A year of the energy diary

### What this covers

This topic follows a longitudinal self-tracking experiment on the channel. It starts with a guest post by Lena Movshina about six weeks of tracking energy in a calendar (Nov 2024), then Katya's two-week time diary (Dec 2024), and then Katya's energy diary kept through all of 2025, with quarterly reports in March, July and October, a year-end summary in January 2026 and a post about a slump in January 2026. Each report has its findings, what she changed, and what she still doesn't know. According to the source, it matters because "Нам часто кажется, что мы все про себя знаем, но это не так" (We often think we know everything about ourselves, but that's not so). Honest numbers and daily notes turn vague feelings ("I work too much", "I am weak", "I'm full of energy") into data that you can act on [#225, #246, #514].

### Core ideas

- Counting brings calm and correction. Lena found that she works plenty, and Katya found that she does not work "all the time" but is simply very tired. Both conclusions came from honest counting, not from feelings. The details are in *Traffic-light calendar* and *Two-week time diary audit* [#225, #246].
- Fragmentation drains more than workload. Task-switching, cramming things between meetings and checking chats wear Katya out more than the tasks themselves do. It is a finding she repeats from December 2024 through to January 2026 [#246, #290, #347, #459].
- The energy scale is miscalibrated by high expectations. Katya lives at a "medium charge" of 60-75%. She later realised that 70-80% *is* her 100%, and that you only see the real scale by comparison. The details are in *Recalibrating the energy scale* [#290, #347, #439, #514].
- Thoughts and emotions are energy leaks. Anxiety, guilt, self-pity, anticipatory suffering and loss of meaning take more energy than lack of sleep or physical load [#290, #297, #351, #439, #514].
- Energy capacity can grow if the basics (sleep, food, quiet, rituals) are in place and you keep a "research mode" of noticing where you sag. The details are in *Energy capacity grows like a muscle* [#347, #514].
- Effects are delayed. A dip comes 1-2 days after the load, and stress can arrive 3-4 weeks later [#290, #514].
- Slumps are normal. The skill is noticing them early. The goal is not "never relapse" but to notice the откат sooner and recover. The details are in *Slump (откат) recovery protocol* [#520].

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### Traffic-light calendar (pacing "светофор" method, digital version) — Lena Movshina
Background and purpose. Guest author Lena Movshina had for years heard an anxious inner voice: "Ты недостаточно стабильная и выносливая, ты слабая, ты делаешь мало" (You're not stable and resilient enough, you're weak, you do too little). Neither years of therapy nor success at work fully helped; she performed well, but any calm moment could turn into an "anxious hell", and she was even diagnosed with an anxiety disorder. A year before the post she learned she has autism (РАС) and ADHD (СДВГ). While researching how to live with this she learned about pacing, "систему менеджемента энергии, которая помогает распределять силы равномернее и сохранять предсказуемый темп работы" (an energy-management system that helps distribute effort more evenly and keep a predictable work pace). She used the "traffic light" pacing method for 6 weeks, after first converting it into a digital version. Katya introduces the post by saying that, although Lena is neurodivergent, in Katya's view what Lena writes may be useful to anyone [#225].

Steps:
1. Use Google Calendar as the tracking surface. Mark time slots in different colours [#225, img #225].
2. Colour-code each slot. Mark energy spending in red, energy replenishment in green, and everything else in yellow. The aim is to "assemble a traffic light" every day [#225].
3. Record after the fact, not as a plan. Lena did not plan work and rest in advance. She marked hours "по факту" (as they actually happened) so she could study her *natural* pace [#225].
4. Ask three research questions of the data. How much do I really work? Do I set aside time for rest, or skip it? How pronounced is my "takeoff-crash" (взлет-падение) cycle? [#225]
5. Label slots in plain, honest language. The screenshot of her calendar for a week in Aug-Sep 2024 shows entries such as "ворк" (work), "сплю" (sleeping), "существую в благодати" (existing in grace), "стресс", "подустала" (got a bit tired), "откисаю" (recovering/soaking), "отмокаю в ванне" (soaking in the bath), "вечер с пиццей теликом и книжкой" (evening with pizza, TV and a book), "ворк в мыле" (working in a lather), plus calls, a massage and surfing. Each has a time range [#225].

Results Lena reports:
1. She learned how many hours a week she can really work. It was "не мало" (not little), and she "fundamentally calmed down": "Против цифр не попрешь" (You can't argue with numbers). Her bands are: 12-20 hours a week is spread across the week almost unnoticeably. 30 hours is comfortably intensive. 40 effective hours is super-intensive. 50 effective hours a week in hyperfocus is her threshold, which she can sustain for about a week if a break follows [#225].
2. She planned a freelance earning schedule based on the hours she actually has. Before, she feared that counting hours would show *zero* and she would be "exposed". "Честный подсчет потребовал смелости, но и дал много уверенности" (Honest counting took courage, but also gave a lot of confidence) [#225].
3. She came to respect her natural pace. She really does need relaxed weeks and ASAP weeks. Demanding evenness from herself is uncomfortable, and she no longer thinks she has to be even [#225].

Verdict and extensions. Lena gives energy tracking 10/10. She notes that a calendar can be used to study other things too. She previously used it to measure how much time she spends mindlessly zoning out, and what time of day she prefers to work. Katya recommends the "calendar experiment" to anyone with a feeling of endless inadequacy [#225].

#### Two-week time diary audit with category breakdown and next steps — Katya, Dec 2024
Purpose. Katya had tried this several times without success. She did it now because she wanted to know how "balanced" she really was, and because her 2025 goal was to learn to earn money. That meant figuring out *when* to do it, given that "сейчас и так времени не хватает примерно ни на что" (right now there's already time for roughly nothing) [#246].

Method:
1. Pick two typical weeks. [#246]
2. On working days, write everything you do into a note from 6:00 to 18:00-19:00. [#246]
3. Categorise and compute shares. Her categories were Self-care, Food & cooking, Social media, and Work, with Work split into Content, Clients and Development. She quotes ChatGPT's commentary on the breakdown, which suggests she used ChatGPT for the analysis [#246, img #248].
4. For each finding, write a "Next steps" list. [#246]

Her breakdown (card titled "На что я тратила время в 2024" (What I spent time on in 2024)) [#248]:
- Self-care: 24.50% (20.5 h). This counts only mindfulness practices, sport and walks, not sleep, showering or eating.
- Food and cooking: 18.15% (15 h).
- Social media: 10.23% (9 h).
- Work: 46% (39 h), made up of Content 17.91% (14.97 h), Clients 19.71% (16.47 h) and Development 8.50% (7.10 h).

The five findings and their next steps:
1. "Была уверена, что очень много работаю. Оказалось, что просто очень сильно устаю." (I was sure I work a lot. It turned out I'm just very tired.) Time splits roughly evenly: work 46%, everything else 54%. She used to fantasise that working 4 hours a day would be great, and it turned out she works on average 4 h 10 min a day. The feeling of working all the time comes from how work is spread through the day, the urge to "cram in the uncrammable", and jumping between tasks. *Next steps:* experiment with the schedule, don't stuff everything into one day, and give different days to different tasks. She has long wanted to try Tim Ferriss's method [#246, img #247].
2. Time spent on herself is more than time spent on clients or on creating content. Self-care at 24.5% is the largest category. She quotes ChatGPT: "Это положительный тренд, свидетельствующий о том, что вы понимаете важность восстановления" (This is a positive trend showing you understand the importance of recovery). Lesson: when she feels like pitying herself (which she calls her "psychological Everest for 2025"), some self-pity is fine, but she should not let "toxic pity" take over, because she spends a lot of time on self-care. *Next steps:* work on self-pity in therapy. If it feels like "I'm working too much", review the schedule and the ways she recovers. "похоже, дело не в количестве, а в качестве" (it seems it's not about quantity but quality) [#246].
3. More time goes to social-media scrolling than to development, finances and study. It is about 9 hours a week, which she extrapolates to 34 hours a month and 408 hours (18 days) a year. *Next steps:* build social-media hygiene (she already doesn't read it in the mornings), set a separate time for reading chats and checking DMs, and stop "polishing" every activity with a visit to social media [#246].
4. Very little time goes to development, and she procrastinates through routine work. Development means thinking up new projects and building a site, bot or infrastructure, and it gets 8.5%. She regularly procrastinates with "useful actions", namely writing content; this very post was written instead of making an offer for online sales. Content takes 18% and, she says, leads nowhere in terms of the financial results that matter to her now. *Next steps:* raise development time to 15%, ideally 20%, in 2025. That needs work on mindset as well as planning: learn to think about development, wake up with those thoughts, not fear and not doubt. For this she went to a coach and resumed therapy [#246].
5. "еда — моя вторая работа" (food is my second job) is no joke. Cooking and eating take 18%, or 15 hours a week, which she puts at "30 days a year". *Next steps:* keep optimising through meal prep. When she first started cooking 1.5 years earlier, the first time in her life, it took 1.5-2 times longer and she "super-suffered" [#246].

Headline lesson. The title says she "achieved the ideal work/rest balance, but it turned out that isn't what I need". The roughly 50/50 split did not stop her from feeling overloaded, so the problem was distribution, switching and quality of recovery rather than quantity [#246, img #246, img #247].

#### Energy diary with ChatGPT analysis prompt — Katya, from early 2025
Why she started. 2025 had two focuses, work and recovery. In January she gained five new clients. In February she added study in English (coaching in English twice a week). Calls multiplied, which she links to "Zoom-fatigue", and she started an energy diary. A photo of her on a Zoom call illustrates the post. By March she had her first observations [#290, img #290].

How the diary is analysed. Katya gives ChatGPT the diary with this prompt [#291]:
- "Проанализируй мой дневник энергии:" (Analyse my energy diary:)
- "какие видишь тренды?" (what trends do you see?)
- "что влияет на спад энергии?" (what causes energy dips?)
- "что помогает поднять энергию?" (what helps raise energy?)
- "какие основные инсайты я выносила (с прямыми цитатами)" (what main insights did I draw, with direct quotes)
- "что вообще можно сказать о моей энергии за последний месяц?" (what can be said overall about my energy over the last month?)

Diary entries Katya shared as examples (Q1), each showing the kind of observation the diary captures:
- *Rushing is not caused by calls.* "Поняла, что чувство спешки НЕ из-за звонков, а из-за того, что переживаю, что не успеваю «другое» - и пытаюсь засовывать что-то между." (I realised the feeling of rush is NOT from the calls, but from worrying I'm not managing "other things" and trying to squeeze something in between.) Proposed decision: when there are many calls in a row, do nothing else [#292].
- *Not giving in to anxiety at night.* She woke up in anxiety right after falling asleep and put herself back to sleep with a book. She is proud that she doesn't follow the anxiety and doesn't turn on TV series at night [#293].
- *Switching again.* "Опять скакала между задачами и чатами." (Again jumped between tasks and chats.) [#294]
- *Stories as fake activity.* She noticed that on a low charge she starts wanting to post many Instagram stories, "Как будто это имитация деятельности, надо что-то кому-то доказывать" (As if it's an imitation of activity, a need to prove something to someone) [#295].
- *Fear as a constant.* In a Kundalini session she strongly felt fear, first about clients who might not come, then about study where she must practise in English. "Как будто страх - моя естественная реакция на все. Наверное, это очень по-человечески." (As if fear is my natural reaction to everything. Probably that's very human.) [#296]
- *Self-pity versus resisting it.* She realised "for the hundredth time" that a lot of energy goes into self-pity *and* into resisting it. All week she felt "poor and miserable" but gave herself no break, and "Это противостояние не дает делать вообще ничего" (This standoff makes it impossible to do anything at all) [#297].

#### Ten unexpected energy-diary findings (Q1 2025)
Katya deliberately leaves out the obvious ones, such as "less sleep, lower energy" or "low energy coincides with an emotional dip". She stresses that knowing something and *feeling it in yourself and starting to change* are different things. Main insight: "Я не просто устаю, а живу в состоянии среднего заряда — обычно 60-75%" (I'm not just tired, I live in a state of medium charge, usually 60-75%). She used to think she was bursting with energy, but the diary showed otherwise. The ten findings [#290]:
1. She doesn't jump up full of energy in the morning. It is often hard at first, but morning rituals energise her.
2. It is not the tasks that tire her but the constant switching between them.
3. When a list of "musts" (надо) piles up, procrastination in social media and an energy drop begin.
4. If she doesn't insert tasks between meetings, energy holds better.
5. One comment on social media can affect her concentration for several hours.
6. The dip comes not at the moment of load but 1-2 days later.
7. Anxiety and overload take more energy than lack of sleep.
8. Guilt ("я должна больше", *I should do more*) drains more than tiredness does. When she lets herself rest, for example lying around with a book, energy rises on its own.
9. Unfinished tasks drain energy even when she isn't working on them.
10. Even when checking chats and social media seems harmless, 30 minutes later she is scattered, and the more often she checks social media, the more her energy jumps around.

Open questions she set herself: Does she not know how to rest fully? Or has she not yet adapted to the load? Is she draining energy mindlessly? Or does she hold a belief, "я не могу долго быть в ресурсе" (I can't stay resourced for long), that keeps her body in the middle range? She also noticed that her tiredness comes in very different kinds, which show up differently and need different recovery. She planned to explore this the following month [#290].

#### Energy changes that worked (Q2 2025)
In July Katya reported mid-year highlights under the title "«Мудрость в отношении энергии — знать, когда перестать в себя пихать»" (Wisdom about energy is knowing when to stop stuffing things into yourself) [#347]:
1. Hearing the body earlier. "Тело будто заговорило понятнее — или я научилась слушать." (The body seems to speak more clearly, or I've learned to listen.) She now senses an approaching dip in advance and can catch and soften it. "Lying around and zoning out" is now needed for at most a day or two, where before it stretched to a week.
2. Recalibrating 100%. This is set out in *Recalibrating the energy scale*.
3. Doing less, getting the same. Fuss and constant switching between small tasks were eating her battery. She cut her daily plan in half and her results did not drop. Instead, pauses of silence appeared. 5-15 minutes with closed eyes, or massage glasses between calls, give more than imitating vigorous activity or chaotic scrolling.
4. Almost no "musts" in the calendar. Some tasks could simply be crossed out, and the rest she reframed ("I'm a coach, after all"). Cooking and cleaning turned from bondage into "выдох и поделать что-то руками" (an exhale and doing something with my hands) between Zooms, and "learn to cook deliciously" even appeared in her 20-year strategy.
5. Best call-free day: Wednesday. Five days of 3-5 calls each was obviously "suicide". She first tried a "methodical Monday", which didn't help, because by Thursday she was squeezed out. A free Wednesday gives a breather mid-week and a sense that work other than client calls keeps moving.
6. Less content, more birding. In spring content physically nauseated her: she would switch on a podcast and couldn't listen, or open a post and couldn't read. She compares it to cigarettes, where lighting up brought not pleasure but self-disgust and "зачем ты это с собой делаешь, Катя?" (why are you doing this to yourself, Katya?). She unsubscribed from almost all podcasts and Telegram channels (Instagram was already cleaned). Nothing collapsed, and room appeared for walks with binoculars (birdwatching) and inspiration.

Why she knows it works: her energy capacity and "Zoom tolerance" grew. What seemed impossible in January, 3-5 meetings a day, became normal, and she did not burn out and even feels good. "Кажется, «мышцы энергии» действительно прокачиваются." (It seems "energy muscles" really can be trained.) [#347]

Diary quotes she shared from Q2:
- She keeps trying to cram far more into her days and weeks than is comfortable and is permanently in "hyper-productivity" mode, while she wants to be calm most of the time. She notes this also depends on goal-setting and asks herself: "Почему я все время недовольна? Почему мне все время недостаточно?" (Why am I always dissatisfied? Why is it never enough for me?) [#347]
- "Делать то, что нужно и важно. И не переживать по поводу скорости." (Do what's needed and important. And don't worry about speed.) The expectation that she must do everything fast and all at once is very draining and devalues what is worth doing. She wants to remember the principle "тише едешь, дальше будешь" (*slow and steady wins the race*, literally "the quieter you go, the further you get") [#348].
- No content gets in any more; it is "coming out of my ears", and it seems there is "nothing interesting" [#349].
- "My time" or "time for myself" should be without the phone. It sounds trite, but over the last week she really felt it, to the point of not even wanting to pick up the phone [#350].
- "Страдать заранее - плохая идея." (Suffering in advance is a bad idea.) More than once she has pitied herself in the morning and suffered, and then meetings got cancelled and the day turned out completely different [#351].

#### Recalibrating the energy scale (real 100%)
This is a model Katya built over the year about how to read your own charge:
1. Start by measuring, not assuming. In March the diary showed she lives at 60-75%, although she had believed she was bursting with energy [#290].
2. Notice that your "norm" is inflated by expectations. For a couple of months she calibrated wrongly: "норма" felt like fireworks at 150%. She recognised this as her constant life companion, inflated expectations. "70–80 % — и есть мои 100 %." (70-80% is my 100%.) [#347]
3. Define a realistic 100% by concrete markers: a clear head, lightness in the body, no urge to collapse on the sofa, and inner resilience to triggers [#347].
4. Re-read the scale in hindsight. In the stressful Q3 she saw that what she had called "80%" earlier was actually the full 100. "Поняла, что настоящую шкалу энергии осознаешь только в сравнении." (I realised you only grasp the real energy scale by comparison.) In autumn she was in the "yellow zone", but without resistance or breakdowns [#439].
5. Stop waiting for a fountain. In the year-end summary: "мои реальные 100 % — это не фонтан, а в лучшем случае ручей с более ли менее стабильным напором" (my real 100% is not a fountain but at best a stream with a more or less stable flow). She no longer waits for a surge of inspiration to do tasks, support clients, write posts or be with loved ones. A basic minimum is enough, and she can run on it for a very long time. "Это понижение ожиданий от себя и своей энергии, парадоксально, дает огромное количество энергии." (This lowering of expectations of myself and my energy, paradoxically, gives a huge amount of energy.) [#514]

#### Four horsemen of the energy apocalypse
Origin. Katya's client Sveta Shedina named these in a video. The phrase "добываем энергию, как нефть" (we extract energy like oil) is what the channel does too. Katya uses the Welltory app and tracks on her watch how her energy drains. Her photo shows a watch "BATTERY" graph falling from about 100 at a 5:26 AM wake-up to 49% by 2:56 on a Wednesday. Energy does not drain evenly: some actions conserve charge during the day, and some burn it out completely, sometimes before lunch [#459, img #459].

Sveta's four horsemen:
1. Multitasking.
2. Ignoring your emotional states.
3. Ignoring mini-rests.
4. "Впихивание невпихуемого" (cramming in the uncrammable).

Katya's own top list: numbers 3 and 4, plus back-to-back Zoom meetings, information noise disguised as a search for inspiration, and rushing. "Если что-то из этого происходит, значит всадники несут меня на энергетическое дно." (If any of this is happening, the horsemen are carrying me to the energy bottom.) She will most likely not feel rested that evening, or even the next day. She invites readers to name their own horsemen [#459].

#### Slump (откат) recovery protocol — January 2026
Context. In January 2026 Katya again failed to notice she had drained herself. She calls it an oxymoron to burn out while running a group called «Отдохнувшие» ("The Rested"), as funny as getting stress (stomach pain) from "Magnesium anti-stress". Contributing factors: winter, the stress of moving, the stress of a new project, and atmospheric pressure in Moscow. She had not felt high pressure for five years and now lies flat every week [#520].

Why it happened: the load she added without noticing, alongside the joy, meaning and drive of the new project [#520]:
- She took away her full day off. A Saturday call of 2-2.5 hours plus preparation makes a working day.
- She added being present in the group chat, and had not read so many messages so thoughtfully in a long time.
- She started reworking and reassembling the programme "on the fly", because she wants content that is relevant, not content for its own sake.
- Plus "the classic set": doubts, anxiety, responsibility and self-flagellation over "am I giving enough".

Detection. By mid-January she found herself burnt out. Attention to her energy and continuous diary-keeping, which she still does and shares with the group, helped her see the problem quickly [#520].

What she did once she saw the slump:
1. She took her day off back: until the end of February, Thursday daytime is free.
2. She strengthened recovery with banya, massage and rituals, including a massage in the banya for the first time.
3. She stopped scheduling social meetings straight after big work meetings.
4. She again made time alone with herself a priority.
5. She planned a mini-study of how much energy goes into chats.

Framing. Through the lens of the theory of change she described in December, she was simply at a logical stage of any change: the rollback (откат), which happens to everyone at every stage. "Фокус тут не в том, чтобы «никогда не откатываться» — это утопия. Фокус в навыке раньше замечать, что ты в откате." (The focus isn't on "never rolling back", that's a utopia. The focus is the skill of noticing earlier that you're in a rollback.) The trap to avoid is getting stuck in the familiar pit of "господи, у меня опять не получилось - какая я никчемная - сколько можно" (God, I failed again, how worthless I am, how much longer). "Отряхнулась — и пошла дальше." (Dusted myself off and moved on.) The adult version of change, in her words, is not "I never fall" but "я быстрее понимаю, что упала, и знаю, как встать" (I realise faster that I've fallen, and I know how to get up). Repetition is the mother of learning, and each cycle makes you a little less harsh with yourself and a little more attentive [#520].

### Detailed insights

#### Q3 2025: anxiety as the dominant energy drain
- The context. July 2025 was her peak of stability and calm for the year, and she recorded a podcast about it. By the end of July her husband had lost his job and a new emigration was looming. "Если говорить по Маслоу — у моей пирамиды выбили фундамент." (In Maslow's terms, the foundation of my pyramid was knocked out.) She learned about her reactions and how much she still has to grow, especially in emotional resilience and trust in life [#439].
- "Я уже и забыла, сколько энергии жрет тревога." (I had already forgotten how much energy anxiety eats.) The brain confuses uncertainty with danger. Even when floating or walking she could not relax, because the inner "Tales from the Crypt" switched on: the critic and the controller. A diary entry reads: "Из меня херачит стресс. Очень сложно не фиксироваться на “что будет”. Сложно признать, что я узнаю это только тогда, когда это произойдет." (Stress is pouring out of me. It's very hard not to fixate on "what will happen". Hard to admit I'll only find out when it happens.) She cried, couldn't sleep, had crazy dreams, and woke at 5:30. The app said she slept fine, at 86, and she went for a run [#439].
- Self-added uncertainty. In therapy she realised that, on top of the external uncertainty, she was adding her own: she hired an assistant, was preparing new products, and cut client hours. "Все это прыжки веры, и страшно. Но, кажется, удачных моментов не бывает." (All these are leaps of faith, and it's scary. But it seems there are no good moments.) [#439]
- Sleep, caffeine, supplements (she labels this "не является врачебной рекомендацией", *not medical advice*). Autumn needs more sleep, which she already knew, and with anxiety sleep simply "steps out for a smoke", something she hadn't experienced for 1.5 years. Diary: "Просыпаюсь ночью, колбасит, в горле ком, в голове каша, не могу спать…" (I wake at night, I'm shaking, lump in my throat, mush in my head, can't sleep…). Under this stress she had for some reason stopped taking magnesium and omega, and her sleep fell fully into the red zone. Restarting the supplements brought RHR and HRV back to normal and sleep back to the green zone. On caffeine: under anxiety it "literally throws fuel on the fire". After a few sips her heart pounds, her chest tightens and breathing gets hard. She cut the dose from 12 to 4 grams and now has no "special effects" [#439].
- Less energy means less inspiration, but routines keep her afloat. She runs, writes and reads serious books less. Anxiety pushes her toward escapism: detective novels instead of philosophy (for the first time in years) and series instead of silence. These are at least safe forms of escape, but she sees her growth point as "не убегать, а смотреть тревоге в глаза" (not running away but looking anxiety in the eye). The routines that hold even without inspiration are morning exercise, meditation, a walk, floating, normal food and intervals of silence. These, she says, have kept her resourced for two months and moving forward confidently [#439].
- Perspective. Things that worried her in January, such as "the number of calls", are now out of the picture. She wants to be wiser, look at herself from the future, and remember the mantra "Так будет не всегда" (It won't always be like this). She is also fully back in therapy [#439].
- Value of keeping the diary all year. She is happy she kept it all year: "вижу настоящую перспективу, а не только сиюминутные колебания. Становится меньше иллюзий, больше ясности." (I see the real perspective, not just momentary fluctuations. Fewer illusions, more clarity.) [#439]

#### Energy capacity grows like a muscle (year-end conclusion)
A year earlier she suddenly became in demand, with 5 new clients in January, and she was literally shaking at the thought of two meetings a day. It seemed a ceiling beyond which lay rollback, tears and "I can't do this any more". Now she has days with four meetings, which she says she does *not* recommend, plus project sales, new product development, and preparing podcasts and talks in parallel: "Это много. Это правда много. Но я не умерла. Не сломалась. Не лежу пластом." (It's a lot. It really is a lot. But I didn't die. Didn't break. I'm not lying flat.) Now completely different volumes scare her. Condition: if your base is built (sleep, food, quiet, rituals) and research mode is on, meaning you keep a hand on the pulse and notice in time where you are sagging, you can carefully go beyond your usual limits and train your energy like muscles. She had written this idea years earlier when founding the channel as "a pretty POV". Now it is "прожитой факт" (a lived fact) [#514].

#### Self-limitation through thoughts (year-end conclusion)
She has repeatedly caught herself de-energising herself in advance. If she thinks in the morning "Oh God, four meetings today", energy is often gone by 9 a.m. If she goes into the day without expectations, drama or a script of "how hard this will be", she often ends the day in the plus. "Мысли — это не просто мысли. Это прямые утечки энергии." (Thoughts aren't just thoughts. They are direct energy leaks.) She now tries to tune into each day neutrally, with the reminder "я. не знаю. как. пройдет. день. и. как. мне. будет." (I. don't. know. how. the. day. will. go. or. how. I'll. feel.) This builds on the Q2 diary note "Suffering in advance is a bad idea" [#514, img #351].

#### Further year-end findings
- Emotions and loss of meaning knock her out worse than physical load [#514].
- Seasonality, PMS and the full moon are "не эзотерика, а реальные циклы" (not esotericism but real cycles) that affect her energy [#514].
- Effects reach her with a delay. Sleep deprivation, too much socialising or a quarrel show up only after a few days, and stress after as much as 3-4 weeks [#514].
- What restores her best is time alone with herself [#514].
- After the slump: "если выстроить отношения с нагрузкой, восстановлением, режимом, — энергии больше, чем кажется. Просто она не бесконечная и не «бесплатная»" (if you build a relationship with load, recovery and routine, there's more energy than it seems. It's just not infinite and not "free") [#520].

#### Culture of admitting fatigue
Katya says the hardest part of a slump is admitting it, to the group, to readers and above all to herself: that she has less energy now and is not all-powerful or perfect. Inside lives the myth that if you are "about resources, resilience, mindfulness" you must always be in perfect shape, and even more so as a woman, compared with the "girls with perfect hair and heels" on Instagram. Her stated aim: "я хочу поддерживать культуру, где не стыдно сказать: «мне тяжело», «я устала», «мне нужен отдых»" (I want to support a culture where it's not shameful to say "it's hard", "I'm tired", "I need rest"), where you don't have to pretend to be superhuman to have the right to a pause. "Если вы сейчас в похожем месте, вы не сломались. Вы просто живете." (If you're in a similar place now, you haven't broken. You're just living.) [#520]

### Practical playbook

- Social-media hygiene (from the time audit): don't read social media in the mornings, set a separate time for chats and DMs, and don't "polish" each task with a social-media check [#246].
- Call-heavy days: don't fit other tasks between calls. Decide that on many-calls days you do nothing else [#290, img #292].
- Weekly structure: keep one call-free day mid-week (Wednesday worked; Monday didn't). During the January 2026 slump, a free Thursday daytime served the same purpose [#347, #520].
- Micro-recovery between calls: 5-15 minutes with eyes closed, or massage glasses [#347].
- Halve the daily plan. In her experience results stayed the same [#347].
- Phone-free "my time" [#350].
- Neutral morning framing instead of anticipating a hard day [#514, img #351].
- Don't schedule social meetings right after big work meetings [#520].
- When anxious: keep the base routines going, watch caffeine (it amplifies anxiety symptoms), and don't drop supplements she was already taking. She frames all of this as personal experience, not medical advice [#439].

## Three questions about energy (mini-interviews)

### What this covers

This topic gathers the channel's recurring rubric «Три вопроса про энергию к …» ("Three questions about energy to …"), in which a guest answers the same three questions: how stable her energy is on a 1–10 scale and what it depends on, what drains her energy most, and what her three main ways of restoring it are. Six interviews are covered (Masha Yolkina, Masha Manaeva, Masha Tsegelnik, Olya Malyuk, Vika Rarog, Lena Zhuravleva). Everything below is the guests' personal experience, not general recommendations from the channel. [#233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]

According to the launch post, the rubric exists to give more attention to the channel's second main theme — energy — and to introduce readers to interesting women; the stated aim is to talk about «что помогает поддерживать ресурс, что его забирает, собираем лучшие инструменты для восстановления» (what helps maintain one's resource, what takes it away, and to collect the best tools for recovery). [#233]

### Core ideas

- Sleep is the most-named foundation. Five of six guests explicitly put sleep (quantity, quality, or a sleep schedule) at the base of their energy or name poor sleep as the top drain; Tsegelnik puts it bluntly that no amount of "pumping" energy can trick a lack of sleep. [#233, #279, #312, #614, #657]
- Energy is cyclical, and dips are normal. Several guests tie energy to the menstrual cycle and to seasons; Tsegelnik frames falls in energy as a necessary phase before renewal rather than a failure. [#233, #279, #312, #531]
- Uncertainty, chaos, unmade or doubted decisions and hanging tasks drain energy; planning and clarity restore it. Yolkina, Tsegelnik and Rarog each describe this, with planning as the antidote. [#233, #312, #614]
- Doing what you do not want to do drains; doing what drives you charges. Malyuk (long imbalance of "have to" over "want to") and Zhuravleva (energy falls when doing what she no longer wants) make this point; Zhuravleva adds that driving activities can energise even through 10 hours of contact with people. [#531, #657]
- Movement, nature and walking are the most-cited restorers, followed by solitude/quiet, and — for some guests — trusted conversations with close people. [#233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]
- Overstimulation (noise, crowds, bright screens, phones) drains sensitive and introverted people, who protect themselves with earplugs, greyscale mode, solitude and phone-free time. [#233, #312, #531, #657]
- Self-observation is the meta-skill: noticing body signals, analysing what one actually does, and regular reflection are how the guests learned what works for them. [#279, #531, #614, #657]

### Frameworks, models and step-by-step methods

#### The three-questions-about-energy self-audit (rubric format)
Purpose: a fixed interview template the channel uses with every guest; read as a method, it is a three-question audit anyone can run on their own energy. [#233]
1. «Оцени стабильность своей энергии по шкале от 1 до 10? От чего это зависит?» (Rate the stability of your energy on a scale of 1 to 10. What does it depend on?) — gives a number and forces naming the factors behind it (in the guests' answers: sleep, cycle, season, stress, health, planning, type of work, age). [#233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]
2. «Что отбирает больше всего энергии?» (What takes the most energy?) — identifies the main drains (noise and crowds, uncertainty, lack of choice, forced effort, imbalance of obligations, chaos, poor sleep, poor lifestyle basics). [#233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]
3. «Три главных способа восстановления энергии?» (Three main ways of restoring energy?) — collects the person's top restorers. Yolkina notes it is hard to pick only three. [#233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]

Numbers given by guests in answer to question 1: Tsegelnik ≈8 on average; Malyuk 6–7; Rarog 7–8; Zhuravleva 7–8. Yolkina describes her energy as «довольно стабильный» (fairly stable) when conditions are met but gives no number; Manaeva calls hers «очень нестабильна» (very unstable) without a number. [#233, #279, #312, #531, #614, #657]

#### Masha Yolkina's routine (highly sensitive person)
Masha Yolkina — mindfulness teacher and embodiment facilitator — describes a set of conditions and protections. [#233]
1. Protect sleep. Her energy level depends first of all on the amount and quality of sleep, so she tries not to sacrifice it for other things. [#233]
2. Track the menstrual cycle in the calendar. Energy also depends on the day of the cycle; she enters the first and last weeks of the cycle into her calendar in advance. [#233]
3. Keep stress at a manageable level — including positive stress. Her energy is fairly stable if she sleeps enough, follows her cycle and has «посильное количество стресса (как негативного, так и позитивного)» (a manageable amount of stress, both negative and positive). [#233]
4. Shield against noise and crowds. As «человек с высокой чувствительностью» (a person with high sensitivity), noisy crowded places and loud sounds take the most energy. Her countermeasures: she almost never goes to events with many guests, wears earplugs on public transport, and plans rest breaks after any events and meetings. [#233]
5. Remove uncertainty and hanging items. Uncertainty, «подвисшие дела и разговоры» (pending tasks and conversations) also drain her, so she plans her day and tasks in Google Calendar and tries not to postpone important discussions. [#233]
6. Restorers (more than three): sleep; lying with closed eyes (especially after a long time at the computer); yoga nidra; a hot shower; massage, including self-massage; walks in nature; and at least one day a week with no tasks at all — better two. [#233]

#### Masha Tsegelnik's three restorers
Masha Tsegelnik (CTO-2 at OzonTech, coach, mindfulness organizer) gives a numbered list. [#312]
1. The base: sleep, food, movement — plus cheat codes. She calls sleep, nutrition and movement «база» (the base): «Сколько не качай энергию, недостаток сна не обмануть» (however much you pump up your energy, you can't trick a lack of sleep). But there are «чит-коды» (cheat codes), for example the NSDR (non-sleep deep rest) protocol. [#312]
2. Silence and greyscale mode on the iPhone. It is important for her to be in contact with herself, which is hard amid external noise, so she always carries earplugs. A recent find: greyscale mode on the phone, to remove unnecessary bright stimuli. [#312]
3. Breathing practices — and any other practices that give connection with oneself. Her favourites have a complex rhythm and breath holds; they act «как мягкая перезагрузка системы» (like a soft reboot of the system). (!) INCOMPLETE: she does not specify which breathing techniques, rhythms or hold durations. [#312]

#### Olya Malyuk's movement rotation and want/have-to balance
Olya Malyuk (36, solo entrepreneur, mother of two) defines energy and then describes her system. [#531]
- Definition. She calls energy «физический и эмоциональный настрой двигаться вперед, продуктивно работать и проявлять интерес к жизни во всех ее сферах» (the physical and emotional disposition to move forward, work productively and take interest in life in all its areas). Her rating is 6–7, so she considers herself «довольно нестабильна» (fairly unstable). [#531]
- Mechanism: physical fatigue drags emotional fatigue. She swings between two states, «то я на дне, то на коне» (now I'm at the bottom, now I'm on top). It depends primarily on physiology: not sleeping enough, a lot of work, the start of the cycle, no breather between main work and switching to parenting (children aged 5 and 8). Physical fatigue pulls emotional fatigue behind it — drive disappears and life looks gloomy; conversely, the better she feels physically, the more joy, optimism and desire to create and learn she feels. Hence her conclusion: it is important to be able to hear the body's signals, know what restores it and do it. [#531]
- Main drain: a long skew toward "have to". As a solo entrepreneur with many processes tied to her, some tasks are creative and nourish her (creating new products, finding and interviewing people for the team), while others are operational — uninteresting, even boring, but necessary for the business to live. Doing only necessary, uninspiring tasks for a long time exhausts her, and she gets the feeling «жизнь живет меня, а не я ее» (life is living me, not I it). She tries to keep a healthy balance of «хочу и надо» (want and have to) at work and in other areas of life. [#531]
- Restorer 1 — daily movement, fixed in the morning. A different workout every day, each with a stated purpose: running and skiing — good for the heart and unloads the head; strength training with weights at home or in the gym — to strengthen muscles; yoga — for flexibility and a calm mind; badminton — develops coordination, plus the sheer enjoyment of the game. Because movement «гарантированно восстанавливает» (restores her guaranteed), the workout is scheduled daily in the morning and almost never moved. [#531]
- Restorer 2 — solitude. She is an introvert and tires from external stimuli: talking to people, noisy places, anything demanding outward attention. The best recovery is to be alone, preferably at home, for at least a few hours without rush or plans. She notes that parents of small children will understand this too. [#531]
- Restorer 3 — a walk with a friend. Although everyday contact with people tends to drain her, a trusting conversation on a walk with a close friend fills her up and «возвращает к жизни» (brings her back to life). Several friends live 15 minutes' walk away, and they meet often to support each other or discuss current matters. [#531]

#### Lena Zhuravleva's low-energy planning, detox day and retreat
Lena Zhuravleva (47, psychoanalytic therapist and executive coach, founder of HFLabs/DaData, mother of three). [#657]
- Energy grew with age after a change of activity. At 47 she expected to have less energy but rates it 7–8. It became much greater when she changed occupation (now a practising psychoanalytic therapist and coach) and as her children grew up. When she got tired of the CEO role, her energy noticeably dropped. Her general observation: energy falls when she does what she no longer wants to do. [#657]
- Reflection as the main tool. Therefore «время на рефлексию – главное» (time for reflection is the main thing). She regularly writes for about 15 minutes whatever comes to mind and goes to therapy; this helps her stop, hear and understand herself. [#657]
- Driving activities charge, even if long. Energy appears when doing what drives you: she runs a film club with difficult films and sometimes talks with people for 10 hours — and does not get tired but gets charged. [#657]
- Main drain: poor sleep. At 20 she could skip sleep and work the next day; now she can't — she needs to sleep enough. [#657]
- Living in low-energy mode. She has learned to live in a low-energy mode: when she knows there is little energy today, she plans the day differently and may cancel or postpone something. [#657]
- Restorer 1 — walking. Anywhere, but especially in a forest; afterwards she reliably feels better. On average she walks 12,000 steps. [#657]
- Restorer 2 — a day for herself. She does only what she wants: if she wants to lie down all day, she lies down; if she wants to go to friends, she goes. [#657]
- Restorer 3 — digital detox day. She does not go near gadgets all day. If an idea to do something or call someone comes up, she writes it down on paper. She lies, walks, reads paper books. [#657]
- Extra: a sanatorium as a retreat. When she feels she is getting tired, she goes to a sanatorium for 3–4 days; for her it works as a retreat. The key condition is no phone. On the first day she feels periodic impulses to grab the phone, but then the brain calms down. [#657]

### Detailed insights

#### Masha Manaeva: seasonal energy and the "banal" drains
- Energy is very unstable and tied to season and cycle. Manaeva says her energy is very unstable; this year she made self-exploration her focus, wanting to understand better what gives and what takes away her strength. Energy definitely depends on the time of year and the cycle. She feels good in snowy winter, spring and early summer; by the end of summer energy drops and she wants to go into autumn hibernation. In her view, January–July are the most active months, and from August it is time «собирать урожай» (to "harvest") the efforts made in the first half of the year. [#279]
- The drains are known — people look for other causes. She calls this banal: «Мы все знаем, что забирает у нас больше всего сил, но зачастую ищем какие-то иные причины» (we all know what takes the most strength from us, but often look for some other reasons). Her list: poor nutrition; the phone; a wrong sleep schedule; lack of sport or everyday physical activity; and, in her case, lack of mental (brain) activity. [#279]
- Under-challenge drains too. If things are too easy for her and she isn't solving hard problems, over time she becomes sluggish and inactive. [#279]
- Her three restorers: (1) a walk in nature — alone or with her fiancé; «Природа + движение — самый быстрый и приятный способ восстановиться» (nature + movement is the fastest and most pleasant way to recover); (2) reading; (3) a hot bath, which she takes every other day. [#279]

#### Masha Tsegelnik: cyclical energy and the cost of indecision
- Momentary vs retrospective view. In the moment it often seems to her that her energy jumps a lot, but looking back she sees she has much more energy now than a year ago or earlier — about 8 on average, and it has become noticeably «плотнее» (denser). [#312]
- "We are not machines." She likes the idea that falls in energy are normal: it is cyclicity, like the change of seasons — there must be a moment of decline and stillness for a new birth to happen. [#312]
- Drain 1 — lack of choice and doubt about a decision made. When a choice is made and she accepts it, it immediately becomes easier to focus. [#312]
- Drain 2 — squeezing out more when rest is due. What empties her a lot is the urge to squeeze «ещё немножечко энергии» (a little bit more energy) out of herself when, properly, she should simply rest. [#312]

#### Vika Rarog: noticing what you actually do, and planning as a support
- Reassessing one's own energy. She once considered herself a low-energy person and admired people who seemed to manage everything — work, friends, family, hobbies, sport. Then she analysed how much she actually does and realised she is one of those people. Her lesson: «Главное — замечать то, что с нами происходит, и не обесценивать даже маленькие результаты» (the main thing is to notice what is happening to us and not devalue even small results). [#614]
- What energy depends on. She rates it 7–8 on average for the current period. First of all it depends on health. Planning also helps: when she knows exactly what to do and why, energy seems to be allocated to those tasks and she becomes «машиной, которую не остановить» (an unstoppable machine). [#614]
- Main drain — chaos in tasks and in the head. She likes everything maximally transparent and clear; this calms her. Periods with a lot of uncertainty oppress her, and she feels the oppression physically. The fix is again having a plan: «пусть он будет неидеальным и поменяется сто тысяч раз, но пока он есть, он послужит мне опорой» (let it be imperfect and change a hundred thousand times, but while it exists, it will serve as my support). [#614]
- Restorer 1 — sleep as the axis of life. Her life is built around her sleep schedule, since student days: no cramming exam tickets overnight — everything is learned in advance, and the night before an exam is for sleeping. Now she guards sleep from all disturbing factors to regularly get her norm of 7–9 hours. [#614]
- Restorer 2 — movement. Tennis, home workouts on a mat, and walking her dog 3 times a day. This helps unload worries from the head and recharge with «гормонами удовольствия» (pleasure hormones). [#614]
- Restorer 3 — communication with people. Being with close ones and having a place to discuss important topics gives her a charge of inspiration and a desire to start something new. [#614]

#### Patterns across the six interviews
- Social contact cuts both ways. For Yolkina (crowds) and Malyuk (everyday communication) people drain; yet Malyuk's walk with a close friend, Rarog's time with close ones and Zhuravleva's 10-hour film-club conversations charge them. The distinction the guests draw is between noisy/obligatory contact and trusted or driving contact. [#233, #531, #614, #657]
- Rest days without tasks appear twice: Yolkina's one to two task-free days a week and Zhuravleva's "day for herself". [#233, #657]
- Hot water appears twice: Yolkina's hot shower and Manaeva's hot bath every other day. [#233, #279]
- Earplugs appear twice: Yolkina on transport, Tsegelnik always carrying them. [#233, #312]
- The phone as a drain appears in Manaeva's list of drains, Tsegelnik's greyscale mode and Zhuravleva's detox day and phone-free sanatorium. [#279, #312, #657]
